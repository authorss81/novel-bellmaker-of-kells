# Chapter 0085 — What The District Owes A Household

The round shut at the end of the sixty-sixth and Hester Vowles left this town on the morning of the sixty-seventh, and the two rooms she had over a dyer's on the north side have been standing empty since, with the round's account books open on the table because there was nobody to close them and no officer to close them with.

He found that out from the woman who lets them, at about the seventh hour of the morning of the seventy-first, standing at the bottom of the outside stair with a lamp in her hand and the lodging book under her arm.

“She went before the light on the sixty-seventh,” she said. “**Carried her own bag down. Paid me four nights at four shillings including the fire and would not take the fifth off me, and I would have taken it off her.**”

“You did not take it off her.”

“I did not, and I am not sorry about it, and I am telling you that before you go up those stairs, because you are going to go up them whatever I say and I have watched men do it twice this month.” She put the lamp down. “**They are empty and the books are on the table and the fire is out, and there is not one thing on that table that has to be done today, and it is going to stay like that until the spring. I am not being kind and I am not being unkind. I keep a book with about nine hundred names in it and I have told you that already for nothing and I am telling you this for nothing as well, and both of those are the same, and that is the whole of my trade.**”

He went up the stairs anyway, because he is a man who goes up, and the second one from the bottom is loose, and the upper room had a cold hearth and a table with three books on it open at different pages and a chair pushed back from it.

There was nothing to ask. That is what four days of a man's life is: he stood in a room over a dyer's in a town of about two thousand people with nothing in it, and he came down the stairs, and the woman at the bottom said, “**Well.**”

---

He found her at about the fourth hour, at the end of the street, on a landing outside four houses that has a drop of about four feet off it and a wall to stand against.

She was standing up. She had a district satchel on one hip and about nine sheets on a string inside it and a pencil, and no hat, and she had been there about four minutes and was going to be there about another hour and a half, because her circuit brings her back through this town for about two hours on her way to the next one, and there is nothing at the end of this town's four counties except a road she has already been down.

“**Mr Vale. You are standing on a landing and I have about an hour and a half and I am on my way to a place where I have not slept.**”

“I am not here about the practice.”

“Then what is this.” She was not unkind. She had a sheet half out of the satchel and she put it back in.

“I am here about the row,” said Tomas Vale. “Nine pitches off a market of about nine hundred, and about thirty-six pounds a quarter over a season, and Ock Randall with twenty years in that row and Jessa Vinch with nine years of butter bought on credit against a pitch that is not there. **And I want to ask you what an office owes a household it has sent to four times, and I want it in a form with your name at the bottom of it.**”

And District Officer Hester Vowles laughed, once, which she had not done in front of him before and did not do again.

---

“That is the best opening anybody has put to me in eleven years,” she said, “and you have asked it in the wrong order and I am going to leave you standing on this landing for about a minute while I tell you why, and then I am going to answer it properly, **and I am going to answer it properly because you are right to ask it and I would be ashamed of myself if I did not, and I am also not going to pretend you have not cost a row.**

“**The order is wrong. You have come to me first and you are going to be at me in nine years and you will not remember this conversation and I will. Ask me the other one first.**”

“Which one.”

“**What is the office for.** Ask me that first and then ask me what it owes and you will find out that the two questions have the same answer and that the answer is no. **Ask me the other way and I will have to give you something and it will be a thing with my name at the bottom of it and it will be worth nothing to a single person in that row, and I have watched a man in another county do exactly that and I have thought about it for about four years.**”

He asked her the other one.

“**What is the office for.**”

---

“The office is for the district's answer,” said Hester Vowles.

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the whole of it and it is four words and it is correct, and I have written it on about nine hundred things in eleven years and I am not going to improve it for a man on a landing.” She put her back against the wall, which took some finding. “**The district has an answer. The district's answer is the thing the office exists to hold and to give and to correct, and I am the officer of it in this piece of country for as long as I hold the post, and when I am moved the answer goes with the office and not with me, and that is the whole of what makes it an office and not a person.**”

“That is not what I asked.”

“**It is exactly what you asked and you will not like it.** You have asked four offices in six weeks what they are for, and every one of my predecessors and every one of me has given you the true answer, and the true answer is that an office is for an answer, and **you have been after a different thing the entire time and the different thing is a person.**”

“And a person is what I have wanted.”

“A person is what you have wanted and a person is what you have been refused, four times, by four people who are all right, and I am going to be the fifth and I am not going to be unkind about it, because I have read what you have written down about all four of them and I do not think there is one thing in any of it you could have done better with a form in your hand.” She was not looking at him. “**You did not want a district and you did not want an answer. You wanted somebody to be the one that said a thing out loud, so that the thing would be in a book, and no office in this kingdom is ever going to be that, and I have told you since the sixty-fifth that a district is a ground and you cannot act on a ground, and you have written that down and underlined it, and here is the rest of it: a ground cannot say a thing either.**”

---

“Then what does it owe them,” said Tomas Vale.

And she said the thing that made him write it down and that he has not improved, and it is the whole of what this chapter is:

“**Nothing.**

“**That is the answer and I am going to say the rest of it so that you cannot go away and think I was being hard. Nothing was done to that household by the district. Nothing was done to any of those nine pitches by the district. There is no clause in this kingdom about a man who is not an officer saying a true sentence in a room in a town where nine hundred people buy cheese, and there is not going to be one, and if there is, I will not use it.**

“**I sent a man. I did not send that man. A clerk in my district's hall has sent that man, correctly, in the correct form, out of the correct column, because you put a true sentence in a room and he had four seconds and he was right. **If you had not been in that room, he would have closed the line on paper at about the fourth hour of the afternoon on the sixty-ninth and the row would still be in the market this morning, and there is no name anywhere in this kingdom for what you have done, and I have looked for one in about four minutes.**”

He did not say anything, and she let him not say anything for about four seconds, which is the whole of the pause.

“Now here is the other half of it and it is the half that costs me and I am going to give it to you anyway, because you have asked me six weeks running and I am not going to be a woman who has a hard thing to say and does not say it.

“**I can put my name to a practice. The practice is the district's and I can stop it here. I have been carrying the paper for it since the sixty-ninth and it is written and it is in this satchel on a string with about nine other things and it has a number on it. I will stop the practice in this district in writing and it will take about a day and there is nothing anybody can stop me doing it, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and it is the right thing to do and I am going to do it.**

“**And it is worth the rest of this month and every month after it, and it is not worth the ninth hour of last night.** It is not worth the row and it is not worth the four shillings and the two shillings and sixpence and it is not worth twenty years and nine years and it is not worth the handcart. **I am not going to tell you that stopping the practice makes the row come back, and I am not going to tell you it does not, because I have not got one piece of paper that says either, and there is no piece of paper in this kingdom that could.**”

“So what happens to the row.”

“**The row is gone. The row was gone the moment that man stood at the end of it on the sixty-ninth and I could have stopped the man as well as the practice and I could not, and the reason I could not is the same reason I cannot act on a person, and I have said that to you in a room over a dyer's on the sixty-fifth and I am saying it to you on a landing for the sixth day running and I do not know whether that makes it better or worse and I have stopped thinking about it.**”

---

He did not ask her to put the row back. He wants that written down, because it is the whole of what happened on a landing and it took him about nine seconds to notice he had not said it and about four minutes to notice why.

**He was standing on a landing with a pencil and a form on a crate and a competent woman who is on his side in front of him, and he did not say *put the row back*, and the reason is that he could hear it.** He has made that move in four counties and at a clerk's hall and he made it in this room six days ago when he asked a clerk to show him a line, and the sentence would have been *will you stop this for them*, and the answer would have been yes, because she will do it for a practice and a practice is a thing she can do, and he would have got it, and the row would still be gone, and he would have carried away a thing with a district number on it and it would have cost her nothing to give him and it would have been worth fourpence.

**That is the same move. That is the whole of the six weeks.** Six weeks of asking four counties for a door, and a door is easier than a household, and a door has a name on it, and a household has to answer.

“You have gone quiet,” said Hester Vowles. “You had a sentence and you have not said it and I can tell you what it was, and I am not going to help you and I am not going to be disappointed either, and I want you to hear the difference, because that difference is the whole of what I can give you and I have never been able to give it to anybody before.”

“It was going to be *will you stop this for them.*”

“Yes,” she said. “**And I would have said yes. And you would have got it, and it would be a paper, and the paper would be about a practice, and the practice stopped, and the row still gone.**”

---

She wrote it out standing up, on a crate at the foot of the steps, on the back of the sheet she had given him on the sixty-fifth, because there was nothing else to write on and she said so out loud before she did it.

> *Put to **Hester Vowles**, District Officer, this town, this date, at about the fourth hour, on a landing at the end of this street: what is the office for, and what does it owe a household it has sent to four times.*
>
> *Answer, in her own hand, and I have it exactly as she said it and I have read it back to her and she has not changed a word:*
>
> ***The office is for the district's answer. It is not a good thing and it is not a bad thing. It cannot say a thing out loud, because a ground cannot say a thing, and a person can, and I am not a person, and neither is this building.***
>
> ***It owes that household nothing. Nothing was done to them by the district. A man at a door is a clerk acting in the correct form out of the correct column because a person who is not an officer said a true sentence in a room, and there is no clause in this kingdom about that, and I cannot act on it, and neither can anybody else.***
>
> ***I can put my name to the practice and I will and I have. Stopping the practice is worth the rest of this month and every month after it and it is not worth the ninth hour of last night and I am not going to pretend to you that I can do both, and if you had asked me for the other one I would have said no.***

She signed it, and put the hour against it, and gave it to him, and did not give him a second copy, and he did not ask for one, and he wrote in his own book that night that he should have, which he has now written down twice about two different people and has not once done anything about.

And then she put the sheet back on the string in the satchel with about nine other things, and said:

“**You have been in this town eleven days and you have asked nine questions and eight of them have been correct and none of them has been about a person.** Ask me one before you go and I will answer it and it will be the tenth, and I am telling you now that I have a carriage on that road in about an hour and I will not say yes to it and I will not say no to it, and that is not modesty, it is that there is nothing in me that a woman standing at the end of a street could not do better with a slate.”

He did not ask her the tenth question, and she got into the carriage, and the two rooms over a dyer's stayed shut with three books open on a table, and the frame was four streets off with four bolts made fast in it and nothing was rung anywhere in Miremarket on the seventy-first, and the receipt was whole.

He sat down on the step of the draper's at about the eighth hour of the evening and wrote for about an hour, which is a duration, and he filled the first and third columns and left the second and fourth as they were, which is also a decision and is also written down:

> *The seventy-first. I went up the stairs to a room with three books open on a table and nothing to do, and I came down them again, and the woman at the bottom told me she was not sorry about it and I have believed her, because she keeps nine hundred names in a book and has never once been asked what it is and told me so for nothing twice in eleven days and never asked me for anything back.*
>
> *And I asked the question I should have asked first and got four words back: **the office is for the district's answer.**
>
> *I have been six weeks asking four offices what they are for and I have wanted a person out of all of them and I have been refused four times by four correct people, and a fifth one stood on a landing for about two hours and refused me again and then gave me a paper anyway, and the paper is the best one I have got in a month and it is a piece of paper with a district number on it saying that nothing has been done to those nine pitches by anybody who can be stopped for it, **and I asked her for that and not the other thing, and I have written down the reason, and the reason is that I could hear myself.**
>
> ***I have known since the fortieth that what I want is not a thing that exists. I have known it in four counties and a clerk's hall and I have carried the knowing about for a month like a man carrying a stone in his boot, and the reason I carried it and did not put it down is that a door is easier than a household. A door has a name on it and a man on it and you can ask it a question at an hour the office is open. A household has to answer, and answering is what costs them.***
>
> *And she is going to stop a practice in this district in about a day and it will hold for the rest of this month and every month after it, and it will not put back the ninth hour of the evening of the sixty-ninth, and there is not one piece of paper in this kingdom that could, **and I have stopped asking offices for pieces of paper and the only thing I have got out of six weeks of asking is a thing nobody is going to hand me and a street at the end of which a woman is going to be poorer than she was on the sixtieth because I asked a question properly.***
>
> *Third column, and the hours in the flat hand, and the two of them separate and not merged: a woman has said no to me on a landing and given me a paper. A woman has said yes to me in a door three days ago and written it down as a no, and it is in a slate that cost a penny and a half, and it is about a child, and I am not allowed to know anything more about it and I am not going to try.*
>
> *And the fourth column has one line in it and I am not going to improve it, and it is the thing I have been carrying since the sixty-seventh and there is nothing under it: **a name on a roll begins, in about four years, to be read as a duty, and a duty is a mouth, and a mouth is for a claim, and nobody has to stand in a lane to ring it, and there is no clause about that in this kingdom either.***
