# Chapter 0092 — The Officer Who Cannot Produce A Document

He wrote to a district at about the fourth hour on the seventy-eighth day of the month, and the writing of it took him about eleven minutes, and the eleven minutes were the easy part.

> *To the district's rooms over a dyer's on the north side, this town. From a man with a barrow at a draper's on the same street.*
>
> *I am not asking for a person and I am not asking for a name. I am asking one question and it is about the work.*
>
> *There is a household of five at the end of the east lane in this town. It has four lines that have been open about four years. Nobody has ever told that household what the question was. **What question does the office put to a household, in the words the office uses.***
>
> *I am not going to be satisfied with a description of the round or of the form or of the season, and I will say so now so that nobody has to find out at the counter.*

He read it four times, which is three more than it wanted, and gave it to the woman who lets the two rooms over the dyer's, at the bottom of the outside stair, and she took it and looked at the outside of it and gave it back.

“**I am not posting this for you. I am putting it in the district's box, which is on that wall by the water butt, and the district's box is a Crown thing and I am a woman who lets rooms and that is all I am. And you are not to thank me, and I am not going to remember that you did, and I have told you what I keep and I have told you twice for nothing and I am telling you a third time and it is still for nothing.**”

“That is four.”

“**It is three times and I have not counted it and it is a figure and I have not counted it and you have, and that is the difference between us and it is why I have got a book and you have got a slate.**”

---

The answer came back at about the sixth hour and it was a sheet in a hand he did not know, with the hour on it, and it said four things.

**One: that there is no document.** Not that it is refused and not that it is lost. There is no document, and the reason is not secrecy, and the office's form for a return has four fields and the four fields are the household and the line and the answer and the mark, and **the question is not one of the four fields and there has never been a place on any form in this office for the words the office says at a door.**

**Two: that the only record of the question in this kingdom is the answer.** That is what a column is. A man asks a question in a form at a door and a hand goes down in a column, and the going down of the hand is the only trace in the world of what was asked, and a man who has not read a column and been told the question has not got the question.

**Three: that a person may come and ask the officer, in the room, in words, and that this is the only way it can be had and it cannot be put in a form afterwards, because a form is about a household and a man in a room is a person, and a person is the only thing in this kingdom that can be asked a question and can refuse to answer it, and that is the whole reason a hall cannot be asked anything and only a person in a room can.

**And four, at the foot, in the same hand, and Tomas Vale read it three times:** *If you want it, come to a room over a chandler's shop four doors down from the draper's, this street, this evening. **Cress Naylor, of the removals. I am not the district. I have a book and a table and a fire I pay for.***

---

He had been in this town twenty-one days and there was a Crown officer four doors down from him and he had not known, and the reason he had not known was not that the officer was hidden. **It was that Tomas Vale had spent twenty-one days going into a clerk's hall, a district office, a room over a dyer's, a yard behind a draper's shop, a counting house, a gate on the water and a smith's yard, and had not once walked up his own street and looked at what was above the shops.**

He said that out loud in the room and she said, “**Then you have been working in four counties and a building and not in a street, and I have been in a street for eleven years, and there is nothing I can do with that this morning.**”

---

Cress Naylor came at about the sixth hour and knocked, which he had not expected, and stood in the doorway of a room he was not going to be asked to pay for and said:

“**I have read your paper and I have written two pages and about half of them are me being careful, and I am going to say the careful part first and then I am going to ask you a question, and if you answer it I will say the rest, and if you do not I will go home to a street in this town and my wife and my boy and that will be the end of it.**”

“Say the question.”

“**What is the document.**”

And Tomas Vale had carried that sentence for thirty-eight days in four counties and into four buildings and a book, and a man said it to him in a doorway in about two seconds, and it went through him like a hand in a sill, and he had to put his hand on the door frame.

“**You did not write it in your paper.**”

“**No. I have written four sentences asking you for a question and not one of them says what a document is, and I wrote them at about the fifth hour and I have been sitting here since the fifth hour working out why, and your paper went into the district's box on the wall by the water butt and I empty that box on my round, which is four doors and one wall, and my four sentences went back into it, and a man brought them to you because that is what a box is for, and that is the whole of the service in this town.**”

---

So Naylor told him, and it was not the sentence the clerks gave, and Tomas wrote that down too.

“**A line is not a document. A line is a place in a book where a thing is entered and I enter about nine hundred of them a year in this district in removals, which is a smaller number than the answering column and a duller one, and every one of them is a household that has gone, or a person that has gone out of a household, and I do not ask why anybody has gone and I am not permitted to ask why anybody has gone and there is no box for it.**

“**And the reason you cannot have what you asked for is not that the office will not give it. It is that there is nothing to give. When a man at a door asks a household a question, the question is in his mouth for about nine seconds and then it is gone, and the only thing in this kingdom that knows what was asked is the hand that went down afterwards. And that hand is the answer and not the question, and it has never been anything else, and there is no form in this district or in any district I have worked in that has ever carried the words themselves.**”

“That is four sentences and it is the fifth time anybody has said a version of it to me and I have never once been able to use it.”

“**Then you will not be able to use this one either and I am going to tell you what I am going to do, and I want you to understand that I have thought about it for about eleven years and I have not been brave about it and I have not been sorry about it and I have not been asked to be either.**”

---

And then he said his part, and it was this.

“**In the winter I closed a line. A household of four in a street off your market. The man went up the water four months before I came, to a place in another district, to a trade, and he wrote once and the writing came back.**

“**I came to the door at about the ninth hour of the evening because that is the hour I do removals at, and I had the book out, and the woman at the door told me before I had the book open that he had gone in the autumn and that nobody had heard since and that she had told the last man about it twice.**

“**And I closed the line. In the correct column, in the correct form, with a mark, at about the ninth hour of the evening, and she did not ask me a question and I did not ask her one, because there is no box for it and I am not permitted to ask it and I did not want to know.**

“**That is my part in it. A man left this town and the only record in this kingdom that he was anywhere at all is a mark in a column in a book, and the mark says a line is shut, and a shut line is a man who has gone and answered, and so he is not lost. He is answered. He is in a district four counties up doing a trade and the district's book has a number against him and the number is a filing reference and not a man, and I have never seen the number and I could not tell you what it means and I would not guess at it in a room over your draper's.**”

He stopped there and did not go on, and Tomas Vale did not ask him anything for about nine seconds.

“**And nobody has looked for him. Not his district and not this office and not the four people who have a pail of water each. Because a shut line is a thing that is finished, and a thing that is finished is not a thing anybody opens, and I want it noticed that I did not do that to him. **I closed a line that had been open, which is the only thing there is to do with a line that is open, and the office does not hold lines open, and if I had not closed it there would have been a man at that door in the spring and another in the summer, and the woman at that door has had four men in four years and I was the one who made it five and the smallest of the five.**”

---

“Do you want it written down,” said Tomas Vale.

“**No.**

“You have just said it to me and I have got a book in this room and you have read about four hundred sheets off a counter in this town—”

“**I have not been in a clerk's hall in this town in my life and I do not want to know about it and I do not care about it, and I want it understood that I am not refusing you out of caution and I am not refusing you for the sake of my office. I have a house on a street in this town and a wife and a boy and I am asking you not to write it down because a thing that is written down goes somewhere, and I do not know where it goes, and neither does the district, and I have been eleven years in this district and I have never found the standing entry.**”

Tomas Vale wrote it down.

He wrote all of it, in the flat hand, with the hour, and there were no names in it to write; he wrote the street off the market and the hour of the evening and the winter and the four months and the mark in the column and the four fields of a form with none of them the words, and he wrote it on a page of his own book on the sill of a room over a draper's shop, and Naylor stood there and watched him do it and did not say one word while he did it, and afterwards he said:

“**There it is. And I want it noticed that I could have stopped you and I did not try, and the reason I did not try is that I have asked you a question this evening and you answered it, and a man who has been answered once does not get to say the answer was not wanted.**”

“Then that is the end of it.”

“**And I will not say it again. Not to you and not to anybody. Because if I say it a second time then the first time was a performance, and it was not one, and I have got about a mile back to a house and I would like to get to it.**”

---

And then Naylor said the thing that was not about the work, and he said it in the doorway with his hat in his hand.

“**In eleven years, in four counties, you are the first person who has walked into a room and asked me what a document is. Every other man who has ever come into this room has come for a line, and I have given him a line, and about nine hundred of them a year, and nobody has ever once come in here and asked me the work.**”

“That is not a compliment. That is a complaint about eleven years and I am the one who caused about four seconds of it.”

“**It is a complaint and it is eleven years and you may go and do the other twenty-four counties with it and I do not care.**”

---

He shut the door and went, and his boots went down the outside stair four times and then he was gone, and there were two people in a room over a draper's shop in a town of about two thousand people.

Tamsin Rook had the slate on her knee with the chalk in her hand and there was nothing on it.

“You did not write it either.”

“**No, and I am not going to argue with you about it.**”

“I am not going to ask you why and I want it in the book that I did not ask and that you did not give it to me unasked.”

“**You are going to write it down in your own book, though.**”

“I know.”

“**Then say what you have just done, in one sentence, out loud, and then you can write it down and I will not listen.**”

And Tomas Vale said it, and it was this:

“I have taken a thing a man with a wife and a boy asked me in a doorway not to take, and I have made a page about it, and that is the third time in ten days that I have made a record about a person who did not ask me for one, and the first one cost a household the front of a row of nine pitches, and nobody has been forgiven for anything and nobody has apologised for anything and I am not going to be told that it was worth it.”

And then he wrote the last entry of the seventy-eighth at about the ninth hour of the evening, on the step of the draper's, in the flat hand, with the hours, and he filled the first and third columns and left the second and the fourth as they were:

> *The seventy-eighth. **The truth does not arrive as a document.** I have been in this business six weeks looking for one and a man opened a book in a room and told me there was nothing in it to find, and he was right, and the whole of what he told me is that a question asked at a door is in the asker's mouth for about nine seconds and the only trace of it in this kingdom is a hand that went down afterwards, and a hand is an answer and has never been anything else.*
>
> *And the harder half, and I am writing it flat: **a hall is not a person and never was.** A hall can be shown a thing and cannot be asked a question and cannot refuse one, and a person can be asked and can refuse, and that is the only reason a hall cannot be asked anything, and it took me six weeks, four counties and a receipt to find out a thing a removals officer has had in his head for eleven years, and he is right and I am not going to be able to use it.*
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, the hours flat against them, and this one is a man's own words and I am putting it in the third because it is a thing a person said to me in a room, and I am not merging it with anything and it is not mine: **a man went up the water four months before a line was shut, and the only record in this kingdom that he was anywhere at all is a mark in a column, and a shut line is a man who has gone and answered, and so he is not lost, he is answered.** He is four counties up doing a trade. I do not have his name and he did not give it to me and I did not ask and he is not going to be looked for, and nobody is owed anything tonight except the woman at that door, who has had four men in four years and a fifth tonight because a line had been open, and the smallest of the five was a man doing the only thing there is to do with a line that is open.*
>
> *And I asked him not to write it and he asked me not to write it down and I wrote it down. **I have now made four records in six weeks about persons who did not ask me for one and every single one of them was me being a careful man in a room, and the fifth clause of my own receipt says I have no standing to speak for any household in Kells or in any ward, and I have not broken it today, and I have not broken it because I was careful, and I am going to write that sentence down and let it stand: careful is not the reason I have not broken it. I did not break it because I wanted it not to be broken. That is a different thing and it is mine and it is the whole of what I have learned in six weeks and it took me a man with a wife and a boy to see it.***
>
> *Fourth column, empty, and it is going to stay empty, and I am not going to fill it in this county.*
>
> *And a clerk worked four streets from this room yesterday, and about nine hundred people in this town heard his name in a market square this morning before I wrote any of this down, and nobody has told him, and I am not going to, and I am not going to go and stand in his street either, and the reason is in the book for the seventy-seventh and the reason is that a man who has begun a list has not got to the end of it and does not know when he will.*
