# Chapter 0100 — A Bell In A Bag, And Two Questions

The rain came in at about the seventh hour of the evening on the eighty-fifth and it had not stopped by the fourth hour of the morning of the eighty-sixth, and he got the barrow into the yard behind the draper's shop at about the fourth hour and the frame went into its reinforced padded cradle and the four retaining bolts were made fast at every place the frame had been set down since the thirty-third, and the bell of fourteen pounds two ounces went into a sack and the sack went on the barrow against the cradle, and that is the whole of what he had to carry and it is a bell in a bag.

“**You can have the yard,**” said the draper, from the door, in the rain.

“I am going.”

“**I know you are going. I am saying that a man with a barrow and a yard is a different thing from a man with a barrow and a lane, and I have had a Crown officer in my yard twice this week and one of them was pleasant about it, and I am not charging you for the outside of my own shop and I would like it entered that I said so and that it is about fourpence a day and that I have never said it to anybody else in thirty-one years.**”

“That is the third time somebody in this town has told me a thing for nothing.”

“**It is four times if you count my own wife and I am not counting her, and I am not going to be thanked and if you thank me I will put the rent up.**”

---

She was at the gate at about the fourth hour with her bag on her shoulder and a slate under her arm and nothing else that Tomas Vale could see, and she has carried less than this for eleven years and she did not say so and he did not ask and he has written down in this book four times that he does not ask her what she is carrying and does not ask her what she can afford and does not ask her why she is here, and it is the arrangement and neither of them wrote it and neither of them wrote it first.

Nobody in this town came out of a door to see them off. That is on the page and it is a fact and not an absence anybody arranged.

**The market keeper was at the trough at about the fourth hour and had about nine rows and about nine hundred people, and he did not come over, and Tomas Vale did not go over, and about a quarter of an hour later the market keeper came over and put a paper bag on the barrow and said nothing else at all, and it was two loaves and a piece of cheese and it was not a gift, because he said, “**That is on the pitch and it is about fourpence and you did not pay for it and I am not going to say you did not pay for it, and I have said that deliberately and I would like it noticed that I said it deliberately.**”**

---

They got out of the town on the road down from it at about the fifth hour, and the road goes down and then along the water, and it was the eighty-sixth day of the month and he had been in Miremarket twenty-nine days and he had come down the water for one reason and he no longer had it and he had not gone looking for another one, and that is the first thing he is going to write down and he is going to write it down here on the last page of this book with a road under it.

> *The eighty-sixth. In the rain. **I am not looking for a place.** I have not been looking for a place for about three weeks and I have only just noticed that I had stopped, and I want it written that I did not decide to stop and I did not notice, and that a habit of carrying is also a habit of not putting down, and I put nothing down between the seventy-third and the eighty-sixth because there was nothing left to pick up, and that is not peace and I am not going to call it peace.*
>
> *Here is the whole of what is on the page at the end of six weeks in this county, and it is all of it, and I am going to write it flat and I am not going to improve it:*
>
> *A practice is stopped in one district in writing and it is worth the rest of this month and every month after it, and about eleven thousand lines a year stand behind it, and it is not worth the ninth hour of the evening of the sixty-ninth.*
>
> *About fifty-one pounds has gone into six hands in a room upstairs over a yard, one at a time, and a firm of nine people now knows why nobody would lend to it, and the firm is answered and the firm is finished and the man who said so said it before the money moved.*
>
> *A room, a bench and five sheets are gone, and the law that took them is a clause in a receipt I signed in another county and asked a clerk to add to because I was right.*
>
> *Nine lines of a book of numbers are in a coat, taken by a man who has no right and said so before he was asked, and the hour was written down by a woman with a book of about nine hundred names who kept the sheet and would not give it to me, and I did not ask for it and did not try, and neither of them is owed a visit and there is no column for either of them and no clerk can enter it and there is no form on which I could do anything for them at all.*
>
> *A bell somebody else made and somebody else certified has been rung once in a market in this town in front of about nine hundred people, and it has no claim in it, and I do not know where it went, and nobody has told me and nobody is going to and I am not going to find out.*
>
> *A child went on a carrier's cart on the seventy-second and is not found and is not placed and is not named and is not rescued and is not brought back, and about nine pitches are off a market and are not on it, and about thirty-six pounds a quarter has not come back to nine households and is not coming back, and a handcart did not earn, and a mother can say a number and the number is no, and **nobody was rescued and nobody was told the refusal was worth it and nobody is going to be told it now.***
>
> *And a clerk of eleven years has not altered one thing in a building because of a sheet of mine, and he did not do it for me and he told me in a corridor, and that is the one thing in six weeks that nobody can take back and nobody has paid for.*
>
> *And a name at Ashlade, four counties up, is a name and not a place. **A boy of eight is a name.** He is in about nine thousand kitchens in four counties and about nine thousand heads, and there is no book of him anywhere in this kingdom and there never has been and there is no form for one, and the only thing that has ever gone to a place to find out whether a person is in it is a person on foot with a bag, and I have got a bag, and I have not got a place, and I am not going to pretend to anybody that those are the same shape.*
>
> ***And I do not know how an office gets into a bell.*** I wrote that in this book on the forty-second day of the month in a district office in a real town, in my own hand, and I have not got nearer it in six weeks, and on the eighty-second a man took nine lines out of a room that had been shut a fortnight and the nine lines were numbers, and a number is a filing reference and not a man, and **I have not got nearer it, and I have written that down twice now, and I am not going to write a paragraph about it, because a paragraph about it is a thing I would be able to carry.**
>
> *And the six certified returns in a drawer two counties back, one name, one hand, five different dates, and a man who has not been in that house for six years. **They are unaccounted for. I have not accounted for them and I am not going to, and I am not going to call them a forgery and I am not going to call them a conspiracy, and I had nine lines of numbers in my hand on the eighty-second and I felt it start and I wrote the sentence down again, and a person who joins them has joined them because they are both dates.***
>
> *And a service entry in another county that has been withdrawn and is genuine-or-borrowed and has been genuine-or-borrowed for six weeks, and I have not settled it and I am not going to, and it is a question about a man and I have never once met him and it is not a stage and it is not a power and it is not this book.*

---

He wrote the two questions down at about the seventh hour, under a hedge, on a page, because a day-book gets wet and this is a thing a man does in his business and not a thing a man does in a feeling.

They were the two he had written at the end of the month before last, in a room in another county, with the hours, and he had carried them fifty-four days and put them into four buildings.

> *One. How does a Crown office turn one clerk's answer into a whole settlement's consent.*
>
> ***Answered, and it is the whole of six weeks and it is a small answer: it does not need to. A roll does not need anybody's answer. A duty is a mouth, and a mouth is for a claim, and the claim is made in a queue, in about nine minutes, in the ordinary voice, by about eleven thousand people a year who are not clerks and are not agents of anything and are usually right about who they are. I do not need a better one and there is not a better one.***
>
> *Two. Where are names sent when they are registered.*
>
> ***Not answered. It is not answered and it is not going to be answered on this road and I am not going to guess at it. A district officer asked his district twice in writing and had no answer to either. A clerk of four years has never been upstairs. A dyer has a key to two rooms that are not his. A man took nine lines out of one of them and they were numbers. That is the whole of what six weeks has produced about it and I am writing it down in a hedge in the rain and I am not going to improve it.***

And then he ruled a space under it, and wrote at the top of the space a numeral, and it is the third question and it is not a better one and he is not going to pretend it is:

> *Three. **Which office is it.***

He did not write it before she said it and he has written down in the book that he did not write it before she said it, and the reason is that a question he has found himself is a thing he can carry and a question somebody has put to him is a thing he has to answer, and he has learned that in six weeks at some cost.

---

He did not ring anything.

**He has said that in a book four times now and he is going to say it once more and then he is going to stop saying it, because a man who keeps saying that he is not doing a thing is a man who wants somebody to know he could have.**

The Crown survey carrier is in its reinforced padded cradle on a barrow on a road with four retaining bolts made fast in it and no clapper in it, and the receipt forbids any person to furnish one, and **it cannot be rung and I did not furnish one and I have not gone near it and I am not going to.**

The certified plain carrier is in a sack against that cradle, and it has no wax in it and no dressed groove in it and nothing on it but a caster's mark and a day, and **I have rung it twice myself, once on a sill in a town four counties behind this one on the fifty-second and once in a market square in this town on the seventy-seventh, and I have never learned where either of them went, I have never once in my life learned it, I wrote that in this book on the sixty-second and I have not learned it since and I do not now, and nobody in that square was told where it went and nobody was told by me and I am not going to be told it as a favour and I am not going to go back and look.**

And he did not ring a bell he has made, **and the reason is not a rule. The only bell I have made is in a sealed yard in a county whose bar I do not argue, it has been barred since the twenty-sixth from any public place in that county, and I did not go near that yard in six weeks and I am not going to, and a bell I made is not in this bag.**

---

He did not write a fourth column entry, and he has written down why, and it is the last thing in the book except the hour.

A fourth column is a thing nobody could answer. I have had two in my life. The first was about a paper and a building and it cost me nothing and it taught me everything. The second was about nine people in a market and it is in this book with the hour against it, and about nine people in that town are not safe because a careful man wrote them down, and I wrote down at the eleventh hour of the evening of the seventy-sixth that I knew and that I was going to do it anyway. So I know what the fourth column is and what it costs and I have had it twice, and I am not going to fill it in again in this county, and I am not going to fill it in on a road either, and a man who has worked out what a column is is not obliged to go on having one.

---

And here is the whole of what he has got, and he wrote it out flat at about the ninth hour, in the rain, on a page, and it is the last page of the book and it is a list and it is not a form.

> *A list of about nine names that cannot be certified at all, in four counties, that cannot be unrolled, made in about nine minutes a year by about eleven thousand people who are not clerks, and there is no form for a list and there never has been.*
>
> *Four columns and a slate, and the price side of the slate is on the other face and it is the other way up, and three of the columns are his and one of them is not and he has not improved the one in twenty-six days.*
>
> *A witness, in a corridor, who was not thanked and did not want to be.*
>
> *A fragment: nine lines, a hand, a column of numbers, a column of days, and a third column empty from end to end.*
>
> *A custody receipt in four clauses and a fifth, all five standing, including this morning, and the second of them is the reason I cannot pay for anything and the fifth of them is the reason there was no room in a form and the reason five sheets went out of it, and I asked for it and I was right and it has cost me a room and a bench and five sheets and I am not going to separate those two things in my own head for the rest of my life.*
>
> *A frame in a cradle on a barrow, with four bolts made fast in it, that cannot be rung and that I have never once been inside.*
>
> *And a woman beside me who has said nine words on a landing on the seventy-second, out loud, in the open, and about four people heard them and one of them put a pail down in the middle of a road, and she has not asked me for anything since and is not going to, and I am not going to thank her for it and she is not going to let me.*

---

And then the last of it, on the last page, and it is the only thing in six weeks he has written that is about the future, and he wrote it flat and he did not improve it and he is not going to.

> *Six weeks and I have got: a careful life is a way of writing things down. **A document is what a person is read by, and I have been manufacturing them since the thirty-second because a man who has nothing to carry makes a document, and a document is the only thing he can be certain of, and a claim in metal is a thing a man cannot edit, and I have had one of those and I do not know where it went.***
>
> *And what I am is a man with a bar on his own bell, a receipt with a fifth clause in it, and a road, and I have not got a place, and I am not going to look for one in this county, and a town of about two thousand people has decided a chore and it is the smallest thing a town can decide, and about a hundred people in it say a name back to each other in a queue, and two of about nine hundred know that their names are on a piece of slate, and nobody has been told what to decide, and none of that is mine and I did not do any of it and it is not going to last longer than about four years unless somebody keeps at it, and I am a man with a bag and a barrow and no standing and I have never in my life been better at anything.*
>
> *Nobody asked her for a reason. **Her hearing is permanent and it is hers and it was priced in a smith's shop on the fifty-first and she is not going to be rescued out of it and she has not been rescued out of it and nothing has been restored to her in six weeks and nothing is going to be, and she rang nothing, and the bell is in her bag and it is hers.** Nobody was rescued. Nobody was told their refusal was worth it. Nobody is going to be.*
>
> *And I have not learned where a warning lands. I have never learned it. I have said so four times in this book and I am going to stop, and if anybody ever asks me in any county in any year, the answer is in the third column of the sixty-second and it has not changed and I have not got a better one.*

---

And then the last page of the book was finished, and the last page of the day was not, and it was about the ninth hour, on a road down off the water in the rain, and she was a step behind him and about ten feet off his left shoulder, because ten feet is not near enough to be part of a thing and it is near enough to write down what it is, and she has been reading his left shoulder from behind him for eleven years and she does it with her chin and not her eyes.

He shut the book and put it inside his coat, on top of the page of his own hand that carries a household of five's sentence and that is not his and that he may not give to anybody and that he did not give to a form on the eighty-first and that is the only reason he still has it.

And she said, from about ten feet off his left shoulder, in the ordinary voice, in the rain, without stopping:

“**You have written that down three times in six weeks. That you do not know how an office gets into a bell, and that you have not got nearer it, and that you are not going to write a paragraph about it.**”

“I never have.”

“**Then you have been asked it four times and you have not once been asked which office.**”

He walked about nine paces.

“**Which office is it.**”

And that was the whole of the last of it, and it was not a promise and it was not a refusal, and it was a question asked by the person who had the right to ask it, and it had been put to him by a woman with a slate, and it was going to be on a page that night under a numeral, and he answered it in four words and did not improve it in the morning.

“**Nobody has told me.**”

And she said nothing at all, because that is her side of it, and it was the answer, and he did not add a fifth word, and the barrow went down the road with a frame in a cradle in it and a bell in a bag against the cradle, and the four bolts were made fast at every place the frame had been set down, and there was no clapper in it, and a receipt in five clauses was in a coat under a book,

and a man who had rung a bell he did not make once in a market and had been told he was right, and had not been told where it went, walked on down a road in the rain with a woman ten feet off his left shoulder and a list that cannot be unrolled and four columns and a slate and a witness and a fragment and thirteen documents,

and about eleven hundred people behind him in a town of about two thousand who had been told nothing at all about what to decide, and a market that had a chore in it, and a building with a set of rolls on a shelf in it and a clerk in it who had altered nothing, and a house at the end of a lane with a book in a drawer that was not going to be asked about,

and a woman at a gate in a yard who had said no on the seventy-second in the ordinary way and was owed nothing and was owed nothing by anybody here, and about forty-four thousand names in four counties, and a boy of eight, and a road.
