# Chapter 0086 — The Child Who Is Sent Because A Name Is Owed

She had a day of nothing, which is a thing she had not had since the boat, and she spent it at the landing because there is nothing else to do at a landing.

That is the honest account of the seventy-second and it is in her own hand in the slate that night, and she did not improve it: **she had no hour, because there was no book in this town that would give her one and she was not allowed to ask, and so she went and stood where the things arrive.**

The stone landing at Miremarket has a crane on it and about nine boats and a road up off the water that the carriers use, and by about the fourth hour on the seventy-second there was a cart standing at the head of it with a man sitting on the shafts and nobody in the road.

She had been there about two hours and a half and had worked out three things in that time, and the first two were nothing.

---

**The first thing she could not do was find the road.**

She knows how to find a road. That is the entire trade and it is eleven years of it and she can find a road to a market, to a landing stage, to a house at the end of a lane, by asking one question of one person and then asking a second person, and the whole of the profession is that you go to a place and the place decides.

**There is no road to this.** There is a lane off the market street that comes down to this landing. There is a cart. There is a day. And the day is on a piece of paper somewhere that she has not read and is not going to be able to ask for, and there is nothing in a market of about nine hundred people or in a lane or at a landing that says *this afternoon* to anybody who has not been told.

**The second thing she could not do was be at a door.**

That is the other half and it is the whole of the other half, and she has written it down in sixteen words and has not improved them: *a courier goes to a door and says her own name and lets the door decide.*

She had said her own name at a door on the sixty-eighth and a woman had said no, and the no had been about a child, and she had been told in a gutter, at some length and with the price named, what the no was going to cost, and she had gone away and had not come back to that door because she had said in a gutter that she would not.

**And what was about to happen at this landing was not a door deciding.** She stood on a stone landing for two hours and a half and got that clear in her own head about four times, and each time it came back in a different shape, and the last shape it came back in was the shape she had spent eleven years not using, which is that a door decides and a person does not, and that the whole of her trade was built on the fact that a person will sometimes decide in a room with a person in it, and that there is no version of that where the person is a cart.

---

**The third thing is what happened, and it began at about the seventh hour and it took about forty minutes and nobody in it was doing anything wrong.**

The cart came up the road off the water at about the seventh hour and stopped at the head of the lane, and about nine people came down the lane to look at it, because a cart at the head of a lane on a day when nothing is going anywhere is a thing nine people look at.

And the talking started about ninety seconds after the cart stopped, and it was in the ordinary way, and nobody was talking to her, and she was standing about eleven feet off with a slate under her arm and a bag at her feet and she heard all of it.

“That is a carrier's cart.”

“That is a carrier's cart and it is going up the water road and it is not stopping until the water is done.”

“How long is that.”

“**Four days. It is four days and I have been told four days by about three people, which is three more than the number of people who have ever asked me.**”

And then a woman at the head of the lane said it, flatly, to another woman, the way you say a thing you have said before:

“**That is the Verrows' honouring it. Four years, two answers, and the third one is the small one, and she has done it right. You do not send a thing and leave the line open, that is what an open line is.**”

“You could have said no.”

“**I have said no. I have said no for four years and I have been asked twice and I said it twice and nothing came and now a cart is here and I am not going to be the woman who has had a line open for four years.**”

“That is not a choice anybody gives you.”

“**That is a choice I gave myself in about two in the morning and it was the wrong choice and I have not been told so by anybody and I am not going to be.**”

---

And that is the whole of it, and Tamsin Rook stood on a stone landing and could not ask a single question, and the reason she could not was the one she had worked out four days earlier in a street and had hoped was wrong:

**A household that believes it has agreed cannot be asked whether it has.**

She has gone to ten doors in four counties and asked a person a question and the person has said no, and a no is a thing her whole trade is built on being able to hear. And this household had not said no. It had said *yes* twice, four years ago, in a hand, against a line, at a door, to a man who asked it as though asking a person for a hand. **And a person who has said yes cannot be asked what they said yes to, because the answer is that they said yes, and the only person who can say anything else is the person who asked, and that person is a man with a book on a stool who has gone to another county.**

So she stood there and did not ask and it was the correct thing and it was the worst hour of the seventy-second.

---

What came down the lane at about the eighth hour was not a person being taken anywhere.

That is the thing she has insisted on and it is in the slate in her own hand and she has not improved it and she has read it back to nobody twice: **the child was not being punished, and is not an offender, and is not on anything, and is not in anybody's book this morning, and the person putting them on the cart is not wicked and has not done one wrong thing this morning or in four years.**

Mave Verrow came down the lane with them and she had her arms round them for the first half of it and then she took her arms off, which was the hardest thing she did in the whole forty minutes and which nobody in that landing saw as anything at all except one woman with a slate.

“**You will be fed on the road and you will be met at the end of it and it is not a punishment and nobody is going to do anything to you.**”

“You are not coming.”

“**I am not coming. There are five of us and two of them cannot do without me and that is the whole of the arithmetic and I have done it about nine times since the sixty-eighth.**”

“Then who is it for.”

And Mave Verrow said the thing that is the whole of the seventieth and the whole of the seventieth is the whole of this batch, and she said it on a landing in the open with nine people in it:

“**It is for the name. That is all it is for and there is nobody at the end of it. That is what I have understood and it took me two in the morning and four years. You send for it and it is done and there is nobody to be angry at and nothing to come after. I have had four years of a line open and I am not having a fifth.**”

And the child got on the cart.

---

**And Tamsin Rook did not get on the cart.**

She had four inches of a reason and it is in the slate and it is the correct rule and it is eleven years old: *a courier goes to a door and says her own name and lets the door decide, and the courier does not get in the cart.* She did not get on it. She did not claim the child and she did not say the child was hers and she did not say the child was on a roll, and she did not say where the cart was going, and she did not ask anybody on that landing a single question, and she has not asked one person since the sixty-eighth and she is not going to.

What she did was say a name out loud.

She said it once, in the open, at ten feet — the distance she has used for eleven years — to a landing with about nine people on it and a cart on it and a woman standing at the foot of it, and the name was the name on the line, and she said nine words, and the nine words are the whole of what she did on the seventy-second and the whole of what she has got, and she did not improve them and has not improved them since:

**“Mave Verrow is a person and not a house.”**

She did not say whose name it was. She has never known whether that name belongs to the woman at the foot of the cart or to a child who got on it four seconds earlier, and she did not say, and she was asked by nobody, and there was no time and she did not want the time.

Mave Verrow turned round.

Nobody else on that landing moved at all, and Tamsin Rook has said since that she does not think nine people heard a word of it except about four, and that the four were the woman at the foot of the cart, a man sitting on the shafts, and a woman going past with a pail of water who had not been going to look at anything and stopped dead in the road with a full pail and put it down.

Nobody said anything.

The carrier took his feet up onto the shafts, and the cart went up the road off the water, and about four people on a stone landing watched a cart with a child on it go away up a road, and one of those four put a pail down in the middle of a road and stood there.

---

She did not know afterwards whether that was anything. She has not decided and she has not been helped, and she wrote at about the ninth hour in her own hand on the back of a slate with the hours flat against it, and this is the whole of the entry and she has read it back to nobody:

> *The seventy-second. I said a name out loud at a cart and nine words with it and nine people were on that landing and about four of them heard it and one of them put a pail down in the middle of a road, and I do not know what that was worth, and I have been trying to work it out for about an hour and a half and I have not got a number and I am not going to make one up.*
>
> *Here is the whole of the finding and it is eleven years long and it arrived in about nine seconds on a landing.*
>
> ***I am the best reader of documents in four counties and I stood on a landing with a name in my mouth, and the whole of what I am good at was worth nothing, and the whole of what I am not good at is that a person can say a name out loud in the open to a stranger, and that is the only thing that happened today, and it is the one thing I have never been trained for and the one thing I would do again.***

Then she wrote four lines under it and the fourth is the one that is the finding of this batch and it is in the third column and not the fourth, because it is a thing somebody said:

> *Four years, two answers, and the third one is the small one, and she has done it right. You do not send a thing and leave the line open, that is what an open line is.*
>
> *A woman said that on a landing in the open, out loud, in thirty-six words, of which the rule is eleven, in the ordinary voice, to another woman, and she was not talking to me and she was not talking to a clerk and there is no mark and no form and no hour against it anywhere in this kingdom. **That is the whole of the engine of this book, and it has been said three times in about five days, twice in a market and once on this landing, by three women who are owed nothing and are not agents of anything and are usually right about who they are, and it is not in a hall, and there is nothing anywhere that anybody can find it in afterwards, and that is why it works.***
>
> *And I did not ring anything. Nobody asked me anything, and nobody held anything over me, and there is no speech, and nobody has said that it was worth it, and I am not going to.*
