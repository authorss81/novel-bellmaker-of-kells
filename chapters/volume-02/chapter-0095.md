# Chapter 0095 — The Room, The Bench, And The Copies

They came up the outside stair at about the fourth hour on the eighty-first day of the month, two of them, and one of them was not in a hurry, and that was the first thing Tomas Vale noticed and it was the thing he went on thinking about for two days.

**An officer of this county called Curtis Ilsley**, with a form folded in the inside of his coat and a satchel, and **Ivo Trant, in charge of the shelves of a clerk's hall in this town, who had not been asked to come and had come.**

Trant said so before they were inside the room. He said: “**I have not been asked to come and I have come, and I would like it noticed that I did not have to and that nobody has asked me anything at all, and that I am here because the answer to a question is in this room and I am the man in charge of the shelves.**”

“Nobody asked you anything in here in twenty-two days,” said Tomas Vale. “I asked you nine things in a street and you answered some of them and you were right about all of them.”

“**Yes. And I have come upstairs, and I want it noticed in the other direction as well, which is that I have come upstairs about four days after a man in your position had my clerk's name in a market square, and I would rather you did not think the two things are in the same pocket. They are not. I would be ashamed of the pairing.**”

---

Ilsley put the form on the table, which was the first time in twenty-three days that anybody had put a piece of paper on that table, and he did not spread it out, and he said:

“**Before I open it, three things, and I am going to say all three of them and then I am going to read you the list and then you may ask me things, and if you do not ask me things I shall be brief, because this is a short piece of work and I have four more of them before the ninth hour.**”

“Carry on.”

“**One: this is not a seizure of the room and there is nothing in it about a room. Two: it is not about the frame in the yard and I have not asked for the frame and I am not going to. Three: it is about papers, and every paper on the list is a copy of a sheet belonging to his Majesty's service, and a copy of such a sheet made in a room is that office's from the moment it is made, and holding it is not a thing that any person may do, and I am not going to argue that with you because there is no argument in it and it is about four hundred years old and older than the county.**”

“Then what is your ground.”

“**A district instruction in the district's own hand, signed, of a day in the winter, and it is folded into the form and I have not got it out because it is not mine to have out.**”

“I would like to read it.”

“**You may read it. That is not a favour, it is what the form is for.**”

---

He read it standing up, and it was about four inches of writing, and it said that a district return of about eleven sheets had been found in a private hand in a private room, and that the sheets were of a district's business, and that the district required the copies returned.

And the signature at the foot was **Pell Rill's**, and Tomas Vale had read the hand of that name twice in four counties and on a sheet of his own, and the hand had not changed.

“**I do not know him,**” said the officer. “**I have his name on a form. That is the whole of what I have got and it is enough, and I have got four more of these before the ninth hour and two of them are in this town.**”

“That is the auditor's own return. I have a sheet in his own hand that he gave me himself, with the hour on it, in a room over a haberdasher's shop, four days' road up here.”

“**Then you will not mind that the sheet in your coat is not a copy and is not on my list, and that I have read the list twice and your name is not against it, and that I would have taken it in about four seconds if it had been on the list.**”

“No. I would not mind at all.”

“**Good. That is the first time in four years that a man has said that to me about a paper he wanted to keep.**”

---

Then he read the list out, and he read it slowly, and Tomas Vale stood at the side of his own table and let it be read.

> *One. A carbon of a form of the second series, entered at a district office in another county, with a reading-out on the reverse in a private hand. **The carbon only. The office holds the original.**
>
> *Two. A fair copy, in a private hand, of one line out of a book of refusals kept at that office, being the hundred and ninetieth refusal in the series, entered on the fiftieth day of the month.
>
> *Three. A fair copy, in a private hand, of the practice as approved, being the paragraph that carries a district's approving signature.
>
> *Four. A fair copy, in a private hand, of the head of a round's schedule, being the ruling and the last rule and the number of the lines, taken from a schedule on a table in a clerk's hall in this town on the sixty-second day of the month.
>
> *Five. A fair copy, in a private hand, of one line out of an answering column, being a household in the east row of this town, taken on the sixty-sixth day of the month from a line shown at a counter.*

That is the whole of the list and it took him about two minutes and there were five things on it.

---

He did not build a case. He did not denounce anything. He did not say a word.

He asked for one thing, and the thing he asked for was the only question available to him, and it took him about nine seconds to get it out and it is in this book exactly as he said it.

“**Who is the room in the form.**”

Ilsley said, “**What.**”

“The form. It is on my table. **Who is the room in the form.**”

And the officer turned it round and put his finger on it and read it out, and it is worth having entire, because it is the shape of a thing and it is four lines long and there is nothing in it but a man and a day and a list:

> *Papers of the district's business found in a private room in this town and copied in a private hand, being five sheets and no more.*
>
> *Found at the fourth hour of the eighty-first day of the month in a room over a draper's shop on the north side, and the room is over a draper's shop and the shop is named at the foot.*
>
> *Taken into the district's keeping and receipted.*
>
> *Signed: C. Ilsley, officer, this county.*

“There is no room in it,” said Curtis Ilsley. “**There is a shop at the foot. There is no room. I have read that form out to four men in this county this month and not one of them has asked me a question about it, and you are the first, and you asked it in about nine seconds, and the answer is that there is no room in it because there is nothing in it that a room is.**”

“Then who is it for.”

“**You are, in one line, and the line is a man being read by, and I have entered a thing that a person may be found by in nine years if anybody wants to look for it, and that is what a form is and I do not make them and I have never made one.**”

---

And then Ivo Trant said the fifth clause out loud, in a street, at about the sixth hour, and he said it because it was the answer to the second half of the question, and Tomas Vale had not known that was coming.

He said it in the street and not in the room, and he said that he was going to say it in a street, and he gave his reason first, which was that a man who has asked a question in a room is entitled to be told the answer where he asked it, and he was going to be blunt about the fact that he had chosen the answer's place and not the question's.

Then he read it, in a clerk's flat voice, in about nine seconds, from memory, which is the fact that made Tomas Vale put a hand on the wall:

“**This receipt does not make the holder a keeper of any route, and no answer to any signal carried on or by the object is to be entered as anybody's answer for a household of this ward, and the holder has no standing to speak for any household in Kells or in any ward, and the ward's records are the ward's.**”

“That is a clause of my own receipt. I asked a clerk in a watch house in another county to add it, at about the fifth hour of the morning of the thirty-third, and it took about four minutes, and it is the only thing I have ever asked an office for that I was glad to have.”

“**Then it has taken your room off you and it has done it in nine seconds and neither of us had to do anything.**” Trant put his hands behind his back. “**I have been at that door twenty-two years and I have never once had to use that clause and I have never once had to know what it said, and a man from four counties has put it in a piece of paper to protect himself from an office, and it has just been read out in this town to the man who put it there, and it is the reason there is no room in that form. It is the reason there is no household in that form. If you could speak for a household then a form could put a household in you, and there is no box for it and there has never been a box for it, and that is not a protection anybody designed. It is a protection you asked for.**”

“**And it is on my side.**”

“**It is on your side and I want to tell you exactly how much, and I have thought about it in the street for about an hour and I have not come round to a kind word about it. There is one thing that paper can do to you and it can read you. It cannot enter a claim for you and it cannot answer for you and it cannot make you responsible for anybody, because you have no standing to be responsible for anybody. So the form has a man in it and nothing else. That is the whole of your paper being on your side and I would not build a fire on it, because the day a man with a standing in this county wants that room he will have it, and you have none, and that is the only reason he cannot.**”

And then he said the thing that made the day:

“**And I want it noticed that the same clause is the reason five sheets went out of that room, and I do not know that it is and neither do you, and I have been in this building twenty-two years and I have never once thought about who copies what, and a man from four counties has been in my hall twice and read four hundred sheets off my counter and the reason he was able to copy them is a clause about standing, and a clause about standing is not a clause about paper.**”

---

They took the five sheets at about the sixth hour and Ilsley receipted them, one at a time, on a printed slip with a number on it, and gave Tomas Vale a receipt for each, in his own hand, with the hour, and read the numbers out loud twice, and the receipts were correct.

The carbon was the third one he put in the satchel, and he said, unasked:

“**That is the only copy outside that building and there is a copy inside it, and after the fourth hour today there is one copy of that form in the world and it is in a district office in another county, and that is not a loss. It is a form with a refusal on the back of it in your own handwriting, and a form does not care what is written on the back of it, and there is nobody in that office who will ever read the back of it.**”

“No,” said Tomas Vale. “There is not.”

The bench went at about the seventh hour, and the bench went because it belonged to the draper's shop and had been lent, and the draper said so himself and said it in one sentence and was not unkind about it and did not put it in a form:

“**That is my bench. It has been my bench for about thirty-one years and it was in your room because I have a room and you had nowhere to put a barrow, and I would like it back, and I am not going to charge you for the room to-day because I have taken the bench.**”

“I will take it.”

“**And you are out on the eighty-second and not before, and I have said that because a man who finds out on the morning is a man who has been told he can go tonight, and I would rather you had it now.**”

---

The frame was in the yard behind the shop and it was not in the room, and it was not touched.

Ilsley said so in the yard, at about the seventh hour, standing four feet off it, with the satchel shut, and he did not go nearer:

“**I have not asked for the object and I am not going to and it is not on my form. It is in a cradle with four bolts made fast and there is no clapper in it and I have been told so by the man in charge of the shelves of a clerk's hall and I have believed him, and I would like it entered that an officer of this county stood four feet off a Crown survey carrier in a yard behind a draper's shop and did not ask for it, and would like it entered that I did not want to.**”

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

“**You want it entered that you did not want to.**”

“**I want it entered because I am going to be asked in about four years by somebody whether this town was clean, and the answer is going to be that a man came out of this county in about five days and he took five sheets and he did not take a bell, and I would like both halves of that on a piece of paper with my name at the bottom of it, and I would like the half I did not want to on it, because the half I did not want to is the half that will not be believed.**”

---

He wrote the last entry of the eighty-first at about the eighth hour of the evening, on the step of the draper's, in the flat hand, with the hours, and he filled the first and third columns and left the second and the fourth as they were.

> *The eighty-first. I have lost the room, the bench and the copies, and it took about three hours, and I did not resist and I did not denounce anything and I did not build a case, and I want it written down that I could have built one in about eleven minutes and that I did not, and that the reason I did not is that there is nothing in it to build one out of.*
>
> ***The thing I have been building for six weeks had no owner and no claim and could be taken by a man with a form in about two hours, and the law that took it is a clause of a receipt I asked a clerk to add to in another county, and I asked for it because I was right, and it has cost me a room and a bench and five sheets, and I want both halves of that in the same sentence and I am not going to separate them.***
>
> *And the harder half, and I have been putting it off since about the seventh hour: **the right I have felt about this room for six weeks was not a right. It was a habit.** I have been carrying things for six weeks.
>
> *I carried a sentence for thirty-three days and it was never a door. I carried nine names in a coat and they were a list.
>
> *And I have been carrying a room over a draper's shop as though a room over a draper's shop were mine in any sense a man can hold, and it was not mine, and it was let to a man fourpence an hour and a shilling for the fire, and a bench was lent to a man by a shop, and the five sheets were copies of sheets another building holds, and **a habit of carrying is not a thing anybody can defend.
>
> *A right is a thing you can put in a form. I have not got one and I did not know that was what I had been doing instead, and I have found out about four hours after a man with a form read it out to me in a room I was going to be out of in two days.***
>
> *And the thing that was on my side, and I am going to write it flat and I am not going to enjoy it: **there was no room in the form.** There is a shop at the foot and a man in one line and five numbers and a signature, and the reason there is no room in it is the fifth clause of my own receipt, and the reason there is no household in it is the same, and a form cannot enter a claim against a man who has no standing to hold a claim.
>
> *And so a piece of paper came into this room this morning and could do exactly one thing to me, which was read me. **That is the first time in six weeks that a paper has been on my side and it is on my side because it is empty, and I am not going to be told it was a comfort and I am not going to take it as one and I am going to remember it because I am afraid I will need it.***
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, the hours flat against them, and I am merging nothing: **a clerk in a hall read a clause of my receipt out loud in a street at about the sixth hour because it was the answer to a question I had asked, and said that it had taken the room off me and that neither of us had to do anything, and he was right, and he said out loud that the same clause was the reason five sheets went out of that room and that neither of us knows it and he has been in that building twenty-two years and has never once thought about who copies what.***
>
> *And an officer of this county stood four feet off a Crown survey carrier in a yard and did not ask for it, and asked for the half he did not want to be written down to be written down, and gave me a receipt for each sheet with the hour, and told me unasked that after the fourth hour today there is one copy of that carbon in the world and it is in a building in another county and nobody in that building will ever read the back of it, and I said no, there is not, and he was right and I was right and neither of us has any use for it.*
>
> ***All five clauses stand. None of them was broken today by losing the room and none of them was broken by keeping it and I am writing that down because a man who has just lost something is the man most likely to tell himself a clause was broken. Nothing was rung. The frame is in this yard with four bolts made fast and no clapper and I have not been inside the cradle. I have five receipts and thirteen documents and a barrow and a road and a woman who did not write one word on a slate in a market yesterday and has not asked me for anything. And I am out of this room on the eighty-second and I am not going to be a man who is told it was worth it, and nobody is going to come and tell me it was, and if one does I will write down that he said it and the hour.***
