# Chapter 0098 — What The Town Does With A Week

She stood in the middle of the market of about nine hundred people on the morning of the eighty-fourth day of the month with three questions in her, and she did not ask one of them, and that is the whole of the first hour and a half of the eighty-fourth.

**A household is five people and a door and about an hour. A town is two thousand people and no door and no hour.** She had worked that out on the eightieth and written it down and it had not improved by the eighty-fourth, and she has put those three questions to ten doors in four counties and to a great many more than that in eleven years, and she has never once put them to a market, and she was not going to start because a man had stood on a block in one on the seventy-seventh and said four sentences.

So she did the other thing, which is the one thing she has, and she stood at a queue of about five at a pitch of needles and thread and she watched a market of about nine hundred people say the same nine-minute thing to each other about nine hundred times.

---

Because that is what a town does with a week, and it is not what anybody expects, and it is not a refusal and it is not a cart.

It is a queue.

Two women in a line at a fish pitch said a name to each other, flatly, in the ordinary voice, in about eleven seconds, and the woman in front turned round, and then said it back to her, and about nine people heard it, and then a third woman at the back of the line said it to nobody at all, in the ordinary way, the way you say a thing you have said before.

And a man at a pitch of nothing in particular said his neighbour's name to his neighbour and the neighbour said it back, and a woman with a barrow of onions said a name to a man she did not know and the man said it back to her before she had got to the end of the sentence, because he had heard a woman say it at a well and he had said it himself and he knew what it was.

Not the names. The *act.*

A claim said twice in a market is a thing a market does now, and it started at ten feet on the seventieth from a woman with a slate, and nobody voted on it and nobody said it out loud as a thing anybody had decided, and by the eighty-fourth it is the ordinary way a queue works in this town, and about a hundred people did it yesterday, and none of them knew they were doing anything at all.

That is what the road found. She has been on a road for eleven years looking for a place and a road does not go to a place, and on the eighty-fourth the road found a queue, and **a queue is not a place and cannot be looked up and cannot be sent a rider and has no name on any map in this kingdom, and I have spent eleven years learning to go to places.**

---

And the figure, and she will not improve it.

**A bell in a market takes about nine days to change a town.** She has got one bell and it is **seven**, from the seventy-seventh to this morning, and she is going to write the nine down because a rule wants more than one, and she is going to write the seven down because the seven is what she has, **and I am not going to be the woman who improves a figure she has one of, and I have watched four people in this town do that about four times this month and it is the whole of what a careful life is.**

---

The first of the four was at about the fourth hour of the morning, in a queue of about six at a pitch of butter and nothing else, and it took her about nine minutes including the queue.

A woman of about five in the house said the name of her own house in the way people say it in a queue, flatly, to a woman in front of her who was not listening, and said that the line was the house's and not hers and had been the house's since before she was married, and that she was owed by it and did not know by how much and had asked twice and been told the price of a pitch, and said all of that in about eleven seconds, to a stranger, and did not mind who heard.

Tamsin Rook stood at ten feet and said the whole of it back.

Not to the stranger. **To the woman.** In the woman’s own words, in the ordinary voice, at ten feet, so that about nine people heard it and so that anybody within about nine feet of a pitch of butter in a market of about nine hundred heard it, and it was the same four inches it had been on the eightieth and it did not feel like the same thing at all.

And the woman at the pitch of butter turned round.

“**Say it again.**”

And that is the whole of the act, and it is four inches long, and it has been the whole of it for eleven years: **you say a person's own words back and they turn round, and the turning round is the part that does the work, and nothing else does.**

---

The one question was at about the fifth hour of the morning, and it was one question, and it was not one of the three, and it took her about nine seconds to ask and about nine seconds to get a straight answer, and it went through her like a hand in a bell and she had to put her bag down.

She asked a man at a pitch of cloth about four feet long: **“Who said that to you.”**

He said: “**My brother. At the well, in the spring. And I have said it here twice since and about nine people heard it the first time, because a woman with a slate stood ten feet off and said it back to me in my own words, and I have said it four times since then and I am going to keep saying it, and I have not stopped and nobody has asked me to stop.**

“**And I will tell you what nobody has told you, because you have got a slate and you have not written anything on it in an hour and a half: I said it at the well in the spring and I said it here last week and a woman said it back to me at ten feet and about nine people heard it, and it is the same as saying it at a well, except that at a well it is gone in a minute and this time it is not, because nine people heard it and one of them is my neighbour's wife and she said it to her brother in a queue on the seventy-eighth.**”

“That is four times.”

“**Four is the count and I have not counted it and you are not going to count it for me, and I am not going to say a number, and you can go away now.**”

And he turned back to his cloth, and that is the whole of the one question, and she did not ask a second one.

---

And about nine people in this town know that their names are on a piece of slate, and she knows two of them, and this is how she knows and it took her until about the sixth hour of the morning to see it.

It is not that anybody has said so. A town of two thousand is a small thing and nine is a number a town can hold, and what she has seen since the seventh hour of the morning is **two women in this market who said a sentence and then said, in the ordinary way, as an aside, *and I have said that before, and I have said it here twice now*** — which is the ordinary way a person tells you they have already been written down, and neither of the two of them knew that they were telling her anything at all.

**Nine is not a large number. Nine is a number a market can hold and a town cannot forget and a clerk's hall cannot even see.** He wrote nine names on a slate on the seventy-sixth because he could not stop, and he wrote it down again at the eleventh hour of that evening with the hour against it, and he went on to a market the next morning and put a bell on a block.

And about nine hundred people heard a bell on the seventy-seventh and I have been in nine of them this morning, and none of the nine has a cart coming and none of the nine has a man at the door, and two of the nine are standing in a market saying that they have already been said.

---

And at about the seventh hour she was in the east lane, at the end of it, at a gable with a lean-to, and the door was shut and she did not knock, and then the door opened, and it was Bess Marrow, and Bess Marrow said the thing that is the finding of the eighty-fourth before anybody had said one word.

“**You have not come back to this door.**”

“No, and I am not going to.”

“**There was a bell in the square on the seventy-seventh. There were about nine hundred people in it. There was a man's name in it and it was a clerk's name and I heard it and I have said so to about four people and I have not said it to my mother because she cannot read and I am not going to have it in this kitchen twice. And nobody has come to this door.**”

“Nobody has come to this door for what.”

“**For the book. Which is in the drawer, where it has been for nine years, and which you did not ask me for and have not come back for, and I have been sitting in this kitchen for seven days with a fire in it waiting to be asked about the drawer, and nobody has, and I have had my mother's tea in here for seven days and I have not had to be careful of anything at all.**”

She stood in the doorway and did not come in, because she was not asked and she has never once been asked.

“And what has changed.”

“**The market has started saying it back.**”

---

They stood about two feet apart in a doorway for about four minutes and a half and it was not a conversation and neither of them improved it.

“**I am going to tell you the two things and then I am going to shut this door, and the first is that the thing I have been afraid of for nine years is a cart, and there has not been a cart, and I have not got a reason and I am not going to be given one and I am not going to ask.**

“**And the second is that the market saying it back is worth more to this house than the fire, and I am not going to say why, and I have not got a number for it and I am not going to make one up, and I have watched the nine hundred in that square over seven days and I have counted the things that have changed and I have counted about four.**

“**Four. One, a queue says a name back. Two, a man with a barrow put a clerk's name in a square and nine hundred people heard it and the clerk is right and everybody in this town now knows that a clerk can be named. Three, about eleven men who work with their hands met in a room over a saddler's shop on the eighty-third and about a hundred people have heard that they met. And four, and this is the one I have not got round:**”

“**nobody has been told what to do.**”

“That is the fourth thing.”

“**That is the fourth thing and it is the only one I have not got round, and I have been sitting with it since about the fourth hour this morning, and here it is and then I am shutting the door: a town has decided a chore. Not a policy. Not a practice. **A chore.** Somebody has to go and put his own hand on his own line, and that is the smallest thing a town can decide, and it is the only thing anybody has decided, and I do not think it will be enough and I am not going to be the woman who says it will not be enough, because the four things I have counted are the only four things I have got and I am not going to be told they are nothing.**”

She shut the door on about the seventh hour and it was not hard, and Tamsin Rook stood about nine feet from it in a lane in a town of about two thousand people and did not knock and did not follow her and did not say one word about anything else.

---

She wrote the last entry of the eighty-fourth at about the ninth hour of the evening, on the stone landing, with her boots off, in her own hand, with the hours, and she filled the first and second columns and left the third and the fourth as they were, and the fourth had one line in it and she has not improved it and is not going to.

> *The eighty-fourth. A week. **The road has found something.** It is a queue. It is not a place, it cannot be looked up, it cannot be sent a rider, and it has no name on any map in this kingdom, and I have spent eleven years learning to go to places and I did not have to go anywhere at all and it was in this market the whole time.*
>
> ***And here is the whole of it, and it is four inches long and it is the same four inches I have had since the seventy-second: a list made in queues can be said twice, and saying it twice is the only thing in this kingdom that has ever moved a line. Not an office. Not a practice. Not a form. A person saying another person's own words back to her at ten feet in the ordinary voice in front of about nine other people, which is what the whole of this business has been, and about a hundred people in this town did it yesterday without knowing they were doing anything, and two of the nine hundred know that their names are on a piece of slate, and a town of two thousand is a small thing and nine is a number a town can hold.***
>
> *A bell in a market takes about nine days to change a town. I have got one bell and it is seven. I have written the nine down because a rule wants more than one, and I have written the seven down because the seven is what I have, and I am not going to be the woman who improves a figure she has one of, and I have watched four people in this town do that about four times this month.*
>
> *And a household at the end of the east lane has a book in a drawer and nobody has been to her door about it for seven days, and a man rang a bell in a square on the seventy-seventh and about nine hundred people heard a clerk's name, and nobody has been told what to do, and the town has decided a chore, and the chore is the smallest thing a town can decide, and I do not think it will be enough and I have not got a figure for that either and I am not going to make one up.*
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and there is one line in it and I am not going to improve it: **and it is not a cart.** Nobody in this town has been sent for. Nobody has had a man at a door this week. Nobody has been rescued and nobody has been put out and nobody has been asked for anything and there is not one refusal in this market that has cost anybody a pitch or four pounds a quarter, and the nine pitches that came off that market on the sixty-ninth are still off it, and about thirty-six pounds a quarter has not come back to nine households and is not coming back, and I have not asked anybody about it and nobody has told me it was worth it and I am not going to.*
>
> *Nobody asked me for a reason. I did not ring anything. **The four columns stand and nothing is merged and the fourth has one line in it and I have not improved it and I am not going to. I have got a slate with four things said twice in it and the price side is on the other face and it is the other way up, and I have given away two slates in a month and I have not asked for either of them back, and I am not going to start.***
