# Chapter 0083 — The Cost Of Being Right

He had eleven days and a town of two thousand people and he had four correct answers in four hands and no lever, and on the morning of the sixty-ninth he worked out that he was going to make one true sentence, out loud, in a room, in his own name, about a household he had no standing in, and that the reason he was going to do it was that if he did not there would be a cart in that row a fourth time.

That is the whole of the decision and it took about nine minutes and he wrote the time down.

He did not want to do it. That is in the book in his own hand and it is the first line of the sixty-ninth and it is not improved: *I have not wanted to do anything in this town for about two days and this is the first thing I have wanted to do since the sixty-first and it is the wrong thing and I know the shape of it and I am going to do it anyway.*

---

He went to the door of the clerk's hall at about the seventh hour of the morning.

Ivo Trant was behind the counter and looked at him for the length of one breath and then said, before he had got to the door, "**You are standing in my street again.**"

“I am. I am not going in.”

“Good.” Trant put down a pen, which was the whole of the difference between the two men and it was the entire of it. “**I told you not to stand in my street again and I meant it the way I say things, and here is the other half of that sentence: I have not closed the door, and I have said that out loud twice now in a fortnight and I would like it noticed that neither of those two facts is a kindness. Ask me about the work.**”

He asked about the work.

“**Which clerk closes a line that is in dispute.**”

“**Nobody. A line in dispute is not closed by a clerk.** A clerk enters it. A line in dispute is a line where the office and the household do not have the same account, and it cannot be closed on the office's own motion, and it is not shut by anybody in this building, and it is not shut by me, and I would like that in whatever you are keeping.” He squared a sheet. “**What happens to a line in dispute is that the schedule wants it shut, and the schedule does not want, and so a man goes to the door. That is what a cart is. It is not a punishment and it is not the office being cross. It is the only instrument there is for a line that cannot be shut on paper.**”

“How many times can that happen to one household.”

“**As many as it is in dispute.**” He said it without any weight. “It is a working day for a clerk and a working day for them and it comes on a day nobody chooses.”

“Can a room be hired in this town where a person may put a thing in front of nine people and have it read out.”

“That is a question about the work too and I do not know why you are asking it the way you are.” Trant looked up. “**You are not asking me about a room. You are asking me whether Mr Nabb will come to it.**”

“Yes.”

“He will come if you ask him **here, at this door, in front of me**, and I will not stop you and I will not help you, and I will not pretend I have not worked out what it is. **The hall puts up no name and the hall is not in the room and the room is yours. Fourpence an hour and it is a shilling for the fire and it is to a woman with a draper's shop and she will want it in advance because everybody wants it in advance.** And I will tell you the thing I have not told you in this fortnight, which is that I have known a man in this town be right about a column and go out of the room and do nothing with it for a month, and I have watched it twice, and **it is not a satisfaction in the room. It is a satisfaction in the room and a cart in a row in about four days, and the row is in this town and the cart comes from four miles away and nobody in the room is in the row.**”

He put the pen down again.

“Ask him. Not in here. **At this door.**”

---

Elias Nabb came to the door at about the second hour of the afternoon and Tomas Vale said the whole of it in about eleven seconds in the street, standing on the step, and it was one sentence and it was in his own name and it was not a case and it did not denounce anything.

“**I was in a room on the sixty-sixth with nine people in it, and a household said a sentence in their own words, and the sentence went into a column in their own words, and a clerk asked for a hand against the line about four seconds afterwards, and the hand was not the hand of the person who spoke, and the record is good, and the record is the thing that is going to cost them, and I have known that since I stood against a wall for two hours and ten minutes and I did not say one word, and that is my part in it and there is no part of it that anybody else did.**”

Nabb did not move for about four seconds.

“**Then you have said a thing in a street that you cannot take back, and there is a person in this town who is going to have to act on it, and he is me, and you have not asked me to, and that is the only reason I am still standing here.**”

“I have not asked you to do anything.”

“You have said it **loudly enough for me to hear it and in my own hearing and in front of a man who is in charge of the shelves**, and there is no form in this kingdom for what a man is supposed to do with that, and I have about four seconds to work out what I am going to do about it, and I have already worked it out, and I am going to tell you what it is, **and then you are going to tell me you knew, and then we are both going to go to a room.**”

---

The room over the draper's on the north side of the square cost him a shilling for the fire and the fourpence and he paid it in advance, and about eleven people came, and the draper was not one of them and knew what was in it and went to bed.

There were nine people and two of them were not his. Ivo Trant came and sat at the back and did not speak once. A carter from a row off the market came because a room was open, and one of the market's own people came, and the rest were people who had been in the room on the sixty-sixth and had come again out of what was, in the end, a habit.

**Nobody in that room knew they were in a hearing. That is the sentence the day-book is built on and it was written at about the eleventh hour of the evening and it is the finding of the sixty-ninth:** *I did not denounce anybody and I did not prove anything and I did not build a case, and every person in that room was there to be in a room, and four of them are the reason a cart is going to stand in a street tomorrow evening, and not one of them knew it when they were there.*

He read the line out. It was the same line. **Household of five, east row, a cheese and butter pitch, third along and then at the end of the row, line open four years, answered no twice, in dispute, entered in a room at an hour the office was open, any person with business in the line may attend.**

Then he said the sentence again, in the room, out loud, in his own name, and the second time it went further, because a room is a different animal from a street.

Elias Nabb stood at the counter side of it with his coat on and asked him, in front of everybody, and in nine seconds, and not unkindly, and the room heard all of it:

“**Mr Vale. On the sixty-sixth, in the room you have just described, in a line about a household of five, a hand went against the line. Was the hand the hand of a person of that household.**”

“No,” said Tomas Vale. “**It was the hand of a person of that household and it was not the hand of the person who had spoken, and I was the only person in that room who could see both of those, and I saw both, and I said nothing, and I have said this in a street and now I am saying it in a room, and my name is on it.**”

---

Nabb took about eleven seconds. Tomas Vale counted them, because counting things is what he does, and it was eleven, and in another room on the sixty-second it had also been eleven minutes and he was not going to improve the coincidence.

“I want to say four things and then I am going to do one, and I am going to do the one, and I am not going to enjoy any part of it, and I want it understood that I have not been asked to and that I would rather not have heard it.

“**One. I am a clerk and I hold no office and everything I have written in four years is mine and nobody else's. I have said that in this town twice and I am saying it a third time and I am saying it now because a man has just put a true sentence about a line in front of me in a room and I have about eleven seconds and I need to know where I am standing.**

“**Two. The practice in this hall is a settlement of a difficulty and the difficulty is a household and I did not write that and I think it is right. I have read it about nine times. I have thought about it since the sixtieth, when I wrote a sheet for a stranger, and I have thought about it this morning. It is right. I am not going to take it back off a counter because a man has been in a room with nine people in it and has been in a room himself.**

“**Three —**” and here he stopped, and looked at the fourpence sheet, and did not go on for about four seconds, and Tamsin Rook, who was at the back and had not been asked to be and had come, watched his shoulders and not his face, because that is the whole of what eleven years of a road has taught her and it is still true —

“**Three. You are right. I have read that line since the sixty-sixth. The line cannot be shut on the office's motion. It is in dispute and a line in dispute is not shut by a clerk and the schedule wants it shut, and the schedule does not want.**

“**And four. I am going to close it, and I am going to close it the only way there is, and the only way there is is a man at a door.**”

Nobody said anything.

“I have to enter it, because that is the fourth column and it is four inches wide and it is the best box on the paper. And I am going to write the whole of it, and I am going to write that a person at this address has said, in a room, that the record is the thing that is going to cost this household, and that the person is a man with no standing and I have entered his name because I am obliged to and not because I wished to.” He had the pen out. “**And a line in dispute goes down for a man at a door. And I have never in four years sent a cart to a row that was not in dispute, and this one is in dispute now in a way it was not in dispute on the sixty-sixth, and I did that, and I have entered that I did it.**”

---

**And then the market keeper stood up at the back, which nobody had expected, because he is a man with a book of his own and no office and about nine rows, and he had been in a room twice in three days and had said in the first one that he wished it noticed that nobody had asked him to.**

“**Then that is three,**” he said.

Nobody looked at him.

“**I said it in this room on the sixty-sixth and I want it written down that I said it and nobody asked me to again, and it was in front of about nine people and one of them had a sheet. A cart came down that row on the sixty-third at about the ninth hour and stood at the end of it. A cart came down it on the sixty-sixth at the ninth hour and stood at the end of it. A third one comes tomorrow because a clerk has entered a line in dispute in a room over a draper's shop.**”

He put his hands in his pockets.

“**So I move that row off the market. Not one pitch of it. The whole of it. Nine pitches, and there is nowhere else in this town, and I have said that in this room on the sixty-sixth and I am saying it now.**”

Nobody said anything. It went on about four minutes and it was the longest four minutes of the sixty-ninth and there was nothing in it to look at.

“I am not saying it to the house at the end of the row. I want that said out loud. **I am saying it to nine pitches and I am saying it to a man with a shilling's worth of fire in this room, and one of those two is why.** I would have said the same thing in a week if a third cart had come down that row, and I have said so on the day it happened and I will say it again.”

---

The cart came at about the ninth hour of the evening. It was not a market day and it did not need to be. A line in dispute is a working day for a clerk and a working day for a household, and it comes on a day nobody chooses, and there is nothing left in this town this month for the schedule to be shut on paper, so it had nothing to do at all except stand at the end of a row.

**Ock Randall** took a stall of nothing in particular off that row and had about nine pitches and had been in the row twenty years. **Jessa Vinch** had a pitch of butter and had been in the row nine years. There were nine pitches and about nine households behind them and the numbers in the room and outside it were these and they are the whole of the cost and they are not one of them a child:

**Nine pitches off a market of about nine hundred, and nowhere else in this town to go, and about four pounds a quarter off each of them over a season, and about thirty-six pounds a quarter off nine households in a year when the season is what it is.** The Larkins had already lost the front of the row and were at the end of it and would now be at nothing, because the end of a row that is not there is not the end of a row. Ock Randall's twenty years were in a lane. Jessa Vinch had bought her butter on credit against that pitch every market day for nine years, and the credit was the pitch, and there is no other pitch.

**Nobody was rescued. Nobody was told this was necessary. Nobody was told it was anybody's fault, because it was not, and he heard that said four times in a room and once in a street and it made no difference whatever and he wrote it down with the hour against it because he is that sort of man and because it is the only thing he has ever been able to do about a thing of this kind.**

---

He did not sleep much and he wrote for about two hours, which is a duration, and he put the hours in the flat hand and he filled all four columns and he did not merge two of them, which is the only thing he is still good at.

> *The sixty-ninth. I said one true thing out loud in a room in my own name about a household I have no standing in, and it cost nine pitches, and it is the first time in six weeks that being right has been worth anything to anybody and I am not going to be allowed to put it in a column as a thing I did.*
>
> *Let it be written in the plainest words I have and then I will shut this book until tomorrow.*
>
> ***Being right and being useful are two different tools and I have only ever been issued one of them, and I have spent six weeks learning that in the correct order, in four counties, at four counters, in a market town, and I did it by carrying the one I had and telling myself it was the other.***
>
> *On the fortieth of this month a woman on a road in the rain gave me a sentence and I have carried it for a month and put it in four buildings. A district officer has told me a district is a ground and I cannot act on a ground. A clerk has told me that a household that cannot agree has never been asked, and a household can be asked, and a household is the only thing in this kingdom that can refuse. Both of them told me the truth. **I used both of them. That is what nobody has told me in five weeks and I have worked it out standing in a lane about half an hour ago and I am not proud of it in the way I expected to be not proud of it.***
>
> *An office can act on a practice. A person can be acted on by a person. **There is no clause about that anywhere in this kingdom. There is no form for it, no box, no notice on any wall, and nothing anybody can be hanged for, and that is exactly why it is the only thing in the world that works on a person in a way nothing else does.** And I did it with a sentence that was true. I have been careful for five weeks about what is true and I have got the use out of a true sentence in a room tonight for the first time in my life and I did it to a man with a coat on who is right, who has a child and a house and about eleven years, who did not ask to be in the room and who is going to send a cart down a row tomorrow because a stranger made being right worth something.*
>
> *And nobody in that room knew they were in it. I have been five weeks asking rooms questions and putting things in front of people and I have never once stopped to ask what a room is, and a room is nine people who came because a door was open, and what happens in a room happens to them afterwards in a street I will not be standing in. **I did not build a case. I did not denounce anything. I said a true thing once, in my own name, and a man who is right did the rest, and he did the rest correctly, and I would have done it differently if I had been able to do it at all, which I could not.***
>
> *And the sentence that did it is not a secret. It is a line in a book in a hall and a mark in a column and the office's own fourth column, which is four inches wide, which is the best box on the paper, which has no box for a reason on it, and which was written by the best trade worker in this town about four years ago and who is going to be read by it for the rest of his life. **A record is a sentence other people may read. I have had that in my own book for three days and I wrote it as a thing I had found out. It is a thing I did.***
>
> *I have lost the last thing I came down the water for and I did not lose it to a man. I lost it to a method I was proud of. **And a method is not a tool. A tool is a thing with a handle and a cost on it, and a method is a thing that fits you, and the whole of what I have learned in this town is that mine fits me and does not fit anybody else and I have been carrying it round four counties like a lamp I could not switch off.***
>
> *What I have left is one true sentence a week, and no standing, and a receipt with five clauses in it, and a frame in a yard four streets off with four bolts in it that cannot be rung, and a bell in a smith's yard in this street that has no claim in it and that I am not going to ring in this county at all.*
>
> *And there is a woman in this town who is better at this than I am and has not said so and I am not going to put it in this book, because it is not mine and it is not this week.*
>
> *Nine pitches, thirty-six pounds a quarter, a market of about nine hundred, and nowhere in this town to go. **Ock Randall, twenty years, to a lane. Jessa Vinch, nine years of butter bought on credit against a pitch that is not there. The Larkins at the end of a row that is not there.** Written down with the hour against them, in the flat hand, in the third column, and I am not going to call it necessary, and nobody is going to come here tomorrow and tell me it was necessary, and if one does I will write down that he said it and the hour.*
