# Chapter 0063 — The Name On The Return

The district renewal office at Helling is on a street of four houses and a yard behind it and a door that has been painted twice, and the district's number is on a plate by the knocker, and there is a notice nailed beside the plate about the receiving and the going out of returns, and the notice is a good notice, and it has a number on it, and the number is the number of a desk.

Tomas Vale read the notice from the steps at about the ninth hour of the forty-eighth and then went in and said the thing he had said at a door with a duck's-egg colour on it in a town three counties back, in almost the same words, because he had practised it on the water for a day and a half and had not improved it.

“I want to put four questions to a district office in the same form I put four questions to one at Fourmile, and I want the answers in the same hand, and I am going to ask the questions in the same words and I am not going to tell the office anything the office has not got already.”

The deputy district officer read the sheet standing up.

Her name is on the plate inside and it is **Ilma Carrow**, and she had been in the post about four years and had been a clerk in it for six before that, and she read the four questions in about two minutes and then looked at the barrow in the yard behind him with a frame in a cradle on it and four bolts in it, and then back at the sheet.

“You have been to Fourmile,” she said.

“On the forty-second.”

“Then I know what this is, and I am not going to pretend to you that I do not, and I am not going to be unkind about it either, because you have written the four questions out in your own hand and dated them and put the hour against each, and nobody has ever put the hour against a question for me before.” She turned the sheet over. “And I am going to tell you two things before I answer anything, because both of them are about this office and not about you.”

“All right.”

“**The first is that there is no form here for a person putting a question.** There is a paper you have brought and a book we write our answers in, and there is nothing that says a person may give a question in writing, because a person giving a question in writing is not a thing this trade does, and I have been four years in it and I have had about nine of them and eight of them wanted a name out of a file. You may leave the sheet. I will answer it in our book and you may read our book at the end of the day if you want, and I will enter the requester as *a person* unless you tell me otherwise, and you are entitled to be recorded and I am telling you the instrument so that you do not think you have been refused.”

“Thank you. I will be recorded. My name is Tomas Vale, of Kells, without district.”

“Then you will be recorded as that, and I will enter the hour, and I will not enter a reason, because this office has no field for a reason and has never had one, and I am not going to invent one at eight o'clock in the morning because a person in a barrow has brought me a sheet of paper.”

“No,” said Tomas Vale. “You are entitled to a reason. A district is entitled to know why a person is asking a question, and I did not write one, and I have known that since about the tenth mile below the head of the tide and I did not stop, and I am going to stop now and write one, and I am going to write it in the margin of my own copy because there is no field for it, and the margin is the only place a person can put a thing when the form has not got a box for it.”

“Write it in the margin,” said the deputy district officer. “That is the correct place and nobody has ever done it and I am not going to stop you.”

He wrote it standing at her counter in about four minutes and it reads, in the margin of his own copy, in his own flat hand, with the hour:

*Because I am not able to ask a man in a room a question about a bell and get an answer I can act on, and because a document is the only thing anybody can be held to in nine years, and because I have four sheets from a district office at Fourmile signed in a man's own name within the hour and I have understood none of them, and because there is a hand on four returns in a file in that building and I have the same hand in a smith's book in another county and on a form at a door in a fourth, and because I have no household of my own and no standing in any of them, and because on the forty-second a man in that building refused me one question in his own name with a reason and filed the refusal in a book where any person may ask for it, and nobody had ever asked for one, and I would like to know whether that is a thing that has happened in this office too. Nothing else. I am not asking for a name and I am not asking for a household and I am not asking this office to do anything about anybody.*

He read it back to her and she made him change one word, and he changed it, and both the word and the correction are in the sheet.

---

**The first answer, in the office's own hand, in their own book, entered with the hour against it, at about the eleventh hour:**

*Q. Does this office hold a sheet of bells, and are there fields in it, and is any field in it empty where a person should be?*

*A. We hold a sheet of market and landing bells for this district. It has six lines. Four of them have a keeper's name in the keeper's field. Two of them have nothing in the keeper's field, and one of the two is a landing bell rung by whoever is on the stage and one of the two is a water bell at a village about four miles up, and the field is empty on both for the same reason as the reason given in your fourth sheet, which is that the form has four fields and none of them fits a bell that has no route and no hour anybody can fix. We do not have a sheet of water bells as such. We have a sheet of bells, and two of the six on it are water bells, and it has been that way since before I came.*

**The second answer, in the same hand, at about the first hour of the afternoon, and it is the one that took the rest of the morning to get out of her:**

*Q. Does this office hold, or has this office ever held, a document in which one hand signs as the answer of more than one household?*

*A. Yes. Eleven years of it, and not four documents. **Four was a count of one file in one building and you have brought the count with you from Fourmile, and the count is wrong, and I want you to sit down before I go on, because the count being wrong is the only interesting thing in this office this morning.**

*In this building, on this district, in eleven years, I have a file of returns, and the returns are of about nine hundred households a year, and there are about four thousand signatures in that file if you count every confirming line, and in the box for the name of a person present at the answering, in eleven years, one hand has signed about two hundred and six times.*

*Two hundred and six is not four. I have not counted them for anybody in eleven years, because nobody has asked, and because a count is a thing a clerk does to a file for himself and not for a district. I counted them yesterday afternoon, after you had read the notice on the door, and it took me until the light went, and I counted twice.*

*And I am going to say the same six words that the officer at Fourmile is recorded as having said, and I have not seen his sheet and I have never been to that office, and I would like that in whatever you are keeping: it leans forward. The descender of the g hesitates and comes back. The downstroke of a d and of a g stop and start in the same place. And it is the same in two hundred and six places and I have read four different receiving clerks' copies of it, and the fourth of them was me, in my first year, and I did not notice it, and I am telling you that because you will want to know whether I am the kind of man who noticed.*

*And the household is not on this sheet. I am not going to give you the household and I am going to say why in one sentence and then I am going to stop on it: **a household in this district is a thing with a door on it and I do not put doors in a book that a stranger is going to read, and I have read what a document is made of this week and I have read it because you have not said it out loud and I have been in this trade for ten years and I already knew it.***

---

He did not ask her the fourth thing for about an hour, and she did not wait for it, and at about the fourth hour of the afternoon he asked for something else.

“Q. Third question, which I am going to ask now instead of asking it second, because I have read the answer to it in a building in Fourmile and I want to know whether yours is the same answer. Does this office transmit any schedule, roll or list of names to any other office, and if so to which, and by whose order.”

“**No.**” She said it before he had finished the sentence, and then stopped, and then said the rest of it. “No, this office transmits nothing, and I have never transmitted anything, and the outward bag in this building went out nine times in four years and eight of them were money. And I am going to say the second half of it, because you have been in this building ninety minutes and you have not told me one story, and I have had a month in which everybody in this district has been told a story by somebody, and the stories are all better than the thing and there is always somebody in them who deserves it.”

“Thank you.”

“And I will say the part I am not certain of, and I want it entered that I am uncertain. **What comes in is a different question and I have never been able to ask it.** We receive. Every year this office receives a schedule from a district office above us, and the schedule is a list of households, and it comes in a sealed wrapper with a number on it, and I take the wrapper and the number and I enter the number, and I do not open the wrapper, and I send the schedule on to two sub-offices and a clerk's hall in this town, and the sub-offices open it and the clerk's hall opens it, and it is in a clerk's hall in this town and I have been four feet from that hall twice a week for four years and I have never once been in it, because it is not my building and there is nothing in my office that requires me to be in it.”

She shut the book and put her hand flat on it.

“That is what I have. It is not an answer. It is a thing I have not been able to ask about in four years, and you are the first person to walk in with four questions and a form and no story, and I would be obliged if you wrote it down, because I have never written it down and I have told it out loud twice now in eleven years, and a thing told out loud is not a thing anybody can find in a year.”

---

**And the fourth answer, at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, in the same hand, with the hour on it, and it is the shortest thing on the page and it is the reason for the whole of this chapter:**

*Q. Is the schedule for route L-4 a return?*

*A. There is no L-4 in this district. L-4 is a Kells route and I have never seen the sheet and I could not certify it if you put it on my counter. What this office holds on one route is a schedule, and it is at the back of the file, and it is the largest thing in this building, and it is not a return, and I am not going to say the rest of that in a document, and the rest of that is the thing the officer at Fourmile has refused in his own name, and I have read his refusal, because it is in the book of refusals in his office and this office has a standing request on that book, and it came down on the ordinary post, and I have had it about nine days, and I have not shown it to anybody.*

He looked at her for slightly longer than a man needs to.

“You have a standing request on another district's book of refusals.”

“This office has had a standing request on that book since before I came here,” said the deputy district officer, “and I have never sent anything, because there has never been anything to send, and I have sent the refusal to the man in the room when I was told a person in his own name had asked for one, because I did not think I was allowed to keep it.”

---

And then, because he had read the second answer and had understood most of it, Tomas Vale asked the question he had come four days to ask and it is in his book in his own hand and he wrote it out before he asked it, which he has not done before:

“Deputy District Officer. Is there anything in this building about a practice. Not a practice anybody does. A practice somebody has written down as a practice, and recommended, and had approved by a person in another office in a document with a number on it.”

She did not say no.

She got up and went into the passage and came back with a different file, and it was thinner than the first one, and she put it on the counter and opened it about two thirds of the way down and turned it round and put her finger on the head of a sheet and took her finger off it.

“There,” she said. “That is the schedule for this district for the year that is closed. And behind it, on the back of the last leaf, is a return from the district office above this one, and it is not a renewal return and it is not a schedule and it has nothing to do with any household in this town. **It is a return of a practice.** And it quotes a practice in one paragraph and recommends it in the next, and the second paragraph has a reason, and the reason is good, I have read it twice, and then somebody has approved it in the third paragraph, and the third paragraph has a name at the foot of it and a date and the district's number.”

“May I read it where it sits.”

“You may read it where it sits. **You may not have it and you may not have a copy of it, and I am not refusing you a copy, I am telling you that this office has no instrument for copying a return of a practice, and that there is no form on which a person may ask for a copy of a thing that is not a return, and if I copied it for you on a sheet of my own I would be making a return in this office of a document that is not one, and I have been in this trade ten years and I am not going to do that in my first month of being a deputy.**

“And I am going to say the other half of that, because you have not argued with me once today and I have been waiting for it. **I have had it in this file for four years and I have read it about nine times and I have not known what to do about it, and I have not sent it to the district, and I have not sent it to anybody, and if you take it away and show it to somebody, then this building will have a person in it who has read a return of a practice and done nothing with it, and I would rather it had a person in it who has read it and is frightened of it, because frightened keeps a person in post.**”

“Read it, then,” said Tomas Vale, “and I will not take it and I will not copy it and I will not ask you for it, and if I write anything down I will write it with the hour and in my own hand and you may read it before I close the book.”

She turned round the sheet and pushed it two inches towards him and put her hand flat on the counter on the other side of it and let him.

---

It is a single side. It has the district's number at the head and a date and a reference, and it is four paragraphs, and the first paragraph describes the practice without naming it: **that where a household cannot make up its mind in the time the district allows, the office records the hand of one member present, and the line is thereafter treated as answered, and the office does not go back to the household.**

The second paragraph recommends it, and it recommends it in the language of a person who has thought about cost, and it is the best paragraph on the page: **that a return obtained under difficulty is more likely to be a true return than a return obtained under ease, and that a household which cannot agree is very often a household which has not been asked the question in a way it can answer, and that the office's time is the office's time and cannot be lengthened by any household's difficulties.**

The third paragraph approves it, in three words and a half, and says that the practice is adopted generally.

And the fourth paragraph is a name and a date and the district's number and a reference to a file in a town four counties off.

**PELL RILL.**

It is the first time that name has been on any page in this book. It is a good hand. It is a small hand, and the letters are upright and even, and there is a very slight thickening on the downstroke of the *p* and of the *g* that a man puts in after about twenty years of writing in a book, and it is the same thickening on both letters, which is the tell of a hand that has been used and not a hand that has been practised.

Tomas Vale read it four times. He was not allowed to take it and he did not try, and he read it four times in about nine minutes with his hands behind his back like a man in front of somebody else's fire.

Then he wrote in his own book, and this is what he wrote, and he read it back to the deputy district officer before he shut the book, and she read it and said it was right and said nothing else:

*The name is Pell Rill. It is on a document and the man is not in the room.*

*And here is the thing I have got wrong for six days and this afternoon put right for me, and I am going to write it in the plainest words I have: **a name on a return is a piece of paper about a person and not a person.** I have spent a month finding out that a document is not a fact about anything, and that a clerk who holds a thing is not a thing anybody can be held to, and I have spent a month learning to ask a room what a document is, and I have been doing all of it on the assumption that when I got to a name it would be like the other end of the road. It is not. It is like the beginning. A name on a return is a mark in a column about a man who is somewhere else, and it cannot be refused and it cannot be argued with and it cannot be asked a single question, and I cannot act on it, and I have no household and no standing and nothing to do with it but write it down.*

*And it is the first name on any page in this book and I have got four sheets from Fourmile and a book of refusals I have not read and a form with a reason in the margin and two hundred and six returns I have not seen, and a name. And a name is not a household. A household is the only thing in this kingdom that can say a thing a document may not say for it, and the name is four counties off, and the man, if he is a man and not a post, is four counties off.*

*So I have a name and it is worth nothing this afternoon, and I want it written that I said that at the time and did not write it afterwards.*

*And the fourth thing, and it is the only thing in this building I can act on tonight, and I am not going to act on it tonight either: this office has a standing request on another district's book of refusals, and it came down on the ordinary post, and it has been in a file in a building in this town for four years, and nobody in this town has read it, and the man who sent it is the man who put his refusal in a book because I asked him to.*

*The machine is not broken. That is what I keep arriving at and I keep being angry about it as though it were a fault. A man in a chair wrote a refusal down in his own name and put it where any person may ask, and a machine carried it ninety miles on an ordinary post to a building that had sent a form asking for it years ago, and the building put it in a file, and nobody read it, and the reason nobody read it is that it arrived properly and nothing happens when a thing arrives properly.*

---

And about a hundred yards from the district office there was a lane off a street, and at the top of it there was a house with four people in it and a door with their name on it, and two streets away there were two rooms over a yard with four people in them, and a man in a yard told him about it in the open, at more length than anybody else had, and none of which length was an answer, and he wrote down the fact that the house existed and the fact that the two rooms existed and he wrote down neither of them as true and he did not go up the lane that night.
“Deputy District Officer. **What is the schedule for this district?** Is it a record of what the households of this district said, or is it a record of what somebody in this building said about what the households of this district said?”

Ilma Carrow looked at him for a moment and then she went and got a sheet and wrote for about eleven minutes and gave it to him, and it is in his book and it is the fifth sheet he has, and it is the only one he has got that was not written by a man who was being careful:

*It is a record of what this office holds to be the answer of a household. It is not a record of what any household said, because no household in this district has ever said anything to this office in a room with a person in it. It is made out of returns received at this counter, entered by the hand of whoever brought them, and where a household has sent a return it is a record of what the household sent and it is a good record. Where a household has not sent a return and the year closes, the line is carried. I do not know what it is carried from. I have asked twice and been told it is carried from the standing entry and I have never found a standing entry and I have been four years in this post and I have never asked the district.*

— I. C., Deputy District Officer, this office, this day, about the sixth hour.*

He read it at the counter and then he read it again on the steps outside, in the street, with the frame in the cradle behind him in the yard and the bolts made fast at the place it was set down.

And then he went up the street to find somewhere to sleep, and they took two rooms over a shop off the market square on the terms that the barrow stood in the yard behind the shop and the bolts were made fast at the place it was set down and nobody came up the stairs about it, and the man who let them the rooms counted the bolts himself before he agreed to the yard, and the frame stayed in its cradle all night, and nothing was rung, and the receipt was whole.
