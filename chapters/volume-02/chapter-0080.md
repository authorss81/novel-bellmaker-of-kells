# Chapter 0080 — The First Household To Say No On The Record

The room was over the draper's on the north side of the square, and it was the room they had slept in for eight nights, and about nine people came to it.

That was the rule and the rule was correct. **A disputed line is entered in a room that is not the hall, at an hour the office is open, and any person with business in the line may attend.** Ivo Trant read it out from the counter on the morning of the sixty-sixth in a voice he did not enjoy, and then he said, in the same voice, that a person with no business in the line would be asked to stand outside, and Tomas Vale said nothing at all and was not asked, because nobody in that room could think of a reason to put a man out of it, and that turned out to be the whole of how it happened.

Dory Larkin saw him against the wall at about the fourth hour and looked at him for about a second and a half, and he did not nod, and she did not nod, and neither of them said anything, and then she turned round and got her brother to move a chair so that the old woman could sit down by the fire.

That was the whole of the two people in that room who had spoken to each other for two days. It is in the day-book and it is four lines long and it is in the third column.

---

**Ivo Trant** had the chair. **Elias Nabb** had the counter and a sheet on a board and it was the only sheet in the room with anything on it. **Hester Vowles** was standing by the window with her round's accounts under her arm because she was in the town for the length of the round and had not gone back. **Bryn Pethick** was in the corner and did not speak once.

There was a man from the district with a book on a stool, off the stool, holding it. There was the market keeper, who had come because there is a dispute on his row and he wanted to be in the room. There were four people off the row who had come because there is a door open and a thing happening in it and about nine houses in this town had a reason to want to see it.

And there were three Larkins. Dory Larkin, who said no. Her brother **Sam Larkin**, who has a handcart and a strap over his shoulder and does the carrying for two houses on this row and one on the next. And their mother on a chair by the fire with a blanket round her and her hands in her lap, who had not wanted to come and had come.

**And Tomas Vale stood against the wall for about two hours and ten minutes and did not say one word, and he is a man who has spent five weeks asking people questions at their own counters, and nobody in that room thought to ask him one.**

---

Elias Nabb read the line out. It took him about four minutes and he read it the way he reads everything, and he read it twice, and nobody in the room stopped him the second time.

> *Household of five. East row, third pitch, then end of row. Asked on the sixtieth. Answered, no, on the sixty-first, in words, at about the middle of the day, and entered. Answered, no, a second time, this day, in words, and entered. **The line is in dispute and the schedule does not hold open and the line is closed at the end of the round, and this entry is made in a room at an hour the office is open, and any person with business in the line may attend.** Signed, Elias Nabb, clerk, this hall, this town, this date, about the fourth hour of the afternoon.*

Nobody said anything. A fire moved.

“And the answer,” said Elias Nabb, “is entered in the answering column, in the household’s own words, and the household has chosen the column and I am entering it in the answering column, and the words are theirs and I have not put a heading on them and I have not shortened them.”

He put the sheet down and squared it and read out what a woman had said to a man with a barrow at the end of a row six days ago, in a market, and had changed one word of when it was read back.

> *We are five and we do not agree, and we are not going to be sent for again, and we mean it, and any hand on that line is one of the five of us and not the house.*

“**Is that your words.**”

“That is my words,” said Dory Larkin, “and he wrote them and read them back and I changed one word, and it went from *asked* to *sent for*, and he made the change.”

“Then it is entered as spoken and nobody may shorten it.”

And then Elias Nabb did the next thing without a pause, in about four seconds, because it is what the form is.

**“I want a hand against the line.**”

And Sam Larkin put his hand on the counter.

Nobody looked up. Nobody in that room saw anything happen. **Elias Nabb wrote the hour against it and the mark of one person in the column of a household of five and went on with the entry, and the entry was then a complete record, and the record was good, and it is on a shelf in a hall in this town with a stamp at the foot of it, and the whole of what this volume has been about happened inside ninety seconds and was in the correct form.**

---

**And Tamsin Rook saw it, and she was the only person in the room who saw it, and she did not say so.**

She was standing at the back on the stair side, and she had not come in until about the fourth hour, and she had not been asked to and had not offered, and she had been on the stair for two hours and ten minutes and she had heard every word of it through a door that was open four inches.

She saw Sam Larkin's hand go down on a counter about nine feet from her while Dory Larkin was sitting down, and she saw that the hand was not the hand of the person who had spoken the sentence, and she saw that the sentence said *any hand on that line is one of the five of us and not the house*, and she wrote it on the back of her own hand with the stub of chalk out of the slate pocket and she did not say one word to anybody in that room, and afterwards she did not say one word about it to the man she travels with, and it is the only thing in this month she has kept off a slate.

She has not been asked for a reason for anything by anybody in this batch. Nobody has held anything over her. Nobody has said one word to her about the fourth of last month. She is standing in a room and she is the only person in it who knows what just happened and she is going to keep it, and that is hers, and it is not a favour to anybody.

---

Then the market keeper said the thing nobody in the room had said, and he said it because he had come, and it took about ninety seconds.

“I am going to say the whole of it and then I am going to go home, and I have said it in a row and I am saying it in a room because there are people in this room who do not know what a row is and they should.

“**A second refusal is a dispute and a dispute gets a cart.** Not my cart. The district’s. It comes and it stands in that row and a man stands at the door with a book and it stays as long as it likes. **That is the whole of it and it is not anybody here’s fault and I have no power to stop it and I would not stop it if I had.** I have got nine pitches on that row and a market of about nine hundred on a good day, and a row with two carts standing in it is a row with half of it shut, and every one of those stalls is somebody’s season.

“**It has not happened on that row in about nine years and it happened on the sixty-first and it is happening again today.** If it is a third time then I move the whole of that row off the market, and there is nowhere else in this town, and I am telling you that now, in a room, and not at the end of a row in a week, because I am not going to be the man who tells a woman in front of a market. **And I would like it noticed that I have said it in a room and that nobody in this room has asked me to.**”

The old woman by the fire said nothing at all.

Dory Larkin said, “Then it will not be a third time.”

“That is on you and I have put it on you and I have said it out loud,” said the market keeper, “and I am going to bed.”

---

The district man with the book was at the door with his hat in his hand when he said the arithmetic, and he said it the way he had said the line's words on the sixty-first, which is to say as though he were reading out a price.

“**A cart and a man and a day.** That is what a second round is. It is not a punishment, and it is not a dispute, and nobody is going to be told anything they have not been told already. It comes on a day you do not choose and it stands in the row and a man stands at the door and looks at the line, and it is a working day for a clerk and it is a working day for you, and I have been on both sides of it and I am telling you it is the same day.**

“From the ninth hour this evening until the second hour in the morning, on this pitch, with a cart in it. **That is the whole of the cost and there is no other cost and I am not able to reduce it, and the only part of it I am able to tell you that is not in a book is that it does not have to be a whole day and it will not be, because you will be at the door at the fourth hour and there is nobody left in this district who would send a cart to a house that is at its door at the fourth hour.**”

Nobody thanked him for that either.

---

The cart came at about the ninth hour of the evening, earlier than it should have, because the round was closing and because the round had four pitches left and the Larkins' was the one everybody in that row could see.

It came up the row and stopped at the end pitch and it stood with its shafts out, and a man got down off it with a book, and Sam Larkin went out with him and stood in the road in the dark for about two hours and ten minutes, because his pitch was the end one and it was his cart, and the row was shut from the ninth hour and there was nothing to sell.

The four shillings and no more was the day's takings off a cheese and butter pitch on a market of about nine hundred, and the goods came off the pitch and went into the yard behind with a handcart and there was no room in the yard behind and they were stacked against a wall in it for the night.

And the handcart did not earn on the sixty-sixth. Two shillings and sixpence is a good day's carrying and about four houses in this town pay it, and a carter who stands in a road for two hours and ten minutes with a district cart at the end of his own row does not earn it, and a carter who misses a market is moved to the back of a board in this town and a carter at the back of a board is a carter who is offered the work after anybody else.

**The old woman by the fire said the number once and would not say it twice.**

“Sam's two and six is gone and the four shillings is gone and the pitch will be shut until the second hour and I have done that sum nine times since she sat down and it comes to the same figure every time. **The figure is no.**”

And then she said, to nobody, in a room with nine people in it, at about the eleventh hour of the evening:

“And it is the season.”

And that was all of it. Nobody said anything. The old woman was still on her chair by the fire with her hands in her lap, and the figure she had said was no, and the number stayed in the room.

---

Tomas Vale stood against the wall for two hours and ten minutes and did not speak, and he is the man who has spent five weeks asking people questions at their own counters, and he did not ask one.

The reason is in the day-book and it is short and it is the last entry of the sixty-sixth and it is the fourth column, and he wrote it at about the eleventh hour of the evening in a room with nine people in it and a woman on a stair and a man in a road:

> *The sixty-sixth. I did not speak.*
>
> *On the sixty-fourth I was asked what I wanted and I said I did not know, and the woman I asked it to was right that I had never wanted a document that I could not get and had wanted one that I could not refuse, and it is not available and it has not been available since the thirty-second. On the sixty-second I found out that the whole of my argument for five weeks is an argument about consent and the roll is not a document about consent. On the sixty-fourth a woman at the end of a row told me that refusing is free and refusing is invisible and the only defence anybody has found against a line is to be inconvenient, and it costs the front of a row and four pounds a quarter and a mother who can say a number.*
>
> *And today I stood in a room for two hours and ten minutes and watched a record be the thing that hurts them, and I could not make it stop, and **I asked them to be on it.** Not in words. In a book, four times in six days, in the flat hand, with the hours, and one of those four was a woman at the end of a lane who told me the reason she will not take her book out of her kitchen, and I wrote it down, and I did not ask her what it would cost. **That is the whole of what I have been doing since the thirty-second and I have found out about it in a room with nine people in it, and nobody had to say anything to me, and the man at the counter is the one who did it in about four seconds with a sentence about a form.***
>
> *And the thing I have learned and cannot get round, and I have had two hours and ten minutes to get round it and I have not:*
>
> ***A refusal is a record. A record is a sentence that other people may read. And the whole of what she has done for eleven years on a towpath is to keep a refusal from being read as nothing. Nine doors, a slate, eight refusals written as eight refusals and not as eight silences, in a ward of about four hundred people, and it was the correct practice and it worked and it is the best thing anybody in this book knows how to do.***
>
> *And in this town a refusal is read as a household that has not answered. And a household that has not answered is a line open. And a line open is a second round. And a second round is a cart and a man and a day, and the cart is in the row now and a man is in the road and his handcart is not earning and the number his mother says is no.*
>
> ***The two practices are not opposites. They are the same practice in two places, and one of them is protecting a refusal and the other one is not, and neither of them knows the other one exists. And I have spent six weeks believing that the ward was in the right place and the office was in the wrong place, and I have never once walked eleven yards in the same lane as a woman with a slate, and that is the whole of what I have been wrong about and it is not a discovery about this town. It is a discovery about a street.***
>
> *And this is what I have left and I am going to write it down in the plainest words I have and then I am going to shut the book:*
>
> ***I have a sentence in a household's own words, in a column they chose, in a room with nine people in it, and I did not say a word while it was read out, and it is the only thing I have made in six weeks, and it is not a stage and it is not a power and it is not going to change anything at the level of a hall, and I have not rung the bell and I am not going to, and the crate is in a smith's yard in this street with the sacking off, and the frame is four streets off with the four bolts made fast in it, and the receipt is whole, and none of the five clauses is broken, and I wrote the fifth one.***
>
> *She was on the stair for two hours and ten minutes and she is the only person in this building who knows whose hand went on that line and she will not tell me and I will not ask her and I am not going to be thanked by anybody for anything tonight, and there is no speech and nobody has said it was worth it and I am not going to.*
>
> *And the things that are open, and I am writing them down at about the eleventh hour of the evening in the order they are going to hurt, and the order is not the order I would have chosen:*
>
> *A column that cannot be certified and about eleven thousand lines a year and four pounds a day of a clerk's wages that nobody can find a hole in.*
>
> *A woman at the end of a lane with a book in her kitchen drawer that disagrees with a roll in eleven places, in nine of which the day in the hall is a day the round was not in that street, and she will not take it out of the house, for a reason that is right.*
>
> *A boy of eight taken six days before the day any of this started, whose only papers are a line on a schedule and a piece of a household paper with his line cut out of it, and a woman who has been walking four counties for a month for him and has not asked about him once and is not going to.*
>
> *And a thing that has not started yet and that I have had in the back of my head for four days and cannot put down, and it is one line, and it is the reason this volume is not finished:*
>
> ***A name read on a roll begins, in about four years, to be read as a duty. And a duty is a mouth. And a mouth is for a claim. And nobody has to stand in a lane to ring it.***
