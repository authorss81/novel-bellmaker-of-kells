# Chapter 0088 — What He Says In A House Of Five

He went to the gable with the lean-to at the end of the east lane at about the fourth hour on the seventy-fourth with a thing to say, and he did not have the book, and he had never carried it, and he had not asked to.

That was the first fact and he had known it at the end of the lane and he had known it at the cross and he had known it about four hundred yards back, and he went anyway.

**Bess Marrow opened the door before he got to it, which nobody in this town does and which she did not explain then or afterwards.** She had her sleeves up. There was a fire. There was a smell of a house where a fire has been kept in all day because there are five people in it and one of them cannot get about.

“You have not brought it,” she said.

“No.”

“**Good. I want you to hear me say that properly, because I have thought about this for fifteen days and I have had it ready: good.** Fifteen days ago I told a woman with a slate that the day that book leaves this house it stops being the book of my house and starts being evidence about my house, and I have not moved from that and I am not moving from it for a man with a barrow.”

“Then what have you come for.”

---

He had three things ready and he said the first one and it was not the one he had thought was first.

“You have not been asked the three questions.”

“No.”

“**I know them. I have known them since the fifty-ninth and I am not going to ask them, and you may ask me why, and I would rather you did it now than at the door in about a minute.**”

“Why not.”

“Because the woman who asked them is better at it than I am and she is not here, and you have already answered them, and I have read the answers, and I am not going to come into a kitchen and do a trade I do not know in front of a woman who cannot write and a fire.” He stopped. “And because you have been asked by me once, in a row, in a market, three days after saying no, by a man with a barrow, and Dory Larkin told me what that is worth and it is not worth much, and I have been thinking about it for eleven days and I have not worked out how to be asked and not also be the one who is owed something for asking.”

Bess Marrow put her arms on the door frame.

“That is the first true thing anybody has said to me in this lane and it is the first time in about four years that anybody in this town has said they were not going to do a thing, and it has taken you fifteen days and a barrow, and I am not being kind about that either. **Sit down. No. Stand. Stand there, because I want to be able to shut that door and I cannot shut a door with a man sat down in front of it.**”

---

And then she told him the thing, and it was the reason he had not been able to sleep on the seventy-first, and she told it standing in her own doorway with her hands on the frame, and it took about a minute and a half.

“**I have worked out what you have come for. I worked it out on about the fourth day and I have been sitting on it nine days waiting to see whether you were going to do it.**

“**The only thing in this county that can act on a book is a household.** That is not a clever thing and it is not new. I have known it since I was about your age. A clerk can certify a column. A man in a chair can stop a practice. A woman with a slate can write down what a person said and read it back to them. **And not one of the three of them can go and stand in a room and say no to anything, because a person is the only thing that can be asked, and I am a person, and you have come to my door to use it.**”

“I have not come to use it.”

“**You have come to my door to ask me to be a place where your thing is kept.**” She said it perfectly level. “And I want you to stand there and hear it, because I have said it to my own two brothers in this kitchen and I have said it to a woman with a slate and I have never once said it to a man who had come about a barrow, and I have thought about it for nine days and I have got no better way of saying it and I am not going to get one now you are here.”

And Tomas Vale stood about four feet off in a lane in a town of about two thousand people and heard the whole of six weeks of his own argument come back at him out of a door in a voice that was not angry.

**Because she was right. Because he had spent five weeks in four counties telling four correct people that a person is not a shelf and that a document is not a household and that the only thing in this kingdom that can be asked a question is a person — and then he had walked up a lane and put his hands on a person and asked her to hold a book.**

“Yes,” he said.

“Yes,” said Bess Marrow. “**And I want you to have it in your own mouth before I say anything else, because if you had said it for me I should have shut this door and it would have been the right thing to do and we should both have been right and I should not have got anything.**”

---

And then he did the thing he had come two hundred yards to do, which was that he did not say it.

**“I am not going to ask you to take the book anywhere. Not to the hall, not to Fourmile, not to a room over a draper's shop, and not out of this house for one hour, and I am not going to ask you to show it to anybody, and I am not going to ask your mother to look at it, because she cannot write and I have known that for a month and I have never once said it out loud in a room and I am saying it now because I am in a kitchen and there is a fire.**

“**And I am not going to ask you to be a shelf. I am going to ask you one question and it is about a thing that does not exist, and if you tell me to go I will go.**”

She did not move.

“What is the question.”

“**If there was a line. In your own name, in the house's own name, or in nobody's name — a line, with a mark in a column against it, and a day on it, and a stamp somewhere at the foot. And nobody came to your door about it and nobody told you what it was and nobody has ever asked you. If there was a line like that in this house.**”

“I would answer it.”

“You would answer it.”

“**I would answer it and I would not be frightened of doing it and I have thought about that for nine days and I am not ashamed of it. I am not frightened of a line. I have four of them in a drawer that have been open four years and I have never once gone up the road and slept. I am frightened of a cart, and a cart only comes for a line that is not answered, and so I would answer it and I would be quit of it.**”

“All right,” said Tomas Vale.

“**Then you have come to my door to tell me the only thing I have.**”

“Yes.”

“And you walked four counties to tell a woman at the end of a lane something she worked out on the fourth day by herself, and you have not got a barrow-load of anything out of this and you are going to go back up that lane and say it to about nine people, and if one of the nine is a clerk he will write it down.”

“That is what is going to happen.”

“Then I want the whole of it first,” said Bess Marrow, “**because if I am going to be the household that is asked, then I am going to be asked with the whole of the thing on the table and not with the half of it that a man has decided I can carry. My mother cannot write and my two brothers have not been in this kitchen for four years and there is nobody in this house who can be told a thing in a room and be certain of it. So say it. All of it. From the beginning, in the order it happened, with the hours if you have got them, and I shall ask you about four of it and you shall tell me if you do not know.**”

---

So he said it.

He said it in a kitchen at the end of a lane in a town of about two thousand people, standing up, to one woman and to a fire, and it took about an hour and ten minutes, and he had told it in four counties to four people who were all right and none of whom had asked him for all of it.

He said the round that came in on the sixtieth and went out on the sixty-third and shut at the end of the sixty-sixth with four pitches left on it. He said the notice on the wall of the hall and the nine seconds and the twenty-two years. He said the man in charge of the shelves and the clerk of four years and the clerk of eleven years who put a sentence on a sheet nobody asked him for, and the fact that he had said he would not be asked again and had not been, twice. He said the district officer in two rooms over a dyer's and what she said about a ground, and the paper she gave him on a landing three days ago, and that she is poorer for it since the sixtieth.

He said the market of about nine hundred people. He said the row, and the cart that came on the sixty-third and the cart that came on the sixty-sixth, and the four shillings and the two shillings and sixpence, and the four pounds a quarter over a season, and the mother on a chair who can say a number.

**And he said the room.** The room over the draper's on the north side and the shilling for the fire and about nine people who came because a door was open, and the sentence he said in it in his own name about a household he had no standing in, and the clerk with a coat on who had four seconds, and the cart that came at about the ninth hour of the evening because a line in dispute is a working day for a clerk and it comes on a day nobody chooses, and the market keeper who stood up at the back and said *then that is three*.

He said it all, and he did not build a case, and he did not denounce anything, and he did not say it was necessary, and nobody in that kitchen said it was necessary.

And when he had done, Bess Marrow said:

“**Then the nine pitches are off the market.**”

“Yes.”

“And no cart will come back for them because you have said so, and the practice has been stopped in this district in writing by a woman with a number on a piece of paper, and neither of those two things puts a pitch back at the end of a row.”

“No.”

“**And the four pounds a quarter does not come back and the twenty years do not come back and the butter that was bought on credit against a pitch for nine years does not come back.**”

“No,” said Tomas Vale. “And I am not going to be the man who tells you it was worth it, and nobody is going to come and tell you it was, and if one does I will write down that he said it and the hour.”

---

And then she asked him the fourth thing and it is the thing the chapter is for, and she asked it about a quarter of an hour later, with her arms still on the frame, and she had not sat down once in an hour and ten minutes.

“**Right. You have given me the whole of it and it is in this kitchen and there is nobody in this house who can keep a book — my mother cannot write and my two brothers and my sister have not been in this kitchen in four years and are not going to be told a thing in a room by me on a working day — and I am not going to be thanked for any of it, and I want you to understand the position I am in before you say the next thing.**

“**I have a book in a drawer that a man from another county wants to take to a building. I have a woman with a slate's two answers in a slate that cost a penny and a half. I have a market of about nine hundred people and nine pitches off it that were on it on the sixty-ninth. I have a line of my own that has been open four years. And now I have all of it, in my head, in a kitchen, because a man stood in it and read it out.**

“**And every single thing that has come to this door in four years has come because somebody wanted something in it, and I have never once had anything brought to me that I did not have to be careful of afterwards. So I am going to be careful of this one as well and I am not going to pretend otherwise and I am not going to pretend I am too proud to be careful, because I have five people in this house and two of them cannot do without me, and a thing that is in my head and not written down is a thing I carry on my own, and I am the only one in this house who can read.**”

“I am not going to leave anything here.”

“**You are going to leave all of it here. That is what it is.**” She said it without any weight at all. “**And the reason I know you are not going to take it away is that a man who has a book in a coat does not stand in a kitchen for an hour and ten minutes and read it out to a woman who cannot write. You have told me things tonight that you did not have to tell me and about four of them have cost you something, and I have been in this lane fifteen days waiting to find out what you were after and I have got it and it is nothing you can have.**”

---

She shut the door on about the seventh hour and he stood in the lane for a bit and then walked back up it.

And at about the ninth hour he was at the cross, in the dark, with the day-book shut on his knee, and he did not write anything for about a quarter of an hour, and then he wrote one line, and it is the only entry of the seventy-fourth, and he wrote it straight across without ruling anything, because there was nothing in it that belonged in one column and nothing in it that belonged in another:

> *The seventy-fourth. I went to a house and asked a woman a question about a line that does not exist and she told me the answer before I had finished the sentence, and I have been four counties learning that a person is not a shelf, and I walked up a lane and used one, and she named it, and she was right, and I have not got a better answer to that than I had this morning.*
>
> ***The way to protect a household is to be left alone by a man with a book. I had that on the fiftieth and I did not know I had it, and she said it to me in her own doorway with her hands on the frame, and she is the daughter of a woman who cannot write and she is a woman who has been asked nine times in four years, and she is right, and I am the man with the book, and I am going to have to work out what I am instead, and I have not got it, and I am not going to pretend I have got it by about the second hour of the morning.***
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, the hours flat against them, and I am not merging two things. **She has got it. I have got nothing. A household asked a man with a book for the whole of it, in her own words, in a room with a fire in it, and there is no copy and there is nobody else in this town who has it, and she is the one carrying it, and I told her the price of it out loud before I began — that a man with a barrow is going to be in her house for an hour and come out with nothing and the hour will be spent — and she said she would rather have it, and I have not improved that either.***
>
> *And a line. **The only power a household has is the power to answer, and answering is what closes a line, and a closed line is the one thing in this kingdom that stops a cart. That is in this book now and it is the first thing in six weeks that a person has handed to me instead of the other way round, and it is not a stage and it is not a power and I cannot do it for anybody.***
>
> *I did not ask her three questions. She asked me why not and I gave her a true answer and it is the first true thing anybody in this lane has had off a stranger in about four years and I have no use for it whatever.*
