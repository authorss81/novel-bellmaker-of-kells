# Chapter 0065 — The Office Used Properly

They left Helling at about the ninth hour of the evening of the forty-ninth and were on the bank road at about the eleventh hour with the barrow between them and the frame in its cradle on the boards and the four bolts made fast where the frame was set down, and they came to Fourmile at about the fifth hour in the morning of the fiftieth.

It is eleven hours up the bank road and the water does not run at night and the road does, and Tomas Vale wrote that sentence down on the last milestone and it is the whole of the decision and the whole of the cost of it.

They slept in a stable at about the eighth mile and were on the road again before light and the frame never came out of the cradle and nothing was rung.

---

The thing he got wrong, and it took him about nine minutes at the counter to get wrong and about an hour to find out he had got it wrong, is that he came in with a demand.

He had it written out. He had it in his book in four lines, and it was the best four lines he had written in a month, and it began: *I am the person who put the request in your book of refusals on the forty-second day, and I am the person who sent your refusal down the ordinary post to a district office in a town called Helling, and I am the person for whom that refusal was not a refusal but a way of finding out whether it had been received.*

District Officer Marten Cope read it standing up, which he had done last time, and read it twice, and then he put it on the counter square with something else on it, which was a book.

“No,” he said.

“You have not said why.”

“I am going to say why, and I am going to say it before you are angry, and I am going to say it in about eleven seconds so that you cannot get two sentences in.” He put his hand flat on the book. “**That is a demand. This office does not answer demands. That is not in a rule and it is not in a book and it is not how the district is written down, and there is no instrument in this kingdom by which a person may walk into a district renewal office and require an answer in a certain time, and if there were I would want to know about it, because I would want to refuse it.**”

“Then what is there.”

“There is a form. It is a Crown form, it is Form nine of the second series, it has a box for the person and a box for the hour and a box for the office to enter what it has done, and there is no box for a reason, and I am going to tell you two things about that form in the order I am going to say them, and the first is that it is a good form and the second is that it is the machine, and both of those are true at once and I have not got nine months to argue with you about it.”

---

He filled the form in himself, at the counter, in about four minutes, and gave it to Tomas Vale to look at before he entered it.

*Name and parish of the person asking. Tomas Vale, of Kells, without district.*

*Hour. The ninth hour.*

*Office to enter what it has done.*

“There is no box for a reason,” said Tomas Vale.

“There is no box for a reason,” said Marten Cope, “and I have wanted to have that argument with a person about four times in eleven years and I have lost it four times, and I am going to tell you what the argument is and then I am going to enter the form, and you may write the argument in your own book and I will not stop you and you may not put it in mine.

“**The argument is this. If there is a box for a reason, then the office reads the reason before it decides, and then the office is deciding about the reason. And a person who has written a reason in a box has put the reason of the thing in front of the office, and the office is a person with a district above it and a file in front of it and eleven years, and a person who has written down *a landlord was sent a paper about an address and a family was moved out of a house* in a box on Form nine has handed this office a reason to be frightened, and a frightened office does one of two things and neither of them is answer the question.**”

“Then what is the box for.”

“The box for what the office has done is the best thing on that form and there is no box for a reason on it and I have never asked for one, and I have written that down in this room, in my own file, in a book that is not a form, and if you want to see it I will show you it, and it is the only thing on this counter that I have wanted to say out loud to the district in eleven years and I have not said it, and that is a thing I have done, not a thing that was done to me.”

He entered the form. It took him about two minutes and he gave the carbon to Tomas Vale without being asked, and the carbon is the only copy of that form outside this building.

---

**And then he went and got a chair out from under the desk and set it on the wrong side of the counter, and said:**

“Sit down. I have been an officer for eleven years and I have answered about nine thousand things in this room and I have never once had a person sit down in that chair and ask me for a *shelf*, and I want to say that before I answer it, because I know what you came for and you did not get it and I would rather we both had that in the open.”

“What is a shelf.”

“It is this.” He put the book on the counter. It is a quarto with a half-leather spine and about two hundred and forty entries in eleven years and the entries are numbered and dated and each has a ground written against it. “It is the book of refusals. It is not open to anybody. It has never been open to anybody. And you may have it for as long as you want, and I am going to do the thing I have wanted to do for eleven years, which is sit down next to a person and read a book out loud to them while I read it, because I have read it alone and I have read it in eleven years and there are things in it I do not know what I think about.”

---

He read for about two hours.

Two hundred and forty entries, and the great majority of them are one kind, and the kind is: *a district has declined to supply a document on a ground of privilege or of another district's business, the ground is entered, the person who asked is entered or is not entered.*

And at about the one hundred and ninetieth entry he stopped and put his finger on it and did not read it out for about a minute.

“This one is not mine.”

“Whose is it.”

“It is in a clerk's hand. There are about eleven of them in this book in a clerk's hand and every other one of the two hundred and forty is in mine, and I have known that for eleven years and I have never been able to account for it, and I have never asked, and you may guess what I have been supposing for eleven years and you will be right, and I would rather you heard the truth than the guess.”

“What is the truth.”

---

He read the entry out.

> *Refused. Requested: a record of the making of a bell, to show the metal of it. Ground entered: declined; another district's business. The person asking is entered and is of this district.*

And the date against it was a year back from the twenty-sixth of this month.

Tomas Vale did not say anything for about four seconds.

“I had the shop then,” he said.

“You had the shop and a licence in a workshop and a bellhouse with an archive in it and a sign board,” said Marten Cope. “And you did not know, and I want to be very careful with you about the next part of this, because I have watched four people go into a piece of paper in eleven years and three of them went in to find somebody, and I am not going to stand in a room and be a door again.

“**I have never seen the person who asked.** The name is entered and it is of this district and I do not know it, and the name is not in the refusals book because the refusals book has never had names in it until about four years ago, and the names were put in by a different officer, and the one hundred and ninetieth entry is a clerk's hand and the clerk is not here and has not been here since before I came.”

“Then the entry names the district and not the person.”

“It names the district. **That is the whole of the engine and it has been in my own book for eleven years and I have read it about four hundred times.** A person asks a question. The office cannot answer it, and writes down a *ground*, and the ground is a district, and the district is a place, and the person is in the book for four years and nowhere else, and if you want to know who asked, you have to go and ask the person, and if you want to know what the person wanted, you have to ask the person, and the office cannot tell you either, and the office is not lying in the book. The book says a district declined. The book does not say a person asked, because a person asking is not one of the things this office has an instrument for. **We have an instrument for a ground. We have had one for eleven years and it is very good.**”

---

“May I go on,” said Marten Cope.

“Please.”

He read on. And at about the two hundred and seventeenth entry he stopped again, and this one was in his own hand, and it was dated the forty-sixth, four days after he had answered three of four questions in this room and refused the fourth.

Tomas Vale read it standing up, and then read it a second time, and then put his hand flat on the counter the way a man does with a document he has decided to take seriously.

*Refused. Requested: an instrument for a water bell. Ground entered: no instrument is provided for a water bell. The field for a keeper of a water bell is not to be supplied. Reference: district, this office, this date.*

“There is no reason on it.”

“There is no reason on it and there is no instrument to put one in, and it came down on the ordinary post on the forty-seventh, and it is in that file and it is dated, and I entered it the day it came,” said Marten Cope, “**and I am going to tell you what it is and I have not told it to anybody: that is the first time in eleven years that this office has been refused something by a document and not by a person, and I entered it, and I have read it about eleven times, and there is nobody I can ask about it, because the district does not take questions by post.**”

“Is it a refusal or an answer.”

“It is an answer,” said Marten Cope, “and it is the worst answer I have ever entered in that book, and I entered it in a good hand, and I will tell you why it is worse than the other two hundred and thirty-nine. The other two hundred and thirty-nine are refusals to give a person a thing they want, and I can stand in a room and say *I cannot give you that* and I have said it about two hundred and forty times. **This one is the district telling this office what a form is, and the form has no box for a keeper of a water bell, and so there is no keeper, and a bell that has been rung every morning for thirty years by a woman nobody has told anything about has been answered, in writing, by a desk, in the negative, with a date on it.**”

He shut the book.

“I am not sorry. I want that said before you ask me. I put that entry in this book the day it came and I have not looked for a reason to take it out and I have not found one and I am not going to. I asked for the instrument. I asked in my own name and in this office's file and I asked properly, and I was refused, and a refusal is a thing somebody did, and I can be angry at somebody. **A negative is not a thing somebody did. That is the whole of it.**”

---

And then he read the fourth thing out of a different file, and it took about nine minutes, and it is the answer to the question he had been carrying for four days and had not known he was carrying.

“The file of four returns,” he said. “I told you on the forty-second that it is a file of four returns in four districts in eleven years, and I told you I would not describe the households. I am going to tell you the districts this time, because you have asked me a question I have not been asked and I have thought about it since the forty-second and I have not stopped thinking about it.”

He turned the file round.

“The four are this district, and the district to the north of it, and the district above that, and the district at the top of the water where the county changes. **They do not share a bellwright. They do not share a market day. They do not share a parish and they do not share an office and the post goes to three different places.** And I have asked, since the forty-second, in about eleven towns, the three questions that would find out whether there is a man who does all four, and I have had three answers that were no, and one answer that was *I do not know and it is not my district*, and one answer from a man who has been in the trade about forty years and who said a thing I have not been able to put down yet.”

“What did he say.”

“He said, *there is a man who does a fair bit of it, and he is not a bellwright, and he is not a clerk, and he has got no stock, and he is very good at writing, and there is more than one of him, and if you want to know where the hand goes you want to ask the paper and not the man.*” Cope shut the file. “And I asked him which paper, and he said he would tell me in a fortnight, and he wrote to me in a fortnight, and the letter said that he was sorry, and it did not say anything else, and I have not asked him again, because he is a man of forty years in a trade who has never owed me anything and has now told me twice that he does not want to be asked.”

---

“And the four returns,” said Tomas Vale.

“Share one hand,” said Marten Cope. “**That is the only thing they share and it is the only thing I have ever been able to put in a sentence about it.** And I told you on the forty-second that a clerk's hand is never the same twice and a signer's hand is the same every time, and that is a trade point and you know it, and it is the reason I believed it in eleven years without a document. And I will now tell you the thing I have not said to anybody: **I have this morning, in this room, with the book open in front of you, gone through the two hundred and forty entries and the four returns, and there is nothing else in any of it that is the same. Not a ground. Not a date. Not a reference. Four returns and two hundred and forty refusals and a marginal note of mine, and the one thing that runs through all of it is a hand.**”

He squared the book on the counter.

“That is not a discovery. That is a man counting. But I have never been able to do it in a room with somebody in it, and I have wanted to since about the ninth year, and I have not asked anybody, and I have just done it in front of a person, and I would like that entered in your book with the hour, and I do not want anything said about it afterwards.”

“I will write it with the hour and I will not improve it.”

---

And then Tomas Vale shut his own book and wrote the question out before he asked it, and it is the fifth question and it is not one of the four, and it is the question Tamsin Rook gave him on a road in a different month, and it is the only question anybody in this building has ever been asked and not answered.

“District Officer. **What is the book of refusals? Is it a record of what this office has refused to supply, or is it a record of what the district has declined to supply?**”

Cope read it twice.

“That is not a fair question,” he said, “and it is a correct one, and I am going to answer it in my own name and with the hour, and I am going to answer the whole of it, and there is a piece of it I have not got.”

“Answer what you have got.”

“It is a record of what **this office** has refused to supply. In two hundred and forty entries, in eleven years, this office has refused to supply the thing asked for, and in every one of the two hundred and thirty of them that are in my hand I am the person who did the refusing and I can say why and I could say it again tomorrow. **And about eleven of them are in a clerk's hand and they are not refusals of this office at all, and I have never known that until this morning, and they are the years before I came, and in those eleven the clerk has entered the district's ground in this office's voice, and has entered *this office declines*, when what had happened was that this office had put a question to the district and the district had written back a word.**”

“So a refusal in this book can be a thing this office did not do.”

“A refusal in this book can be a thing this office did not do, in a clerk's voice, with a date, in a book with a number on the spine, and there is no field for who wrote it, and the person who is asking is not in the entry, and I have kept that book for eleven years.” He took his hand off it. “**And that is the answer. A book of refusals in this building is a record of refusals in a building where a man writes *refused* and does not have to say who refused. It is not a record of what the district declined, because those are two different offices and the district is not in this book. It only looks like the district's book because of the eleven entries, and in about four years there will not be one of them left and then it will be a clean book, and it will be a clean book that is not true.**”

He wrote it out on a sheet and signed it and timed it and gave it to Tomas Vale, and it is the sixth sheet he has, and it is the second one a man in that building has written in his own name that he did not have to write.

“District Officer,” said Tomas Vale.

“Mr Vale.”

“About the withdrawal. Your office’s finding of the twenty-eighth.”

“I filed it and it was not mine and I have not been asked for it and I am not going to be asked for it in this room, and if a person asks me for it in this room I will hand it over and it will take four minutes, and that is the whole of what it is worth, and it is worth nothing, and there is a building three counties down a river in which about four hundred people could ask for it and not one of them has.” He put his pen down. “**It is not a win. It is a document that says my district was wrong about a set of bells, and there are about nine hundred bells in this district, and it is the only thing in eleven years that I have been able to put in a sentence in your favour, and it is not in your favour.**”

---

He walked with him to the door, which he had not done last time.

“Mr Vale,” he said, at the duck's-egg door, with the yard behind him and a barrow in it. “You came here last time with four questions and a chair and a bell in a yard and I gave you three answers and a refusal and it was the best afternoon I have had in about four years, and I have been waiting all morning for that to happen again and it has not happened, and I want to tell you why, because you will not be able to work it out and it is not a fault of yours.

“**Last time you asked a man for a door. Today you asked a building to be a building, and it was, and I have been a building for a day, and I was a man last time and it was restful.**”

“That is not an answer to anything.”

“No,” said Marten Cope. “It is the only honest thing I have said to anybody who has walked out of that door, and I have said it to about four of them, and I would like you to have it and I would like you to go back to a book and put it down.”

---

And on the steps, with the frame in the cradle in the yard behind and the bolts made fast where it was set down, Tomas Vale read the one hundred and ninetieth entry out of the book of refusals from his own copy of the carbon, and then he wrote for about eleven minutes in his day-book, at about the fifth hour of the afternoon of the fiftieth, in the flat hand, and it is the passage that decides what he does for the next four days and it is in his book entire:

*He gave me a shelf. I asked a building for a door and got three answers and a refusal and the best afternoon I have had since I left a town with a wax on its door, and I have come back for a second one and there is nothing to feel, and I did not understand that until about the ninth hour of the morning when I put my demand on his counter like a man at a counter, and he said no in about eleven seconds and was right.*

*Here is what I have got wrong for a month and a man in a chair corrected in a corridor in about nine words: **a door is a thing you walk through and a shelf is a thing you can be shown.** I have spent a month asking for doors. I have asked Oren Pike for a door and I have asked the Measure for a door and I have asked a district officer for a door and I have asked a smith for a door and I have asked a ferry-man for a door, and every one of those was a man deciding something about me. He is not a door and he did not open one and I said so in my own book on the forty-second and I meant it as a compliment, and it was not a compliment. It was the complaint.*

*A shelf is a thing a person is shown. He took it off a shelf and I read it out loud with my hands behind my back while he read it with his finger on it, for two hours, and at the end of two hours I knew more about his own office than I know about the market lane in Kells, and he did not refuse me one thing, and I did not get a single thing I asked for.*

*And the one hundred and ninetieth entry is a year back from the twenty-sixth of this month, in this district, in a clerk's hand, refusing a record of the making of a bell, to show the metal of it, and the person asking is of this district, and I had a shop and a licence and a bellhouse and a sign board and I did not know.*

*And I am not going to build a house on one refusal in a clerk's hand in a book that a man has kept for eleven years and has told me has no field for the person who asked. **One clerk's hand, one entry, one year, and a ground that is a district and not a person. That is not a conspiracy. That is a book.** And the eleventh question I have wanted to ask in a month is the one I have just been told is the wrong one, and I am going to write the right one down and I am not going to ask it here, because he has been asked twice and I am not going to be the reason he is asked a third time.*

*And the one thing I know and can act on: somebody in this district, a year back from the twenty-sixth, asked this office for a record of the making of a bell, to show the metal of it. That is not a Crown question. That is not a question about a household or a schedule or a return or a stamp. **That is a smith's question. That is my trade's question, asked in a form, in this county, in my own lifetime, by somebody who wanted to know where their metal came out of, and it was refused and the refusal says another district's business and the district is a place and not a person.**

*And I know within about a mile of this door how that document would be found, because I have stood over it for two days. It is the ninth column of a ruled book with a smith's name at the top of it, and it is the only column in that book that is not about money, and it is called the where-from, and it has the place and the day and the man in it, and a man who has kept that book for nineteen years has never sold a page of it and told me why.*

*He is two days from this door. I have been to his shop and been refused and I have a reason in writing in my own book and I put it in his hand myself, and I am going back, and I am going to ask him the fourth question instead of asking for the page, and I do not know what the fourth question is yet and I have got two days' road to work it out on.*

*And I have not asked Cope about the L-4 lines and I am not going to. That is a household's question and I have no standing in it and I have said so in writing twice and I am not going to spend a shelf I have been given on it.*
