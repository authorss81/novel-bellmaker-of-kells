# Chapter 0094 — Six Households And Nine People

She had three questions and she had never once in eleven years put them to a town, and on the morning of the eightieth day of the month she stood in the middle of a market of about nine hundred people with a slate under her arm and worked out that she was not going to.

That is the whole of the decision and it took her about four minutes.

**A household is five people and a door and about an hour. A town is two thousand people and no door and no hour, and there is no version of my three questions that a market answers, and I have been in this town twenty-three days and I have never once put them to it and I was not going to start on the eightieth because a man rang a bell in it yesterday.**

So she asked one question, of one person, four times, and it was not one of the three.

She had worked this out on the boat, three days before the fifty-eighth, and it had been in her for twenty-two days and she had not used it, and it is the only thing she has that her three questions do not have, and it took her eleven years and it is four inches long.

**You say a person's own words back to them, out loud, at ten feet, in the ordinary voice, so that about nine other people hear it.** That is all. A thing a woman has said to her about a name, said back to her in her own words, in a market, in front of strangers, so that it has now been said twice.

She had done it once in this town, on the seventieth, at a pitch of needles and thread, and about nine people heard it, and she has written since that it is not the same act as a woman saying her neighbour's name in a line. She had said that in a lane and it was the truth and it is on the page and she has not improved it.

And on the eightieth she did it four times, and the fourth one was to a merchant, and it is the first time in eleven years she has done it to somebody who is owed money.

---

The first was at about the fourth hour, in a queue of about six at a pitch of butter and nothing else, and it took her about nine minutes including the queue.

A woman of about five household said the name of her own house in the way people say it in a queue, flatly, to a woman in front of her who was not listening, and said that the line was the house's and not hers and had been the house's since before she was married, and that she was owed by it and did not know by how much and had asked twice and been told the price of a pitch, and said all of that in about eleven seconds, to a stranger, and did not mind who heard.

Tamsin Rook stood at ten feet and said it back.

Not to the stranger. **To the woman.** In the woman's own words, in the ordinary voice, at ten feet, so that the queue of six heard it and so that anybody within about nine feet of a pitch of butter in a market of about nine hundred heard it.

And the woman turned round.

“**What.**”

And that is the whole of the act and it is four inches long and it has been the whole of it for eleven years: **you say a person's words back and they turn round, and the turning round is the part that does the work, and nothing else does.**

---

The second was in a lane off the market at about the fifth hour and it was a man who had four years on a line and who had asked, in a front room, to take the second one out of the room like a man carrying a parcel he was not addressed for, and who had said *take the kingdom out of it* and had not been afraid of anything.

He had said, to a woman with a slate, that a mark in a column is not the trouble and an open line is the trouble, and that he had worked that out on his own and it had taken him four years and a fever, and that nobody had ever asked him.

She said it back to him in a lane, at ten feet, in the ordinary voice, and about four people heard it, and he did not turn round, because he had heard it before, in a front room, and this time it had been said twice and the second one was in a lane in the open.

That is the difference and she did not know it was a difference until it happened and she has written it down and not improved it: the first time a person says a thing, they have said it to a woman with a slate. The second time they have said it in a lane in the open, and a lane in the open is not a woman with a slate, and a thing that has been said in a lane in the open is a thing that happened.

---

The third was at about the sixth hour, back in the market, at a pitch of needles and thread, and it was the same woman as the seventieth, and the same line of about six, and it was the reason she had learned the act in this town and not anywhere else.

She had a claim and she had said it once, in a queue, on the seventieth, and Tamsin Rook had said it back to her at ten feet, and about nine people had heard it.

**It had not held.** A thing said back once in a market on the seventieth is a thing said twice on the seventieth, and by the eightieth it was a thing said once again, because the second telling had been to one woman at ten feet and a woman at ten feet is not a place anybody has to keep anything.

So she said it back again, in the ordinary voice, at ten feet, in front of about nine people, and the woman's hands went to her board and stayed there.

“**You are going to keep doing that.**”

“**I am going to do it four times today and I have done it twice.**”

---

**And the fourth one was at a gate on the water at the west end of this town at about the seventh hour, and it was to a merchant, and it is the whole of why this chapter is on the page.**

She had been in that yard the day before. She had not been in it today and had no business in it and nobody had asked her to be in it and nobody knew she was going, and she had walked up off the water with a slate under her arm and a bag on her shoulder and about nine minutes of a queue in her and no price on anything.

**Marden Vey was in his own yard, on his own day, at about the seventh hour, doing a thing to a boat, and there were about nine men on the books and three of them were his nephews and one of them had been in that yard since he was a boy, and about fifty-one pounds had gone into six hands in a room upstairs the day before, and about nine hundred people in this town had heard his partner's name in a market square the day before that.**

And what he had said, on the seventy-fifth, in a counting house, in his own name, about his own trade, and had said once and would not say again in another room to another man, was this:

*that a claim cannot be shown, and a claim that cannot be shown cannot be paid, and that is not a hardship, and that the mark in the column is not what is ruining him, and that what is ruining him is that the line has been open four years, and that the line being open is four men not putting a hand down, and that a chore is a thing a man does for himself in his own yard on his own day.*

And he is owed about ninety pounds and about forty and about eleven and four and about six by four families in a town of about two thousand, and he is going to lose a yard in the spring, and he said all of that in one sitting and would not say it again.

She said it back to him at the gate, at ten feet, in the ordinary voice, with about nine people in a yard who work for him.

And the price of that is about a hundred and fifty pounds, and about a third of the carrying, and a handcart's worth of somebody's winter.

---

He stopped what he was doing, which is a thing a man does once and not twice.

“**Say the rest of it.**”

“No. **That is the whole of it and it is yours and I said it in your words and I have not added anything and I am not going to, and you have got a yard and nine men in it and about four of them were behind you and they heard it, and it is said twice now.**”

“It is said once,” said Marden Vey. “**You have said a thing to me in my own gate.**”

“**It is said twice. You said it in a counting house on the seventy-fifth to a man with a barrow, and I was not in the room and I did not ask to be, and I have been carrying it for three days and I have said it back to you at your own gate in front of your own men, and that is twice, and I did it on purpose, and I have not got a number for what it is worth and I am not going to make one up.**”

---

He put the thing down he was doing.

“**Then you had better tell me what it costs, because I know what you are and I have read what you have written, and a woman with a slate who says a thing twice is a woman with a slate who has made a fact about four people in this town, and I have about nine men on my books and four households that owe me money and every one of the four is in this town.**”

“**It costs you about a hundred and fifty pounds, and a third of the carrying, and the four households will hear it, and one of the four is a household of five, and a household of five that hears that a firm cannot show its own answering is a household of five that stops answering its own lines.**”

“All right.”

“**And I have said it anyway, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I am not going to be told it was worth it, and I have not got a reason you will like and I am not going to improve one for you.**

“**Here is the reason and it is eleven years long and it is not about you. For eleven years I have read people's words back to them and it has never once cost anybody anything, because the people I read them back to were people who owed nothing. A woman at a pitch who owes nothing says a thing and I say it back and the queue hears it and nothing happens, because there is nothing in her that a thing can be a price of.**”

“**And you are the first one who is owed something.**”

“**I am the first one I have said it to who is owed something. And the whole reason I do this is that a thing a person has said once has not happened yet. It happened when it was said the second time, in a lane, in the open, and a lane in the open is not a woman with a slate and not a market of about nine hundred and not a man with a barrow. That is the whole of what I have and it is four inches long and it is not a document and it is not a form and it stops nothing.**”

---

She wrote it down, and that is the thing she has not done in eleven years and she has written about it, and it is on the back of a slate she bought for a penny and a half off a stallholder in a landing stage, and **the price side of that slate is on the other face and it is the other way up, and it has been the other way up since the sixty-first, and a slate that carries a price and four columns and a sentence somebody said at a gate is one object and there is no way to file it and no clerk will ever see it.**

She wrote under the four columns and not in one, because the four columns are ruled and the rule is that a column is a thing somebody can certify and this is not a thing anybody can certify, and she has one line in her fourth column and she has not improved it and she is not going to improve it, and it is not on the back of this slate.

> *The eightieth. Four times. A queue at a pitch of butter, a lane off the market, the same pitch of needles and thread as the seventieth, and a gate on the water at about the seventh hour to a merchant who is owed about a hundred and fifty pounds by four families in a town of two thousand and who is going to lose a yard in the spring and who said all of that once, on the seventy-fifth, in a counting house, to a man with a barrow, and would not say it again in another room to another man.*
>
> ***The whole of it, and I have not got a better way of putting it: a name said in a queue in one street and a name said in a counting house in another street are the same kind of act. The reason they are the same kind of act is that both of them are a person telling a person a thing they have not been told, in the ordinary voice, and both of them take about nine minutes.***
>
> *The practice that made me the best reader of documents in four counties is the practice that makes a market queue work and a counting-house door work. It is the same four inches. It has been the same four inches for eleven years and nobody has ever asked me what it is for, and I have not been thanked for the four of them and I am not going to be.*
>
> *And the whole difficulty and I have had it since about the fourth hour and I have not got round it: **the six households and the nine men are about a hundred yards apart and neither of them is going to go to the other, and there is no room in this town big enough for both of them, and I was in the market this morning and then I was at a gate on the water and I could not be in both and I was not going to try, and I am the only person in this county who has a hand in both, and I am one woman with a slate and a deaf ear and no standing, and nobody has asked me to be in either place and I went to both.***
>
> *And here is the price, in my own hand, because I am the only person who will write it: **a thing said twice to somebody who owes nothing is free. A thing said twice to a man who is owed a hundred and fifty pounds in a town of two thousand is a document about four households, and I have made it, and four of them have not been asked, and I have not got a number for what it will cost them and I am not going to make one up. It is fourpence a day and I have never found a price on a sentence in a lane.***
>
> *I did not ask anybody anything. Nobody asked me for a reason. **I rang nothing.** The four columns stand and nothing is merged and the fourth has one line in it and I have not improved it and I am not going to.*
