# Chapter 0027 — The Surveyor Who Will Not Sign

Rhea Collis spent the whole of the twelfth day in the survey store, because a surveyor’s return is written where the survey was made and because the survey store on this street had a table in it that a person could sit at for nine hours without being interrupted, which was the finest thing about Crown property he had ever come across.

He came in at the sixth hour of the afternoon with a sheet in his hand and stood at the end of the table for a while without saying anything, and she let him stand there, and neither of them pretended that he was not there.

The shelves behind her were bare. She had watched them empty on the tenth day — a clerk and two porters, twenty minutes, a chest, a fastening — and she had said nothing to them then, and she said now, without looking up:

“They have taken everything with L-4 in the heading. I asked for the list of what was collected and they gave me a list. There is no reason a collector may not take the office duplicates, and I am not going to pretend there is, and I will tell you what is left.” She turned her head. “Two bundles of returns from before your time with no route in the heading, a roll of wire, a broken theodolite case, and about four hundred blank forms of four different kinds.”

“Blank.”

“All four kinds. Which means the office is not short of paper, which means the paper is not the constraint, which means somebody in a building with four hundred blank forms of four kinds is not running out of anything.” She went back to her report. “I have been a surveyor for eleven years and I have never once been able to make that sentence mean anything. It has never mattered before.”

He put the sheet down on the end of the table.

“I want you to countersign this.”

She read it. It was two-thirds of a page and it had been in his coat since the ninth evening and he had rewritten it four times.

*That the answers recorded on the Crown response schedule for route L-4 were entered in an office, without signature, on the day the round was posted and before the round was held; and that they were therefore not the answers of the households named in them, and that the schedule is not evidence that any household of the Low Ward gave any answer.*

“You want me to say that as a surveyor,” Collis said.

“Yes.”

“I did not see the schedule when I certified the round.”

“I know.”

“I was conducted to nine houses, by a Crown officer, off a page I had not seen, and I asked to see it at the gate and was refused. I put that in my own report in my own hand before the round opened, and my report is the one document in this business that nobody can say was written afterwards.” She set the sheet down. “I can say that a certification of a visit is a statement that a visit happened. It is not a statement that the thing the visit was conducted under is true. Those are two different documents and a great many people in the district town believe they are the same one, and I am not one of them, and I cannot put that in a countersignature without a lawyer and about a week.”

“And if you put it in words you can support?”

“Then I will put it in words I can support, and it will be narrow, and it will not be what you came for.” She looked at him directly. “You know what you did with the custody receipt.”

Tomas said nothing.

“You changed the description,” Collis said. “You did not change the custody, because you could not, and you did not try, which I found remarkable and have thought about since. You changed the words that describe a thing that has already happened to somebody else. That is a different and much smaller power, and you used it correctly, and you also used it about a girl of sixteen without asking her, and I have heard about that too, because half this town has heard about that.”

“I heard about it from her,” Tomas said.

“Of course you did. Nobody hears it from you.”

“Then I will not make your countersignature into a repetition of my receipt.”

“You cannot do that.” Collis turned the sheet round and drew a line under his paragraph with her own pen. “You cannot do that, and I want you to understand the mechanism you are actually up against, because you are treating it as a matter of drafting and it is not. If I write *a certification of a visit is a statement that a visit happened*, then a man in a district office reads it and he reads, correctly, *and here is a surveyor telling this commission that a certification means nothing*. And next time there is a round on a route, and a surveyor is standing at a gate at four in the morning, and something in that ward needs her to certify it, she will be thinking about the two sentences she wrote at a table in a survey store in a river town. And then a round will be certified by a man who will not do it.”

She set the pen down.

“That is what my word costs. Not mine. The next one’s.”

“Then the next one has a bad surveyor’s case.”

“The next one has a bad round,” Collis said. “Do you see the shape of it? You are asking me to spend a thing I have, to stop somebody spending a thing they have not got yet. I will do it. I am going to do it, because I stood in a lane on the tenth day and watched a boy of nine get onto a wagon in his shoes and I have not been able to put anything else in my report and I have written four drafts of it. But I would like it entered, in a document, that I was asked what it would cost and I answered.”

“Enter it,” Tomas said.

---

They did it at the market court at the seventh hour, in the open, with the lamps lit, because Oren Pike had established on the tenth day that a thing said in a room is a thing that arrives in a file alone.

The clerk read the statement before it was signed, and read it again after, and Oren read the refusals out in his flat hand, and the crowd that had come for the second evening running was about forty people and half of them were there to argue about the wire.

*I, Rhea Collis, surveyor, of the northern district, appointed to certify a renewal visit on route L-4 at the rescheduled dawn of the tenth day, do state as follows, and each of these statements is within my own commission and within my own knowledge.*

*One. That the visit on the tenth day took place, that I was conducted to nine of the twenty-two doors marked, that I did not see the other thirteen marked at all, and that I certified it as a visit.*

*Two. That a certification of a visit is a statement that a visit was made. It is not a statement that the answers under which the visit was conducted are true, and it does not become one by my sealing it.*

*Three. That I was not shown a schedule of household responses before the round opened, that I asked to see it at the north gate and was not permitted to, and that the round was conducted to me as a renewal visit against a ward roll, and that I certified the round on that basis and no other.*

*Four. That I will not countersign any record which asserts that any household on route L-4 gave, assented to, or received an answer, because I did not witness any answering and cannot certify one.*

*And I decline, and I ask that these refusals be entered in full, and not summarised.* I will not say the schedule is false, because I did not see it. I will not say who entered it, because I do not know. I will not say whether the district office keeps a practice or an individual keeps a habit, because that is an instruction and I am not permitted instructions. I will not identify the Crown officer who held the schedule on the tenth day, because I am not going to be the cause of the end of a twenty-six-year-old man’s career in exchange for eleven words of mine. And I will not say that this commission has done anything wrong, because I have not seen the thing it would have done wrong.*

“I am told,” she said, “that the person named on the withdrawn service entry for this route is a bellwright of this town.”

“I am not that person and I will not be made into him,” Collis said, immediately, and with a heat that surprised Tomas. “I have been asked that twice since the eighth day, once by your captain in a market lane and once by a woman in a court, and I would like it on this paper in my own words: I am a surveyor from the northern district. I have never held a Crown commission in this ward. I did not sign anything and I have never seen the entry I am supposed to have signed, and I will not be produced at a hearing as a shape that a crowd can throw at somebody else. If the town wants a missing man, it does not get one in me.”

The clerk wrote that down in full and read it back, and she signed it, and Oren witnessed it, and Ferrow put three copies in the market court book and the watch archive and a covered box on the board, which was now going to need a bigger box.

Halden Crake was in the arcade for the whole of it and did not interrupt once, and when it was done he came up the steps.

“Surveyor. You have put three sentences in the town’s hands that read, to a filing clerk, as a Crown officer declining to warrant her own certification.”

“They are true, Deputy.”

“They are true and they are also the four words that a commission needs in order to say that the round was not properly constituted.” He was not angry. That was the thing Tomas had learned about him and still could not get used to: Halden Crake got colder and more precise, and the only time his voice rose was on things he believed. “I am going to file them, and I am going to file them with your report, and I am going to file both alongside a round that was made, at nine houses, in one morning, without injury to any Crown officer and without disorder in the ward. And I am going to say this in the book, and I would like you both here for it: I would rather have nine houses and a clean return than forty-one houses and a clerk in an office guessing.”

“Then file that,” Oren said, “and file this beside it, that the round was made against a page nobody in the ward had seen, and that three children were taken at the doors of two households that had answered at the board in person and held the slate for it, and that one of those two had a woman on the tailboard who was not on the warrant.”

“I will file that too,” Crake said. “I have been trying to file it since the tenth day. It is the only part of this morning I have not been able to get into a sentence.”

He went down the steps. Nobody stopped him. It had begun to be a thing in this town that the deputy reeve could be shouted at and would answer and would come back, and Tomas wondered, not for the first time, whether that was a public service or a habit everybody had run out of the energy to break.

---

The court emptied. Collis packed her report into her case, which took a while, because a surveyor’s case is full of paper about paper.

Tomas stood at the end of the table in the survey store with the three copies and did not know what to do with the evening, which is a thing that had happened to him perhaps four times in eleven years and always felt like the hole in the floor.

He had come in with a paragraph and gone out with three sentences, and the three sentences were worth more than the paragraph, and he was going to be honest with himself about why, which was that the paragraph had been the thing he wanted and the three sentences were the thing she could stand behind, and a record of the second kind can be argued with, and a record of the first kind can only be admired and then set aside.

He wrote it up in the day-book, in the yard, with the lamp out on the millrace bell’s side of the shop, in a hand that was not good and did not improve.

*Entered, in the market court book, in the watch archive, and on the ward board, in the words of Surveyor Rhea Collis: that a certification of a visit is a statement that a visit was made and not a statement that the answers under it are true; that she was not shown a schedule and asked for one and was refused; and that she will not countersign a record asserting that any household gave an answer.*

*I asked her for a paragraph and she gave me three sentences and was right to, and I want it written that I know she was right to, because the part of me that is still the man who cut the rope wanted to go back and ask for one more sentence, and the part of me that has learned anything in the last two days knew that one more sentence would have cost her the next one.*

*I have one Crown officer on the record saying that the certification is procedural. It is not enough. It will not put a child on a wagon or off it. It is a nail in a board and I do not yet know what it is holding up.*

He shut the book.

Collis was in the doorway with her case under her arm and her rod over her shoulder, and she was obviously leaving, and she stopped, and for a moment it looked as though she was going to say nothing at all.

Then she said, quietly, without coming further into the room, “I am going to say a thing and then I am going to deny that I said it, and you are not to write it down.”

“I am not writing anything.”

“You are writing something on that bench, I can see the light of it.” She did not smile. “A page like your schedule does not appear by itself. A page like that is entered by somebody, into a form, in an office, by a hand. And an office does not enter pages. A person does, and the person is a person with a post, and the post has a docket number, and the number is on three papers in three hands, and I have watched three of those papers this week and I have said so and I will say it again if I am asked in a room where somebody is keeping a record of it.”

“Then you will not be asked in a room like that,” Tomas said, “because I will not say it.”

“Write it,” Collis said. “No. Do not write it, and understand exactly why I am telling you and not writing it. If it is in your hand, then in six months a clerk in that office will know that a Crown surveyor said a page is entered by a person, and the only question left for him is which surveyor, and there is one surveyor, and she is going to Fourmile at the end of this week. If it is in nobody’s hand, then it is a thing two people said in a doorway in a survey store, and it is worth nothing to anybody, and it is still true, and you may use it in a year when it will cost neither of us anything.”

She shifted the case.

“That is the whole of my professional advice to you and I am giving it for nothing, which I have told you twice already and am telling you a third time because I expect to be the kind of man who mentions it later. I am not the missing bellwright. I am not going to be the missing anything. And I am going to Fourmile at the end of this week with a return that says nine houses, and the reason I can do that is that nobody in this town has yet put a sentence of mine in a place where it could cost a person their post.”

She went up the lane. Tomas stood in the doorway of a Crown survey store with a day-book under his arm and the whole of the eleventh day’s finding in his head, and understood that he had been given the last piece of arithmetic he was going to be given for free, and that the woman who had given it to him had decided, in advance, that she was not going to be the person who put it in a file.

He did not write it down. That cost him more than the receipt had cost him in the yard, and he could not have said why, and it took him a long time to work out that it was because the whole of the last eleven days had been spent putting things in writing, and this was the first time he had been given something that was better kept out of a book, and he had not known until that moment that a man could be given a fact that was improved by being lost.

Then he went up the hill to a workshop with one bell in it that he was not allowed to ring.
