# Chapter 0149 — A Man Reads A Charge And Will Not Be Told What It Means By The Man It Is About

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and thirty-fifth day and Tomas Vale went up four steps and through a door propped with a bucket into the long room on the middle terrace, and there were about forty people in it in the ordinary way, and there was a sheet on the rail with a nail in it that he had not seen there before.

A charge.

**Four paragraphs and a day at the top of it and a number under the day, and a man in the room at the end of the rail reading it out loud to about nine people, in the ordinary voice, the way a man reads a thing out loud because he cannot read it quietly to himself and does not know that there is a difference.**

He got to the end of the second paragraph and stopped.

“**It says the thing is made in a public place by a person whose name is given.**” He read the line again. “**It does not say what the thing is.**”

Nobody answered him.

“**It has got four paragraphs and about four minutes of a thing in it and it does not say what the thing is, and I have to stand here and read it out, and I have got a matter of my own on the twenty-eighth and I did not come for this.**”

Tomas Vale was four feet off him with his hands behind his back and the whole of the fourth paragraph in his head, and the fourth paragraph said exactly what the thing was, in eleven words, in the plainest language anybody has ever put in a document, and he could have said so in about four seconds and he did not.

He wrote it down that night and this is the sentence:

> ***I did not tell him. He asked in the ordinary voice, in a public room, in front of about nine people, and I knew the answer and it was eleven words, and I did not say them.***
>
> *And the reason is four inches long and it is a habit and not a rule and it is the hundred and twelfth's. **Whenever a building shows me a way of reading a person's own sworn word, I think about hers.** There is a row on that wall in a hand I have never seen, and I found out there was a row there, and I did not read it, and I am not going to. **And that is not the reason tonight. The reason tonight is this: if I tell a man in a room what a charge says, then that man will say a name four times inside a year in four towns, and every one of the four will be right the first time and more wrong after that, and in about two years there will be a version with a detail in it that was never on the sheet, and by then nobody will remember which of them added it.** About nine of the two hundred who read my four sentences on that wall will do that in a year without any help from me. **I am not going to be the ninth's instrument as well as its cause.**

---

Coll Salkeld had a pen behind his ear and did not look up, and Tomas Vale stood at the end of the rail and did not ask him one question for about half an hour, and then asked him two.

“**Is that a charge or is that a hearing.**”

“**That is a charge.**” He put the pen down. “**A charge is a sheet. A hearing is a room where one person's answer goes into a column and comes out as the answer of the whole matter, and I have put about four hundred a year into that column and not one name on that wall is a disgrace. **A sheet can be produced by a stranger. A room cannot be produced by anybody, because a room has to be there on the day.** Those are two different objects and about forty people a week come through that door and not one of them is a person with a column, and I have not told you that for your own good, I have told you because you asked and I am tired.**”

“**Whose charge is it.**”

“**The district's.**”

“**And who may answer it.**”

“**A party.**” He turned the pen over. “**And a person with no standing cannot be a party to a matter, and a person who has been entered in a column once does not get entered twice for nothing, and I have been in this hall about six years and I have never once looked at the same row twice, and that is the whole of my defence and it is the only one there is.**”

He read the second paragraph off the rail once more to himself, about half through, and then he read the rest of it out loud to the nine people, in the ordinary voice, wrong, all the way to the end.

**He is right about the charge and he is right about the room and he got eleven words of the fourth paragraph wrong in front of nine people, and he is going to go and tell four people in a market that a man is the keeper of a thing that is not known, and I am not going to stand in that room and stop him, and I have thought about that for about four minutes and I am not going to improve it.**

---

At about the sixth hour he went down to the arch at the foot of the top terrace and stood at the end of a counter about nine feet long under a notice that says any person may bring a paper to this counter and ask what it is, and there was no queue.

Perrin Lowe had the shutter half down.

“**You have not got a paper.**”

“**No.**”

“**Then I have got about two minutes and then I have got to shut this.**” He put his pen in the drawer. “**I will say the one thing and then shut it.**”

“**Say it.**”

“**The notice that called you to a hearing is off the board.**” He shut the drawer. “**It is not off because anybody was angry. It is off because it was done, and there is a form for a notice that has been done, and a notice that has been done is not a defeat and it is not a rescue and nobody came down four flights to take it off.**”

“**The warrant.**”

“**Stands.**” He said it the way he says everything. “**And the man who is holding it is not going to be relieved of anything for doing his job, and he is right, and if you were to write to anybody about that you would be writing about a man who is doing it properly.**”

“**I am not going to write to anybody.**”

“**No. And I have been here about nine years and I have never once wanted a form filled in, and you have not asked me a question in about a month, and I have noticed, and I am not going to say anything about the noticing.**”

He shut the shutter and Tomas Vale went away from the counter about a foot higher than he had come to it.

---

The letter came at about the seventh hour, into a room over a shop on the middle terrace that he had paid for by the hour, and it had come by a road post and it was from Kells and it was from Oren Pike.

**One letter from Kells in a hundred and thirty-five days, and I have been in this city thirty-five days, and nobody from Kells has come up this road and nobody is going to.**

He opened it and it was four sides and the first two were about a thing that had gone wrong in a yard in a county he has left, and the third side was this:

> *I have the first charge against the Office's paper. It came down the road on the district's cart with about nine others and it has been in my hand since this morning.*
>
> *I have read it twice and I have a thing of my own about it and I am not going to put my thing next to anybody's, including yours, and I am not going to say what I think it means.*
>
> *There was a paragraph with your business in it. You sent a paragraph with it. **I have not answered the paragraph and I am not going to, and I am telling you that I have not answered it so that you cannot say afterwards that I was told what it meant by the man it is about.**
>
> *I have a chair and a building and a lamp and about nine people come through the room a day and not one of them has a column. I am not a door and I am not sorry and I am not a district. **If I write down what I think it means then in about four years there will be a piece of paper in this county with a man in it and he will not be me, because I will have handed a sentence to a stranger and that is the last thing I am going to do in a chair.***
>
> *Do not come and tell me. Do not write again about this. **You are not owed a visit and I am not owed an explanation and if you turn up at that gate I will not let you past the lamp.**

**A man in a chair in a real building four days' road away has read the thing that is about me, and has decided what it means on his own account, and has written down that he has decided it on his own account, and has not let me tell him, and has told me that he has not let me tell him.**

He read it four times and he did not write back.

**And nobody is thanked. He is not owed a visit and I am not going to give him one and he did not do it for me and I did not ask him to. And being believed by somebody in this kingdom is worth exactly nothing: there is no form on which belief is entered, he cannot enter my paragraph in a column, and about four days' road away is four days' road away and there is no form on which a man may be believed at anybody's desk and act on it. It is not a consolation and it is not a defeat. It is a man in a chair, on his own account, in the ordinary voice.**

**And it is the first time in a hundred and thirty-five days that a man has done a true thing about me without being asked and without being thanked, and I did not arrange it and I did not know it was going to happen and I am not going to write to him this month or next.**

He put it in the inside pocket of the coat with the eleven lines, and it is the fifteenth sheet in a coat and it is not a fourteenth document, because a letter is a letter and it is somebody's and it is not in my register and I am not going to count it and I have not counted it.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step at the foot of the top stair with the four bolts made fast where they were made fast at the fourth hour, and a tarpaulin over a frame, and a sack rolled against a wheel.

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and thirty-fifth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and thirty-fifth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A charge on a rail in a public room, four paragraphs, no words in the second one about what the thing is, a man who could not read it, eleven words I did not say, a notice off a board because it was done, a shutter half down, and a letter of four sides from Kells.**
>
> *Second column, and the finding. **A charge is not a hearing. A hearing is a room where one person's answer goes into a column and comes out as the answer of the whole matter, and about four hundred people a year go into that room and it moves nothing and not one name on that wall is a disgrace. A charge is a sheet, and a sheet can be produced by a stranger I will never meet, and a room cannot be produced by anybody because a room has to be there on the day.** Two different objects. About forty people a week come through that door and not one of them is a person with a column.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. **He asked me what the thing was, in the ordinary voice, in a public room, in front of about nine people, and I knew and I did not answer him, and the reason is four inches long and it is the hundred and twelfth's habit and not a rule: whenever a building shows me a way of reading a person's own sworn word, I think about hers.** About nine hundred forms a year come off one plate in a printer's room two streets off and **one of them has an empty fourth box and nobody has struck a line through it, and a form that has been argued with has a line through it, and it can be checked in about four seconds by asking one clerk one question, and it moves nothing, and that is the only proof I have ever been able to make in this kingdom.** And **if I had said eleven words to a stranger in a room then he would have said a name four times inside a year in four towns, and every one of them right the first time and more wrong after that, and in about two years there would be a version with a detail in it that was never on the sheet. I am not going to be the cause of nine of them and the ninth's instrument as well.** He got eleven words of the fourth paragraph wrong in front of about nine people and I stood four feet off and let him and I have thought about that for about four minutes and I am not going to improve it.
>
> *And Oren Pike, in a chair, in a real building, four days' road away. **He read the first charge against the Office's warrant in his own hand and he has written down that he read it on his own account and he has not answered the paragraph I sent and he is not going to, and he told me he had not answered it so that I could not say afterwards that he was told what it meant by the man it is about.** He has a lamp and about nine people a day and not one of them has a column. **It is the first time in a hundred and thirty-five days that a man has done a true thing about me without being asked and without being thanked, and I arranged none of it, and I am not going to write to him this month or next, and he is not owed a visit and I am not going to give him one.** And being believed by somebody in this kingdom is worth nothing: there is no form on which belief is entered and about four days' road away is four days' road away. **It is not a consolation and it is not a defeat and I am not going to put it in the same list as a bell.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no document produced, given or asked for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no official above a district named, and no name transferred, and no child found by a document, and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and no officer relieved of anything.** The notice is off the board because it was done and the warrant stands. Four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and I was in a room over a shop for an hour and the barrow was at the foot of the stair. **The carrier in the sack, not rung, fifty-eight days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about me, and a letter in the inside pocket with a man's own hand in it, and a letter is a letter and not a document in the register: it is the fifteenth sheet in this coat and it is not a fourteenth and I have not counted it and I am not going to.** I have produced nothing and asked for nothing today.
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a rail with a nail in it and a sheet on it and a man reading it out loud wrong, and a counter with a shutter half down, and a chair four days' road away with a lamp on it and nine people a day coming through the room.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A charge is a sheet and a hearing is a room and a notice that has been done comes off a board in the ordinary way and nobody came down four flights to take it off, and a warrant stands, and a man who is doing his job properly is not going to be relieved of anything for doing it, and a man in a chair has decided what a thing means on his own account and has told me he has, and that is worth nothing and it is not nothing, and I have been putting those in the same list for thirty days and I am not going to tonight.**
