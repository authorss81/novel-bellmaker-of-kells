# Chapter 0123 — The Same Four Boxes In A Different Room

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and ninth day of the month and Tomas Vale stood in front of four boards screwed to a wall in a room three doors along from a hall where about forty people a week went to swear things, and he was going to read every line on all four of them, and there were about nine hundred.

Hale, at the desk, put the lamp out.

“**You will not want the lamp.**”

“**I will want it at about the sixth hour. This room has one window and it faces a wall.**”

“**Then you will want it at about the sixth hour and you will be here at about the sixth hour, and I am here until the shutter, and I am not going to ask you for a reason, and there is no box on this building that would let me if I wanted to.**”

He put his chin on his hand.

“**Go on, then. And I will tell you the one thing that will save you the afternoon: you read the way a man reads when he is looking for one line and not for nine hundred, and if you are looking for one line, do it at the end of the day, because a man who goes over a board from the top for four hours is doing something you cannot do.**”

---

**And he did it in the wrong order on purpose, and he has written down that he did it on purpose, and it is the first thing he has done in a public room in a hundred and nine days that was not simply the next thing.**

He read the four boards from the top, all the way down, at the rate of about a board an hour and a bit, and he did not stop at anything, and he read the fourth column of every line on the way, because the fourth column is where the ninety words live and he has a hundred and nine days of practice at looking at a column and not at a line.

**He is a maker. He has looked at nine sheets, four hundred words, five returns, a carbon and about nine hundred entries in a hundred and nine days, and there is nothing in this kingdom he is better at than standing in front of a long thing and reading all of it.**

And about two hours in he understood the thing the clerk had told him, about two hours after it would have been any use.

**A man who goes over a board from the top for four hours is conspicuous. A man who goes over it from the bottom up, once, having a reason and a number, and stops, is a man who has found what he came for and is going home. And the only way to be inconspicuous for two hours in a public room is to be doing something you would be doing anyway. Which is reading. And he is conspicuously good at reading, and there is no way round that, and he went at it anyway because there is nothing else to do with four hours.**

---

## The columns are the same five, and the fifth is empty, and it is empty for the reason it is empty four streets and four flights up.

> *number — district — day — the thing — and then nothing*

He read that fifth column on all four boards from end to end, at the rate he read the store's list, and it took him about forty minutes of the four hours, and it is empty on every line, and the reason it is empty is the reason it is empty in a store, and he can say the reason in one sentence and it is the sentence a man says about a man who will not fill it in.

**It is the same virtue. A man who keeps a list for a district will not write down a word he cannot read back to somebody who stands in front of him and asks. One of those men is four streets and four flights up this minute and the other one is in a room with no district of its own and a board screwed to a wall, and neither of them has ever heard of the other, and the column is empty in both, and there is nothing whatever at the middle.**

---

## And then the second thing, and it is the seed of the two days after this one, and he counted it twice.

**The third column is the day. And the day is written on every line of all four boards except about nine.**

He found them going down. He counted them going back up. He got nine both times and he did not get a different number the second time, which is the only test he trusts.

They were in one run, on the second board, nine consecutive lines, and then the days started again.

**And a thing that is written nine hundred times and left empty nine times is not carelessness. A clerk in a hurry drops a stroke. A clerk in a hurry does not leave nine consecutive spaces in a column, and a clerk who wanted nine matters to have no day would have had to stop and think about nine matters, and nobody stops to do that.**

So the cell is a choice. **A cell that is filled on nine hundred lines and empty on nine is not a form that was not filled in properly. It is a form somebody chose, and there is no way of telling from a board which of those two things has happened, and there is no column for it, and there is no man in this kingdom he could ask, because a man who asks has to be given a sentence.**

He wrote the count of nine and nothing else about them. He did not look for the numbers on the nine lines and he did not look at the fourth column on the nine lines, and he has written down that he could have done both and did not, and the reason is that a man who opens nine doors in a row is a man who has begun a thing he has no standing to begin.

---

**And at about the sixth hour he put the lamp where Hale had put it out, and the window really does face a wall, and he went on.**

The four boards are ruled in five columns and the fifth is empty, and there is nothing else about them but the number, the district, the day and the thing, and he read all nine hundred lines of all four boards standing up, and he was in the room for most of the day.

**Nobody asked him anything. Nobody asked him anything on the hundred and ninth, in that room or in the corridor or on the steps outside, and Hale did not ask him, and the man who swept that corridor at about the seventh hour said good evening to about nine people including him, and it was the third building in this city where nobody has wanted a thing off him and the third reason it is the same reason.**

And the thing that is different about this room and about that counter four streets and four flights up is four inches long and there is no villain in either of them, and it is the difference between an office that files and a court that publishes, and it is not in favour of either one.

---

## And he found it at about the seventh hour, on the third board, and he did not write it down.

> *4, 1 8 2 — **Netherby** — the eleventh — **coupling***

He stood in front of it for about a minute and a half and then he went back to the top of the board and read the whole board again, which took him about forty minutes, and then he read the third board again from the top down to the line, and the line was where it had been.

**Four one eight two. Netherby. The eleventh. Coupling. The same four things the store's book says, in the same order, in the same five ruled columns, four streets and four flights up and eleven days ago.**

The boards are headed with a year each and he did not count them and did not write any of them down, and that is the same rule as the one about the number, and it took him about nine minutes to decide that.

---

**And he stood there with a stub of chalk in his hand — it was in his coat because it is always in his coat, and it had been in his coat on the ninety-second and on the hundred and first and on every market day since — and he did not write the number on the back of a page, and he wrote down afterwards why, and the why is the whole of the hundred and ninth.**

> *A hand against a line is the only record in this kingdom that a person stood in front of somebody and said something.*
>
> **If I put four one eight two on the back of my own page in a public room, then there is a page in this kingdom with my hand on it and that number on it, and there is about forty people a week through that door, and any one of them could be asked about it later by somebody with a column, and I would have made a link between myself and a matter by writing four figures, and a link on paper is a claim, and a claim is a thing I have spent a hundred and nine days refusing to make about other people's sentences.**
>
> **And I would have written it nine feet from a board it is on, which is the same act as copying a court list into a coat, and I have refused that four times and I wrote down all four reasons in two different books and I am not going to do it a fifth time with a piece of chalk in a public room because I am tired.**

**So the number is in his head, and it is on a wall, and it is in a book four streets and four flights up, and he took nothing, and there is no difference between him and a man who has not been in the room at all except that he now knows what is at the end of it.**

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step by the river with the tarpaulin over the cradle and the four bolts made fast, and the sack rolled against the wheel, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and ninth, and it is the shortest one he has written in nine days, and he filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.**

> *The hundred and ninth. **Nine hundred lines standing up in one day and a word in a column that is the same word in two buildings, and an office and a court are the same kind of room with the same kind of counter and the same kind of man, and the difference between them is four inches long, and it is not in favour of either one.**
>
> *I have been looking for a villain in two buildings that do not have the word. **There is not one in this room and there is not one in the store and there is not one in this city, and I have been three days looking for a man behind a number and a number is not a man. A number is a place a thing is filed at, and that is what a man behind a counter told me at the fourth hour yesterday and it took me a whole day to believe him.**
>
> *Second column, and the finding. **Five columns on four boards and the fifth is empty on every line, and it is empty because a man will not write down a word he cannot read back.** Same room, same rule, same virtue, four streets and four flights and eleven days, and neither man has ever heard of the other. **And the third column is filled on every line except nine, and the nine are together, and a cell that is filled nine hundred times and empty nine times is not carelessness, it is a choice, and I counted them twice and got nine both times and I did not look at the other columns on those nine lines.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. **I had a stub of chalk in my hand and I did not write four one eight two on the back of my own page.** A hand against a line is the only record in this kingdom that a person stood in front of something and said something, and if I write a number down in a public room I have made a claim, and the claim is about me, and a claim about me is the one thing I have not got and the one thing I have spent a hundred and nine days refusing to make. **So it is in my head and it is on a wall and it is in a book, and I have taken nothing, and that is now five things in a hundred and nine days that I have looked at and not taken, and I have written every one of the reasons down and not one of the reasons was fear.**
>
> *And the record. **A clerk at a desk with a lamp said anybody may read the record of a matter in that room, and that the record is the court's and the file is the district's, and that there is no box anywhere in this kingdom that says who may read a thing, and the absence of that box is the whole of it.** The file is not mine and the district will not show it to me and the clerk is right and he keeps the door and does not know who opens it and said he is not permitted to say. **So I have a number and a day and a district, and the district's record of that number is on a rail in a public room and I may read it tomorrow, and there is nothing anywhere in this kingdom standing between me and it except the fact that I have no standing at all, which has turned out to be worth nothing at nine counters and eleven days.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, and nothing said, and no shelf opened, and no number written down anywhere but in a head.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and I stood in a room for most of this day and it makes no difference to the frame. The carrier in the sack untouched, thirty-two days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells. **Thirteen documents in a coat, and four boards of nine hundred lines that are the court's and are read standing up and left where they are, and those are correct figures and neither of them is a fourteenth.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a room with a lamp in it and a board on the wall and a list of about nine hundred matters a year that nobody is unrolling, and the same five columns in it that are in a store's book, and the same empty fifth.** Tomorrow I am going to read one record off a rail and I am going to stand up and I am not going to be asked for a thing, **and if there is nothing on it then a hundred and ten days have bought me a word in a column and a man with a lamp, and I will write that down too.**
