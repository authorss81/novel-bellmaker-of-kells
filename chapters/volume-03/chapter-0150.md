# Chapter 0150 — A Road Out Of Sennet On Foot, And Not A Victory

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and thirty-sixth day and Tomas Vale stood on a step at the foot of the top stair of a city of about nine thousand people with a barrow, and worked out, standing up, in about four minutes, how he was going to leave it.

> *A matter with my name at the head of it is going up the river on a district's cart to that district's own court, and the cart goes in a day or two, and I am not going to be put on a cart. **A cart is the district's and a barrow is mine, and a man who is put in a cart has been conveyed, and a man who walks has gone.** And a person named in a paper goes with the paper, because there is no form in this kingdom that lets a district keep a man and there never has been, **and that is not a door anybody opened for me. It is a hole in a set of forms about four days old and I have walked through it and I am not going to thank anybody for the hole.**
>
> *And a warrant stands in a city I am not going back to. **There is nothing in this kingdom that lifts a warrant and there is no form on which a man asks for one to be lifted, and a notice comes off a board when it has been done and a warrant comes off a board when the thing it is for is finished, and that may be a year, and I am not going to be here for it.**

He made the four bolts fast at the fourth hour where they were made fast at the fourth hour, and put the tarpaulin over the frame, and rolled the sack against the wheel, and took the bar and the spade off the flat and left them under the loft frame, because a post in a bank with an empty bracket on it is not his to leave and the bar and the spade are not his either and there was no form in this kingdom on which he borrowed them.

**Nine feet of rope went back on the frame on the hundred and twenty-eighth and has been hanging where it hangs since, and I had it for a morning on the hundred and twenty-seventh and put it back the next morning, and it belongs to the loft, and it is going to go on belonging to the loft after this, and that is the correct arrangement and I am not going to improve it.**

---

At about the fifth hour he came down the middle terrace with the barrow and about nine hundred people were in the market and about two hundred of them in front of a wall, and he went through it the ordinary way, at walking pace, with nothing in his hands, and a woman selling needles put her head round and said good morning and he said good morning, and that is all of the two conversations he had in a market of about nine hundred people on the last day.

**Nobody stopped me. Nobody asked me one question in a city of about nine thousand people on the way out of it, and I have been in forty-one days of it and not one person has ever once asked me what I am doing, and it is not kindness and it is not a door. It is that there is nothing here to be asked about, and the same absence that let me stand at a rail for an hour and put my hand on a bell on the evening of the hundred and thirtieth has now let me walk out of the middle of this city with a barrow.**

At about the sixth hour he was down on the flat and out along the Wharf Road, and he walked the mile of it, and he counted the bells because he counts things and he cannot help it.

**Nine. Nine bells in a mile, and none of them sounding, and none of them broken, and the count of them is a figure nobody in this city keeps and I am the only man in this kingdom who has it twice.**

He did not ask three questions at any of them. He walked past nine bells and did not stop at one.

**They are all the hour. They have been the hour since the hundred and twenty-fifth and they are the hour now, and none of them is the one I struck. That one is up on the top terrace over a gate with a socket and a wire going down the inside of a post, and it is not in a mile of river road and it is not a tenth of anything. I put a sound into it once, on the evening of the hundred and thirtieth, with a person in it, and about forty people were standing in that yard and not one of them is an answer, and there is no form in this kingdom that can tell a bell telling you the time from a bell telling you a true thing, and that absence is four inches long, and it is going out of this city with me and it is the whole of a hundred and thirty-six days.**

---

At about the seventh hour he stopped on the road where it starts to climb and got the whole of it out of himself, in order, in about nine minutes, and it is the shape of every bell he has rung four times in his life.

> ***I arranged none of it.** There is no plate and there is no clerk and there is no form and there is no copy, and not one person in this city knows there are three of us, and I did not tell a single one of them, and I did not stand in three places and say nothing this time because I was in a room with a form in it and then I was on a road with a bundle in my hands, and neither of those was arranged either.
>
> ***And the arranging is the whole of the damage, and I am writing that down in the same words I wrote the asking down in on the hundred and sixteenth, because it is the same sentence with one letter changed and I have had twenty days to find out that it is one sentence.** I asked a man at a rail for a true sentence and that was the damage. I paid for a room and put three sets of terms into it and that was the damage. I filled in a form on the hundred and fifteenth with four sentences of my own for fourpence and that was the damage. **Every time anything has moved in a hundred and thirty-six days, the thing that did it was me organising people, and not one of them has ever been thanked for it and not one of them ever will be, and the reason is four inches long: the instant a man with no standing arranges a thing, the thing stops being about anything except him.**
>
> ***And the best thing in a hundred and thirty-six days is a thing I did not arrange and cannot attend and am not going to find out about: a woman inside a building chose a record over a man, and she did not come, and she was not asked, and I do not know whether she did anything at all and I am not going to find out and I am not going to thank her.***

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step by the river on the flat, about a mile out of Sennet, with the tarpaulin over the cradle and the four bolts made fast and the sack rolled against the wheel and a woman ten feet off his left shoulder whose hearing is worse than it was ten days ago and who has not said so and whom nobody has asked.

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and thirty-sixth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and thirty-sixth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A step at the foot of a stair at the fourth hour, a mile of river road with nine bells in it, and a step by the water at the ninth, and a road out of a city of about nine thousand people that I am not going back to.**
>
> *Second column, and what a hundred and thirty-six days bought. **I arranged none of it and I have written that down in the same words as the asking because it is one sentence and not two.** The best thing in a hundred and thirty-six days is a thing I did not arrange: **a woman inside a building chose a record over a man, and she did not come, and she was not asked, and I do not know what she did and I am not going to find out and I am not going to thank her for it, and nothing between us is resolved and I am not going to say it is.**
>
> *And the second best thing is a sound. **About forty people in a city of about nine thousand heard a bell once, in a yard, in the ordinary way, on the evening of the hundred and thirtieth, and not one of them is an answer, and not one of them will be able to tell anybody what it was, and it is the only sentence I have ever managed to get in front of about forty doors for nothing and it does not carry words and it does not carry who struck it and I cannot tell anybody I did it.** About four hundred people a year put a true sentence on a public wall for fourpence and it moves nothing. **The two are the same act at two prices and I did not know that until this week.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the arithmetic, and I have had it since the hundred and twenty-sixth and it has not moved a finger in ten days. **I have got one of the three answers and not two and not three, and the one I have is my own, and a man can be one of the three answers and cannot be two, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom on which I could be two. That is a rule about counting and it is not a power and I am not going to write it down as one.** And a no said in advance is not an answer to a claim, because there was never a claim, **so the three of us are three refusals and not one agreement, and the one of the three that is an answer is me, and the other two said no in their own words on their own days and neither of them has improved what she said since and I have not read either of them again and I am not going to.**
>
> *And the cost, and it is the cost and I am not going to put it in a different column. **Her hearing is worse than it was ten days ago and it is worse for the rest of her life and nothing is restored and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of it, and I did not ask her for a reason, and that is about four inches long and it is the hardest thing I have done in a hundred and thirty-six days and I am not going to describe how hard it was, because a man who describes how hard it was for him is asking to be thanked for not asking.** She took the worst of it because she was holding a sill and a floor and a wall and she is the one who reads vibration, **and I was given the choice and it was not the choice, and I am not the person who got to make it, and she had decided before she said a word and I was not asked because I could not have been asked, and that is the whole of what I have failed to learn in four counties and a city and it cost her the use of a thing she will not get back.**
>
> *And a man on a bench in a room off a lane is still on a bench in a room off a lane. **I did not say his name and I am not going to, and there is no form in this kingdom for a story, and I have not got him out of it and I am not going to be able to and nobody is going to be able to.** The arrest stands. **And nobody is going to be relieved of anything for serving a warrant properly, and the man who served it is right, and a notice came off a board on the hundred and thirty-fifth because it was done and nobody came down four flights to take it off, and a store issued a fitting on somebody else's instruction on the hundred and twenty-eighth and wrote it in a book with a number and a district and a day against it, and a warrant stands, and no Crown office has been defeated this month or any other month by me.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no official above a district named, and no name transferred, and no child found by a document, and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything.** Four bolts made fast at the fourth hour, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it has been in the open every hour of every day of a hundred and thirty-six days and it is on a road now and a road is not a building.** The carrier in the sack, **fifty-nine days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not rung and it is not struck and I am not going to strike it in this county at all, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I am not going to find out. I have put my hand flat on its crown four times in a hundred and thirty-six days, on the hundred and sixth, the hundred and twenty-second, the hundred and twenty-fifth and the hundred and twenty-seventh, and the fourth was the last one and I knew it was the last one while I was doing it.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **No bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight and none of them is going to be.** **Her bell is in her bag and she rang it once in a market of about nine hundred people on the ninety-second on her own account and it did not help me and nobody asked her why.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, which is four hundred words in an office's own hand whose last line says the person named produced nothing.** The two things in this coat that are about me are **a leaf in my own book with eleven lines on it that nobody may verify and anybody may produce, and a copy of four sentences of mine in a district's keeping going round on a cart.** **A copy of eleven lines out of a head is not a copy of a court record and is not a fourteenth and the thirteen are the thirteen.** A schedule folded in three is a map and not a document and I have not asked anybody for it and nobody gave it to me. A letter in the inside pocket is a letter and not a fourteenth. **The thing in this coat that is a copy is the one the district made of my four sentences, and I did not make it, and I am not going to be able to take it back, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom by which a man who has been named on a board can be unnamed.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is about nine hundred rooms and about nine hundred bells and about nine hundred entries on nine sheets in a book behind a counter and about nine hundred matters a year into a room with a lamp in it, and the four nines are told apart by which room they are in and none of them is a quantity of anything and there is not going to be a fifth.** It is a plate cutting about nine hundred forms a year with a four-inch box empty on one of them and no line through it, and it is checked in about four seconds by asking one clerk one question and it moves nothing. It is a counter that takes papers and not people, a court that publishes, a file that nobody may read, a return with a box for a keeper that a man could have filled in and did not, a form with four boxes and one of them a day, a fourth box that says the sound somebody made in a yard, and a bundle of about nine hundred lines going up the river in a day or two. **Nobody in it is wicked. A notice comes off a board when it is done. A warrant is what an office does with a real signal. A man in a chair four days off decides what a thing means on his own account and is not thanked. A woman in a building chooses a record and is not met. And a bellmaker walks out of a city with a barrow because a form about holding a man was not written, and that is not a victory and he is not going to say it is, and the sentence at the foot of this page is the sentence the next hundred and thirty-six days opens on and he has still done nothing about it.**
>
> *One of the three answers. Not two. Not three. **If there were three of me.**

---

Behind them a capital of about nine thousand people in which a bell goes on a schedule every day and the man who puts his hand on it does not know whether he is to and there is nobody above him to ask; a warrant that stands in a room off a lane; a clerk at a desk who has never once looked at the same row twice and is owed nothing; a woman on a public wall with four drawing-pins in her sheet; a woman inside a building who chose a record over a man and has not been met; a man on a bench over four inches at ten feet who cannot say what put him there; an archive four streets and four flights up that is intact, because the person who could have given it away did not; a store that has issued a fitting this month against somebody else's instruction and written it in a book; and about forty people in a yard on the top terrace who have been told nothing at all about what to decide, and are not going to be.

And on a road above the water, a mile out, a bellmaker with a barrow and a frame in a cradle four feet off him with four bolts made fast, and a plain bell in a sack he has not rung in fifty-nine days, and a map he cannot read with a rectangle about two hands together out of it in the same place, and eleven lines in a coat that nobody may copy, and a woman beside him whose hearing is worse than it was ten days ago, walking on, on foot, to nothing he has arranged.
