# Chapter 0124 — A Matter With A Name On It And A Man Who Cannot Claim It

He went back down the corridor at the fourth hour of the hundred and tenth day and Hale had the door open and did not look up, and the lamp was lit, and on the desk beside the lamp there was a tray with about nine folded forms in it and Hale was working on the top one.

**And Tomas Vale stood in front of a rail nine feet long with four or five volumes on it and a bundle of about forty sheets tied at the middle with a string, and he found four one eight two on a slip tucked into the string, and he took the bundle down, and he undid the string, and it was the record of one matter of the district of Netherby, and it is about four hundred words.**

He read it standing up.

He has read four hundred words standing up before, on the hundred and fifth, in a room on this terrace, about himself. It took about eleven minutes then. This one took him longer and he knows exactly where it stopped working, and it is not at the end.

---

> *The district court of Netherby, sitting in this building by the arrangement of eleven districts up the river from it, in the third room along the north corridor.*
>
> *The matter is one in which a person was charged with concealing goods of the district, being nine of them, and with refusing to name the persons from whom she had them.*
>
> *The person named is **Lira Vale**.*
>
> *The matter came before the court on the eleventh. The court sat four days.*

That is the form, and the form is four boxes, and three of them are filled in.

**One is the matter. One is the person. One is the day. And the fourth is a space with nothing in it, and it is the fourth box, and it is the box on every form in this kingdom that says why.**

---

## And he read the rest of it, and here is the whole of the four hundred words:

> *The person named was told in this room what she was charged with, and was asked, on four separate days, whether she wished to answer, and on the fourth occasion she said that she would answer once and not again, and the court allowed it.*
>
> *Her answer is set out at the end of this record in her own words, as she gave them. Nothing has been entered in this record that was not said in this room.*
>
> *The goods in question were not produced. No person was examined on the question of from whom the person named had them, and the court has not thought it right to ask a person that question a second time.*
>
> ***The court has not found that the person named did, or did not do, the thing charged.***
>
> *The matter is closed.*

---

**And he read it a second time, because he is a man who compares forms, and comparing forms is the whole of his trade and it is the first time in four counties and a city that the trade has been the instrument.**

He read it and then he looked at the tray on Hale's desk.

There were about nine forms in it. Hale was on the top one and the other eight were folded and stacked and waiting for whatever waits in a tray in that room. Tomas could see the head of each one from nine feet, upside down.

**On every one of the nine, the fourth box is filled in.**

He did not ask to see them. He did not lean over Hale's hand. He read nine heads of forms at nine feet and he did it in about as long as it takes to count nine things.

---

## And then he went outside, and he did not open the last fold, and neither of those is a decision he is proud of and both of them are on the page.

The bundle was tied at the middle with a string. Inside the string, at the back, folded once in a sheet that is a different paper from the rest, there was something that was not part of the four hundred words.

He had his thumb on the fold. He did not open it. He tied the string back the way it had been and put the bundle on the rail, and he went down four steps and out into a street in a city of about nine thousand people and stood about nine feet off the door.

**It was not because he was made of glass and it was not because the four hundred words were hard, and it was not because he had not the right.**

It was because the fourth box on that form was empty and the fourth box on every other form in that tray was not, and he did not yet know what that meant, and he was not going to find out what it meant in a corridor with a lamp in it in front of a man who had told him he was not permitted to say one particular thing.

**He wanted a room. There are about four rooms in this building and he did not have any of them and he was not going to take one. So he stood in the street, and then he went and sat on the low wall at the end of the steps where there was no room at all, and the wind off the river was cold, and he read the four hundred words again in his head, which is not the same as reading them.**

---

**And the thing he is not going to put at the bottom of this, and puts at the bottom of this, is this.**

The record is four hundred words about a room and about a woman.

**And it does not say one single word about what happened to her.**

Not in the form. Not in the four boxes. Not in the paragraph about the goods not being produced. Not in the finding, which is a sentence shaped exactly like a thing that has been cut and left, because a court that cannot find either way has to write something and a district's clerk has about a hundred and forty such sentences a year and every one of them is the same four inches long.

**And the box where it would be said is empty. And on nine forms in a tray nine feet off it is not.**

He stood in the street and he was aware — for about nine minutes and possibly for the rest of the day — that he had been handed a document about his own mother, and it did not tell him one thing about her, and that this was not a matter of a court keeping a file, because a file is the district's and he is the man and he has known that for two days.

---

## And he did not ask who she was, of anybody, and he did not say her name aloud in that room, and here is why, and it is four inches long.

There were about nine people in that room and corridor over the day: the clerk, a man sweeping, two men who came in at about the fifth hour and stood at the far end of the rail for about four minutes and left, a woman with a tray of her own, a man who asked Hale for something in a low voice and got it.

**A name said out loud in a room is in nine people's ears.** And a name in nine people's ears is a name that any one of them can be asked about afterwards by somebody with a column, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom by which a person who has said a name in a room can have the hearing of it taken back.

He wrote that down at about the seventh hour, on the low wall at the end of the steps, in four inches, and it is the same four inches he wrote on the ninety-first in a market two days' road back and eleven weeks ago about a leaf pinned at an angle under a drawing-pin, **and he is not going to say her name in a public room, and he wrote down that he did not, and the reason is four inches long and it is not about her.**

---

**And the other thing he did not do, and it is the fourth clause, and it is the reason he could not say one word about who she was to the one man in that room who would have let him.**

The fifth clause of his own receipt, in his own hand, in a watch house, in a county he has left, on the fifth hour of the morning of the thirty-third:

> *The receipt does not make the holder a keeper of any route, and no answer to any signal carried on or by the object is to be entered as anybody's answer for a household of this ward, and the holder has no standing to speak for any household in Kells or in any ward.*

**He asked a clerk to write that clause because he was right. And the whole of the hundred and tenth day is what that clause costs, and it is four inches of standing and it is the only thing he has ever written down that was worth writing and it has never once cost him nothing.**

If he had said, in that room, *that is my mother* — then he would have been a person with a claim on a matter, and a person with a claim is a person the court must deal with, and the district would have had to open a box about a relation between two people, **and there is no box in this kingdom for a son. There is a box for a party and a box for a person named and a box for a day and a box for a reason, and not one of the four is anywhere near it, and a man with no standing who puts himself forward as a relation in a public room is a man who has made himself a party to about nine hundred lines of a board in one sentence.**

So he stood nine feet off a rail in a public room and he read a document with his mother's name at the head of it and he was nobody, and the fifth clause is the reason, and he asked for it himself.

**He is not going to complain about it in public, and he is not going to complain about it anywhere else either, and he has written down that he is not, and the reason is that the clause has done five jobs in a hundred and ten days and every one of them has cost me something and not one of them has cost anybody else anything.**

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening he was on the step at the foot of the top stair with the tarpaulin over the cradle and the four bolts made fast, and the sack rolled against the wheel, and Tamsin Rook nine feet off his left shoulder with a bell in her bag, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and tenth, filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was, and it is the longest thing he has written in three days.**

> *The hundred and tenth. **I have been a person who may read for eleven days, and today a form told me that my mother was tried, and the form did not say one word about how.**
>
> *Four one eight two, Netherby, the eleventh. Nine days in a store's book and a district in it I have never stood in, and about four hundred words on a rail in a room I have never been in, in a hand that is not hers and that I cannot put a name to, **and not one word in the four hundred about what was done to her afterwards. None. Not a day, not a place, not a box, not a word in a margin, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom that would hold it, and I have looked for it for about nine minutes in a street.**
>
> *Second column, and the thing I am not going to say in the morning. **I did not ask who she was.** I did not say her name out loud in that room. There were about nine people in it and a corridor over the day and a name said out loud in a room is in nine people's ears, and a name in nine people's ears can be asked about afterwards by somebody with a column, and there is no form anywhere by which a person can have the hearing of a name taken back. **That is four inches long and it is the same four inches I wrote on the ninety-first about a leaf and a drawing-pin, and I wrote it then without knowing I would need it again.**
>
> *And I did not say who she was to me, either, **and that is the fifth clause and it cost me the whole day.** It says the holder has no standing to speak for any household in Kells or in any ward, and I asked a clerk in a watch house to write it because I was right, and this morning it stopped me being a man in a room. There is no box in this kingdom for a son. There is a box for a party, a box for a person named, a box for a day and a box for a reason, and not one of the four is near it, **and a man with no standing who puts himself forward as a relation in a public room is a man who has made himself a party to nine hundred lines of a board in one sentence, and I could have done that, and nobody would have stopped me, and the clerk at the desk would have had to write something.**
>
> *So I was nobody. I stood nine feet off a rail in a public room in a city of about nine thousand people and read a document with my mother's name on it and I was nobody, **and that is four inches of dignity and it is the price of a clause and I am not going to complain about it in public or anywhere else.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the thing I have not got round. **The fourth box is empty on that form and it is filled in on all nine forms in a tray nine feet off it, and I did not ask to see them and I did not lean over a clerk's hand, I read nine heads of forms at nine feet, which is what I have been doing in four counties and a city for a hundred and ten days.** I do not know yet what the fourth box is for. I have a strong idea it is a reason, because a reason is the only thing a district's form has ever wanted, and there is no box in this kingdom for a son and no box for a why a woman went to a court, and I have got two empties in a day in two buildings and I am not going to join them. **If a box is empty on one line and full on nine hundred, that is either a clerk who was not paid enough attention or somebody chose it, and I have not got the least idea which, and I have not got anybody to ask, and the man at the desk would not know.**
>
> *And the thing I am going to have to do tomorrow, and I know it before I have written it down, **which is to go back into that room and open the fold.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, and no question asked of anybody.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and I have been in two rooms today and it makes no difference to the frame, and the fourth clause is a clause about a carrier and a road and a box handle, and the fifth is a clause about a household, and neither of them has anything to do with a court three streets and four flights down, and I have found out today that the fifth one has been in a court with me all along and I did not know it was in the room.*
>
> *The carrier in the sack untouched, thirty-three days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents in a coat, and a court record that is the court's and is on a rail in a public room, and those are two correct figures and neither of them is a fourteenth, and a man who puts a court record in a coat has mistaken a thing he may read for a thing he has got, and that mistake is a hundred and ten days old and it is the only mistake I have that I know of.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a form with four boxes and three of them filled in, and a hundred and forty words of a room, and a finding that says the court has found nothing either way, and a box where the rest of it would be and nothing in the box.** And I have not got a reason and I am not going to be able to get one, and there is no box for a son, and tomorrow I am going to open a fold.
