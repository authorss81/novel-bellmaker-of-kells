# Chapter 0144 — A Warrant Is What An Office Does With A Real Signal

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and thirtieth day and Tomas Vale went along the middle terrace with about nine hundred people in the market and about two hundred of them in front of a wall, and he was not going to the wall, and then he went into a lane off the terrace and up a stair and into a room at the top of it.

There was a form on the table and a bench against the wall and a lamp and there was nobody in the room, and a man in a coat was sitting on the bench filling in a Crown market slip with a lead weight and a string.

**Six fields. The market, the day, the thing repeated, the person who repeated it, where it was said, and who heard it. Four of the six can be filled in by anybody standing there and one of the six wants a name, and about four hundred people a year put a name in it and none of them are wicked.**

He read it upside down from about nine feet, standing at the end of the table, and the man on the bench did not ask him anything, because there is no box in this kingdom that lets anybody ask a stranger what he is doing in a room.

**And there is no field on it for a sound.**

He stood there and looked at six boxes for about four minutes.

> *I have been looking for a place to put a claim for three days and the place is not the problem and the mount is not the problem and I have got a post in a bank with an empty bracket on it. The problem is that a claim in metal is a sound and a sound is not a thing anybody can write down, **and I have gone into a room with a form in it to find out what a form does with a sound, and the answer is that it has six boxes and not one of them is for one, and the man sitting at the table did not look up, and about four hundred of those a year are filled in by people standing there and none of them are wicked and none of them has ever put a sound in a box.**

---

At about the fifth hour he went up four flights and stood at the foot of the stair and did not go into anything, and then he went into a yard.

The yard had about two hundred people in it, as it has on the sixth hour, and there was a bench under a gate frame about nine feet high, and on the frame there was a bell about as big as a cartwheel with a bracket and a socket and a wire going down the inside of the post.

**That is the third kind. It is not a keeper's bell, because the man at the rail has six years at it and a book of his own and no office and he is not the keeper of it, he is the man who is standing next to it. And it is not a plain bell, because nobody decides to hit it; it goes when the paper says it goes, and the paper is four streets and four flights up and the man who decides is not the man who reads the sheet.**

Marek Douse turned a plate over and struck it four times whether or not there was anything on it, and about two hundred people let it ring and made no noise, and that has been the case every day of the six years he has stood at that rail.

Tomas Vale stood nine feet off the rail for about an hour and asked him nothing.

**I have been in this city thirty-five days and not one clerk and not one man at a counter and not one woman with a tray of pins has asked me what I am doing, and that is not a door and it is not kindness. It is the same absence of a reason-box that has let me into every building in this city, and tonight it lets me stand at a rail and put my hand on a bell.**

---

At about the seventh hour the yard was emptying. About forty people were still in it and the light had gone grey and the plate on the frame was turned over with nothing on it, and the bench under the gate was empty because the man at the rail had gone inside and would be back.

He put his hand on the bell and struck it once.

**It is four inches across the lip at the sound bow and it is heavier than it looks and it is the dullest note I have ever struck and it does not carry far inside a yard, and it is not a note anybody would mistake for anything, and I struck it once, and four strokes has meant one thing since that frame was new.**

Nobody stopped him.

**Nobody stopped him and nobody asked him and there were about forty people in that yard and not one of them turned round, and that is not permission and it is not a signal received. It is the same absence of a reason. A man may do almost anything in a yard in this city and about nine hundred bells a day in this city are struck by somebody who is not the person it is about.**

He did not put the four sentences in it. **He wanted to. That is the whole of what happened at that frame and I am going to write it down once and not improve it: I wanted to put a claim in a bell and I could not, because a man ringing a bell he did not make says whatever the bell's claim is and cannot add to it. And the claim in that bell was the hour, because that frame is a district's frame and the wire in the post belongs to a line, and the hour is the only thing that has ever gone into it.**

**So the truest sentence I have ever made in a hundred and thirty days went into a bell that had the time in it, and a claim in metal cannot be edited, and I could not take the time out of it, and there is no form in this kingdom that can tell the difference between them afterwards, and that absence is four inches long.**

He came down off the top terrace and went along the middle terrace and got to the foot of the stair at about the ninth hour, and the four bolts were where he had made them fast at the fourth hour.

---

At about the ninth hour, standing at the foot of the top stair, he worked out what he had done, and it took him about four minutes and it is four sentences long.

> *I have put a sound into a city of about nine thousand people. I did not tell anybody it was coming and nobody was asked to be in it and nobody was asked to listen to it and I have not the least idea whether one person in that city heard it as anything other than a bell going.*
>
> *And about nine minutes after I struck it, a form in a room four floors up had a line in it, and the line had a day on it and a number at the top of it, and the fourth box on the line was not empty, **and the fourth box said that a person made a signal in a yard in this city on the day named, and that is the truest thing a form has ever said about me and it can be produced against me by a stranger I will never meet for forty years.**
>
> *And the number at the top of that form is a filing reference and not a man, and the day is a day and not a reason, and the fourth box is a ground and not a why, and the hand that wrote it did the ordinary thing in the correct column in about four seconds and it is not going to be sorry, **and the whole of it is lawful, and there is not one step of it that anybody could have done differently, and the Office is right about the procedure.**
>
> *And the Office is wrong about the standing. **A signal is a real signal. A warrant is what an office does with a real signal. And I have no standing to be wrong about, and that is the whole of the asymmetry, and it took a hundred and thirty days to arrive at it and it has cost me a bell and a person and about nine hundred people who came out of their doors because that is what they do at that hour.**

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and thirtieth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and thirtieth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **I struck a bell once, in a yard on the top terrace, at about the seventh hour, and about nine minutes later there was a form four floors up with a day on it and a number at the top of it and a fourth box that said a person made a signal.**
>
> *Second column, and what it is. **A public claim made once, in metal, in the ordinary way, in a yard in a city of about nine thousand people, by a man with a bar on his own bell and no keeper and one answer, and it is the shape of every warning I have ever made and it is not a stage and it did not make me stronger.** I did not tell anybody it was coming and nobody was asked to be in it. **The claim names what is known, names what is not known, names my own fault, and tells nobody what to decide**, and I wanted to put that in the bell and I could not, **and the reason I could not is that a man ringing a bell he did not make says whatever the bell's claim is and cannot add to it, and the claim in that bell was the hour, and I could not take the hour out of a bell, and no form in this kingdom can tell the time off a true thing afterwards.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. **The only record of it in the world is what about nine hundred people did when they heard a sound, and a district's paper about it is four fields and one of them is a day.** The fourth box is not three words tonight. It is the sound somebody made in a yard, and it is true, **and it can be produced against me by a stranger I will never meet for forty years, and it is the truest thing a form has ever said about me, and I have got it for fourpence in a public room on a wall and a copy of it on a cart and this is a third one and I did not pay for it and I did not ask for it.**
>
> *And the best thing I have done in a hundred and thirty days is something nobody told me to do and no office arranged. **And it is the cheapest act and the most expensive one and they are the same act, and I cannot tell anybody I did it, because the only instrument that would carry the fact is the one I have just used, and a bell does not carry words and does not carry who struck it.** Nobody stopped me and nobody asked me anything and about forty people in that yard did not turn round, **and that is not consent and not a signal received, it is the same absence of a reason-box that has let me stand at a rail for an hour and has let me into every room in this city for thirty-five days, and I have now used that absence twice and it is the same fact both times.**
>
> *And the fifth clause has done its sixth job in a hundred and thirty days, and it is this. **The signal was carried in a yard in front of about two hundred people, and if one of them had come up and said a word to me I should have had two answers, and there is no form on which one of them can be entered as anybody's answer for a household of this ward, and the clause is why I did not ask, and asking would have been the damage, and it is the asking and the arranging and it is one sentence and I have written it three times in three months.** Nobody asked her for a reason at any hour in a hundred and thirty days and I did not ask her about the bell and I am not going to.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and no document produced or given or asked for, and no name transferred, and no child found by a document, and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything.** Four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a yard is not a building and a gate frame is not a building and I was in a room off a lane for four minutes this morning with the barrow at the foot of the stair. **The carrier in the sack, not rung, fifty-three days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, **and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight, and the bell I struck tonight in that yard is not mine, and I could not add a word to it and there was nothing in it but the hour.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about me, and a form four floors up with my name nowhere in it and a person in its fourth box, and a day on a line, and a number at the top of it that is a filing reference and not a man.** None of them is a fourteenth and I have produced nothing and asked for nothing today.
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a yard, a gate, a bracket, a socket and a wire going down the inside of a post, and a bell about as big as a cartwheel, and a man with a book of his own and no office who strikes it four times whether or not there is anything on the plate, and a plate with nothing on it this evening because I put nothing on it and I was not going to.** It is a room four floors up with a lamp and a board and a hand that put one line in one column in about four seconds. **It is a rule that says a sworn answer is a thing about a person and must be sent round, and a district that makes about eleven thousand lines a year, and a form with four boxes and one of them a day and one of them a ground.** Nobody is wicked. **A signal is a real signal and a warrant is what an office does with a real signal, and the Office is right about the procedure and wrong about the standing, and I have no standing to be wrong about, and that asymmetry is the whole of a hundred and thirty days and it has cost me a bell and a person and about nine hundred people who came out of their doors at the hour because that is what they do.**
