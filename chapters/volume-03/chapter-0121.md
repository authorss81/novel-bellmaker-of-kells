# Chapter 0121 — The Book Is Open And The Shelf Is Shut

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and seventh day of the month and Tomas Vale went up four flights of stairs on the top terrace of Sennet with nothing in his hands, and the room at the top of them was open, and the man behind the counter was writing a line in a book, and the wall behind him was shut.

He was twelve days into the city. He had been refused a shelf on the hundredth and had read nine sheets in this same room on the hundred and first and taken nothing off the counter, and he had come up again because there was one thing left in the room he had not asked for, and he had known for six days that he would ask it and had not come up.

**The book is open. That is the whole of what is different about this room and it took me a week of standing at counters to see that it is the only thing about it that anybody could object to, and nobody does.**

---

“**I have come for the shelf.**”

Osmund Tarrant did not put his pen down.

“**No.**”

“**You said that on the hundredth and I have come back to say it twice.**”

“**Then it is still no, and I would say it the same way a third time, and I am not going to shorten it for you because you have come a second time. The shelf is shut because a store shows a shelf to an instruction and you have not got an instruction, and if I open that wall for a man who asks twice then I have opened it, and there is no way to open it twice.**”

He wrote a figure in a column and moved the pen along the line.

“**You have not got an instruction, and you have not got a reason either, and the reason you have not got a reason is not in this room and it is four inches long and it is yours.**”

---

**And then he did not ask what he had come for, and he has written down that he stood in that room about two minutes and did not open his mouth, and that the man behind the counter let him stand there, which is a thing a man does.**

When he did speak, it was not a question.

“**I want to tell you what is in a frame four streets and four flights down from me, and I want you to look in that book, and I am not asking you for anything when I say it.**”

Tarrant put the pen down that time.

“**Go on.**”

“**A fixed stripped survey carrier. Waist high on a mount, in a mounting frame, in a padded cradle with four bolts made fast. The coupling on the swing is cut nearly through and there is a thin copper jumper soldered across it from the inside. There is a wax handprint in the crown the size of a child's and the lip is warped.**”

He stopped.

“**I have had that frame since the morning the carrier came into a yard in a highland town, which is a hundred and seven days, and I have never once been inside the cradle and I am not going to be. I am telling you the outside of a thing because you keep a book about what goes out of this room and I want to know what went out of it for it.**”

“**That is not nothing,**” Tarrant said. “**That is a great deal to ask a man who writes a book for a district. Most of what comes down those four flights to this counter is a number and a man who wants the number.**”

---

## And then the book, and it took as long as it takes to find a line in a book of about nine hundred.

He did not open the wall. He turned the book.

That is the thing, and Tomas Vale wrote it out flat afterwards and it is the finding of the hundred and seventh: **the most guarded room he has ever stood in, in this kingdom, keeps the one book in it that is open, and the wall behind the man is shut because a wall can be shut and a book cannot, and both of those are correct, and there is no clause anywhere that requires either one to be different.**

“**You are going to want to know why I can do that,**” Tarrant said, without looking up. “**So I will tell you why in one sentence, and then you can read it yourself and I will go back to my line. The district's business in this room is to know what went out and when. That is not a thing a district has any reason to hide, because a book that says what went out is a book about objects, and a book about objects cannot be against anybody.**”

“**And nobody is looking at it.**”

“**Nobody is looking at it because it is a book of objects, and there are about nine hundred of them a year, and the man who could use this book is a man with an instruction in his hand, and there has not been a man with an instruction in his hand in this room for about four days.**”

He turned a page. He turned another. He ran a finger down a column the way a man does who has read the same nine hundred lines nine hundred times and could find a thing in it with his eyes shut and does not enjoy the party.

“**There. Twenty-two back, and it is the only one of about nine hundred that says what you have just said to me.**”

---

## And the line is this, and there are five things in it and four of them are printed:

> *4, 1 8 2 — **Netherby** — the eleventh — **coupling***

“**Netherby,**” Tarrant said. “**Not this district. That is a store number in this room and it went out on somebody else's instruction, and the district on that line is not the district this room is in, and if you want to know that they do not even send a repair back where it came from, I have never known one to.**”

“**How many districts.**”

“**I do not know, and I have never been told, and I would not go and find out. There is a number at the top of an instruction and a district on it and a day on it and that is the whole of what a sheet carries.**”

He put the pen back in the drawer, which in that room is a thing a man does at the end of a sentence and not the end of a day.

“**And the number.**”

“**Four one eight two.**”

“**That is not a man.**”

“**No. A number is not a man and I have had about nine hundred of them come out of that wall in nine years and not one of them has come back and told me anything at all, and you are the first person in this room in nine years to ask me what one of them was for.**”

He shut the drawer.

“**I will tell you the one thing I know about a number and then I am going to write. It goes to the district. And if the district ever enters it as a matter, it is entered as a matter, and the courts keep their matters by the number and not by anybody's name. **That is all a number is. It is a place a thing is filed at.**”

---

**And that is the whole of the hundred and seventh as far as the store is concerned, and it is four inches long, and it is the smallest thing anybody has given him in a hundred and seven days, and he carried it down four flights like a man carrying a cup of water full to the brim.**

> *A reference going somewhere is not a person. **A store number is a place a thing is filed at, and a matter number is the same number in a different room, and a man who has been carrying a number about four counties and seventy miles has been carrying a filing reference and calling it a man with no face.**
>
> *I have been three days in this city looking for a face. **There is a number on a line in a book and the line says a district I have never stood in, and the district has a court, and the court keeps its matters by the number and not by anybody's name.** That is the way in. It is not a door and nobody opened it for me and it cost me nothing, and a man told me it in one sentence at a counter and then put his pen in a drawer, and he is right, and I am not going to be thanked, and he is not going to know he did it.*

---

**And there was a second thing he wanted in that room and he did not ask for it, and it is on the page because a man has to write down the thing he did not do or he will do it in a month and call it new.**

The word in the fourth column on that line was *coupling*, and he had guessed it right, and he had been carrying the guessing since the first week, and it had got him a word in a column and nothing else on earth.

**And the word was *coupling*, and the coupling is the thing that is cut nearly through in a frame he has never been inside, and so either the man who fitted that jumper in another county put a word about a piece of metal into a district's book, or he put in a word that means the whole job. And there is no column in nine hundred lines that says which, and there is not one person in this kingdom who could tell me, and one of them is behind this counter and has told me twice in nine years that he is not going to try.**

“**You are going to ask me what that word is for.**”

“**I have not asked you.**”

“**You have not, and I have watched you not ask it for about two minutes, and I want you to know that I noticed, and I am going to say one thing and then I am going to write, and then we are both going to pretend this morning was about a shelf.**

“**The reason I am not going to help you is that it is the fourth time in nine years and I have said no three times and the answer is the same every time, and the answer is not mine. **You want a man in a book to put a word in a column that says what a thing is for, and I have not been told what the column is for, and if you make me put something in it then in four years there will be a man at this counter reading it and it will be in my hand, and he will be asked what I meant by it, and I will be in this room, and I will not be able to stand in front of him and say I put it there, because I will not have known.**”

He wrote a figure in a column.

“**Now go down your four flights. And take the book with you, which you cannot, because it is nine hundred lines and it weighs a great deal more than a barrow.**”

---

He went down. He was on the step at the foot of the stair at about the seventh hour with the tarpaulin over the cradle and the four bolts made fast where they were made fast at the fourth hour, and the sack rolled against the wheel, and the frame had not been in a building since the thirty-third and had been thirty feet off him in the open all day.

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening he wrote the last entry of the hundred and seventh on that step and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.**

> *The hundred and seventh. **The store is a room, a counter, a book, a man, a shelf and an instruction, and I have now been in it three times and the only one of the six that has ever done anything in front of me is the book.***
>
> *I went up at the fourth hour with nothing in my hands and I asked for the shelf and I was refused in one sentence by a man who was not going to shorten it the second time, and he is right: a wall can be shut and a book cannot, and both of those are correct and I have never once been able to make them both correct at the same time and neither can he, and he has said so out loud in nine years and I have it in a book now and he does not know that either.*
>
> *Second column, and what I got. **One line, and it is four one eight two, and the district on it is Netherby and the day is the eleventh and the word is coupling. That is all.** And a man told me in one sentence that a number is a place a thing is filed at, and that a district's courts keep their matters by the number and not by anybody's name, and he put his pen in the drawer afterwards like a man closing a door he is not being paid for. **A reference going somewhere is not a person, and that is a smaller thing than anything I have been carrying, and it is the way into the next four days, and it cost me nothing at all.**
>
> *And the thing I did not do, which is the whole of the day. **I did not ask him what the word is for.** He said the words *you are going to ask me what that word is for* before I said anything, and I had been carrying the wanting for six days, and the reason I did not ask is the reason I wrote out flat on the hundred and first: **a man asked that has three answers and all three of them cost him something.** He can say *I do not know*, and that is a thing he will have said with his own book open in front of him. He can say *that is for the district*, and that is a form of words with nothing in it and I have read it off about nine hundred men in four counties. And he cannot say the true one, because there is no true one. So I would have been handed a sentence to carry four hundred miles, and the sentence would have been false, or empty, or a man standing in a room wishing he had not been asked. **None of those three is worth a man losing his morning over, and I have known that for six days and I came up the four flights anyway.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. **The word is coupling, and I guessed it right.** a hundred and seven days of a jumper soldered across a cut that a man in a smithy at a crossroads fitted with the same hand in three counties, and the district's own book calls it a coupling, and that is the entire reward. It is a word about a piece of metal or it is a word about a whole job and there is no column in nine hundred lines that says which, **and I have found out that being right about a thing is worth nothing in this kingdom, and that is the first time in a hundred and seven days that being right has cost me instead of being worth something, and I am going to have to unlearn something I have had since I was nine.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, and nothing shown to me, and no shelf opened, and no word said to me that I did not have already.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been inside a building since the thirty-third and I was in three this week and it makes no difference to the frame, because there is no clause about where a man may be and one about where a cradle may be. The carrier in the sack untouched, thirty days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents in a coat, and a store list that is a book of objects and is looked at and is not taken, and a number I did not write down because it is in my head and it is in nine hundred lines on a wall, and those three figures are correct and none of them is a fourteenth.**
>
> *And the five dates in a drawer in a county I have left are where they were, and I have not touched them in a hundred and seven days and I am not going to touch them this month, and nobody in this city has asked me about them and nobody is going to.*
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a book that is open and a wall that is shut, and a number that goes to a district I have never stood in, and a court in that district that keeps its matters by the number. That is the whole of the new thing and it is four inches long.** I have a district and a matter number and nothing in my hands, and tomorrow I am going to find out whether a person who is not a party to a matter may read the record of it, and if a man with no standing is refused there then I have come four hundred miles to be told no in a new building, and if he is not refused then something in this kingdom has no box on it that I have not found yet, **and either of those is a thing I can stand on a step about tonight.**
