# Chapter 0110 — The Third Terrace

He got up to the top terrace of Sennet at about the fourth hour of the ninety-sixth day of the month and left the barrow at the foot of the stair with the four bolts made fast and the frame in its cradle, because the stair was about four flights and a barrow is not a thing a man takes up four flights, and nobody asked him to and nobody stopped him and he wrote that down because it is the first thing in eleven weeks that he has done in a public place in this city and nobody has made a thing of it.

The top terrace is not a street of shops. **It is four long buildings, a rail, a yard, and a bell.** The bell is on a frame over a gate about nine feet high and it is about as big as a cartwheel, and there is a bench under it, and about two hundred people were standing about in the yard at about the sixth hour because it was the hour the bell went and about two hundred people in a city of about nine thousand had come out of doors to hear whether it would.

**He had not known they would come out. That is on the page and it is the finding of the ninety-sixth and he did not have it in the morning.**

A man in a crown yard has about nine hundred things to do and he does not walk into a yard. A man in a town of two thousand walks into a market. **A man in a city of about nine thousand with a bell over his own gate comes out into his own yard at the hour the bell goes, because in a place like this the hour is the thing the day is built out of, and he has been doing it for a very long time and nobody has ever told him there is anything else.**

---

It rang at about the sixth hour.

Not the way a market bell rings when a man pulls a rope and is pleased with himself. **It rang four times, at intervals, and the yard did not make a noise, and about two hundred people stood in it and let it.**

Then a shutter came up on the far building and a man came out and turned a plate over on a board, and about two hundred people read it, and Tomas Vale read it too from the bottom of the stair, about nine flights of distance and one street off, which is close enough for a shape and not close enough for words.

And then, about four minutes after that, he got up the last flight and onto the terrace and read it at about nine feet.

---

It was the ordinary shape. He has read about nine hundred of them in four counties and he can tell a sheet of paper by its head from forty feet.

> *NOTICE. Public. Sennet, top district.*
>
> *By the fourth hour this day, at the office of the Quiet Office, Sennet, there is to be produced by the person named: a paper or instrument of which that person is the keeper, of a kind used to repeat an answer, being of the kinds entered in the store list of the district.*
>
> *The person named has no standing entered in this district or in any district. This notice is posted and is not carried.*
>
> *The store list of the district is open to inspection at the counter on the third hour of the day named in the hearing notice posted at the foot of this stair.*
>
> *By order. A district of the Quiet Office. [number]*

---

He stood under the gate frame and read it twice and then he did the thing that the whole of the ninety-sixth is.

**He looked for the name.**

It is on the notice. It is the third line down and it is his own, spelled the way it is spelled on the L-4 schedule of a district in another county, and there is nothing above it and nothing below it and no district and no form and no hand.

**And the notice is the ordinary shape, and it is correct, and every word of it is true, and a man in an arch four streets and four flights below me said yesterday at about the sixth hour that about four thousand people a year bring him a paper and that the reason nobody has ever got a form about the second kind is that everybody is frightened of it, and being frightened of it is the correct thing to be.**

**And it has taken me ninety-six days to find out that the reason there is no form about the second kind is not that everybody is frightened of it. It is that a form is a thing you fill in with a reason, and there is no reason anybody is allowed to write, and I have had eleven weeks of finding out that a reason is the hardest thing in this kingdom to get anybody to put their name to.**

---

**And there was no such thing as the Office.**

He had carried a question for ten days. She had put it to him on a road in the rain, at ten feet off his left shoulder, in the ordinary voice, and he had answered it in four words and had not improved it in the morning, and the four words were *nobody has told me*.

**Nobody has told him, because there is nobody to tell him.**

There are about nine hundred rooms. In this city there is a store on this terrace with a counter and a book and a man; in a market town of about two thousand there is a sheet on a table and two sheets in a court; in a village of about two hundred there is a man with about nine shillings a quarter and a book and nine years; in a town of eleven hundred there is a board under a roof with about two hundred and forty things in a column; and in a town of about nine hundred there is a board with a roof and a leaf pinned to it at an angle by a woman who is inside all nine hundred of them.

**And the same six words on the same kind of sheet at the head of all of them, and a number, and a date, and a form with four boxes and none of them the words.**

*A name is a thing you can be refused. A hall is not a person.* He has that in the second column of the fortieth, and he carried it eleven days and made it into a key and it was not a key, and it is true, and it is not the answer to this.

> ***The Office is a format.***
>
> *I have been looking for a room with a man in it for ninety-six days and there is no room. There is a heading, and a number, and a date, and a form, and about nine hundred copies of the form, and a man behind each counter who is competent and right and does not want anything and goes home at the sixth hour. **And a format does not have a door, and a format cannot be refused, and it cannot be asked a question, and it cannot be argued with in a market, and it does not have a face, and I have spent eleven weeks learning how to ask a person and be refused, and I have got here, and there is nothing at the end of it to ask.***
>
> *And a name is a thing you can be refused. I wrote that on the fortieth and I have carried it fifty-six days and it is a true sentence about a name and it is not a true sentence about an office, and **that is the difference between the door in Miremarket and this, and the door in Miremarket opened because a man in a room was willing to be asked, and this does not open because there is nobody in it to be asked.***
>
> *And I do not know how an office gets into a bell. I wrote that on the forty-second day of the month in a district office in a real town, in my own hand, and I have carried it fifty-four days, and I have got nearer it than I have ever been, and I am not going to write that I have got it, because **a store is a counter and a book and a man and a shelf, and I have not been upstairs and I have not seen a shelf and I have not seen the thing itself, and the man at the counter told me it is not his business and he was right and I have no standing in that building and a form in my coat that I cannot fill in.** I have got a description of a thing that says it for you. That is the fourth time in ninety-six days that I have written down the name of a thing I have not seen and I am not going to do it a fifth time without seeing it.*

---

**And there was a second leaf on the Office's own board.**

It is under a roof, at the end of the terrace, with about nine sheets on it, and eight of them are printed, and one of them is a leaf of paper folded once and pinned at an angle under a drawing-pin, and it is the only thing on that board that anybody has put there with their own hands.

He read it at about nine feet and he did not touch it and he did not go through the gate.

> *Read the store list. Not the room.*

Six words. **Her t, which she does not cross. The space she leaves after a full stop. The downstroke on the g that stops and starts.**

And on the back of it, in the same hand, a date and a store number, and the date is the third day of the month following, and the number is the number on the slip in his coat.

**Which means she knows the number of the store and she knows the day of the hearing and she has put both on a public board in a capital city in a street about ninety feet from the gate of the building, and the gate was not shut, and the gate is not guarded, and there is a man in the arch at the foot of the stair who would have walked a stranger up four flights and did not, and I have been in this city about a day and a half.**

He stood in the yard at about the ninth foot from the board for about four minutes.

**And then he turned round and went down the four flights, and that is the whole of the ninety-sixth and it took him about four minutes, and I am not going to say that I did not want to go in, because I wanted to go in more than I have wanted anything since the seventy-seventh, and a man who is going into a building at a quarter to seven in the evening with a hearing in seven days and no paper to produce and a woman inside it is not doing anything to the woman.**

---

He wrote the last entry of the ninety-sixth at about the ninth hour of the evening, on a step at the foot of the top terrace, in a city of about nine thousand people, with the frame four feet off him and the four bolts made fast, and he filled the first and third columns and left the second and the fourth as they were.

> *The ninety-sixth. **The Office is a format and it does not have a door.***
>
> *A bell over a gate about nine feet high, about as big as a cartwheel, four rings at the sixth hour, and about two hundred people in a yard of a city of about nine thousand who came out of doors to hear it and did not make a noise. I have never in thirty-three years stood in a place where bells were not sounding and were not broken. **They were waiting. Nine hundred of them in this city and about nine hundred hours and about nine hundred men and about nine hundred reasons, and every one of the reasons is written down in a room.** A man in a town of two thousand walks into a market. A man in a city of nine thousand comes out into his own yard at the hour. That is not a fact about bells and I have got it exactly wrong and I am writing it down wrong on purpose so that I have to look at it again.*
>
> ***And the third question, and it is the answer and it is worse, and I am writing it flat: there is no such thing as the Office. There are about nine hundred rooms and one format, and the format is a heading and a number and a date and a form with four boxes and none of them the words, and it has a store on this terrace with a counter and a book and a man and a shelf, and it has a store eleven miles from a ford in a village of two hundred, and it has a board under a roof in a town of eleven hundred, and it has a board at the foot of this stair with my name at the third line. Nobody has told me which office it is because **nobody can, and the question is not about a room, and I have been asking it wrong for ten days and for eighty-six before that.** A name is a thing you can be refused and a format is not a name.*
>
> *And the notice on this board, and it is the ordinary shape, and it is correct, and every word of it is true: **they want a paper or instrument of which I am the keeper, of a kind used to repeat an answer.** And a clerk at a counter four streets and four flights below me said yesterday at about the sixth hour that about four thousand people a year bring him a paper and ask what it is and not one of them has ever asked him for the second kind, and that they are all frightened of it, and that being frightened of it is the correct thing to be. **And I have got here and the Office has written a notice about the second kind on a board in the open, and it did not hide it, and it did not have to, and I want both of those in one sentence and I am not going to separate them.** I have been eleven weeks in this kingdom with a bell in a bag and a barrow and a third question and I have felt as though I had a thing nobody else had. There is no thing. **There is a sheet of paper with a number at the top of it and a man who is not frightened of it and four flights of stairs.**
>
> *Second column, and it is the finding and I am not going to improve it in the morning: **I do not know how an office gets into a bell.** I wrote that on the forty-second day of the month in a district office in a real town in my own hand, and I have carried it fifty-four days, and on the ninety-fifth a clerk told me the second kind of paper is a fitting or an instrument or a box with a handle, issued out of a store against a district instruction with a number, and **that when it is fitted the answer is made for you and the answer it makes is in your own voice and it is not a lie.** That is the nearest I have been. It is not it. **A description of a thing is not a thing, and I have written down the name of four things I have not seen in ninety-six days and I am not going to do it a fifth time without seeing one, and the store list is open at the counter on the third hour of the day named in the hearing notice, and that is seven days off and I have got a form I cannot fill in and a slip I cannot use and no standing, and I am going to stand at that counter and ask about a list and it is going to be a person behind it, and that is all I have and it is not nothing and it is not a door.***
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the day, and it is four lines and I am not going to put more under it than is there: **a second leaf, on the Office's own board, in a street about ninety feet from the gate, pinned at an angle under a drawing-pin, and six words on it, and a date and a store number on the back.** Read the store list. Not the room. **She is inside it. She has been inside it for the whole of ninety-six days and she has answered twice, both times on a public board, both times for nothing, and both times in a direction that is a room with about nine hundred copies of itself in it.** And the gate was not shut and the gate is not guarded and there is a man at the foot of the stair who would have walked a stranger up four flights and did not and told me why in four seconds and was right.
>
> *And I did not go in. I stood at about nine feet from a board for about four minutes and I turned round and went down four flights, and it took about four minutes, and **I am not going to write down that I did not want to go in, because I wanted to go in more than I have wanted anything since the seventy-seventh and a man who walks into a building at a quarter to seven in the evening with a hearing in seven days and no paper to produce, about a woman who has told him twice to ask the board, is not doing anything to the woman.** In Miremarket I went back to a door after a household had refused me twice and I was refused a third time and it cost that household the front of a row of nine pitches. **I have had six weeks of learning the difference between asking and being owed, and I do not have the standing to ask after her, and the notice on that board says so in the fourth line, in about eleven words, under my own name, and the Office is right and I have no answer to it.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is not a room. It is about nine hundred rooms, and there is no such office as one office, and I have been asking the question wrong since the thirty-second day of the month, and the answer I gave her on the eighty-sixth was correct and it was the answer to a question about a building and the question was never about a building.** A format does not have a door and cannot be refused and I have spent ninety-six days learning how to ask a person and being refused and I have got to the end of the road and there is nothing at the end of it to ask. **And that is worse, and it is not a defeat, and nobody in this city is wicked, and about four thousand people a year take a paper to a counter and are frightened of the second kind, and being frightened of it is the correct thing to be, and nobody has ever written a form about it.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing was rung in ninety-six days.** Four bolts, made fast at every place the frame has been set down since the thirty-third, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, and it stood in an arch and on a wharf and at the foot of a stair in a city of about nine thousand people and none of those is a building. The bell in the sack is untouched since the seventy-seventh and I do not know where either of my two ringings went and I am not going to be told and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents, unchanged, and a printed slip in a coat that is the Office's, and a form in a coat that I cannot fill in, and a leaf on two boards that I have not taken and am not going back for, and a hearing on the third day of the month following at the second hour at the upper room of a building about ninety feet from a board where the woman inside it has told me twice to ask the board.**
>
> *And a woman ten feet off my left shoulder, reading it from behind me, the way she has done for eleven years, with her chin and not her eyes. And the fourth column has two lines in it and she has not improved them and I have not asked her what they say, and I have wanted to ask her what they say every hour since the eighty-third and I am not going to, and **that is the only thing I have got in ninety-six days that I have not had before, and it is the shape of a thing and not a thing, and I am not going to call it progress and I am not going to write a paragraph about it.***
