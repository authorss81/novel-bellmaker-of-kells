# Chapter 0103 — The Same Hand

The smithy at the crossroads was a building with one wall of open arch and a fire in it and a smell that Tomas Vale had been away from for seven weeks and had not known he was missing until about the fourth hour of the eighty-ninth day of the month.

The smith was called **Doss Harrow** and he was working a piece of flat iron when the barrow came into the arch and stopped, and he did not look up, and he said:

“**If you have come about the wall clock I am not doing it this month. If you have come about the church bell you are about four miles wrong and the church has no bell.**”

“I have come about a bell on a post at a crossing,” said Tomas Vale.

Doss Harrow put the iron down.

“**Then put the barrow outside the arch and do not bring that frame in here, and I want to say that I said it and not that I would want to. There is nothing in my yard that can hold that and I have had one come through that arch in about four years and it went out through the same arch about a minute later and did not stop.**”

“It does not leave the cradle.”

“**Then it can stand in the arch and I will come out to it.**”

---

He came out to it, and he looked at it for about four seconds, and then he went back in and got a lamp and came back out, and that is on the page because Tomas Vale wrote it down and it is the first thing anybody has done on this road that he had not expected.

“**Right. I can see the frame and I can see the cradle and I can see the four bolts and I am not going to ask you about any of that. I am going to ask one question and you may say no and I will not come back.**”

“Ask it.”

“**Who carried it.**”

“An officer of a county I have left, on the thirty-third day of the month, in a watch house, at about the fifth hour of the morning, and he read out five clauses and I asked for one of them and he wrote it in.”

“**A man who asks a clerk to add a clause.**” Harrow took the lamp in his other hand. “**I have heard of about two of those in thirty-one years and one of them was me and I got the wording wrong.**”

---

Then he said the thing that made the morning, and he said it standing at the arch with the lamp in his hand and he did not go near the frame at all.

“**There is work in this cradle that I did not do and I have been looking at it since you came into the arch, and I am going to tell you what it is and I am going to tell you what I am not going to tell you, and then you can decide what to do with the rest of the day.**”

“Go on.”

“**A coupling on the swing, cut nearly through. And a thin copper jumper laid across the cut, and the jumper is soldered at both ends and it is not a patch anybody makes in a hurry because it is too neat, and it is done from the inside, which is the only way you can do a proper one.**”

Tomas Vale had to put a hand on the barrow.

“**And that is all there is in that frame that I can see, and I have not touched it, and I am not going to touch it, and I want it entered that I said I would not.**”

“That is the whole of it.”

“**That is the whole of what I can see. And I have fitted the same hand of work to three bells in this county and I will tell you about all three and you may write them down.**”

---

**The three bells.**

“**A market bell at Marrow Bridge, about five years back. Same coupling, same jumper, and the man who came had a sheet with a number at the top of it and he did not give it to me and he read the number out twice and I wrote it down wrong the first time and he read it out a third time and that is the only time in thirty-one years that a man has read me a number three times.**”

“**A bridge bell at Pyle, about two years back. Same coupling, same jumper, and that one was a different man and he was in a hurry and he left, and I never saw the sheet and I have thought about that about once a fortnight for two years.**”

“**And a water bell. That one is not a bell anybody can get at, it is on a sill in a village and I went out about nine years back and it was the coupling again and the jumper again, and I have not told anybody I went out to that one and I am telling you and I would like it noticed that I said that.**”

He put the lamp down on the sill.

“**That is three. And I have not seen a fourth and I am not going to pretend I have, and I have not had a fourth in my head at night, and that is the truth.**”

---

“**Now. What I am not going to tell you.**”

“All right.”

“**I am not going to tell you what it does, because I do not know what it does, and I have had a working theory for about five years and I have not tested it and I am not going to hand a theory to a man with a barrow who is going to write it in a book and take it four hundred miles.**”

“That is fair.”

“**And I am not going to tell you who fitted them, because a sheet with a number at the top of it is not a person, and if I had said a person to you this morning you would have gone looking for the person, and there is not one. There is a sheet and a number and a man who comes and does it, and the man who comes is not the same man twice.**”

“Then where does a sheet with a number come from.”

And Doss Harrow said the sentence that the whole of the road is standing on, in about four seconds, at a smithy arch, and he said it without any weight at all.

“**From a store. There is a store. Every district has got one and ours is in Ashmoor and it is a room with a counter in it and a book on the counter and a man behind the counter, and you go in with a district instruction and he gives you the thing out of a shelf and writes what he gave you in the book, and if you are the sort of person who wants to know what a thing is for then there is a column on the form for that and nobody has ever filled it in in my hearing.**”

---

He wrote it down standing at the arch, on a scrap of a slate off the top of the wall, and he read it back to the smith before he put it away, which is a thing he has done four times in six weeks and which is in the book four times.

“**Read it again.**

“*Ashmoor. A store. A counter, a book, a man, a shelf, and an instruction, and a column for what a thing is for that nobody has ever filled in in his hearing.*”

“**That is right. Do not go and ask him what the column is for.**”

“Why not.”

“**Because it is his book and not his answer and because a man at a counter will give you a thing in about nine seconds and he will not give you a reason in nine minutes, and if you ask him for the reason in front of other people he will be right and you will be the one who has spent the morning.**”

---

He left Ashmoor about the seventh hour and the road went on inland and up, and he wrote the last entry of the eighty-ninth at about the ninth hour of the evening, on a wall at the fourth milestone out of Ashmoor, with the frame four feet off him and the four bolts made fast, and the second half of that day is the part of it he wrote out flat because it is the finding of ten days and it took a smith to say it.

> *The eighty-ninth. **The same hand.***
>
> *In a yard behind a draper's shop in a town four days behind me a Crown carrier sat in a reinforced padded cradle with a coupling cut nearly through and a thin copper jumper soldered across it, done from the inside, too neat for a patch in a hurry. And a smith at a crossroads about eleven miles up this road has fitted the same hand of work to three bells in this county over nine years, and two men came with a sheet with a number at the top of it and one of them read the number out three times.*
>
> *And I have been carrying that repair for about ten days as a **who**, and it is not a who. It is a **where from.***
>
> ***There is a store. Every district has one. Ours is in Ashmoor and it is a room with a counter and a book and a man and a shelf, and you go in with a district instruction, and he gives you the thing out of the shelf and writes in a book what he gave you, and there is a column on the form for what the thing is for and nobody has ever filled it in in his hearing.*** That is the whole of it. That is what a smith with thirty-one years said at an arch in about four seconds and did not put any weight on at all, and it is a smaller thing than a man in a room with a plan in it and it is nine days up the road and I could walk to it and I have not walked to it and I am not going to today.*
>
> *And I have got to be honest about what it does and I am not, and I am going to write that down before I get the chance to improve it: **I do not know what a coupling and a jumper do. I have looked at one for ten days. I have a bell in a cradle that has no clapper in it and a receipt that forbids anybody to put one in, and I have not once asked what the fitting is for, because I have been asking who cut the coupling and not what the cut is.** That is a whole trade's worth of not asking and I have been in it for thirty-three years and Sabin Fell would have had my hands for it.*
>
> *Second column, and this is the one I am going to be careful with. **I have not got nearer how an office gets into a bell. I have got a shop.** A store is not a mechanism and a store is not an office and a smith who has fitted three of these in nine years does not know what they do and told me so to my face in four seconds and would not hand me a theory. **And I said no to the theory in about a second and a half and I want it written down that I said no and that I did not want it, and that is the first time in six weeks I have refused a thing that would have been easier.***
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and I am merging nothing: **the sheet has a number on it and a number is a filing reference and not a man, and I have now heard that sentence from a district officer in another county, out of a clerk in a hall, and out of a smith at an arch, and it has been on my page for forty-seven days and this morning it turned out to be the answer to a question and not a dead end.** A man who cannot be found is a different thing from a counter that can be stood at.*
>
> *All five clauses stand. Nothing was rung. Four bolts, and I stood it in an arch for about a minute and a half and it did not leave the cradle and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is not going to be today. The bell in the sack untouched. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents and a slate and a scrap of a slate off a wall at a smithy which is not a document and is not going in the coat.** And a woman ten feet off my shoulder who said three sentences at a ford yesterday and has not said anything since and is not going to be asked.*
