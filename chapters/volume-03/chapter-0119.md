# Chapter 0119 — The Thing That Was For A Clerk And Not For The Man

The middle terrace market was doing what a market of about nine hundred people does at about the fourth hour of the hundred and fifth day of the month, which is nothing at all, and then at about the fifth hour it did the other thing, and about four hundred of them were standing about in a queue that was not a queue saying names back to each other.

**Nobody in that market had heard of a hearing in the upper room on this terrace two days ago. He asked nine people between the fourth hour and the fifth hour and eight of them said *what hearing* and the ninth said *there is one, there is always one*, and neither of those answers was wrong, and there had not been about four hundred people in that market at the fifth hour on the ninety-second either.**

**A thing that happened in a room in a capital does not travel to a market in a capital. It is a room, and it is four streets off, and a market is three streets off in the other direction, and the paper that came out of the room went into a file, and a file does not walk.**

And four hundred people were saying names back to each other in a queue anyway, in the ordinary voice, at about nine feet, and about nine of them had sheets, and it was the third time in a hundred and five days he had watched a market do that and he stood at the edge of it for about an hour and did not go in and did not write down one name.

---

**And at about the fifth hour a market keeper with about nine rows and a book of his own and no office told a stranger what it costs to be named, and told it about somebody else, and Tomas Vale stood there and let him.**

That is the whole of the first half of this chapter and there is nothing in it that has to be explained twice.

The man was **Ord Marren** and he had nine rows in that market and had held them nine years and he had a book of his own and no office, and he had a piece of paper in his coat with nine words at the top of it and his own name at the head of it, which Tomas Vale did not know about and did not ask about and would have recognised in a court on the middle terrace a day later.

“**There was a man at a board in a market four days back,**” Marren said, “**with a notice in his own name on it, and his name at the third line, and about two hundred people read it. That is all I know and I have not been there and I do not know the town.**”

“**What did it cost him.**”

“**About four hundred people read it, and about nine of them will say it in a different town inside a year, and the price of that is he cannot un-name himself. **There is no form anywhere in this kingdom by which a man who has been named on a board can be unnamed, and there is no clerk who can do it, and I have looked, and looking is what I do.**”

He put a thumb on his own book.

“**And I will tell you the part nobody says. **The reading is the cheap part.** Two hundred people reading a sheet in a market costs a district about fourpence. What costs is the about nine. A man who says a name about four times inside a year in four towns has made it into a thing, and there is no undoing it, and every one of the about nine gets it right the first time and gets it more wrong after that, and in about two years there is a version of it that has a detail in it that was never on the sheet, and by then nobody remembers which of them added it.**”

“**Nine.**”

“**Four in ten of a village does not come to a hall when a bell says there is a hall. That is a man I know, four counties back, and it is the same arithmetic and it is the only arithmetic in this business.**”

---

**And Tomas Vale stood at the edge of a man's nine rows in a market of about nine hundred people and understood every word of it, and did not say one word, and did not correct him, and did not thank him, and did not tell him that the man he was talking about was standing four feet off him with a barrow.**

**Because a stranger who is told *there was a man at a board in a market four days back* can say *was it you* and can go and find out and can be reported. And a man who tells a stranger a true thing about a man he has not met, in the ordinary voice, in a market, and does not point, is doing the only thing in this kingdom that can be done for a person at no cost to anybody, and it is on the list with everything else that cannot be done twice by one man.**

**And he is going to be told, later, by somebody else, that a man in a market told him. And he is not going to be told it was a kindness, because it was not a kindness, it was a man talking in a market, and it is going to be on the page as that and not as anything else.**

---

**And then he went and read the record of his own hearing, at a court on the middle terrace, standing up, in about eleven minutes.**

The court publishes. That is the whole of what happened in Chapter 0118's last paragraph and it is the only reason he could do this at all: a record of what a court has done is open to any person who asks for it, at a counter, in the ordinary way, and about four hundred people a year go into that room and have a sentence of their own written down, and about four thousand people a year take a paper to a counter at the foot of the top terrace and ask what it is, **and not one of the nine clerks at that counter has ever been asked by any of the four thousand for the record of a hearing they were not in. Not once. He asked a man in an arch on the ninety-fifth whether anybody is frightened of the second kind of paper, and the answer was that about four thousand people a year are frightened of it and that being frightened of it is the correct thing to be. Here is what he is also frightened of, and it is a sheet of paper they may all have in their hands at any hour of any day, and nobody has ever thought to ask for one.**

> *An office files. A court publishes. **The difference between those two words is four inches long and it is the only thing in a hundred and five days that anybody in this kingdom has got wrong in their own favour, and they have got it wrong on purpose, and I do not think anybody in the Quiet Office has ever been told it is a choice.** A form has four boxes. One of them is a day. **There is no box for *who may read this*, and the absence of that box is the whole of the coercion and it is not in a room, it is in a form, and it took a hundred and five days and a city to find it.**

---

**It is about four hundred words.** He read it standing up at a rail in a public room and he read all of it and it is in the hand of the office and it is about him and nobody else.

It has a heading and a date and a room. It has the name of the person, and the name is his own, spelled the way it is spelled on an L-4 schedule in a county he has left.

It has the matter: *a claim made in public in the hundred of Wendle and Ashmoor by the person named, that a certain child was taken.*

It has a paragraph about the notice and about the posting, and it is correct, and it quotes the line about a person having no such paper, and it sets out that the office had not been able to serve the notice for the reason given, and it is four lines and every word of it is true.

**And it has the answer he gave, which is a paragraph, and the paragraph is the office's account of what he said and not his words.**

**And then it has the last line, and the last line is the finding of the hundred and fifth day and it is nine words long:**

> *The person named produced nothing.*

---

**And then he stood at a rail in a public room for about a minute and a half and did not move, and here is what is in the four hundred words and here is what is not in them, and he has written both halves out flat because the second half is the one he could not have got to on his own in a year.**

> *The record does not say why he produced nothing. It does not repeat the four lines of the notice. It does not say that he was not required to account for the absence of a paper, and it does not say that nothing in his possession was to be taken from him, and it does not say that the capacity assigned to him was a capacity assigned by the office and not one he holds. **Four protections, all of them printed on a nail in a market in a town two days' road back, and not one of them is in the file.** The sheet in the street protects a man. The record in the file does not, and the record is the one that goes to Ashmoor in a barrow, and there is nothing in it that says the man in it was owed anything.
>
> ***And it does not say what happened either. It is four hundred words of the shape of a room and not one word of what was in it.*** He stood nine feet off a door for four minutes yesterday and did not go in, and there is a man in that building who put his own hand against a line on a public wall about what he did in it, **and the file four streets from me has four hundred words and not one of them would let a stranger find the hand.**

---

**And he did not copy it, and he did not ask for a copy, and he did not leave the room with anything.**

**A record of a hearing is a public document. It belongs to the court and it is on a rail in a public room and any person may read it at any hour. It is not evidence. It is not in his coat. The number of non-bell documents he carries is thirteen this evening and it was thirteen on the ninety-fifth, and those are two different objects and a man who puts a court record in a coat is a man who has mistaken a thing he may read for a thing he has got, and that is the mistake this whole book has been about for a hundred and five days.**

---

**And a letter came at about the seventh hour, and it was eleven days late, and it was four lines, and it was not a charge.**

> *To the bellhouse, Kells, and to any hand that is at the barrow, and it is eleven days on the road and I am sorry for that and I would do it again.*
>
> *The yard is as it was. Nothing has been taken out of it and nothing has been put in. I go in on the sixteenth and I have told the one man who asked me that I would tell nobody anything about the yard and he said that was the correct arrangement.*
>
> *The Crown notice about the originating workshop is still on the ward board. The parish has painted round it. **It is the only painted thing on that board and it is the ugliest thing on it and I have looked at it every morning for eleven days and I have not stopped looking at it.**
>
> *A district sent me a paper in the winter asking for the making of that bell and I sent them the certificate and nothing has come back. I have not chased it and I am not going to chase it, and I want that in a letter to you and not to anybody else, because you would chase it.*
>
> *I have not rung anything. I have not gone in. I will not without you.*

It is signed with a hand that is not his and it is not signed with a name, and it is the fourth person in a hundred and five days who has written to him or spoken to him about a thing in a county he has left and asked for nothing.

**Nobody has asked him to read a charge, and there is a reading of a charge in a volume that is not this one, and a man in Kells who reads a thing for himself and does not let anybody do it for him, and that is not this chapter.**

He put the letter in the coat with the thirteen and did not count it again and wrote down that it is not a fourteenth because it is not evidence of anything and it is a letter to a man and not a record of a fact, and that is a distinction he is drawing for himself and has not got to the bottom of and is not going to tonight.

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step at the foot of the top terrace with about nine hundred bells in this city and not one of them going.**

**And in a file in a room four streets below there are four hundred words in the office's own hand, and the last line of them is that the person named produced nothing, and it is the only paper in this kingdom that anybody could put on a table and say *this man*, and he has wanted one for about ten days, and it is an inventory of his own hands, and it does not clear him and it does not condemn him and it does not say he was owed anything, and it is the only document about Tomas Vale that exists in the world, and it was written by somebody who has never seen him.**

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and fifth at about the ninth hour of the evening and he filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and fifth. **A court publishes and an office files, and the difference is four inches long, and it is the only thing anybody in this kingdom has got wrong in their own favour, and I do not think anybody has ever been told it is a choice.***
>
> *I went up to a court on the middle terrace at about the fifth hour and read the record of my own hearing standing up, in about eleven minutes, and it is about four hundred words, and it is the only paper in this kingdom that anybody could put on a table and say *this man*. **I have wanted a document of my own for about ten days and there is one and it says that the person named produced nothing.**
>
> *Second column, and the half I could not have got to in a year. **The record does not repeat one of the four protections that were printed on a nail in a market two days' road back.** It does not say a man with no paper is not required to account for the absence of one. It does not say nothing in his possession is to be taken. It does not say the capacity was assigned by the office. It does not say he was posted and not served. **The sheet in the street protects a man and the file in the building does not, and the file is the one that goes in a barrow, and there is nothing in it that says the man in it was owed anything.** And it does not say what happened either. **Four hundred words of the shape of a room and not one word of what was in it**, and a man in that building put his own hand against a line on a public wall about what he did in there, and the file four streets from me would not let a stranger find the hand.
>
> *And why I could read it at all. **Because a court is a room people are sent to and an office is a room people are kept out of, and a room people are sent to has to be a room they can be seen in.** About four hundred people a year in this city have a true sentence of their own written on that wall for fourpence and it moves nothing and it is there for forty years. About four thousand people a year take a paper to a counter at the foot of these stairs and ask what it is, **and not one clerk at that counter has ever been asked by any of the four thousand for the record of a hearing they were not in, and a clerk there told me on the ninety-fifth that all four thousand are frightened of the second kind of paper and that being frightened of it is the correct thing to be. Here is what they are frightened of and nobody has thought to ask for it: a sheet of paper any of them may have in their hands at any hour.** That is the whole of the coercion and it is not in a room, it is in a form, **and there is no box on any of them that says who may read this, and the absence of that box is a hundred and five days of work and it is four inches long.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and I am merging nothing. **A man at a market told me what it costs to be named and told it about somebody else, and I stood four feet off him and let him and did not say one word.** About two hundred people read a sheet, and about nine of them will say it in a different town inside a year, **and the reading is the cheap part at about fourpence and the cost is the about nine, and a man who says a name four times inside a year has made it into a thing, and in about two years there is a version with a detail in it that was never on the sheet, and by then nobody remembers which of them added it.** He said the man was in a market four days back and he did not know the town and he did not point, and he was not talking about a stranger. **I have been named for eleven days and a man in a market of about nine hundred people has just taught me the price of it in about four sentences, for nothing, out loud, at about nine feet, and he does not know he did it and I am not going to tell him, and I am not going to thank him, and I am not going to write a paragraph about it.** Four in ten of a village does not come to a hall when a bell says there is a hall, and it is the same arithmetic, and it is the only arithmetic in this business, and it reached me in a market this morning from a man who has never met the man at the board who counted it. **I could have gone back and asked him about it myself. I have said in this book that I will not ask him anything else this year and I meant it on the ninetieth and I mean it tonight, and the fact that I have now heard his own figure second-hand in a market is not a reason to go.**
>
> *And a letter came at about the seventh hour, eleven days late, four lines, and not a charge and not a reading. **The parish has painted round the Crown notice on the ward board and it is the ugliest thing on it.** A district asked for the making of that bell in the winter and Jerm sent the certificate and nothing has come back, and he is not going to chase it, **and he wrote that in a letter to me and not to anybody else, because he knows I would chase it, and he is right, and I have wanted to chase something for a hundred and five days.** He has not rung anything and has not gone in and will not without me. **It is the fourth thing anybody has said to me in a hundred and five days that was true and cost somebody nothing and asked for nothing at all.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and I have now been inside two buildings in this city and it makes no difference to the frame. The carrier in the sack untouched, twenty-eight days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents in a coat, and a court record that is the public's and not mine and that I stood at a rail and read in about eleven minutes and took nothing from, and those two figures are correct and they are two different objects, and a man who adds a record to a coat has mistaken a thing he may read for a thing he has got. A letter from Kells is not a fourteenth because it is not evidence of anything and it is a letter to a man.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a format, a column, a rail in a public room, and about four thousand frightened people a year, and the whole of it is four inches long and there is no box for it anywhere in this kingdom.** I have read about myself for about four hundred words and it is an inventory of my own hands, **and there is a bell in a street in this city that nobody has rung, and a frame four streets and four flights down that cannot be rung, and a hearing behind me that did not clear me and did not condemn me, and tomorrow is the last day I have got before whatever comes next, and I have a plain bell in a sack that I have not rung in twenty-eight days and a route map I have not got.**
