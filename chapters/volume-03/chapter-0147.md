# Chapter 0147 — A Room With A Form In It, And The Form Is A Copy

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and thirty-third day and Tomas Vale was on the middle terrace with about nine hundred people in the market and about two hundred of them in front of a wall, and a man in a coat came up the middle of the terrace with a printed sheet in his hand and the number at the top of it showing, and he came the way men come who are not in a hurry and are not going to be hurried, because they have done this before and there is no part of it they are enjoying.

“**Tomas Vale.**”

“**Yes.**”

“**I have a sheet here with your name at the top of it and a day on it and I am going to read you the first line of it and then I am going to take you up a stair.**”

“**Read it.**”

He read it.

“**That is the whole of it. You do not have to say anything at this point and there is nowhere on it for you to put anything, and I am not asking you for a reason and there is no box on it that wants one.**”

“**What is it called.**”

“**I have not got to call it anything. I have got to give it to you and take you where it says.**”

---

**The sheet is a copy of a bell.**

Not a copy of the words. There were no words in it and there was nothing in the yard to copy. It is a copy of a sound that was made once on the evening of the day named, in a yard on the top terrace, in this city, and a thing I have known since the hundred and eleventh is that a thing which comes out twice is a claim and a copy of a claim, **and a copy is how a sentence stops belonging to the person who said it, and I worked that out about a woman in a room eleven days ago, and it is on my own paper now and it is in a hand I cannot check.**

**And it is not a forgery. That is the whole of the thing and it took me the whole of the stair to understand it. A copy of a Crown sheet is that office's from the moment it is made, and a signal is a real signal, and what an office does with a real signal is a warrant, and every step of it is correct, and the officer who is giving it to me is right, and the man who wrote the fourth box is right, and nobody in this kingdom is wicked, and there is not one step of it that anybody could have done differently.**

**The Office is right about the procedure and wrong about the standing, and I have no standing to be wrong about, and that asymmetry is the whole of a hundred and thirty days and it has been sitting in front of me since the hundred and sixth and I did not see it until a man in a coat handed it to me on a street.**

He did not say any of that out loud. He walked up a stair with the man in the coat and did not ask him one question.

---

The room was off a lane on the middle terrace and it had a bench against the wall, a table, a lamp, and a form on the table with a day written at the top of it and a number under the day.

**Four boxes. The ward, the day, the thing, and the ground. And I had stood in a room exactly like this one yesterday morning with nobody in it and read a form off a table because there is no form in this kingdom that says who may read a thing, and I wrote in my own book last night that there is no form for asking what the ground is going to say.**

He read the fourth box standing up and it took him about four seconds and it is the truest thing a form has ever said about him.

> *Four words and a place. Not *the district's business*, which is three words and goes in that box about nine hundred times a year and is not a lie and is not a reason. **Four words that name a sound somebody made in a yard, and the sound is mine, and the yard is named, and the day is named, and it is true. It is the truest thing a form has ever said about me and it can be produced against me by a stranger I will never meet for forty years, and I cannot get at it, and I cannot cross anything out, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom that lets a man ask a room why it wrote that.**
>
> ***And the other two documents I have are both inventory. About four hundred words in an office's own hand of the shape of a hearing I could not answer and did not attend, and its last line says the person named produced nothing. And a district's paper about a person in a market with three words in its last box, in a room off a lane, with a man on a bench in it. Neither of them says what I am for, and neither did this one until yesterday, and this one does, and I did not ask it to, and I am not sorry for it and I am not going to be sorry for it, and I cannot make it untrue, and there is no form that would let anybody make it untrue, including me.***

---

At about the fifth hour he worked out what he was, standing in that room with the lamp and the bench, and it came out in about four seconds and it is the ordinary way round.

**A person with no standing cannot be a party to a matter. A person who has been entered in a column once does not get entered twice for nothing, and a man who has been named on a board cannot be unnamed, and I said all three of those things in the ordinary voice on a step on the hundred and twenty-seventh, six days ago, and I said them to nobody, and here is a man holding them in the ordinary voice and being held on them.**

He said nothing else all day.

He did not claim to be anything, of any kind. He did not say her name. He did not say the name of the woman who is not in this building and is not in this city and whose name is at the head of a sheet four streets and four flights up that he has never decided about. He did not ask the man in the coat a single question that would put any of the three of them on a second form, and he went through it in his own head once, the way a man goes through a line of plates, and there was one question he wanted to ask and it was about a bench in a room off a lane and there was a man on it over a true thing said out loud, and **he did not ask it, because if he had said that man out loud then a district would have a matter with his own account at the head of it, and there is no form by which a man who has been named can be unnamed, and the man on that bench would still be on that bench.**

**Nobody is rescued. I did not say his name and I am not going to and there is a man who has been on a bench since the hundred and eighteenth over a true sentence that somebody said back to him at ten feet and he cannot say what put him there and I know half of it and he knows the other half and there is no form in this kingdom for a story.**

The slate was under his arm the whole of the day and he did not put a question on it.

---

At about the sixth hour, before the lamp was lit, a door at the end of the corridor opened and closed again.

**She is in this building. I do not know which part of it and I did not look at the plate on that door and I am not going to look at it, and I am not going to stand under a door on her account, and I am not going to write to her, and I have not done any of those things in ten days and I am not going to start now because a man is on a bench in a room and it would be the most convenient hour in a hundred and thirty-three days to do one of them.**

He wrote it down that night, in his own book, and this once he wrote down the not-asking, because it was not a non-use of the slate and there is a difference and the difference is four inches long.

> *She is in this building and she did not come to me, and I did not ask her to, and I am not going to. **The fifth clause says no answer to any signal carried on or by the object is to be entered as anybody's answer for a household of this ward, and the holder has no standing to speak for any household in Kells or in any ward.** And if I had asked her to come to a room in this building then a man with no standing would have asked a woman inside an office to come and see him, **and that is a man putting his own business in front of a person who published an instruction saying she will not be found and will not answer and will not be sent for, and the asking would have been the damage, and it would have been the fifth clause thing to do and the whole of a hundred and thirty days is that I did not know that until the hundred and twenty-seventh.**
>
> *And she published what I would have preferred hidden, six days before we ever met, and it stands, and she is not thanked and I am not going to thank her, **and I do not know whether she did anything about this and I am not going to find out, and a man who finds out what his sister did for him has had a sister who did something for him, and I have not got that and I am not going to go and get it.**

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening they put the lamp out and he was on a bench in a room with a form in it on a table, and a form that has four boxes and one of them a day and the fourth one full of a thing that is true, and a man who put a sound in a yard is in it, and he is not sorry for the sound.

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and thirty-third on the back of his own hand, because the book was in his coat and the coat was across the room with a man who had not asked him for it.

> *The hundred and thirty-third, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **I was arrested on the middle terrace at about the fourth hour on a sheet with my name at the top of it and I was taken up a stair into a room with a form in it and the form's fourth box is four words and they are true.**
>
> *Second column, and what it is. **The warrant is a copy of my own bell.** Not of the words — there were no words in it. **A copy of a sound, made once, on the evening of the day named, in a yard on this city's top terrace, and a thing I have known since the hundred and eleventh is that a thing which comes out twice is a claim and a copy of a claim, and a copy is how a sentence stops belonging to the person who said it, and I worked that out about a woman eleven days ago and here it is on my own paper.** It is not a forgery. It is a copy. **A copy of a Crown sheet is that office's from the moment it is made, and a signal is a real signal, and a warrant is what an office does with a real signal, and the officer is right, the form is right, the district is right, and nobody in this kingdom is wicked, and not one step of it could have been done differently.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. **The Office is right about the procedure and wrong about the standing, and I have no standing to be wrong about.** I have known that a person with no standing cannot be a party to a matter since the hundred and twenty-seventh, six days ago, in my own hand, and I said it to nobody, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom by which a man who has been named can be unnamed, **and a man who says a thing about himself in the ordinary voice and is then held on it is not a paradox, it is the ordinary way round.**
>
> *And the price of it. **The cheapest and the most expensive things I have ever done happened inside four minutes of each other on the evening of the hundred and thirtieth: I put a sound into about forty people for nothing, and it moved nothing, and it has produced two documents in three days, and both of them are about me, and neither of them says what I am for, and the second one does, and it is true.** Being right about a column and being right about a man are the same skill. It does not carry.
>
> *And I am not sorry for the bell. **I struck a bell I did not make, in a yard, in the ordinary way, once, and I could not add a word to it, and the word in it was the hour, and about forty people were standing in that yard and not one of them is an answer, and the rest of a city of about nine thousand did not find out for about nine minutes that there had been anything in it at all, and I cannot tell anybody I did it, because the only instrument that would carry the fact is the one I used and a bell does not carry words and does not carry who struck it.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no document produced, given or asked for, and no name explained, and no child found by a document, and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and no officer relieved of anything and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast this morning where they were made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and the barrow is on the flat under a tarpaulin and this room is a building and the frame is not in it. **The carrier in the sack, not rung, fifty-six days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and she is somewhere in this building and she did not come and I did not ask.**
>
> *The slate was under my arm all day and I did not put a question on it.
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about me until yesterday, and a form on a table in this room that is about me and is the district's, and I have not got it and could not carry it if I had it.** None of them is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing today.
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a room off a lane with a bench, a lamp and a form, and a form has four boxes and one of them a day and one of them a ground, and the ground on this one is not three words, it is the sound I made in a yard, and it is true.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A warrant is a copy of a bell and a copy is not a lie and I am not sorry for the bell and I am on a bench.**
