# Chapter 0146 — He Is Given The Choice And Is Not The Person Who Gets To Make It

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and thirty-second day and Tomas Vale went into a room off a lane on the middle terrace that had a bench against the wall and a lamp on a table and nobody in it, and there was a form on the table with a day written at the top of it and a number under the day.

**He read it standing up, and there is no form in this kingdom that says who may read a thing, and there is nobody in the room, and a form on a table in an empty room is a form anybody in this kingdom may read.**

Four boxes. The ward, the day, the thing, and the ground. The ward was a ward of this city and the day was the day before yesterday and the thing was a bell in a yard. **And the fourth box on it was four words long and the four words said what the thing was for, and I stood in that room and read a form about a thing that was not me and the fourth box was full and it was true, and I have been in five counties and a city and a fourth box has been three words in every one of them and this one was four and it was the truth about a bell.**

He tied the string back the way he had found it. It had not been tied, and he noticed that, and he left it as it was.

**So the shape of the thing that is going to happen to me is four boxes and a day and a ground, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom for asking what the ground is going to say, and I am going to find out on the ground floor of this city on a day this week and I am not going to write a single word about what I think it will say.**

---

At about the fifth hour he was in a lane two streets off that road and he saw that the trench in the road was being worked.

**There were three men in it and a lamp between them and they were not digging. A bar, a lamp, and a man standing at the top of the trench with his back to a wall about nine feet high and a plate in his hand, and that is what a man looks like when he is waiting for something to arrive, and I have stood at a rail for six years myself and I know the shape of it.**

He went back down the lane to the shuttered shop and she was on the sill with her feet on the boards and her hands flat on the frame of the opening, and he was in the road and not inside the room, and he had not asked her to be there and she had not said where she was going to be.

**She is holding a sill and a floor and a wall and she has been reading that building for eleven years and there is nobody above her in this city and there is nobody below her and she does not charge anybody for it.**

At about the sixth hour it came.

**It is not a sound. I have not heard one and I could not have, and I am not going to write one word about what it was like because I do not know what it was like, and anybody who tells you they know is selling it.** It came up out of the ground in the cut and into the wall on my left and along the sill I had both hands on, and my hands had been on the sill of the shuttered shop for about four minutes without my putting them there, and it went through the stone and through me and it was in my teeth and in the soles of my feet and in the barrow thirty feet up the hill under a tarpaulin, and it took about nine seconds and I know that because I was counting, because I count things.

**I did not perceive anything. I did not learn where it went. I did not hear it and there is no form in this kingdom that would have told me what I was being shown and nobody showed me anything.**

She took the worst of it.

**She is on a sill and a floor and a wall and she is the one who reads vibration, and when it came it came up through three floors and a road and a trench and it found the hands that were reading it, and she went down on the boards on her side and did not put a hand out, and afterwards she was on the sill for about a quarter of an hour and she could not tell one thing from another and she did not ask anybody to say it twice.**

Nobody in that street asked her for a reason. A woman at a door looked at her and looked away. A man carrying sacks looked at her and carried the sacks.

---

He got up off the boards and he could not keep his feet, and he did not fall down, and it took him about four minutes to be sure of the wall.

Then she said it. One sentence, in the ordinary voice, at ten feet, without asking him anything first.

“**You take it or I take it, and I have already decided, and I am telling you after.**”

He took it.

**And I did not decide anything. I want that written in the plainest words I can find. I did not decide where it went, I did not decide what it was for, I did not decide whether it stopped, and I did not put my hand on a bar and I did not cut a wire, and the only thing I did was take a thing that was coming through me because there was nobody else standing in it, and that is not being the man who decided and it is not being the man who cut it and it is not either of those two men.**

The line was worked for about two hours and then it stopped, and it stopped the way a thing stops that has been run to the end of itself and has nothing left to run on.

**A forced relay runs the line to exhaustion. That is the whole of what it is. Nobody chose it and nobody refused it, and it cost a woman most of what she had left, and it cost me my balance for a day and a half afterwards and I have not been able to hold a straight edge since and I do not expect to be able to.**

---

At about the seventh hour a clerk came down four flights with a printed sheet in his hand and stopped in that road and looked up at a frame over a gate with a bell on it about as big as a cartwheel, and he went up to it and he turned the plate over and he struck it four times.

**He had a number at the top of the sheet. He did not read the sheet. He struck it four times and he put the plate back and he went up, and he was right, and he was striking somebody else's instruction, and he is not the man who decided that the bell rings at that hour and he is not going to be.**

About two hundred people came out of that yard and made no noise and went in again, the way they have gone in since the frame was new.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step at the foot of the top stair with the four bolts made fast and his hands on his knees, and she was on the step above him with her bag on her shoulder and the bell in it where it has been since before we left Kells.

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and thirty-second and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and thirty-second, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A form in an empty room with a fourth box four words long and true on it, a trench being worked, nine seconds through a sill, and about two hundred people letting a bell ring four times on somebody else's paper.**
>
> *Second column, and the day. **A forced relay ran the line to exhaustion and it came through the ground and the wall and a sill and me, and I took it because there was nobody else standing in it, and I did not decide anything, and I did not cut anything.** Nobody in this kingdom is wicked and nobody in that street chose it. **It is not a sound. I did not perceive it, I did not learn where it went, I am not going to be told where it went, and I am not going to write one word about what it was like because I do not know what it was like.** A bell is a wire and a bracket and a post and a sheet, and a line that answers can be worked to the end of itself, and that is all of it and I am not going to say one word more about it than that.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the whole of the thing. **She took the worst of it because she was holding a sill and a floor and a wall and she is the one who reads vibration. She has lost more hearing. Nothing is restored. Nothing will be.** And nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of it, a woman at a door looked at her and looked away, a man carried his sacks, **and I did not ask her for a reason, and that is about four inches long, and it is the hardest single thing I have done in a hundred and thirty-two days and it is four inches long and I am not going to describe how hard it was, because a man who describes how hard it was for him is asking to be thanked for not asking.**
>
> *And the choice, and I am putting it in the wrong order on purpose because I got it in the wrong order at the time. **She said: you take it or I take it, and I have already decided, and I am telling you after. And she had decided before she said it, and she was not asking me, and I was not asked because I could not have been asked, because a man who decides and a man who is a channel are two different men and I have been taught the difference for three volumes and I have not learned it until this evening.** A man who decides is a keeper. A man who is a channel is iron. **I was given the choice and I am not the person who gets to make it, and that is the whole of what I have failed to learn since I was nine years old and it cost a woman the use of a thing she will never get back.**
>
> *And the clerk at the gate. **A man came down four flights with a printed sheet and a number at the top of it and struck a bell about as big as a cartwheel four times and did not read the sheet.** He is right. He is not the man who decided that the bell rings at that hour and there is nobody above him to ask and he did not ask. **He is striking somebody else's instruction, and that is the third kind of bell, and this kingdom has no name for it on a form, and I have now watched one being rung by a person, and about two hundred people came out and made no noise and went in again, and that is what a schedule sounds like from underneath, and I have heard it now and I cannot unhear it.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no new sense, and no perception attempted and none had, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no name transferred, and no child found by a document, and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything.** Four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a room, a lane, a road, a shuttered shop and a step are not a building, **and the frame was thirty feet off me for the whole of the two hours and I did not go near it.** The carrier in the sack, not rung, fifty-five days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, **and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight, and there is not one in this city that is either.** **Her bell is in her bag and she did not take it out and nobody asked her to.**
>
> *The slate was not used today and I am not going to write down that it was not.
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about me, and a form I read standing up in an empty room and tied nothing up and took nothing out of, and a clerk with a printed sheet who is not mine and whose number is a filing reference and not a man.** None of them is a fourteenth and I have produced nothing and asked for nothing today.
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a trench, a lamp, a bar, a clerk with a number at the top of a sheet, four strokes on a frame over a gate, a fourth box four inches long that wants to know what a thing is for, and a woman on a sill two streets off who is the one in the road and is not going to be asked for a reason by anybody including me.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A forced relay runs the line to exhaustion and it takes the worst of it out of whoever is holding, and I was holding because there was nobody else, and she was holding because she has been holding for eleven years, and I am not the man who cut it and I am not going to be forgiven for being the man it came through.**
