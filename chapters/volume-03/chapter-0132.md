# Chapter 0132 — A Person Who Is Right, On His Account

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and eighteenth day and Tomas Vale went down off the top stair with the barrow left where he left it, and he had been in the city twenty-three days, and on the middle terrace there is a trestle at the side of the market with a Crown slip on it and a string and a lead weight, and any person may fill in four of the six fields, and about four hundred people a year do.

**Nobody in this kingdom is wicked. That is not a softening and it is not a mercy, it is the ground, and the ground held for one hundred and eighteen days and it held this morning while a man in a room off a lane was told that a thing he had done in the open had a number.**

The slip was a Crown market slip and the six fields were the market, the day, the thing repeated, **the person who repeated it**, where it was said, and who heard it. Four of the six could be filled in by anybody standing there. The fourth one wanted a name and about four hundred people a year put one in and none of them were wicked and none of them were brave.

**A box is cheaper than a witness and a witness can refuse. That is the whole of a market slip and it is four inches long and it has been true for eleven years in eleven counties.**

He stood at that trestle and read the string and the weight and did not touch anything.

At about the fifth hour he asked a man in a market a question. It was a fair question and the man's name was Hob Kersey and the answer cost the man a market.

Kersey had a pitch and a hand-cart and a licence for both and had held them long enough to have a worn place in the ground, and he was standing at his own pitch in the ordinary way.

“**Did you read the sheet on that wall.**”

“**I read it. Everybody read it.**”

“**Did you say anything about it.**”

“**I said three things about my own pitch in front of the market keeper and about nine people heard me and he did not argue, and that is the whole of what I did, and I did it because there is a man in this city who put four true sentences on a wall and nobody asked him why, and I wanted to see what would happen if I did the same.**”

**And that is the whole of it. He was right about his pitch. He is still right about his pitch. He did the ordinary thing with an instrument that had been lying in the open in a market for a day, and the instrument was not his, and the cost of it is his.**

Kersey had said his three things in the open and had got nowhere with them the first time, which is what happens to everybody, and then somebody in that market had come about ten feet off him and said his own three sentences back to him in the ordinary voice, and that had moved.

**He does not know who. He has been asked twice in his life and both times he has said the same thing: *somebody. It was not you and it was not him and it was a woman with a tray and it was four inches long and she was gone before I got to her.***

**And there is no name on the form for who said a thing to a man in a market. The six fields are the market, the day, the thing repeated, the person who repeated it, where it was said, and who heard it. Not one of the six is a field for the person who said it first.**

**So the four inches were free, and the four inches are always free, and the free ones are the ones nobody is asked about, and the ones anybody is asked about are the ones a man put up himself in his own name for fourpence on a wall and cannot take down.**

**At about the sixth hour he found out where Hob Kersey was, and it was not a market. It was a lane off the middle terrace and a door at the top of the lane and a stair, and a room at the top of the stair with a form in it and a bench and a lamp on a table.

The man who kept the room was called Nyle Prout and he was the district round and he had a printed slip with a number at the top of it, and he had been in this city for two days and he was doing a round and the round goes ward by ward and he was four wards from done.**

Kersey was on the bench. He had his coat over his knees and his hands together and he was not angry, and when the door came he looked up and said *they have not let me have anything to sit on but that is a bench* and the man at the table said *that is a bench* and meant it.

“**You are not on the round.**”

“**I am not on your round.**”

“**No. And I am not sorry you are here, because you are not, and I have a slip and a number and I have about nine of these a day and about forty of them in a year and this is the first one in two days that has got a name in the fourth box that is not the market keeper's.**”

“**What is he held on.**”

“**On the district's paper.**”

“**And what is on the district's paper.**”

“**That a person of this city repeated a thing in this market on the day named and that the person who said it to him first is the person at the head of the sheet on the wall.**” He turned the form round and put a finger on the fourth box. “**And it says there what the matter is for, and it is *the district's business*, which is three words and it is not a lie and it is not a reason, and it is the same three words that go in that box about nine hundred times a year.**”

“**Why is he held.**”

“**Because the district has given him a capacity.**”

“**What capacity.**”

“**A claimant.**” Prout said it without any weight on it at all. “**A person whose name is on a market slip asserting that a thing was said in a market is, on a district's paper, a person who has made a claim. And a claimant can be ordered to produce. A witness cannot.**”

“**Produce what.**”

“**The thing he claims.**”

**And Tomas Vale stood in that room and understood the whole of it in about four seconds, and it is the ordinary way of a thing and there is not one step of it that anybody could have done differently.**

*He claimed a thing said at ten feet in the open. There is no form in this kingdom for producing a thing said out loud. He has nothing. He is a claimant and a claimant is a person a district can put in a room with a form until somebody with a standing comes, and nobody with a standing is coming, and the round goes on to the next ward in the morning. The fourth box on that form is the ground, and it is full on about nine hundred matters a year, and a ground is a thing a matter is stood on and not a reason for it.*

“**He is in here on a paper about a man who has been named. You are not a party to it. You have no standing in this district and I cannot enter a word of yours on this form, and if I could I would not, because a word of yours would be the sixth name on a sheet about a man who has been named and this form has four boxes and one of them is a day.**”

“**Then say to him that the sheet on the wall and the slip at the trestle are the same chain and that the chain started with a true sentence.**”

“**I am not going to say that to him.**” Prout put his pen down. “**Say it to me instead and I will tell you why it does not help and then you can go.**”

“**Why it does not help.**”

“**Because the chain is correct. A sworn answer is an answer and a district is required to send an answer round, and that rule is older than me and older than this room and it does not have a face on it, and it does not need one, and nobody broke it. Somebody put a true sentence on a wall for fourpence and it went where it is required to go, and a man used it in a market the way a man uses a thing he has found, and he filled in a box that is there for him to fill in, and I came round and I did the ordinary thing with it.**”

“**And I am the man at the top of the sheet.**”

“**Then I am not going to write it down.**”

**And he said the rest of it in the same flat way he said all of it, and it is the whole of what Tomas Vale learned in a room off a lane on the hundred and eighteenth.**

“**If you tell me that, then this district has a matter with a name at the top of it that a man has sworn to in open court, and a sworn answer goes round, and you have told me yourself that you cannot get a thing off a board once it is up. **And there is no form anywhere in this kingdom by which a man who has been named can be unnamed, and I would be doing it to you and not to him, and he would still be on the bench.** And I have a slip and a number and about nine of these a day and I am not going to be the man who puts your name at the top of a second one.**”

**He stood in a room with a form in it and there was a man on a bench who had been held since the fourth hour on account of four inches and a box and a name, and the name at the top of the sheet was his, and he could have said so in about four seconds and it would have made a second matter with his name at the head of it and it would not have got the man off the bench.**

> *A man can put his name on a district's paper exactly once by accident and he cannot take it off, and I found that out a day ago from a woman with a bag of pins, and I have spent the whole of today finding out what it costs when somebody else is standing in the room when it happens.*

He did not use the slate. It was under his arm and it had been under his arm since the fourth hour and there is no box on anything in that room and he did not put a question on it, and he did not write that down either, because a man who keeps a count of what he does not ask is a man waiting to be owed.

At about the seventh hour he did the arithmetic standing on a step outside, and it came out where it always comes out.

**Nine days.**

Not nine days of a lighter and a cart — that was Fourmile, and that was a man refusing to own a mend, and he has written the reason for it down twice and it is the same reason as this and the objects are not the same objects and he is going to write both of them out tonight so that nobody, including him, may add them.

**Nine days here is three market days and a licence and a pitch and a hand-cart that stands on a worn place in the ground, and the reason a man loses nine days over a thing he did in the open and in the correct voice is exactly the reason the wharfman lost nine days: nobody in this kingdom will own a thing they cannot account for, and a thing said at ten feet in a market cannot be accounted for by anybody, including the man who said it, and that is why a district may order him to produce and he has nothing to produce.**

**And the instrument was four inches long and it cost nothing and it was somebody else's and he will never know whose, and the man on the bench is going to pay nine days for it, and Tomas Vale put the first four inches of it into the world on a public wall for fourpence and is not going to be told that by anybody in this city.**

> *The hundred and eighteenth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A man is in a room with a form in it and it is my account, and every step of it is correct, and nobody in this kingdom is wicked, and I am not going to be able to do one single thing about it.**
>
> *Second column, and it took me about four seconds in that room and it is the whole of the day. **The office gave him a word and the word is the room. A name on a market slip saying a thing was said makes you somebody who has made a claim, and the whole of what that word buys the district is the power to ask him for it.** There is no form in this kingdom for producing a thing said out loud at ten feet, so he has nothing, and a person with nothing can be kept in a room until somebody with a standing comes, **and nobody with a standing is coming, and the round goes on to the next ward in the morning, and a witness could not have been put in that room at all and a witness is me and I have been let into every building in this city.**
>
> *And the instrument. **Somebody said his own three sentences back to him at ten feet in the ordinary voice before he said them, and it moved, and he does not know who and he will never know and I have asked him twice and both times he has said the same thing.** A market slip does not want the person who said it first. It wants the market, the day, what was said, who repeated it, where, and who heard, **and the field I have wanted for two days is not one of the six and there is not a seventh.** Four inches, and the four inches are always free, and the free ones are the ones nobody is ever asked about.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the thing I cannot get round. **He said his three things about his own pitch in front of the market keeper and about nine people heard him and he was right, and he did it because there is a man in this city who put four true sentences on a wall and nobody asked him why and he wanted to see what would happen if he did the same.** That is the whole of it and it is on me and it is not on the four inches, which were somebody else's. **I have been carrying a sentence round a district in a cart for two days and I thought the cost of it was nine of two hundred people in four towns, and the cost of it is a man on a bench.**
>
> *And I did not say who I was. **He offered me the whole of it if I would say it, in about four seconds, in the ordinary way, and the reason he gave is that if I say it then a district has a matter with my name at the head of it and a sworn answer goes round and there is no form by which a man who has been named can be unnamed, and he would be doing it to me and not to the man on the bench and the man would still be on the bench.** I have been in this city twenty-three days and that is the first time anybody has used my own sentence against me and done it in the correct form and been right, and it cost him about four seconds of politeness to do it and it was not politeness.
>
> *Nine days. **Three market days, a licence, a pitch and a hand-cart on a worn place in the ground, and the same arithmetic as a wharfman in Fourmile, and the objects are not the same objects and I am writing both of them out so that nobody adds them.** Nobody in this kingdom will own a thing they cannot account for. That is the rule and it has been the rule in four counties and a city and he is right and so am I and neither of us can do anything.
>
> *Nobody asked her for a reason at any hour in a hundred and eighteen days and I did not ask her about the four inches and I am not going to, **and there is a thing I have done for two days in my own head which is to find out whether a woman with a tray was in that market on the hundred and sixteenth, and I am not going to do it, and I want that in the book in that order and not improved in the morning.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, and nothing bound, and no sheet taken off a board, and no document produced and given and asked for.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it has been in the open every hour of this day, and it was thirty feet off me when I went up a stair to a room in this city this afternoon and it makes no difference to the frame. The carrier in the sack untouched, forty-one days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Her bell is in her bag and it has not moved since a market of about nine hundred people on the ninety-second and it is not going to move because of anything I have found out in a room with a form in it.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about me, and a sworn sentence that is the court's, and a copy of it on a district's cart and on a board in a market, and a market slip in a book somewhere with my name in the fourth box of it, and a district's paper with four boxes in a room off a lane with a name in the first of them that is not mine.** None of them is a fourteenth. The slip is a market's. The paper is a district's. I have not got either of them and I could not carry either of them if I had them.
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a market slip with six fields and a box for the name of the person who repeated a thing, and a district's paper with four boxes and one of them a day, and a room off a lane with a bench in it, and a man who came round and did the ordinary thing with a form that was there to be filled in by anybody who wanted it filled in.** It did not break a rule. It is a box and a room and a man on a bench and a capacity called a claimant, and about four hundred people a year put a name in that box and not one of them is wicked, **and one of the four hundred has lost nine days and I am the reason the box wanted a name in it at all, because on the hundred and sixteenth there was a true sentence on a wall with a name at the head of it, and the man read it and did the ordinary thing with it.***
