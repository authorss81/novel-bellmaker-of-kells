# Chapter 0116 — Two People Who Will Write A Thing Down

The middle terrace market in Sennet is on three streets and it is about nine hundred people on a good morning and it is on the ground, which is the first thing she noticed, because eleven years of markets have been on hills.

**You cannot see anybody from the top of it. A man on a pitch at the top of this market cannot see a man at the bottom of it, and that is the only reason anybody has ever been able to say a true thing out loud in a market without an office hearing about it in nine minutes.**

She was in it at about the fourth hour of the hundred and second day of the month with a slate under her arm and the price side on the other face the other way up, and she was not working, and she had not come to work.

> *Hundred and second. I have come to a market of about nine hundred people to find out one thing, and it is this: **can a person be asked to write down a true thing about themselves in a public room, and what does it cost.** Not what does it cost the person who asks. What does it cost the person who has it written down.*

---

**And it was tested in the open, in front of about nine people, at about the fifth hour, and it was tested on him and not on me, and I am putting it in the book before the rest of the day because it is the only thing in a hundred and two days that I have been frightened of.**

A woman at a pitch of needles and thread, who had a voice that carried, looked at the barrow and then at me and then at him and said, to him, in the ordinary way, to nine people:

“**You could ask her. She does that. She stands about ten feet off and makes a person hear their own sentence. Ask her what that notice wants, she will have it in a minute.**”

And he said, in the ordinary way, in front of all nine of them:

“**No.**”

And the woman said, “**Why not, when you have gone up that stair three times in four days with a barrow, and there is your name at the third line of the sheet they nailed at the top of it, and there is a hearing on it tomorrow?**”

And he said, “**Because I have got no standing and she has got nothing to stand on, and I would be asking her to spend the only thing she has got in a room where I would be the only person who heard it, and I have not got a right to do that and I have not got a reason either, and I am not going to make one up at ten feet.**”

---

**And I said the reason, once, out loud, at ten feet, in the ordinary voice, and it is the first time in eleven years I have said a reason for a method to more than one person, and I said it to nine people and not to him.**

“**Because she said it in front of nine people. And a question said in front of nine people is a thing I will be asked again in four counties inside a year, by somebody who heard it offered and not answered. **I have had one of those already. I priced it in a smith's shop on the fifty-first and it took eleven years of work and it is not a thing I am pricing again in a market because somebody thinks it would be useful.**

“**And he did not ask me, and he said the reason out loud, in front of nine people, and he did not stop in the middle of it to find out whether it was working, and he did not make it about me. He made it about him not having a right. **Nobody has said a thing like that in front of nine people for me in eleven years, and I am not going to be grateful about it in a market, and I am not going to let it be a thing that was for anybody else.**”

**Nobody asked me about my ear. Not one of the nine, and not one of about nine hundred people in this market between the fourth hour and the ninth. He did not ask. He has not asked in a hundred and two days and I am not going to ask him why he has not, and that is the whole of what I have to say about it and I am going to leave it there.**

**And it was not a ninth use. He had the slate and the chalk and he did not put a question on it. The count is eight. A thing that is not done is not a thing that has been done, and I have written both halves down because I keep wanting the other half and the other half would be easier and would be a lie.**

---

**And then I did the thing I came for, and it took nine seconds, and I have timed it twice, and I did it four times on other people over eleven years and I have never once done it for nothing.**

There is a court on this terrace with a board on a public wall in it, and I went in at about the sixth hour and stood at the board and read nine rows of what people have sworn to, and the rows go back years, and there is a clerk with a pen behind his ear and no queue at all, because about four hundred people a year in this city go into that room and ask him to write a thing down and pay him for it, **and that is the instrument, and it has been standing there the whole eleven years I have been on a road, and I have been doing a worse version of it in the open for nothing.**

> *Here is what I have learned and it is a hundred and two days late and it is four inches long. **A thing said twice is the only thing in this kingdom that has ever moved a line, and it is about four inches long, and it takes about nine seconds, and it moves about four in ten, and it has been in my hands for eleven years and I have thought of it as weather.***
>
> *And a court will write it down and keep it, and it costs fourpence, and it moves nothing at all. **The difference is not the truth. The difference is who reads it afterwards.** A thing said twice at ten feet in a market is heard by the people who were standing there and it dies with them and it cannot be produced by anybody. A thing written down in a court is read by every clerk who comes after for forty years and it can be produced against you by a stranger you have never met. **I have been doing the expensive one and the unproducible one and I have thought of it as the strong one, and I was half right, and the half I was wrong about is the half that keeps.**

---

**And that is when I found out the thing I did not know, and it came at about the seventh hour, and it is the finding of the hundred and second day, and I have not got a sentence for it yet and I am not going to invent one on a step.**

**Everything I have ever done needed me in it. That is the whole of eleven years and it is the thing I am proudest of and it is also the reason nothing has ever happened twice. I had to be standing there. My voice, my sentence, my hands, my slate, and a person to hear it, and I chose which person and I chose the four inches, and there has never been one single occasion in eleven years on which a thing I had said came back to me as a thing somebody else had said.**

---

**And then two people who have never spoken to me did it, on their own day, without being asked, and I was nine feet off, and I was not in the room for either of them.**

**One is a market keeper with about nine rows and a book of his own and no office, and his name is Ord Marren, and he has held the same nine rows in this market for nine years, and the rent put on those nine rows is the rent put on the wholesale stalls at the end of the row, and he has said so out loud at his pitch perhaps a hundred times in nine years and not one person in this city has said it back to him, including his landlord, who does not need to.**

**And the other is a man called Wil Brand who lets barrows out of a yard near the water, and has held a licence eleven years, and the licence says what a yard may not do, and his yard has been doing the thing the licence says every day for eleven years, and the thing is not a trick, it is his whole trade, and there is no room in this kingdom with a clerk in it where he is allowed to say that, and he has wanted to say it in one for eleven years.**

---

**And I said it back. That is all I did. I said it back, four inches, at ten feet, in the ordinary voice, to a man about nine feet off, and then I went and stood about nine feet further off and I did not say another word to either of them for the rest of the day.**

> *To Marren, at his pitch, at about the seventh hour, at ten feet, in the ordinary voice, his own words, his own order, four inches: **the rent on your nine rows is the wholesale rent and you have said so a hundred times in nine years and it has never once been said back to you.***
>
> *To Brand, at the yard rail, at ten feet, in the ordinary voice, four inches: **your licence says what a yard may not do and you have been doing that thing every day for eleven years and it is not a trick, it is your trade, and you have never been able to say it in a room with a clerk in it because then the eleven years is a conversation instead of a trade.**

**And the yard is at the end of the third street and the rail is his and he was on the far side of it with a barrow out and not working, and I said it at ten feet, in the ordinary voice, four inches, and he did not look up.**

He looked up about a second afterwards. He did not say anything. He turned his left hand over on the rail, took a stub of chalk out of his apron, wrote **eleven years** on the back of his own hand, put the chalk away, and went back to the barrow.

**And that is not a document and it is in his own hand and it is in his own name and it will be off his own skin in about a week, and I have never in eleven years watched a man do the cheap version first and then pay fourpence for the expensive one three days later, and I did not tell him that either, and I stood about nine feet further off.**

**And that is the whole of what I did, and it took about nine seconds each, and I have done that nine thousand times on other people's sentences in eleven years and it has cost me every time, and this is the first time it has cost me nothing, and the reason is that I said their sentences and not mine, and the thing I have been selling for eleven years is not the sentence. It is me.**

---

**And then, on their own days, in their own places, they did a thing I was not in the room for.**

**Marren went to the court on the middle terrace and had a true sentence about his own nine rows entered in his own name, in his own hand, in open court, and it is on the board on the public wall with his name at the head of it, and it took about nine seconds, and it cost him fourpence, and it moves nothing at all, and it is on a board in a public room in a city of about nine thousand people, and it will be there when I am gone.**

**And Brand did the same about his yard and his licence, in his own name, in open court, on the same board, and his is the fourth row down, and the sentence in it is a sentence about him and not about the licence, and he wrote it himself.**

**Neither of them is a hero. Neither of them is owed anything by me. I did not ask either of them to do anything, I have not spoken to either of them since, and I have not been thanked by either of them because neither of them knows I said it.**

**And the reason I did not ask is the reason I have never asked anybody anything in eleven years, and it is not modesty. It is that a man who is asked to put his own true hand to a thing will say yes out of a market to a stranger he thinks has a use for him, and a man who is not asked, and who has just heard his own sentence come back at him in his own words at ten feet, has to decide on his own, alone, on his own day, in his own yard, and a decision a man takes alone is his and cannot be anybody else's.**

---

**I did not ring anything today. I have not rung it since the ninety-second, in a market of about nine hundred, in front of nine hundred people, and that was about the only thing I have ever done that moved anything and it cost me the only cover I had left, and nobody asked me why and nobody is going to, and if anybody asks me in this city I will say there was a man with a box, and that is the true reason, and it is four inches long and it took me four seconds to get.**

I wrote the last entry of the hundred and second at about the ninth hour of the evening on a step by the river with my boots off, in my own hand, with the hours, and I filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth, and the fourth has two lines in it and I have not improved either of them and I am not going to.

> *Hundred and second. **A court will write a true thing down and keep it and it moves nothing. A thing said twice at ten feet in the open moves about four in ten and dies with the people who heard it. Both of those I had. What I did not have is what happened today at about the seventh hour and it took about nine seconds and it was four inches long.***
>
> *Second column, and the hard half. **I have spent eleven years being the person who says a person's own words back to them, and it has cost me every time, and I have thought that was the price of the instrument. It is not. The price is me.** A court clerk writes a sentence for fourpence and it sits on a wall for forty years. I do it for nothing and it is heard by whoever is standing there. **I have been doing the expensive one and calling it the strong one, and the difference between them is not the truth, it is who reads it afterwards, and I have had that the wrong way round for eleven years and a man at a pitch corrected it in nine seconds and he did not know he had.**
>
> *And the finding. **Two people who have never spoken to me went and put a true sentence about their own business in a document in their own names today, on their own days, in their own yard and at their own pitch, and I asked neither of them to do anything, and I was nine feet off, and I was not in the room for either of them.** Ord Marren, nine rows in this market for nine years, the rent on his nine rows is the wholesale rent, and he has said it out loud about a hundred times and nobody has ever said it back to him. Wil Brand, a yard near the water, a licence eleven years, and the thing his licence forbids is his whole trade, and he cannot say so in a room with a clerk in it without the eleven years turning into a conversation, and he has not been able to say it anywhere that counts for eleven years.
>
> *And what I actually did, and it is four inches and ten feet and nine seconds each: **I said their own sentences back to them. Nothing else. I did not tell them there was a court and I did not tell them the fourpence and I did not say it would help.** And it moved nothing. A man said his rent on a board and a man said his trade on a board and neither of those will move a line in this city inside a year, and I know that, and it is the first time in eleven years I have done my own instrument and watched it do nothing, **and I have not been disappointed, and I want that written down in case I am disappointed in about four days and try to improve it.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the day, and I am merging nothing: **the road has found a place after all and it is a queue. I worked that out on the eighty-fourth and I wrote it down and I did not act on it for eighteen days, because I thought a queue was a place a person stands in and it is not, it is a place two strangers can walk into at the same time, and neither of them has to be asked, and the only thing they need is to have heard their own sentence come back at them once, in their own words, in the open, in front of nine people who were not the man who said it to them.** Eleven years to learn that a stranger will do it for nothing if the sentence is his.
>
> *And it is not a system and it is not a network and it is not scalable and it is not going to be a thing I hand to anybody. **Two. There are two, and I have their names, and I am not going to write their names in this book, and I did not say their names out loud today at any hour, and about nine people heard four inches at ten feet today and not one of them has either name in it.**
>
> *I did not ring anything. My bell is in my bag. Nobody has asked me about my ear in a hundred and two days and I have written that down once tonight and I am not going to do it again.*
>
> *And a thing I am not going to write about because it is not mine to write about: **he did not ask, in front of nine people, and he said out loud in front of nine people that he had no right to, and he made it about him and not about me, and I have been on this road eleven years and nobody has ever done that in front of anybody.** The count of the slate is eight. It was eight yesterday. It is eight tonight. **A question offered in a market and refused in a market is not a question that was asked, and I have written that down twice now and I am going to stop writing it down, because the third time I write it I will be counting, and a woman who counts is a woman who is waiting to be owed.**
