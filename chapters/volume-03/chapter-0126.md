# Chapter 0126 — An Empty Box On One Line Out Of Nine Hundred

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and twelfth day and Tomas Vale went down the north corridor for the third time in three days, and he had one question, and he had decided it on a step the night before, and deciding it had taken him about an hour and a half and most of that hour was spent finding out what it was not going to be.

**It was not going to be *what happened to her*, because he had written that down as a thing he would not do and a thing he would not do twice is a rule and not an opinion.**

**It was not going to be *who cut this*, because there is a person in this building who would have to be the answer, and there are four people in this building and one lamp, and if he asked it then he would be told *the office* and *the office* is a name for about nine hundred rooms and he has been three days finding out that a name is not a person and an office is not a room.**

So it was one question and it was the cheapest question in this kingdom, and it was this:

**Is this form the same form.**

---

## And it took about four seconds, and Hale did not put his pen down, and that is how he answered it.

“**The same form.**”

“**You have not looked at the sheet I mean.**”

“**There is one form in this room and there have been about four hundred thousand of them and there is one plate in a printer's room two streets off that cuts all of them, and the man who cuts it has been cutting it since before I came and has never once been asked whether it is the right shape.**”

He wrote a figure in a column.

“**One plate. About nine hundred a year come into this room. That is the whole of it and it is not a mystery and it is not anybody's doing. **You are asking me whether the thing on that rail and the thing on my tray came off the same piece of iron, and they did, and if they had not it would be the most interesting thing anybody has asked me in this room in about four years.**”

---

**And then Tomas Vale stood nine feet off the desk and read the top form on the tray upside down, and Hale said so without being asked, which is a thing clerks do.**

“**You can read that from where you are standing.**”

“**Four words in the fourth box.**”

“**You can read four words from nine feet upside down.**”

“**It is a good hand. It is not a good pen. I have read a clerk's hand off a board at nine feet in four counties.**”

Hale put his pen down then.

“**Then you know more about that tray than the district does. **They send me nine hundred a year and they do not send me a word about any of them, and the four words in that box are what the district says the matter is for, and there are four words in that box nine hundred times a year, and if the four words are wrong then nine hundred people a year have been sent out of this room on a wrong ground and there is nobody in this building who could tell you that, including me, and I have thought about it more than once and I have not done anything about it, because there is nothing to do.**”

---

## And Tomas Vale did not ask what the fourth box was for, because he had already worked it out from nine feet, and because the most important part of all of it cannot be asked anything.

That is a sentence he wrote in his own book on the ninety-ninth, about a wire, at about the seventh hour, with solder in his hand, and it is the truest thing he has written in a hundred and twelve days and it has cost him four counties.

> *A wire can be cut and cannot be asked anything. A box on a form can be empty and cannot be asked anything. **The part of a thing that matters is the part nobody can put a question to, and that is not an injustice, it is a shape, and I have now been in front of four of them and I have wanted to ask about every one.**

So: **the fourth box is the ground. It is where a matter says what it is for, and on a store's list the same job is done by a fifth column, and the two of them are empty or full together, and the reason one of them is full and the other is empty on the same page is a rule and not a man.**

And: **on about nine hundred matters a year the fourth box is filled in. On one of them it is empty. And nobody has struck a line through it.**

He looked at the top form on Hale's tray for a while.

“**What happens to a form that has been argued with.**”

“**It gets a line through it. Where the clerk and the district do not agree, the clerk rules a line, and both the line and the disagreement go up. That is a form that has been argued with and it is the only thing in this building I have ever seen happen to a form.**”

“**And if a box is simply left empty.**”

“**Then it was not argued with, and it was not forgotten, because a man who forgets a box fills it in later and a man who fills it in later does it with a different pen.**”

Hale looked at him for a moment longer than he had looked at him on any of the three days.

“**Say the rest of that again and I will not answer it and you should not have said it.**”

“**I said nothing.**”

“**You said it in your face, and I am not going to pretend I did not see it, and I am also not going to help you, and I want that written down as two different things, because they are.**”

---

## And he did not make a scene, and he did not put it in a market, and it is the fourth time in a hundred and twelve days he has had something in his hands that four hundred people could have watched him use.

He went and stood in front of the board with the nine hundred lines on it, the same board, the same four boards, and he read the fifth column from end to end again, and it is empty on every line, and this time he was looking at it as the twin of a box nine feet off a tray.

**And here is what he joined, and what he refused to join, and the refusals are the point.**

He joined two things. **The fifth column on a board in a room, and the fourth box on a form, both of which he had looked at himself inside one day, in one building, without anybody showing him either.** They are the same shape and they are made of the same virtue and a man nine feet off a desk and a man at a counter four flights up have both refused, for the same stated reason, to write down a word they cannot read back.

**That is a shape. It is not a conspiracy, because a conspiracy is a set of people agreeing, and there is not one person in this kingdom who has agreed to anything, and there is not one person in it who could tell you the other one exists. It is two rules wearing the same coat, four streets apart, made by two men who would both be right and neither of whom is a villain and neither of whom is going to be thanked.**

And he did not join the third one, and the third one was the oldest one he has, and it is a keeper's field on a water-bell schedule in a county he has left, ruled past the end of a line, empty on one line and full on all the others, and the hand that ruled it was a form-writer's hand and not a district's, and it has been sitting in a household's drawer for about two years.

**Two empties in a day he can join. Three empties in three counties he cannot, and he wrote that down on the ninetieth in about four minutes in a square and it is the correct refusal and he is not going to improve it in a month.**

---

## And then the thing that is the finding of the hundred and twelfth day, and it took him about four minutes, and it is not a moral and it is not a law.

He stood in the middle of that room at about the fifth hour with about nine people in it and he looked at the rail and the board and the tray and the window, and he worked out who in that room could have seen the empty fourth box on the hundred and tenth.

**About nine people. A clerk, a man sweeping, two men at the rail for about four minutes, a woman with a tray, a man who asked Hale for something in a low voice, two more who came in later, and himself.**

**Nine people. And not one of them was looking at the fourth box, and not one of them ever is, and the reason is not that they are careless and it is not that they are kept from it.**

The fourth box is not the box anybody looks at. A form is read the way a bell is heard — for the thing that is going off. A man reads the first box and the second box, because the first is what the matter is and the second is who is in it, and he stops. **The empty one is on the end and it is four inches long and it is the only one on the sheet that nobody has ever been taught to want.**

> *The proof of what was done to my mother is a thing about a form. **A form is public. A tray is nine feet off a wall. A wall is a public wall.** And the strongest thing I have ever held against an office in a hundred and twelve days is a fact that a man in a room can see by reading, and it can be checked in about four seconds by asking one clerk one question, and it moves nothing at all.*
>
> *And no office in this kingdom is afraid of a fact that anybody may read. **That is what four boxes and one plate will do for a man.** They have spent a hundred and twelve days being careful with rooms, and with counters, and with shelves, and with men who will not walk a stranger up four flights, and they have never once in my hearing been careful with a form, because a form is not a thing you can keep, and it turns out that is the whole of it and not one of them has noticed.

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step at the foot of the top stair and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and twelfth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was, and in it he wrote down the four nines, because there are four now and there were three four days ago and he is not going to let them get into one column in his own head.**

> *The hundred and twelfth. **One plate, about nine hundred matters a year, and one empty box, and nobody has struck a line through it. A form that has been argued with has a line through it and this one has not been argued with and it was not forgotten either, because a man who forgets a box fills it in later with a different pen.**
>
> *Second column. **The fourth box is the ground. It says what a matter is for, in about four words, and it is full on about nine hundred matters a year and it is empty on mine, and I worked out what it was for from nine feet upside down and I did not ask, and the reason is the wire and it is four inches long and I wrote it on the ninety-ninth: the part of a thing that matters is the part nobody can put a question to.**
>
> *And a clerk said *it is the same form* in about four seconds and went back to his tray, and he is right, and he is not sorry, and he is not a door, and he will help me with nothing and has told me so in words. **I asked him whether a form that had been argued with got a line through it and he said yes, and then I asked him what happens when a box is simply left empty and he told me that was not forgetfulness, and then he told me to say the rest of it again and that he would not answer it, and he was right to say that and I should not have said it in his face and I did.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the two halves. **Nine people were in that room on the hundred and tenth and about forty people go through that door in a week, and not one of them was looking at the fourth box, and none of them ever will be, because the fourth box is not the box anybody looks at, and a form is read the way a bell is heard — for the thing that is going off. It took me a hundred and twelve days to work out that the answer to *who is stopping me* is nobody, and that the question was never the right shape.**
>
> *And the joining. **I have joined two things, and they are the fifth column on a board and the fourth box on a form, and I saw both of them myself inside one day in one building and nobody showed me either.** They are the same shape and they are the same virtue, and there is not one person in this kingdom who has agreed to anything and not one who could tell you the other one exists, **and a conspiracy is a set of people agreeing and this is two rules in the same coat, and that is the difference and it is four inches long.**
>
> *And I have not joined the third one, which is a keeper's field ruled past the end of a line in a drawer in a household in a county I have left, and I wrote on the ninetieth that I was not going to and I am not going to, and three empties in three counties is a man arranging agreement and two empties in one day is a man who looked.**
>
> *And the four nines, written out flat so that I never add them. **About nine hundred rooms on the top terrace. About nine hundred bells in this city. About nine hundred entries on nine sheets in a store's book. About nine hundred matters a year into a room with a lamp in it.** Four, and not one, and they are told apart by which room they are in and not one of them is a quantity of anything. I wrote three of them out in a row on the hundred and first and told the man who wrote them that I was writing them to keep them apart. **There are four now and I have added nothing.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, and nothing asked that I had not decided to ask before I came in, and one question in four seconds and no scene and nothing said in a market.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it makes no difference to the frame that a plate is in a printer's room two streets off. The carrier in the sack untouched, thirty-five days. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells. **Thirteen documents in a coat, and a form that is the court's, and a tray of nine, and a bundle of forty sheets tied at the middle, and not one of them is a fourteenth and not one of them is mine.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a plate, and about nine hundred forms a year off it, and a four-inch box at the end of every one of them, and one of them with nothing in it.** They have not kept anything from me. There is no room, no counter and no man that I have been refused by in a hundred and twelve days except in the ordinary way, **and the thing that was done to my mother is on a tray nine feet off a desk, and it is a thing a man could have seen any day in four years, and nobody looked, and that is not a wall and it is not a door. It is a form.**
