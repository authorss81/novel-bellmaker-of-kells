# Chapter 0128 — A Standing Instruction, Published Before They Have Met

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and fourteenth day and Tomas Vale went up four flights with the barrow left at the foot of the stair, as he has left it every day since the hundred and ninth, and on the top terrace there is a board on a public wall with Crown sheets and a handbill about a horse on it, and between the ninth sheet and the handbill there was a leaf of paper the size of a day-book page with four drawing-pins in it, one at each corner, which is a thing nobody does to a leaf.

**There have been two leaves on two public boards in this business and he has taken neither, and this is the third thing on a board and it is not a leaf.**

He stood in front of it and read it, and it took him about two minutes, and it is longer than five words.

---

> *A standing instruction. It is to nobody. It is to be read by anybody who has come looking for me, and by anybody who has not, and the second kind is the reason it is written.*
>
> *I will not be found. I will not answer. I will not be sent for, and I will not come out of anywhere, and no person is to stand at a door on my account, and I will not write to anybody who comes.*
>
> *There is a board in this city and there are boards in four counties, and what I have to say is for whoever needs it, and it is not for anybody in particular.*
>
> *If you want a thing from me, ask the board. Not me.*
>
> *And this, and I am putting it in writing because I have worked out that I am not able to do it in words: **There is a man walking in this city with a barrow and a coat of papers and no standing in any district in it, and there is a notice up with a name at the third line of it, and the notice is not mine and I cannot get it down.** I can do one thing about a thing I cannot get down and this is the one thing I can do.*
>
> *He is not to be stopped about me. He is not to be asked about me.*
>
> *He has not been asked about me and if he says that his name is on nothing then that is the end of it and nobody is to follow him and nobody is to write about him.*
>
> *I have not asked him whether I might put him in here. **He can take down that I did not ask.**
>
> *Mara Vale.*

---

**And that is the whole of it, and there are about two hundred people a day walking past that board, and it is on a public wall in a city of about nine thousand people, and it has been there — he looked at the paper, which had been out of the weather, not how long — and a man who has been in this city nineteen days can read it as he goes past.**

He read it twice.

The first time he read it for what it said. The second time he read it for what it cost, and it cost four lines, and four of the eight lines on that sheet are about a brother.

**And he found out where she is by not asking, and the difficulty is the whole of it.**

**He did not have to ask whether she was in this city. She put it in a public place in her own hand, in her own name, so that a man with a barrow would find it whether or not he was looking.**

And that is the difficulty, and he wrote it down in about nine words on a step later and it is the whole of the hundred and fourteenth day: **a woman who publishes what you would prefer hidden has taken the asking off you, and the taking is the gift, and it is not a gift, and both of those are true and they do not cancel.**

Six years of not writing. One note in a highland town that never bore her signature. Two leaves he did not take. A store list he read and left. A line in his own book on the hundred and sixth that said he was not going to write to her, because she did not ask him.

**And a sheet on a board on the top terrace of a capital with her name at the foot of it, and four lines in the middle of it that are about him, and no address, and no hour, and nothing to answer.**

**And the four lines are the cost, and he is the only person in this city who will ever know what they cost, and nobody is going to tell him.**

Read them again:

> *He is not to be stopped about me. He is not to be asked about me.*

**She has described a man she has not seen, in a street she is not in, to a public board, in front of about two hundred people a day. And the description is accurate, and the reason it is accurate is that there is a notice in this city with a name at the third line of it and a barrow goes up four flights in front of about two hundred people most days, and she has put the two together and she has been right about both, and it has taken her about four lines and it has taken her no reason at all.**

And this is the part that he will not get over for a long time and has written down once:

**She did not ask him. She could not have asked him. That is the whole reason there is a board.**

Two days in a week, a man with a barrow goes up four flights. There is no other way to say a thing to a man in a capital that does not begin with finding out where he sleeps. **And the way she found out where he was is a public board, and a public board is the only surface in this kingdom with nothing standing on it, and she used it, and it is the correct instrument, and he has been using it every day of his life for a hundred and fourteen days and has never once thought of her as a person who does that.**

**He has been thinking of her as a thing he lost. And a thing he lost is not a person who publishes an instruction. And there is a woman in this kingdom — he does not know where, and he is not going to find out tonight, and the sheet says so — who reads a list of ninety words and a board of nine hundred lines and puts a true thing in a public place and does not get thanked for it and is not going to ask.**

---

**And he did not take it off the board, and that is the third time, and the third time is the same act with a different name each time, and he wrote down that it is the third.**

> *A leaf off a board in a market town, on the ninety-first, and I did not take it because a paper off a public board in a Crown town is the same act with a different name as five sheets off a table in a room.*
>
> *A leaf off the Office's own board on this terrace, on the ninety-sixth, and I did not take it for the same reason and I had known the reason the first time and it was still the reason the second time.*
>
> *And a sheet off a board on this terrace today, with her name at the foot of it, and **I did not take it, and that is the third time, and the three of them are one decision and I have not been able to find a new name for it, and I have been trying since the fourth hour.**

---

**And he did not go to her, and he did not write, and he did not stand outside a building on her account, and the reason he gives is the third of three he wrote out flat on the ninety-first, and it is the worst of the three, and he is not going to improve it in a month.**

> *There are three reasons I have not gone and not written, and the first two are good and the third is the one I am going to use.*
>
> *The first is that a leaf pinned at an angle under a drawing-pin is a thing I have decided not to take and it was the same decision twice, and a man who makes the same decision twice out of habit is a man who has stopped deciding.*
>
> *The second is that if a person in a capital learns a thing about me from a piece of paper then the person has learned it from a piece of paper, and that is a sentence about a person and not about me.*
>
> *And the third is the worst and it is the one I am using: **a man who goes and asks so that he can afterwards say he did not go is doing the thing for himself.** I have got a sheet on a board that says in four lines that a man with a barrow is not to be asked about her, and there is no address on it and no hour on it and nothing to answer, and if I go and look for where she sleeps then I will be able to write in this book that I did not write, **and that entry would be about me, and it would be the fourth one, and I have three and I did not make the first three.**
>
> *So I am not going. **There is now a public sheet of her own hand in this city that has told me where she is and I am not going to use it, and I am not going to be thanked for that either, because there is nothing in it for anybody else to be thanked about. It is a thing I am not doing about myself.**

---

**And he stood in front of that board for about half an hour in the middle of the day while about two hundred people walked past it, and not one of them stopped, and that is the finding of the hundred and fourteenth day and it is the plainest thing he has learned in nineteen days.**

**About two hundred people a day walk past a board on the top terrace of Sennet. They walk past nine Crown sheets and a handbill about a horse and a sheet of paper with four drawing-pins in it, and none of them stops. Two days' road back, in a market, there is a board with a leaf pinned at an angle under a drawing-pin that has been up since the ninety-first, and nobody stops at that one either, and the reason is the same one in both towns, and the reason is that a board is a thing you read while you are walking.**

**A board has no reason-box on it. A board is the only surface in this kingdom that a person can say a true thing on and owe nobody a why, and about two hundred people a day go past mine, and every one of them is a person who could have read it and did not read it, and that is not because of the Office and it is not because of her and it is not because of me.**

**It is a board. About nine hundred bells in this city and about four hundred a year who go into a court and put a true sentence on a wall for fourpence, and both of those move nothing, and this moves nothing, and I have put my hand on the rail of it four times in nineteen days and it has not moved a single thing in either direction.**

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step at the foot of that same stair with the tarpaulin over the cradle and the four bolts made fast, and the sack rolled against the wheel, and Tamsin Rook nine feet off his left shoulder, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and fourteenth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.**

> *The hundred and fourteenth. **She is in this city. She published it on a public board in her own name so that a man with a barrow would find it whether or not he was looking, and there is no address on the sheet and no hour on it and nothing to answer, and about two hundred people a day walk past it and not one of them stops.**
>
> *Second column, and the reason she is right. **She is right about the store list and she was right about the room and the reason is a thing about eleven lines in a file and a column with nothing in it.** She said *read the store list, not the room*, on a board on this terrace on the ninety-sixth, and I read the list on the hundred and first, and the list said nothing about the ninety words, and I have spent nineteen days finding out that the reason the list says nothing is that nobody in this kingdom knows, **and the reason nobody knows is a plate and a four-inch box at the end of a form and a man who will not write a word he cannot read back, and she would not have known that either, and she sent me to the right board anyway.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and it is four lines and it is the price. **She has put me in a public place without asking me, in a street, in front of about two hundred people a day, and she has done it out of the correct instrument, and the description is accurate, and the only reason it is accurate is that there is a notice up with my name at the third line of it and a barrow goes up four flights most days.** A sheet off a board in a Crown town is the same act with a different name as five sheets off a table in a room. **A description of a man in a public board is not that, and I have been telling myself for a day that it is not that, and it is not that, and I have written that down twice and I am going to write it once more tonight and then I am going to stop, because a man who writes the same sentence three times is a man arranging something.**
>
> *And the thing I have not been able to put down. **She has done the thing I do every day of my life and she has done it better and earlier and she has not been thanked for it once that I know of, and I have never once in six years thought of her as a person who does that, because I have been thinking of her as a thing I lost.** A thing I lost is not a person who publishes a standing instruction in her own name on a public board. **I have been talking to a version of a woman made out of a day-book, and the day-book is mine, and the woman on the board has got four lines in her own hand and the day-book has got none.**
>
> *And I am not going. **The third of the three reasons, and the worst one, and I wrote it out flat on the ninety-first and it is the sentence I have now put in this book twice and I am not going to put it in a third time: a man who goes and asks so that he can afterwards say he did not go is doing the thing for himself.** The sheet is on a board and there is nothing on it to answer, and if I go and look for where she sleeps then the only new thing in this book is about me, **and that would be the fourth entry about me and I did not make the first three and I am not going to make the fourth.** I have not written. I have not taken it off the board, and both of those are the same decision and I have been trying since the fourth hour to find a new name for it and there is not one.
>
> *Nobody has been taken off a board in this business and nothing has been taken off any board since the ninety-first, when there was a leaf pinned at an angle under a drawing-pin in a market two days' road back. **And nothing is resolved, and nobody is rescued, and she is not a thing I have been given, and she is not a thing I can call on, and I have not been thanked and I have not thanked anybody.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it has been thirty feet off me in the open all day and a barrow is not a building. The carrier in the sack untouched, thirty-seven days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents in a coat, and a sheet on a board four streets and four flights up that has her name at the foot of it and is not a fourteenth, because it is the court's wall and not mine, and because it is not about me, and I want that written down in that order.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a public board with about two hundred people a day going past it and no reason-box on it, and it is the only surface in this kingdom where a person owes nobody a why, and she has used it against me, kindly, in her own name, and I have been nineteen days in this city looking for a room with a door in it and the door is a wall with a sheet on it and the sheet says go away.** And there is not one thing I can do about that which would not be about me.
