# Chapter 0148 — The Record, The Map, And Eleven Lines In His Own Hand

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and thirty-fourth day and the man in the coat came to the room with a printed sheet in his hand and put it on the table on top of the form, and he did not sit down.

“**I have got a thing to say to you in about four seconds and then we go.**”

“**Say it.**”

“**There is no form in this kingdom that lets a district keep a man, and there is no form in this kingdom that lets a court keep a man either, and I have got a matter with your name at the head of it and a day on it, and a matter goes where a matter goes, and it is going to the court that sits for this district because that is where matters go and there is nowhere else for it to go.**”

“**And me.**”

“**And you.**” He said it without any weight on it. “**I am not detaining you. There is nothing on this to detain anybody with. I am walking you up a corridor to a room where a clerk will enter your matter, and after that you are a man with a matter in his name, and it goes up the river, and you go with it or you do not go with it and either of those is correct and I am not going to argue it in a corridor.**”

“**How long.**”

“**Until somebody with a standing comes.**” He picked the sheet up. “**And that could be a while, and I am not going to tell you it will not be, because a bench in a room off a lane is where men go to be forgotten and I have not got any power over that and neither has anybody else in this building.**”

---

They went up a corridor. And in the corridor he stopped at a table with a bundle on it and undid the string on it, because a bundle has to be undone to find a sheet in it, and that is not a thing anybody does on purpose.

There were about nine hundred things in that bundle. **Schedules. Numbers, wards, days, lines ruled in columns, and a scale in the corner, and a sheet folded in three about the size of two hands together, with the same rules ruled on it in the same way and a different black.**

He read about four inches of it in about a minute and it took him about a minute and a half to find the place in the run where about a third of it was not there any more.

**Not torn. Not cut. The paper is smooth and uncreased in a rectangle about the size of two hands together and the column rules that rule the panels on either side of it stop short and do not cross it, and the ink in the panels either side is a slightly different black from the ink in the rest of the sheet, and the sheet has been folded and unfolded about nine hundred times and this rectangle is the one place on it that has not been folded through.**

**And it is in the same place. That is what I looked at for the minute and a half. It is in the same place in the middle of a run, and there is a run of wards above it and a run of wards below it and one rectangle between them with nothing in it, and the numbers either side of it go on without a break and there is no gap in them and no mark on the paper at all where the rules stop.**

**I do not know whether that is a thing done to every copy or a thing done to the plate, and I am not going to work it out on a table in a corridor, and I am not going to write it in this book tonight, and I am not going to join it to the second empty fourth box on a form in a printer's room two streets off or to the keeper's field ruled past the end of a line on a water-bell return in a county I have left.**

**Because three empties seen in three counties is a man arranging a thing, and I have had that rule since the ninetieth and I have not broken it yet and I am not going to break it in a corridor with a bundle open in front of me.**

The man in the coat tied the string back.

“**That is the district's and it goes with the matter.**”

“**I know.**”

“**It is not a paper I have got any power to give you. The paper goes with the matter and the matter goes with you, because there is no form in this kingdom that lets a district keep a man, and a schedule is part of the paper.**” He put the bundle into Tomas Vale's hands, which nobody had asked him to do, and it took him about a second. “**Do not lose it. There is a copy of that at the store and I have never once been told what is on the copy and I am not going to look.**”

---

He carried it up the stair and out into the street with the barrow thirty feet away on the flat and the four bolts made fast, and he did not ask for it, and he did not want it, and it was not his doing, and he wanted it.

> ***And there is no form anywhere in this kingdom that says who may read a thing, and the absence of that box is the whole of this and it is in a form and not in a room, and a schedule in a man's hands in a street cannot be taken off him because there is no box on any paper in this kingdom that wants a man to give one back.** That is the fifth clause's seventh job in a hundred and thirty-four days and it did not cost me anything, and I am not going to be sad about that, because every one of the other six cost me something and this one gave me a thing.
>
> ***And it is a map and not a key. I cannot read it. There is no form in this kingdom for reading a map, and there is nobody in this capital I could ask, and a map shows where a line runs and cannot show whose hand altered it, and four people in four counties have said that to me one after another and none of them owed me anything and I have not asked any of them anything and I am not going to.** I can see wards and numbers and a scale. I cannot see a run. I have never been able to and there is no trade in it and there is no craft in it and reading metal is the whole of what I can do and a map is not metal.

---

At about the sixth hour he sat on a step on a stair landing inside a building and undid the string on his own book and looked at the fold where the eleven lines have been since the hundred and eleventh.

**I have done this three times. I want the three of them in the right order in one place because this is the third and it is the only one where I am going to keep it.**

The first was on the hundred and fifteenth, on a step by the river, the night before I went and swore four sentences of my own in a public room for fourpence. I wrote the eleven out and I looked at them for about four minutes and I did not take them with me, **and the reason was that a wall is a public thing and about forty people a week read that wall, and a name goes at the head of a line, and she is not in it and I am not going to be the reason.**

The second was on the hundred and nineteenth, in a public room four streets and four flights down, standing nine feet off a rail, and I did not put them in a market of about nine hundred people, **and the reason was a column has no family in it and a name is not a column, and I have still not improved that and I am not going to.**

And this is the third, and the reason is not a reason about her at all.

> ***The only way to get the truest thing I have ever read out of my own head is to write it down, and the moment I write it down it stops being the only instrument in this kingdom that cannot be produced and it becomes the most expensive one there is.** A thing in a head cannot be got off a man by anybody and cannot be given to anybody and the only person in the world who can copy one out of a head is the man it is in, and he cannot hand it to a clerk, and he cannot hand it to a woman, and he cannot say it at ten feet in a market because there are about nine hundred people in there and about nine of them will say it in another town inside a year.
>
> ***So: a maker's whole life is work that cannot be edited. And I have carried the only unproducible thing in this kingdom for a hundred and thirty-four days and I am spending it now, on purpose, on paper, for myself, because I am in a room in a building where a bundle with about nine hundred lines in it is going to a court and I want to be able to look at the truest thing my mother ever said while somebody is deciding what to do with me.** That is the reason and it is about four inches long and it is about me.
>
> ***And the price is that it is in my hands, and the price of that is that anybody can take it off me now.** A man who cannot be found cannot be produced against. I have been unfindable for a hundred and thirty-four days and that has been the whole of my protection and it was in a head, and it was the last of the four instruments, and I have just turned it into the second one, **and there is no plate in a printer's room two streets off that cuts this and there never will be, and I have done it with a pen and about nine minutes and nobody helped me.**
>
> ***And I am not sorry, and I am not going to say it was worth it, and the reason I am not sorry is that a duty is a thing a person accepts and I have accepted this one and it took eleven days and I would do it again and I would do it sooner, and I have never had a sentence at all about what became of her, and there is nothing whatever I can do with a duty, and I have stopped thinking that there should be.***

He wrote them out. In his own hand, on the back of a leaf of his own book, in about nine minutes, and he did not shake, and his hand was as good as it had ever been, and that is the whole of the scene and there is nothing else in it.

> *I will answer once. I told the court I would and I am doing it and I am not going to be asked again in this room.*
>
> *The first thing is that a person is not the only thing that carries an answer, and I did not know that until I read four sills over four nights, and I wrote down what I read, and I did not take the sheet to anybody for nine days.*
>
> *What I read was this. A man's answer going out from a place where he was not standing, in his own voice, and him not knowing that it had gone. Not a bell heard twice by a wall. **The same words, in the same order, in a hand the shape of his, in a district two days' road off, and nobody there able to say it was not his, because it was.***
>
> *That is the whole of what I found. I found it by reading. Nobody told me and I have not got a name to give anybody and I am not going to be asked for one.*
>
> *The second thing is mine and I am not going to be sorry for it in this room or out of it.*
>
> *When I had the sheet I did not take it to the district.*
>
> *I took nine of the things and I hid them and I hid them myself.*
>
> ***I hid the first Crown clappers.***
>
> *I have said it out loud in a room with a clerk in it and a man writing. It is in his book and it was not in mine, and I watched him write it, and I want that entered in the order it happened, and it happened before he wrote it.*
>
> *Nine of them. I put them where a man making a bell would not go. It took eleven days and I have counted the eleven a great many times since and I would do it again and I would do it sooner.*
>
> *After that I answered nothing else to anybody about the nine, or the sheet, or where they went, and I am not starting today, because you have asked me four times on four days and I said yes once, and once is what I said I would do.*

**That is the whole of it. Eleven lines and two things and then it stops, and it stops in the middle of a subject, the way a record stops, which is to say that it does not stop at all.**

**A copy of eleven lines out of a head is not a copy of a court record, is not a document in my register, and is not a fourteenth; the copy in that room belongs to that room and does not leave it, and the copy in my book is mine, and the register is the thirteen and stays the thirteen.**

He folded the leaf and put it inside the front of the book and tied the string on the bundle, and the eleven lines are in a coat in a city of about nine thousand people for the first time in a hundred and thirty-four days.

---

At about the seventh hour, on the middle terrace, in a lane off the terrace, the woman who lets a room by the hour was shutting a window, and Tamsin Rook gave her four lines in her own hand and did not read them to anybody and did not explain them and did not ask for anything back, and the woman took the paper and put it inside her apron and shut the window.

**I saw it from about thirty feet and I did not go over and I did not ask her what it said and I did not ask her who it was for and I am not going to, and the fourth clause of an arrangement I made in a room over that shop on the hundred and twentieth is that I will not ask either of them for anything, and if either of them does a thing I will not ask what it was for and I will not write it down and I will not say thank you.** She is not holding it. It is not in my coat. **If any writing of that arrangement exists at the end of this, it exists because one of the other two wrote it and I have not got it, and that is not my doing, and I wanted it, and I did not ask for it.**

---

At about the ninth hour he went up four flights with the bundle in his coat and the book inside it and did not go into anything, and on the top terrace there are about two hundred people a day who walk past a public wall and not one of them stops, and there are nine Crown sheets on it and a handbill about a horse, and between the ninth sheet and the handbill there is a sheet of paper with four drawing-pins in it, one at each corner, and the woman with the bag of pins had not got to it, and the nails were in the board and the sheet was not on them.

**He could have taken it down. That is the fifth time and it is the same decision and the standing reason is four inches long and it has not improved in forty-six days, and it is a paper off a public board in a Crown town being the same act with a different name as five sheets off a table in a room, and I have refused that four times and I am not going to find a new name for the fifth, and she put it there and she can take it down, and the last line of it says I can take down that she did not ask, and I am not going to be the man who finally takes the thing down because he has been asked.**

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and thirty-fourth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and thirty-fourth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A corridor, a bundle undone on a table, a schedule folded in three put into my hands by a man who was not asked to do it, eleven lines copied out in my own hand on the back of a leaf, four lines written by somebody else and given to a woman who shuts a window, and a board on the top terrace with four drawing-pins in it and the nails in the board and the sheet not on them.**
>
> *Second column, and the two things. **The schedule is a map and it is not a key. I cannot read it. There is no form in this kingdom for reading one and there is nobody in this capital I could ask, and a map shows where a line runs and cannot show whose hand altered it.** About a third of the way along a run there is a rectangle about two hands together with nothing in it, and the paper is smooth and uncreased there and the rules stop short and do not cross it and the ink either side is a slightly different black, **and it is in the same place, and I do not know whether that is a thing done to every copy or a thing done to the plate, and I am not going to find out in a corridor and I am not going to write it down tonight and I am not going to join it to anything I have seen in three counties.** It came with a matter because a schedule is part of the paper and the paper goes with the man because there is no form in this kingdom that lets a district keep one, **and the absence of any box on any paper that says who may read a thing is the whole of this and it is in a form and not in a room, and it is the fifth clause's seventh job and it did not cost me anything and I am not going to be sad about that.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the price. **I have written the eleven lines out in my own hand for the third time in a hundred and thirty-four days and this time I have kept it.** The first was on a step on the hundred and fifteenth and the second was on a rail in a court on the hundred and nineteenth, and both of those were a man refusing to put his mother in a column, **and this one is not about her at all. The only way to get the truest thing I have ever read out of my own head is to make it into the instrument I have been refusing since before we left Kells, and the price of having it in my hands is that it is in my hands.** A thing in a head cannot be got off a man and cannot be given to anybody and the only person who can copy one out of a head is the man it is in. **I have carried the only unproducible thing in this kingdom for a hundred and thirty-four days and tonight I have spent it, on purpose, on paper, for myself, because there is a clerk in this building putting a matter with my name on it into a book and I wanted to be able to look at the truest thing she ever said while somebody decided what to do with me.** I am not sorry and I am not going to say it was worth it.
>
> *And four lines in her hand going into an apron, and I saw it from thirty feet and I did not go over and I did not ask and I am not going to, **and the fourth clause of a hundred and twenty days says I will not ask either of them for anything, and if either of them does a thing I will not ask what it was for and I will not write it down and I will not say thank you.** If any writing of that arrangement exists it exists because one of the other two wrote it and I have not got it. **That was not my doing, I wanted it, and I did not ask for it, and that is the fifth clause doing its work for the seventh time and it has cost me the only thing I wanted.**
>
> *And a board. **I could have taken her sheet off that wall. That is the fifth untaking and it is one decision with a different name each time and the reason is four inches long and it has not improved in forty-six days.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and no document produced, given or asked for, and no name transferred, and no child found by a document, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a corridor and a stair landing and a street and a terrace are not a building and I was in a room yesterday for a whole day and the barrow was on the flat. **The carrier in the sack, not rung, fifty-seven days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about me, and a schedule in a district's keeping that came into my hands because a district cannot keep a man, and eleven lines on a leaf in my own book which is mine and is not a document in the register and is not a fourteenth, and a copy of a sworn sentence of mine that is a district's.** A map is a map. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing today and I have been given a thing and I did not ask.
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a bundle with about nine hundred lines in it, a schedule folded in three, a form with four boxes, a court that sits for eleven districts, and a woman who shuts a window with four lines in her apron.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A record is the court's and does not leave the building and a file is the district's and nobody may read it, and a schedule is part of a paper, and a paper goes with a man because there is no form in this kingdom that lets a district keep a man, and that is not a door anybody opened for me and nobody is thanked.**
