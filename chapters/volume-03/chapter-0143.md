# Chapter 0143 — What Three Are, And Who Said So First

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and twenty-ninth day and Tomas Vale paid a woman cash for an hour of a room over a shop on the middle terrace, and the barrow stayed at the foot of the top stair with the four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it was not in that room.

**A man who is given a room is a man who owes. That is four inches long and it has not improved in a hundred and twenty-nine days and I am not going to improve it now.**

He put the four sentences on the table between them. Not out of his book — he had torn nothing out, and there was no paper in that room and there was not going to be one — but he said them, once, in the ordinary voice, at ten feet, in the order they are in, the same order they have been in since the hundred and sixth.

The woman who let the room was on the hearth with her back to them and she did not turn round, and she has not been asked anything and she is not going to be.

---

He said it once. Nine minutes, and it took nine minutes both times, and it is the shape of every bell he has rung four times in his life and the shape of the whole of the trade.

She let him finish.

“**Say the last part again.**”

He said the last part again.

“**That is the part I can do.**”

“**That is the part anybody can do.**”

“**No it is not.**” She did not move. “**It is the only part I have been able to do for eleven years and I have been doing it at ten feet in a market with a slate under my arm and no paper in it, and about four in ten of the people standing there carry it into another town inside a year, and every one of them says it right the first time and more wrong after that, and the day the last of them is gone there is nowhere anybody can go and find out it was said.**”

“**I know that. It is not a network and it is not scalable and it cannot be done twice by one man.**”

“**It is mine and I built it and it is eleven years old.**”

“**And the other half of it.**”

“**The other half of it I cannot do and I am not going to pretend to you that I can, because pretending is the one thing I have never done and I am not going to start in a room you have paid for.**” She turned her head about two inches. “**What you have written on that table wants a person to be in a road with a thing in their hands, and there is no version of me in a road. I can stand in a market on the ground with nine hundred people under me and I cannot stand in a road and I have never been able to and it is not going to be arranged this afternoon.**”

“**I have not asked you to stand anywhere.**”

“**You have not. And that is the whole of your trouble and I am not going to be kind about it, because being kind about it is a thing people do to each other in rooms they pay for.**”

---

She was quiet for about as long as it takes to count nine.

“**That is my answer and it is not a yes and it is not a no, and it is mine, and I am not going to be asked for it a second time, and I am not going to improve it in front of you so that you can put it somewhere.**”

“**I was not going to write it down.**”

“**I know, and that is why I said it in a room you paid for and not in a market, and if you write it down tonight I will know, and I will not have said it.**”

“**I will not write it down.**”

“**Do not ask me again.**”

“**I am not going to ask you again.**”

“**You will, in a month, and I will say the same thing, and that is the correct arrangement and it is not a wound and it is not going to become one.**”

---

At about the fifth hour he did not ask the other one.

**There is a sheet on a public wall on the top terrace four streets and four flights up with a name at the foot of it and four lines about a man with a barrow, and she set those terms five days before any of this existed and she does not know that any of this exists, and he is not going to go up four flights this afternoon and he is not going to write to her, and he is not going to stand under a building on her account, and that is the third and worst of three reasons he wrote out flat on the ninety-first and it has not improved in thirty-eight days.**

He did not write the reason down this time either.

**I have written the three reasons once and I have not looked at them since and I am not going to write them again tonight. A man who writes his own reasons down every time he does not do a thing is a man building a case, and I have not got a case, and I have got a barrow.**

---

At about the sixth hour they went down through the market, which is on the ground, and where a man on the top row can see a man at the bottom of it and cannot hear him, and there were about nine hundred people in it and about two hundred of them in front of a wall.

**It is the cheapest instrument in this kingdom and it is a fact about the shape of a hill and it is the reason anybody has ever been able to say a true thing out loud in a market and have no office hear about it in nine minutes. It was in my mouth for about four minutes and I did not say it, and she was four feet off my left shoulder and she did not stop me, and neither of us has said one word about that and I am not going to.**

> *If I say it at the bottom of that market then about nine hundred people have it and about nine of them will have said it in another town inside a year and every one of them will be right the first time and more wrong after that, and in about two years there will be a version with a detail in it that was never on anything. And a thing said in a market cannot be entered anywhere and cannot be produced by anybody and dies with the people who heard it, one at a time, over about nine years. And the reason I am not going to say it is not that it would not work. **It would work. That is the whole difficulty. It is the only instrument in this kingdom that has ever moved a line and I have got it and I am not going to use it, because the instant a man with no standing puts a thing into a market, the thing stops being about the thing and it is about the man, and that is the same sentence as the arranging and the same sentence as the asking, and I have now written it three times in three different months and it is always one sentence.**
>
> *And a man who puts a claim in a market does not need a keeper, because a market is not a keeper. **A public signal in a place needs three independent answers, each of them free to say yes, no, later, or this much and not that, and each of them in its own words, and the rule that does the work is four inches long: you set the claim down once and you read the same words to each of them in the same order, and if you improve the words for the second one because the first one frightened you, you are not collecting answers, you are arranging agreement.**

At about the seventh hour he worked out the arithmetic standing at the foot of the top stair with the barrow behind him, and it came out where it always comes out, and this time he did not put it at the bottom of the day as though putting it there were doing something.

> *A man can be one of the three answers. **A man cannot be two of them, and the second one he is, the first one stops being an answer and becomes a man saying the same thing twice.** And there is no form in this kingdom on which a man may take the same words to three people and call the result a count, and if there were one then all three sentences would be mine and not one of them would be anybody's answer.
>
> ***I am one of these three people and I cannot be two. And the two I need are not going to say it, and the reason they are not going to say it is correct.** One of them said it in writing on the hundred and sixth, underlined, and has not changed a word of it in twenty-three days. The other one published an instruction on a public wall five days before I met anybody, addressed to nobody, saying she will not be found and will not answer and will not be sent for, **and she has never met me, and I have read it twice and I am not going to read it a third time, and if I go and ask her then the asking is the only thing in the arrangement and there will be nothing else in it.**
>
> *And so: **the three I need are not a quantity of people. They are a quantity of freedom.** I have been writing *if there were three of me* at the bottom of a day for twenty-four days as though it were a shortage of men, and it is not a shortage of men, **and I could not manufacture a third anybody if I had the whole of this city and a plate and a clerk, and I would not want to, because the whole of what a warning is, is that anybody can walk away from it.**

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and twenty-ninth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and twenty-ninth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **An hour of a room over a shop paid for in cash, a claim read out once in the same words in the same order to one person, and nobody asked anybody anything, and the slate was under my arm the whole hour and I did not put a question on it and I am not going to write down that I did not.**
>
> *Second column, and what I asked for. **Three independent answers to a claim I set down once. She gave me one that is not a yes and not a no and is hers and I am not going to write it down and she told me not to and that is the correct arrangement.** I did not ask the other one and I did not go up four flights and I did not write and I did not stand under a building, and the reason is the third of three reasons I wrote out on the ninety-first and it has not improved in thirty-eight days and I am not going to improve it tonight.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the arithmetic. **A man can be one of the three answers and cannot be two, and there is no form in this kingdom on which I could be two, and that is a rule about counting and it is not a power and I am not going to write it down as one.** I am one short and I know the shape of the two, and going after them is the one thing that would finish this and empty it, **and if I were to go and make them say it I would have the three and I would have nothing else.**
>
> *And I did not say it in the market. **It was on the ground and there were about nine hundred people in it and the four minutes I stood there with it are the four minutes I have now written down twice this month, and the second time is shorter.** She was four feet off and she did not stop me, and that is the whole of what either of us did about it. It is the only instrument in this kingdom that has ever moved a line and it is hers and it is eleven years old and **the reason I am not going to use it is that the instant a man with no standing puts a thing into a market the thing is about the man. I have written that down three times in three months and it has been one sentence every time and I am going to stop writing it down and start obeying it.**
>
> *About four hundred people a year put a true sentence on a public wall for fourpence and it moves nothing. A thing said twice at ten feet in the open moves about four in ten and dies with the people who heard it. **The three I need are not a shortage of men. They are a quantity of freedom, and I cannot manufacture a third anybody and would not want to, and if there were three of me it would still only be me three times and a man talking is a man talking.** I am not going to write that at the bottom of a day again and expect it to be a plan.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no claim made in a bell and no answers collected, and no document produced or given or asked for, and no sheet taken off a board.** Four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a room over a shop is a building and I was in one for an hour and the barrow was at the foot of the stair for it. **The carrier in the sack, not rung, fifty-two days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and she has not taken it out since a market of about nine hundred people on the ninety-second.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about me, and there was no paper in that room this afternoon and there is not going to be one, and a thing said once in a room to one person is not a fourteenth and it is not in anybody's hands including mine.** I have not asked for a copy of anything in a hundred and twenty-nine days.
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a room over a shop with a plate on the table that has been on that table since the room was built, and two people in it, and a third person's terms on a public wall in this city that she has not been asked about and does not know exists, and about four hundred true sentences a year on a wall for fourpence that move nothing, and a market on the ground that is the only place in this kingdom a person can say a true thing and not have an office hear it in nine minutes, and I had a thing in my mouth in that market for four minutes and did not say it.** Nobody in this kingdom is wicked and nobody is owed a visit and nobody was thanked this afternoon and I have got one of the three answers and not two, and the one I have is my own, **and the three I need are a quantity of freedom and not a quantity of people, and if there were three of me it would be me three times.***
