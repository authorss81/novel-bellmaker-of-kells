# Chapter 0117 — The Day Before A Room

It was the second hour of the hundred and third day of the month and Tomas Vale stood about nine feet off a door at the top of a stair in a capital city of about nine thousand people for about four minutes, and then he went down the four flights and did not go in, and the whole of what he is going to say about that room this evening is those four minutes and what happened before them and after them and nothing at all about what happened in it.

**He had five lines on a sheet in his coat. He had written them at about the first hour at a step at the foot of the stair with his knees across his knees and it had taken him about nine minutes and he had not improved any of them.**

---

**The five lines, in his own hand, with the hour against them, and he read the fifth one back to himself at about the first hour and then did not read it again:**

> *One. What I know. I made a claim in public, in a market, in my own voice, at about nine feet off a trestle, in front of about four hundred people. It is mine. I have not said it was anybody else's and I am not going to unsay it here.*
>
> *Two. What I do not know. I do not know what a coupling and a jumper are for. I do not know what any of the ninety words in a store's list are for, because the column for that is empty and the man who keeps it will not fill it in and he is right. I do not know who takes a name off a line.*
>
> *Three. My own fault. I have no standing in this district or in any district, and I put a matter into a hundred's business that was not mine to put there, and I did it in the open, in a market, on a market day, and about two hundred people read my name off a board afterwards and nine of them will say it in another town inside a year, and I knew that when I did it and I did it anyway, and it is mine and I am not going to hand it to anybody in this room.*
>
> *Four. What I am not here for. I am not here to have anything decided. I am here so that a thing is said in a room and entered in a book with the hour against it, and I would like to be told the column it went in.*
>
> ***Five. And I speak for the household of my father's house in Kells, being of it, and any answer given in this room is the answer of that household and not mine alone.***

---

**He stood about nine feet off that door for about four minutes at the second hour.**

The door is a door. There is a step and a rail and a lamp and a board inside the rail with about nine sheets on it, and the sheets are printed, and he did not read them and he has written down that he did not read them, and the reason is that he has read about nine hundred sheets in four counties and a city and there are four boxes on all of them and none of them is the words.

**Nobody came out of the door. Nobody looked at him. At about the second hour on a day with a hearing named in it at the top, in a capital of about nine thousand people, a man with a barrow and a five-line sheet in his coat stood nine feet off a door and nothing whatever happened, and that is not a story about a door and it is a fact about a door, and he has written it down flat and he is not going to make it into anything.**

Then he went down the four flights.

---

**And the hour after that, at about the third hour, a man in a yard at the foot of the stair told him what a hearing is, and the man is called Ormond Bly, and he has kept a yard of packing cases at the foot of that stair for nine years, and he has a gate on his yard with a bell on it that he strikes himself, and he asked for none of this and gave all of it.**

Bly was stacking cases when the barrow came to the rail and stopped, and he did not stop stacking, and he said:

“**You are not going up.**”

“**I have not been up.**”

“**You have been standing at that door at the second hour and everybody on this stair has watched you do it, and there is a woman in a stall on the middle terrace who has watched you do it from about four hundred yards and I do not know her and you do, and I am telling you that you have been watched and not that anybody has come.**”

---

**And then he put a case down and came out to the rail, and he said the thing that is the whole of the hundred and third day:**

“**I have been in that room twice. Once, nine years back, as a witness. And I was in it for about an hour and a half and nobody asked me a single question.**”

“**A witness.**”

“**A witness. That is the word the notice uses. And a witness is not summoned to produce, and I had a bag of papers in my hand that would have answered anything anybody could have asked me, and I sat in that room for an hour and a half with the bag on my knees and I have never found out whether I was permitted to give them or whether I was not permitted to ask.**”

He wiped his hands on his coat.

“**And a claimant is summoned to produce. That is the difference, and it is not a difference of truth, it is a difference of what the room wants off you. A claimant is a person the room expects to have brought something, and the office had to invent a word for you to put in its notice, and I have thought about the word it chose for about nine days, and the word it chose is *claimant*, and the whole of that word is *produce*.**”

---

**And then he said the half that is the finding of this chapter, and he said it standing at a rail with a case in his hand, in the ordinary voice, and it took him about nine seconds.**

“**And here is what I would tell you if you asked me, and you have not asked me, and I am telling you because you have got a barrow and you cannot get rid of it and a man who is going to be in trouble anyway may as well have the true one.**

“**A man who walks into that room with nothing in his hands is a man who will be asked *where is it*.** Not by a man who wants to be cruel. By a clerk with a column and a form, because that is the room, and the room was built by people who were only ever in rooms where everybody brings something. **And there is no sentence in this kingdom for the answer to that question. There is no box for it. There is a box for a person, a box for a district, a box for a thing and a box for a reason, and a man who says *I have nothing* has to say it into a column about a thing, and the column wants a thing.**”

“**And about four thousand people a year bring a paper to a counter at the foot of these stairs and ask what it is. Not one of them has ever been up them. I have not either, and I have wanted to know what is up them for about a year, and I have never asked, and I am not going to now, because you are about to go and ask and I am not going to make a queue of it.**”

---

**And then Tomas Vale heard the half of it that nobody had said to him in a hundred and three days, and it went into him the way a wrong date goes into a man in a market.**

“**Your notice says in about four lines that a man who has no such paper is not required to account for the absence of one, and that nothing in his possession is to be taken from him on account of the notice. Every word of that is true. It is the best sentence anybody has written for me in a hundred days.**

“**And it is a wall and it is a door in one sheet of paper, and it works both ways at once, and I have not seen anybody do that before.** It means nobody can ask you where it is. **And it means nobody can ask you for it. You will not be asked a single question in that room about your hands, or your coat, or what you have got in a bag four streets away, or why a man with a barrow has come four hundred miles, and there is not one hearing in this kingdom where a man with nothing in his hands is asked nothing, because the point of the room is what you brought.**

“**So the sheet has protected you from being searched and it has also put you in the one room in this kingdom where a man is not asked what he has, and I would like to know what you are going to do with that, and I am not going to tell you, and it is not my business and it is not the district's.**”

---

**And he was right, and he was not sorry, and he was not a door, and he did not ask for anything, and Tomas Vale did not ask him one question about the room. Not what was said in it this morning, not who came, not how long it took, not what was entered. He did not ask, and he wrote down that he did not ask, and the reason is nine words long and they are the reason the clerk at the arch gave about a fair copy and a man in a market gave about his own name.**

**A man who says a true thing in a recorded room and then says it to a stranger has said it twice, and the second one is the one that gets used.**

**He was in the arch four streets and four flights below this yard about an hour after the door shut and he was told nothing about any of it and he did not ask, and there is a record of the room somewhere and it is not in his hands and he did not ask for it, and the not-asking is the fourth refusal in a hundred and three days and it is the only one of the four that he is going to be able to point at in a year.**

---

**And at about the seventh hour he sat down on a step at the foot of the stair and struck a line out.**

He did it with a pencil and he did it in one stroke and he did not read the other four again, and this is the whole of the action of the hundred and third day and it took about four seconds.

> *And I speak for the household of my father's house in Kells, being of it, and any answer given in this room is the answer of that household and not mine alone.*

**One line. Struck out. And the word *not said* written in the margin beside it, which is the second time in a hundred and three days he has written those two words in his own hand, and the first time was a fourth column of his own book on the eighty-second about a fragment of nine lines, and there is no connection between the two things and he checked.**

**And the reason is a clause, and the clause is his, and he asked a clerk to add it to a receipt in a watch house in a county he left on the thirty-third of this month at about the fifth hour of the morning, and he was right, and it is the fourth job it has done.**

> *Clause five says the holder has no standing to speak for a household in Kells or in any ward. **I asked for that clause because I was right. A form cannot answer for a household and I have no standing to be one.**
>
> *And this morning at about the seventh hour, on a step, four flights below a room, it stopped me from saying the only true thing I had, and the only true thing I had was the one a room full of clerks would have wanted, and I have been eleven weeks learning how to be refused by a person and this is the first time in my life a clause of my own has refused me, **and the price of an empty form is not only that you cannot be told anything quietly. The price of an empty form is that when you finally get into the room, the thing you most want to say is the thing you are not allowed to say, and the room does not know it is not allowed, and it will hear me not say it and it will think I had nothing.***

**And that was the fifth line, and it is the only one of the five that anybody in that room would have wanted, and he struck it out on a step with a pencil in about four seconds, and he did not put a sixth line in its place, and that is the first time in his working life that he has taken a thing out of a thing he made and not put anything where it was.**

---

**And here is the thing he found out about his own trade, on that step, at about the seventh hour of the day on which a room was built for him out of a clause and a capacity and a number, and it is the finding of the hundred and third day and he has not been able to put it anywhere since:**

> ***A claim in metal is a thing a man cannot edit. It cannot be added to. It cannot be taken back. It cannot have an hour put on it. It cannot be shown to anybody on a page. That is the one instrument I have that is not a document, and that is the whole of why I have carried it for thirty-three years.***
>
> ***And preparing a hearing is preparing a bell.** Four lines and a bar. The thing I have made ten times in my life went out of the yard of the Vale Bellhouse in four pieces of paper and could not be edited by anybody, and it was the making of it that took the whole of a winter, and then it was a bell and there was no room in which anybody could add a word to it.
>
> **And I stood on a step this afternoon and made one in about nine minutes and it had five lines on it and a bar and a keeper, and I struck one of the lines out after I had made it, which is the one thing I have spent thirty-three years not doing. **A bell cannot be edited after it has been rung. I am about to stand in a room and be edited, and the editor will be right, and the form has four boxes and none of them is the words, and I have never in my life handed a thing to another maker to finish.**
>
> **That is the whole of a hundred and three days. I have been a maker in a kingdom that has a bell on every post and a form on every counter, and this morning somebody made a thing out of me in a room with a lamp and a column, and it took about four minutes, and I was not in it, and I will not be able to argue with a word of it, and the fifth clause that stops the argument is a clause I asked for.**

---

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and third at about the ninth hour of the evening on a step at the foot of the top terrace, with the frame in its cradle four feet off him and the bolts made fast, and it is a late entry and it is the shortest he has written in three days, and he filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and third. **A hearing is a room where a man is asked to produce things, and a claimant is a person the room expects to have brought something, and a witness is not, and the difference is not a difference of truth.***
>
> *Second hour, a step, four flights down. I stood about nine feet off a door for about four minutes at about the second hour on a day with a hearing named in it at the top, and **nobody came out of the door and nobody looked at me and nothing whatever happened, and I am not going to write a paragraph about a door.** I did not read the nine sheets on the board inside the rail and I have written down that I did not, and the reason is that I have read about nine hundred sheets and there are four boxes on all of them and none of them is the words. And I did not go in, and a woman four hundred yards off on the middle terrace watched me not go in, and a man at the foot of this stair told me so in nine seconds, unasked, and did not say anything else about it and is not owed anything by me.*
>
> *Second column, and the finding, and it came out of a man with a gate and a bell of his own who stacks cases at the foot of this stair and has been in that room twice and was asked nothing either time. **A man who goes into a hearing with nothing in his hands is going to be asked where it is, and there is no sentence in this kingdom for that answer and no box for it, because the box wants a thing.** And about four thousand people a year take a paper to a counter at the foot of these stairs and not one of them has ever been up them, and I have not either, and I was watched this morning by a man at this rail and by a woman four hundred yards off, and nothing came.
>
> *And the other half, and it is the half I have not stopped turning over. **My notice says in four lines that a man with no such paper is not required to account for the absence of one and that nothing in his possession is to be taken from him. Every word of it is true and it is the best sentence anybody has written for me in a hundred and three days. And it is a wall and a door in one sheet, and it works both ways at once, and nobody has done that in front of me before.** It means nobody can ask me where it is. **And it means nobody can ask me for it. I will not be asked one question about my hands or my coat or what is in a bag four streets off, and there is not one hearing in this kingdom where a man with nothing is asked nothing, because the point of that room is what you brought.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and I am merging nothing. **I struck the fifth line out.** One line, about four seconds, a pencil, one stroke, the word *not said* in the margin, which is the second time in a hundred and three days I have written those two words and the first was a fourth column of this book on the eighty-second about a fragment of nine lines, and there is no connection between the two and I checked. The line was: **I speak for the household of my father's house in Kells, being of it, and any answer given in this room is the answer of that household and not mine alone.** It was true. It is the only one of the five that anybody in that room would have wanted, and **I have no standing to say it, and the fifth clause of my own receipt is why, and I asked a clerk to add that clause in a watch house in a county I have left, on the thirty-third, at about the fifth hour of the morning, because I was right.** That clause has now done four jobs in four counties and a city: a form stopped at a door, five sheets off a table in about three hours, a notice nailed to a board instead of going into my hand, and this afternoon my own mouth, at the only moment in a hundred and three days when I had something worth saying and there was a room to say it in. **The price of an empty form is not only that you cannot be told anything quietly. It is that when you get into the room, the thing you most want to say is the thing you may not say, and the room will hear you not say it and will think you had nothing.**
>
> *And I did not ask a man in that room what happened in it, and I did not ask the man at this rail what happened in it, and the reason is nine words long: **a man who says a true thing in a recorded room and then says it to a stranger has said it twice, and the second one is the one that gets used.** Four refusals in a hundred and three days and this is the only one I will be able to point at in a year.*
>
> *And the finding of the hundred and third day, and it is a sentence about my own trade and I have not been able to put it anywhere since: **a claim in metal cannot be edited after it has been rung. I have known that for thirty-three years and I have used it as a reason to trust myself. And this afternoon I made one in nine minutes on a step, in four lines and a bar, and then I edited it, and I did not put a sixth line in the gap, and that is the first time in my working life I have handed a thing to another maker to finish.** Somebody made a thing out of me in a room with a lamp and a column, and it took about four minutes, and I was not in it, and I will not be able to argue with a word of it, and I did not go in, and I am not going to say that going in would have been better.*
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung.** Four bolts, the frame on the barrow at the foot of this stair with the cradle made fast, and it has not been inside a building since the thirty-third, and I have been inside two and it makes no difference to the frame. The carrier in the sack untouched, twenty-six days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I have stopped wanting to be told and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents, and a sheet of my own hand with four lines on it and a fifth struck out, and a sheet of my own hand is not a document anybody may take off me and is not a fourteenth and is not going in a coat, and I have written that down because in a month I am going to want to call it one.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a room with a lamp in it and a column and about four thousand people a year who never go up the stairs, and it made a thing out of me in about four minutes in the correct form out of the correct column, and I do not know what the thing is yet and I will know tomorrow, and tomorrow I am going to be in a city of about nine thousand people with a hearing behind me, a form I cannot fill in, a column I cannot fill, a man behind a counter who is right and a woman ten feet off my left shoulder holding nothing.**
