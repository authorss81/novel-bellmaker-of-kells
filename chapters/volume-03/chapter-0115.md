# Chapter 0115 — The List Is Open At A Counter

The list was on the counter at about the third hour of the hundred and first day of the month, and it was nine sheets, and Tomas Vale stood at the end of a counter nine feet long in a room eleven feet by sixteen and read every line of all nine of them in about two hours.

**Nobody asked him who he was.**

**Nobody asked him for a standing. Nobody looked at his hands, which have a cut on the side of one of them that has not closed. Nobody asked him for a paper, a district, a reason, or a name, and nobody refused him any of those things either, because nobody thought of asking. He was a man in a room at the third hour, and that was the whole of what was required of him to be there, and it is the first time since a carrier came into a bellhouse in a highland town that no office has asked him who he is.**

He has written that down and he has written it twice, because it is the cheapest thing that has happened to him in a hundred and one days and it is the only one he has not been able to argue with.

---

The nine sheets were the year's entries, gathered, and the columns were the same five as the book on the counter the day before, and every column was ruled the same way.

**The first was a number, against the instruction. The second was a district. The third was a day. The fourth was the thing, in a word.**

**The fifth was what the thing is for, and it was empty.**

He read the fourth column all the way down, on all nine sheets, and it took him most of the two hours, and there are about ninety different words in it, and he can tell you how many words are in that column and what a column is for and he cannot tell you what one single one of the ninety things is.

**That is the first thing he did, before anything else, on a trestle in a market in a town two days' road back, when the same hand was on three bells in a county.**

> *Ninety words. Nine sheets. About nine hundred rows. A number, a district, a day, a word, and a space where the words go.*
>
> ***I have read every word in the fourth column and I cannot say what one of them does, and neither can the man behind the counter, and that is not because he is keeping it from me. It is because the fifth column is empty, and it is empty because a man will not write down a word he cannot read back, and he is right, and I have been in four counties and a city for a hundred and one days and that is the first time I have found a thing that nobody in this kingdom knows and everybody in this kingdom is forbidden from asking about in writing.***

---

**And then he asked his one question, and it is in the book, and it is not the question he came up four flights of stairs with.**

“**How many of these go out of this room in a year.**”

Tarrant did not have to look it up.

“**About nine hundred. All of them. Every line on those nine sheets went out of that wall on somebody's instruction, and every one of them was carried out of this door by a different man, and not one of the nine hundred has come back into this room in nine years to tell me where it went.**”

“**None of them.**”

“**Four came back. Broken, and out of a warranty, and I did about four days' work on them and put that in the book as a repair, and those four are the only four lines in nine years of this book that have anything after them.**”

---

**And then Tomas Vale stood at that counter and understood the whole of what he had come up here for, and it came into him in about four seconds, and it is the finding of the hundred and first day and it is the reason a hundred and two days of a barrow and a coat and a hearing have not moved it one inch.**

**The notice on the top terrace says he is the keeper of a paper or instrument of a kind used to repeat an answer, being of the kinds entered in the store list.**

**The bell in the cradle on the flat has a coupling cut nearly through on the swing and a thin copper jumper soldered across it from the inside, done by a hand that does Crown work to bells in at least three counties, and a smith said there is a store and a clerk said there is a fitting, and neither of them said what it is, and a smith refused a theory to his face four seconds before it was offered.**

**And here are ninety words, and he can read every one of them, and there is no way on this earth for him to find out whether the word for what is in that frame is one of the ninety, because the list does not say what any of the ninety is for, and the reason it does not say is a column and a rule, and the rule is a good one, and the man keeping it has kept it for nine years.**

> *So: **I have carried the question how an office gets into a bell for fifty-nine days, and the answer is that nobody in this kingdom knows, and it is not a conspiracy and it is not a secret, it is a column with nothing in it and a man who will not fill it in and a district that has never asked him to.**
>
> *And I have got a bell in a cradle that has not been in a building since the thirty-third, and a jumper, and a coupling, and I have been looking at the inside of that frame for about three weeks off and on and never from inside the cradle, and I cannot put a name to what is in it out of ninety words, and I could stand at that counter all day and it would not help, because the fifth column is the only place the answer could be and it is empty, and it is empty for the best reason anybody in four counties has given me.*

---

**And he did not ask what the thing is for.**

He stood at that counter at about the sixth hour with ninety words in his head and a man behind it, and the question was there, and it was the question of a hundred and one days, and he did not ask it, and he wrote down on the corner of a leaf in his own book, in his own hand, with the hour against it, that he had not asked it.

**And he wrote down the reason, and the reason is the finding and not an excuse:**

> *A form is a thing you fill in with a reason, and I have spent eleven weeks finding out that a reason is the hardest thing in this kingdom to get a person to put their name to. **If I ask a man at a counter in a room what a thing is for, he has three answers and all three of them cost him something.** He can say *I do not know*, and that is a thing he will have said in a room with a district's book open in front of him, and he will not be able to un-say it to a clerk who does not care whether he knows. He can say *that is for the district*, and that is a form of words with nothing in it and I have been reading that form of words off about nine hundred men in four counties. **And he cannot say the true one, because there is no true one, and a man does not stand in front of a stranger and say *there is no true one* twice.** So he would say the second one, and be right to, and I would have asked a man at a counter a question in order to be given a sentence I could carry four hundred miles, **and I did not do it, and I am not going to do it, and I have written down that I wanted to, and that is the whole of the hundred and first day.**
>
> *And the reason is not a rule and it is not a principle. **The reason is that a man who asks a question he has not earned gets a sentence, and a sentence is a thing I have been trying to stop carrying for fifty-nine days.**

---

**And the three nines, and he wrote them out in a row in his own book at about the seventh hour, on the flat, with the tarpaulin over the frame four feet off him, because this is the sentence the next writer of this book is going to need most:**

> *There are three nines in this city and they are not the same nine and there is nothing in the kingdom that says they are the same nine and nothing that says they are not.*
>
> ***About nine hundred rooms in the buildings on the top terrace. About nine hundred bells in this city. About nine hundred entries on nine sheets in a book behind a counter.***
>
> *I have been writing *about nine hundred* for three days as though it were one number, and it is three, and I can only tell them apart by which sheet they are on, and a number that can only be told apart by which sheet it is on is a filing reference, and I have been told that about a man four days back in a market and did not listen to it.*

---

**He left the room at about the seventh hour with nothing.**

Not a sheet, not a number written on a leaf, not a copy, not a line. **The list is the store's, and it is looked at, and it is not taken, and there is no version of the hundred and first day in which a man walks out of that room with any part of it, and he did not ask for one, and he was not offered one, and the reason he was not offered one is that nobody in that room thought he wanted anything.**

**And a list of fittings is not a document he carries as evidence, and the number of non-bell documents in a coat is thirteen this evening and it was thirteen when he came into this city five days ago, and both of those figures are correct and neither of them is the other one, and a man who adds a store list to a coat of papers is doing the thing this whole book has been about, which is counting a thing he saw as a thing he has.**

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening he sat on a step at the foot of the top terrace and did not go down to the flat, and Tamsin Rook was ten feet off his left shoulder and had said about nine sentences to him in six days and had not been asked for anything by anybody, and the fourth column of her slate has two lines in it and neither of them has improved.**

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and first day and he filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was, and it is the shortest entry he has written in a hundred and one days and it took him the longest.

> *The hundred and first. **Ninety words and about nine hundred rows and a fifth column with nothing in it, and that is the whole of the answer I have carried fifty-nine days.***
>
> *I went up four flights of stairs to find out what a thing is for and the answer is that there are ninety words for the things and no words at all for what any of them is for, and the reason is a rule, and the rule is that a man will not write down in a book he keeps for a district a word he cannot read back to somebody who stands in front of him and asks. **A district of about nine hundred rooms, a city of about nine hundred bells and a book of about nine hundred entries are three different nines and there is nothing anywhere in this kingdom that puts them in the same column, and I have been writing about nine hundred for three days as though it were one number, and it is not, and the only way I can tell the three apart is by which sheet they are on.***
>
> *Second column, and the thing I did not do. **I did not ask what the thing is for.** I stood at that counter at about the sixth hour with the question in my mouth and I did not ask it, and I wrote down that I wanted to, and I wrote down the reason, and the reason is not noble and it is not a rule either. A man asked that has three answers and all three of them cost him something: *I do not know* is a thing he will have said in a room with a district's book open in front of him; *that is for the district* is a form of words with nothing in it that I have been reading off about nine hundred men in four counties; and there is no third answer, because there is no true third answer and a man does not say that twice. **So I would have been handed a sentence to carry, and that is the thing I have been refusing to be handed for fifty-nine days, and this morning I found out that refusing it is a thing I can do on purpose and I did it on purpose and nobody knows I did it except me and a woman standing ten feet off my shoulder who was not asked.**
>
> *And the answer I got instead. **Nobody in this kingdom knows what those ninety things are for, and it is not a conspiracy and not a secret and not a wall, and the man who keeps the book has been keeping that column empty for nine years because he is a careful man and the district has never once asked him to fill it, and he is right, and the district is not wicked, and I have now been in front of two empty columns in four counties and a city and they are made of the same virtue.** That is the thing I have been looking for since the forty-second and it is not a person and it is not a room, and I have to carry it up four hundred miles or I do not carry it at all.*
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the one number: **about nine hundred a year go out of that room, and about nine hundred different men carry them out of that door, and four come back.** Not one of the nine hundred has come back into that room in nine years to say where it went. **Nine hundred objects and nine hundred people and about nine hundred wires, and not one person among the nine hundred could be asked about the wire.** That is a sentence about a store and it is the seed of something I cannot see the shape of yet, and it came off nine sheets in two hours and it is the only thing in a hundred and one days that I have got which is more useful than what I came for, **and I cannot use it before the day after tomorrow at the earliest and I have no idea what I would do with it.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me, by anybody, in that room, on that terrace, on the flat.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been inside a building since the thirty-third and I have been inside two and it makes no difference to the frame. The carrier in the sack untouched, twenty-four days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I have not asked and I am not going to. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents. I looked at nine sheets and took nothing, and a list of fittings is not a paper, and a number is not a reason, and I have written all three of those down in the same breath as the thirteen so that nobody can come along later and add a sheet to the coat.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a format, and it is a column, and the column is empty, and there are about nine hundred of the things a year and about nine hundred men who carry them and nobody who can be asked about any of them.** I have got to the end of the road again and this time what is at the end of it is not a format, it is a sentence with a space in the middle of it, and the space is not a secret and there is nobody in the kingdom who may write in it, **and tomorrow at the second hour there is a room four streets off in which a man is asked to produce things, and I have got nothing in my hands, and I have never in my life gone into a room with less.***
