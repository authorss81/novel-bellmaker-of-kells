# Chapter 0104 — A Bell On A Roll

He left Ashmoor at about the seventh hour on the eighty-ninth and went three miles on and slept in a barn with about nine sheep in it, and the market town three miles beyond Ashmoor had about eleven hundred people in it and a district board under a roof at the top of the square, and on Day 90 Tomas Vale stood in front of that board at about the fourth hour because he had worked out overnight that the thing he had been unable to find in two volumes was not the thing that was frightening.

The thing that was frightening was a name. **The thing he had been unable to find was a list of objects, and it was on a wall, in a town of about eleven hundred people, under a roof.**

---

The board was in three columns of a printed sheet and the sheet had a heading and a number at the top of it, and the heading said what it said, and the three columns were:

> *things entered for district duty in this hundred — bell*
> *things entered for district duty in this hundred — other*
> *things entered for district duty in this hundred — withdrawn*

And the first column had nineteen lines in it, and he read all nineteen standing up, because he had learned in two volumes that a man who reads three of them is a man who has chosen three.

**Eleven market and work bells. Four boundary bells. Two school bells. One bridge bell. And one water bell.**

The water bell was in the last line and he stopped at it for a bit longer than the other eighteen and the reason is in the book and it is a reason and not a discovery.

**The water bell was entered the same way as the other eighteen. It had a place, which was a hamlet name and a ford. It had a district number. It had a date entered.**

**And the keeper's field was empty.**

Not struck through. Not filled with a dash. **Empty**, and ruled in the same hand, and the rule went past the end of the line the way a rule does when a clerk is going down a column and has not been told there is going to be anything in that box.

He wrote the number down. Not the name. There was no name on it; there was a hamlet and a ford.

And then he stood in the square of a town of about eleven hundred people and had the thing he had come four days up the road for, and it went through him like a hand in a bell, and it was not what he thought it was going to be.

---

**Because Marta Vell's water bell is in that column.**

Not that village. A ford four miles from here, and a number that is not her number, and a hamlet that is not her hamlet. **The same form. The same empty box. The same rule running past the end of the line.**

Two counties back he had read a schedule in a building in Fourmile in which a water bell was entered with the keeper's field empty, **because the district's form has four fields and none of them fits a water bell**, and he had written that down then, and he had written *a village can be entered in a schedule it never answered to*, and he had been carrying it for fifty-one days.

And here it was again, four miles up the road, in a different county, on the same paper, in the same empty box.

---

The impulse — and he wrote it down in the third column and it is the only line in the fourth column he has written in two volumes and he is not proud of it — was to join them.

**And he did not, and he took about four minutes to not do it, and the four minutes are on the page, and the reason is one he has had since Day 82 about a fragment of nine lines and a book of numbers.**

> *I want to join them. I stood in a square a few minutes short of the fifth hour of the ninetieth and I wanted it more than I have wanted anything in six weeks.*
>
> *And the reason I do not is that they are both **empty boxes** and there is no third thing to join. Two empties in a column is a clerk doing a form. A third empty in a third county is not a clerk doing a form, and if I cannot say what the third thing is then I do not get to have the second one, because the second one is what I am using to hold the first one up.*
>
> *And a person who joins them has joined them because they are both empty, and I wrote that sentence about a man who has not been in a house for six years on the eighty-second and I am not going to write it again about two pieces of paper.*

---

Then he asked the man at the board, who was there because there is a man at a board, and who was called **Ottmar Dace** — a different Dace from the Foreman Dace in the Low Ward, and he is a different person, and there is no relation and there is no need for one.

“**What is the other column.**”

“**Things.**”

“That is the heading of the sheet.”

“**Yes. And the middle column is things that are entered and not about a bell, and there are about two hundred and forty in it and about nine of them are a gate and about four are a bridge, and the rest are such as.**” Dace turned the sheet over. “**The third column is withdrawn. And that is about four years of this sheet and there are about nine in it.**”

“Who enters them.”

“**Ashmoor. On the instruction. It comes in a barrow with the rest of it.**”

“And a keeper.”

“**There is a box for a keeper. There has been a box for a keeper on this form since before I came, and I have not filled it in about four times in nine years and I filled it in twice and I did not know what I was writing either time, and I have not been told what it is for, and it is not on the sheet.**”

He put his finger on the box, and it was the fourth time in a month that a man had put a finger on a box in a form and told a stranger it did not mean what it appeared to mean.

“**What happens to a bell on that sheet.**”

“**Nothing, until somebody wants it.**”

“That is not an answer.”

“**It is the only answer there is and I have had four years to think of a better one. Nobody comes. There is no round that takes a bell. What happens to it is that a district can write to a hundred and say *this bell is entered for duty in this hundred* and that is a sentence, and the sentence is true from the moment it is in the book, and then anybody who writes to Ashmoor afterwards about that bell is writing about a bell that is a district thing, and about a hundred years ago that was a convenience so that a road closure could be argued about.**”

He said the next part in the ordinary way and Tomas Vale wrote it down in the third column and it is one of the four sentences in four counties he has underlined twice.

“**And I have thought about it for about eleven years, on and off, and this is what I have got, and you may write it down and I do not want it read out: a bell on that sheet is not a bell that is taken. It is a bell that is claimed, and a thing that is claimed can be given away to somebody else, and nobody in this hundred has ever had a bell taken off them because of that sheet and nobody ever will, because you cannot take a bell off a village, there is no form for taking a bell off a village, and if there were, it would be a form about a door and not about a bell.**”

---

**And then he asked the question he had carried for fifty-one days, out loud, in a square, in front of about forty people, and it is the first time he has asked it of anybody who was not a district officer or a clerk.**

“**Where does a name go when it is registered.**”

Ottmar Dace looked at him for about four seconds.

“**I have been keeping this board about four years and I have never been asked that and I am not going to guess at it, and I will tell you why in four seconds: I have got about nine hundred lines a year coming in from Ashmoor and about two hundred and forty of them are on this sheet and about eleven thousand come into this district every year in books, and I enter what I am given and I do not go upstairs, and there is nobody in Ashmoor to go upstairs to because Ashmoor is a store and a room and a man with a book, and a man with a book is not a thing you go upstairs from.**”

“So a name does not go anywhere.”

“**A name goes on a line and a line goes in a book and a book goes in a barrow to Ashmoor and after Ashmoor I do not know and I have never known and I am not going to tell you that I have.**”

He put the sheet back under the roof.

“**And I will say the other half, because you have got a bell in a cradle and you cannot get rid of it and a man who is going to be in trouble anyway may as well have the true one: I do not think a name goes anywhere. I think a name stops. There is about eleven thousand of them a year and there is a man and a barrow and about nine of these sheets and I should think a very great many of them do not go past Ashmoor at all, and that is not a conspiracy and it is not a wall, it is a queue with nobody at the front of it who wants anything.**”

---

He wrote the last entry of the ninetieth at about the ninth hour of the evening at the edge of the market square with the frame four feet off him and the bolts made fast, and he filled the first and third columns and left the second and the fourth as they were, and the fourth was empty and he had had a thing in it four days ago about a fragment of nine lines and had struck it through with a line and the word *not said*, and it is empty.

> *The ninetieth. **A bell can be on a roll.***
>
> *Nineteen of them at a board under a roof in a town of about eleven hundred people, in a column headed *things entered for district duty in this hundred*, and one of the nineteen is a water bell with a keeper's box empty, ruled past the end of the line, the same paper and the same empty box as a water bell entered on a schedule two counties back in a building in Fourmile.*
>
> *And I have been looking for a list of people for two volumes and there is a list of things, and it is the one that matters, and I have been carrying fright about the wrong object since the fifty-eighth day of the month.*
>
> ***A thing that is claimed can be given away to somebody else.*** A man with nine years at a board said that in about nine seconds and asked for nothing and did not want it read out, and he is right and it is worse than what I was afraid of, and it is worse because **I was afraid of a list of people and a list of people is a thing a man can be angry at, and a list of things is a thing a district can hand to a district, and a bell does not have to be taken off a village to be a district's, there is no form for taking a bell off a village, and if there were it would be a form about a door.***
>
> *Second column, ruled apart: **a name does not go anywhere, a name stops.** About eleven thousand a year, about nine sheets, a man and a barrow, and about four in ten of the lines in a village of two hundred never get attended. And the man at the board does not know what is past Ashmoor and has never known and refused to guess, and he thinks — he *thinks*, in his own mouth, and asked for the word to be the word — that a great many of them do not go past Ashmoor at all, and that it is a queue with nobody at the front of it who wants anything. **And that is the closest anybody in four counties has come to the second question in fifty-one days and he said it in a square in about nine seconds and I asked him nothing else and I am not going to go back and ask him anything else this year.***
>
> *And the thing I did not do, and it took about four minutes standing in a square, and it is on the page because the whole of six weeks is a man learning not to do a thing: **I did not join two empty boxes in three counties into a pattern. I wanted it more than I have wanted anything since the seventy-seventh. Two empties in a column is a clerk doing a form. If I cannot say what the third thing is then I do not get to keep the second one, because the second one is what I am holding the first one up with.** A person who joins them has joined them because they are both empty.*
>
> *And I have not got the answer to the second question and I am not going to write one down, and it has changed shape and that is not the same as having got nearer it, and I want both halves of that in one line: **a name goes on a line, a line goes in a book, a book goes in a barrow to Ashmoor, and after Ashmoor there is a room with a counter in it, and I am going to stand at that counter and it is going to be a person behind it and not a shelf, and that is the only difference I have got this month and I am not going to make more of it than that.***
>
> *All five clauses stand. Nothing was rung. Four bolts and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third. The bell in the sack untouched since the seventy-seventh. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents, a slate, a scrap of slate off a wall at a smithy that is neither, and a bell on a roll four miles from this square with nothing in the keeper's box and nobody within about a day's road who has ever asked.***
