# Chapter 0129 — The Door With No Reason On It

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and fifteenth day and Tomas Vale went up four steps and through a door propped with a bucket and into a public room on the middle terrace where about forty people go in a week, and there were about nine of them in there, and the board on the far wall had about nine rows of sworn sentences on it and the top one was his.

**He had stood in this room on the ninety-eighth and had not gone through the door, and the reason he had written down that day was that he had nothing true of his own to say, and that reason had been true, and he had also written down a second reason and the second reason was the one that had stopped him.**

---

## And the sentence took him about an hour and a half to get to and it is four sentences long, and it is about him, and it is the first thing he has put in a document that is about himself.

He wrote it out on a step at the foot of the top stair on the hundred and fourteenth at about the ninth hour of the evening and then he crossed out about half of it, and what is left is this:

> *I am the keeper of a paper or instrument of a kind used to repeat an answer, being of the kinds entered in the store list of the district. I have been in a store and read the list. **The list does not say what any of the ninety words in it is for.** And a column headed what the thing is for is empty from end to end, and I know a man who will not fill it in and I do not blame him for it.*

That is the whole of it. Four sentences, about ninety words, and not one word in it is about another person, and it is the plainest thing Tomas Vale has ever put in a document in his life.

**And he did not put the other one in, and the other one was about eleven lines in a fair hand in a fold in a bundle on a rail, and a court on this terrace will write down a thing about a man in the time it takes a clerk to enter one line, for fourpence, and he had eleven lines that were the truest thing he has ever read, and he wrote them out on a step and he looked at them for about four minutes and he did not take them with him.**

*Not because a thing written down is a bad instrument. Because a wall is a public thing and about forty people a week read that wall, and there is a name that goes at the head of a line, and he has got a brother-in-law's arithmetic to prove that four hundred people reading a thing about a person is not the same as four hundred people reading a thing about a column.*

**A column has no family in it. A column can be said out loud in a market in about four hundred people's hearing and every one of them will say it the same way tomorrow, because there is nobody in it to be frightened and nobody in it to be angry.**

---

## And it cost fourpence and it took about nine seconds and nobody asked him for a reason.

Coll Salkeld had a pen behind his ear and there was no queue, and Tomas Vale stood at the end of the rail and said it out loud, in his own voice, about himself, in a public room, and the clerk entered it against the day and the hour.

> *I am the keeper of a paper or instrument of a kind used to repeat an answer, being of the kinds entered in the store list of the district. I have been in a store and read the list. The list does not say what any of the ninety words in it is for. And a column headed what the thing is for is empty from end to end, and I know a man who will not fill it in and I do not blame him for it.*

The room did not stop.

---

**And then the thing that he did not do, and it is the whole of the hundred and fifteenth, and it is four inches long, and he has written it out and he is not going to improve it.**

Nobody asked him why.

He stood at the rail for a moment afterwards with fourpence less and a line on a wall behind him and he waited for it — the way a man waits for it, who has been handed a form in four counties and a city and a barrow and a plain carrier and thirteen documents and has had to fill in a box for a reason every single time, **and there is no box for a reason, and there has never been a box for a reason on anything a person swears, because a person swearing to a thing about himself does not owe anybody a why.**

He noticed it. He noticed it in about four seconds and then he went and stood about nine feet off the rail and he did not say a word to anybody for about a quarter of an hour.

> *Nobody asked me why. **I have been in this city twenty days and I have been asked for a reason every single time I have asked anybody anything, and I have been given a form in one of those rooms, and about nine seconds ago a man with a pen wrote down the truest thing I have got and did not ask me one question about it, and he did not do me a favour and he did not notice that he was doing anything.**
>
> *That is the door. **It was open on the ninety-eighth and I stood in it and did not go through, and I have known for seventeen days that it was open and I have not gone through it until this morning because I had nothing of my own, and that is still the reason, and the second reason was the real one.** The second reason is that I have wanted a document about myself for about ten days, and there is one, and it is about four hundred words in a file in the office's own hand and the last line of it says the person named produced nothing, **and I have been carrying that for five days the way a man carries a stone he cannot put down, and it is not a stone and it is not a finding. It is an inventory of my own hands, written by a man who has never seen me, and it is the only paper in the world anybody could put on a table and say *this man*, and I have wanted a second one for ten days and I have not been allowed to have it because every paper I could get my hands on is about somebody else.**
>
> *And this one is about me and it took nine seconds and it cost fourpence and it says nothing in it that I did not know on the hundredth day of this month, and there is a man in a city of about nine thousand people who can now say that I said so, in my own name, in a document, in a public room, and it is on a wall for forty years, **and I cannot get it down, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom by which a man who has sworn a true thing in open court can be made to have not sworn it, and there is no clerk who can do it, and one man has looked and looking is what he does.**

---

## And he did not thank the clerk, and he did not improve it, and he went and stood about nine feet off the rail and said nothing to anybody, which is what about four hundred people a year do in that room.

He was not the only one. There were about nine people in the room at the fourth hour and he counted, standing nine feet off, without meaning to, because counting is what he does with a room.

**About one in ten of the people in that room on any day is standing at that rail because of a matter of their own. The other about nine in ten are waiting, or watching, or asking a clerk what a paper is. And about four hundred people a year go through that door and put a sentence of their own on that wall, and the wall has about nine rows and the rows go back years, and not one name on it is a disgrace.**

**He was one of about forty this week and nobody in the room knew which, and there is no column for that, and there is not going to be one, and he has not written it down in this book either, because a man who counts himself in a room of nine people has started the thing he has been refusing for nine weeks.**

---

**And Tamsin Rook was outside on the step, nine feet off his left shoulder, and she had a slate under her arm with the price side the other way up, and she had not come in, and nobody in that room knew there was anybody outside at all.**

She said one thing to him at about the sixth hour, at ten feet, in the ordinary voice, and it was four inches long, and it was about the price of a wall and not about him.

“**Fourpence.**”

“**Fourpence.**”

“**I told you that number in a market on the hundred and second and you have written it in a book and you have not acted on it in thirteen days, and today you have paid it on purpose, about yourself, and you did not tell me you were going to, and I am not going to ask you about it.**”

“**No.**”

“**Good.**” She turned the slate over and did not put anything on it. “**And I am not going to say one word about the price being worth it, because it is not worth it, and you know it is not worth it, and you have known it for thirteen days, and it moves nothing, and it is a wall and it is there for forty years and you have put your own name at the head of it.**”

“**Yes.**”

“**Then that is the whole of what I have to say and I am going to go and stand about nine feet further off, which is what I do.**”

She went and stood about nine feet further off.

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step by the river with the tarpaulin over the cradle and the four bolts made fast, and the sack rolled against the wheel, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and fifteenth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.**

> *The hundred and fifteenth. **I went through the door.**
>
> *Four sentences, about ninety words, in my own voice, in my own name, in a public room, at the fourth hour, in the time it takes a clerk to enter one line, for fourpence, and it is on a wall on this terrace and it will be there when I am gone. **I am the keeper of a paper or instrument of a kind used to repeat an answer, being of the kinds entered in the store list of the district. I have been in a store and read the list. The list does not say what any of the ninety words in it is for. And a column headed what the thing is for is empty from end to end, and I know a man who will not fill it in and I do not blame him for it.**
>
> *Second column, and the cost, and I want it in black and white because I have got it exactly wrong for ten days. **A court will write a true thing down and keep it and it moves nothing.** It costs fourpence. It is read by every clerk who comes into that room after me, for forty years, **and it can be produced against me by a stranger I have never met and will not meet, and it does not need me in the room for that to happen.** She told me that on the hundred and second at about the seventh hour at ten feet in a market and I wrote it in this book and I have carried it for thirteen days, and she was right about the price and I have paid it on purpose, **for a thing about myself, and I do not know what it is for and I am not going to be told.**
>
> *And the thing I found out by doing it. **Nobody asked me why.** Not once, not in the room, not in the corridor, not on the steps. There is a box for the thing sworn, a box for the person who swore it and a box for the hour, and that is all three of them, **and the reason there is no reason is that a person swearing to a thing about himself does not owe anybody a why.** I have known that for seventeen days and I have never once been on the other side of it, and I have been on the other side of it for about as long as it takes to read four lines off a wall, **and it is the cheapest thing that has ever happened to me and it cost fourpence and it cannot be got back.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the two halves do not meet. **I did not put the other one in.** I have eleven lines in a fair hand in a fold in a bundle on a rail, and I wrote them out on a step last night and I looked at them for about four minutes and I did not take them with me, and the reason is that a wall is a public thing and about forty people a week read that wall, **and a column has no family in it and a column is said the same way by four hundred people tomorrow, and a name is not a column, and a man who puts a name in it puts a family in it, and she is not in it, and I am not going to be the reason.** That is the third time I have refused that thing and the third has cost the most and I am not going to write down why any of the three twice.
>
> *And the other half of that, which is worse. **I wanted a document of my own for ten days and there is one and it says the person named produced nothing, and I have made a second one and it says something, and the two of them are four hundred words and ninety words and about fourpence between them, and I am not going to be a man who says the second is a victory and the first is a defeat, because they are both inventory. One of them says I had nothing. One of them says I had a column. Neither of them says what I am for.**
>
> *Nobody asked her for a reason. **Nobody has asked her for a reason at any hour in a hundred and fifteen days, and I have not written one word about it since the hundred and second.** She said the number fourpence at ten feet and I said it back and that is all, and she is not thanked and I have not thanked her and she went and stood nine feet further off, which is what she does, and that is her instrument and not mine and I have never once been able to use it and I am not going to start tonight.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, and nothing bound, and no sheet taken off a board, and nothing taken out of a room.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it has been in the open every hour of this day. The carrier in the sack untouched, thirty-eight days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents in a coat, and a sworn sentence that is the court's and is on a board on a public wall, and those are two correct figures and neither of them is a fourteenth, and a man who puts a board in a coat has mistaken a thing he may read for a thing he has got, and that is the same sentence I wrote on the hundred and fifth about a record and it is the same mistake for the fifth time and I am not going to stop making it in public.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a wall with nine rows on it, and about four hundred people a year add a row, and there is no room above that room and no man in this city who decides what goes on it, and a man with a barrow can put a sentence of his own there for fourpence and nobody has to have a reason.** And a sworn answer with a name at the head of it is a thing about a person. **That is the thing I have not thought about until this minute and I am going to write it down and then I am going to go and find out what a district is required to do with a thing about a person, because there is a rule about it somewhere and I have read about nine hundred lines of a board in a room in the last three days and none of them was that.**
