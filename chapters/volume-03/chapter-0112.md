# Chapter 0112 — A Counter That Takes Papers And Not People

He went up to the middle terrace at about the fourth hour of the ninety-eighth day of the month with a form in his coat that he cannot fill in, and he got to the arch at the foot of the top and stood at the end of the queue with the other four people, and he worked out, standing there, that he had not got a question.

**He had used it. The one question he walked into this city with was *what is the document*, and he had put it on Day 95 at that counter and a clerk had given him the whole of it in two halves, unasked, in the ordinary voice, and had shut the shutter afterwards, which is a thing a clerk does.**

**You cannot walk into a building twice with the same question. By the second time it is a thing you are making people hear again, and a man with a barrow and no standing has one question and it is spent.**

---

The arch is the same. The counter is about nine feet long and there are **three clerks behind it, as there were on the ninety-fifth, and about nine clerks in the room over the arch**, and he has not been in the room over the arch and is not going to be, and the difference between the three and the nine is the difference between people who answer and people who wait, and it took him about as long as it takes a man to look along a counter and back, and it is the only arithmetic about this building he got right on his own.

There is the notice on the wall. **Any person may bring a paper to this counter and ask what it is.**

It had been repainted. The paint is a different colour and the old colour is showing at the edges of the letters, and somebody had gone over the last word — *it* — twice, which is not a thing anybody does to a notice that has not been argued with.

**A counter takes papers and not people, and a repainted word is a counter that has been in use.**

---

**Perrin Lowe was at the end of it.**

He did not look up when Tomas came to the end, and then he did, and he said:

“**You again.**”

“**I have not got a paper.**”

“**Then you have come to the wrong end. The other end is where people come who have not got a paper, and they come about four in ten, and I do not know what to do with them, and I have not found out in nine years.**”

Then he said, in the ordinary way, and it is the second time in three days he has said a true thing to a stranger at that counter and it is the reason the man is worth knowing:

“**And I am not going to help you find a way round to the other thing. You asked me three days ago what a thing that says it for you is, and I told you it is a fitting, and I have not got one and I do not want one, and the fact that I told you is the last thing I am going to add to it.**”

“You are right.”

“**I know that I am right. I have been right at this counter about nine thousand times and there is no column for it and I would like that entered in the room over my head, and it will not be, because the form has four boxes.**”

---

**And then he said the thing, and Tomas wrote it down at about the fifth hour on a step, and it is the reason the ninety-eighth is in the book at all.**

“**There is no box for a question on anything that comes out of this building. I have said that and I will not say it again. There is a box for a person, a box for a district, a box for a thing and a box for a reason, and the box for a reason is the one that beats you, and it wants a person with a standing in a district, and you have not got one, and I have not got one, and there is nobody in this arch who could put a reason in it for you even if you asked him, and he would not, and he would be right.**”

“And a Crown notice.”

“**A Crown notice about you is nailed to a board four flights up because it could not be put in your hand. I know why it is on a board. You told me why three days ago and I did not ask you to. **A notice that cannot be served is a notice that is posted, and a notice that is posted is a thing about four hundred people read out of a market, and you have already paid for that, and the man who named you on it invented a capacity for you so that he could order you to produce a thing, and he was right to, and I have nothing to say to you about any of it and I have said it in about nine seconds.**”

**Two reasons on the page in one morning, both of them his own emptiness, both of them the fifth clause, and neither of them is going to change: a Crown sheet cannot be served on him and is therefore nailed up, and a form cannot be filled in for him and is therefore carried unfilled. The register is thirteen this evening and it was thirteen on the ninety-fifth, and there is nothing he can do about either fact except carry them.**

---

**And then he did the only instrument he has got, which is the one he has had for nine weeks and has been throwing away.**

He did not ask a question. He said, at the end of the counter, to a clerk who had not been told to do anything for him:

“**Can a person read the board in the river court.**”

And the clerk said, “**Anybody can read it. It is in a public room.**”

And Tomas Vale stood in an arch four streets and four flights below a building he has never been inside and felt a thing go through him that is not a power and is not a stage and is not a stage at all, and it is this:

**A board in a court is the same surface as a board in a market, and a court is a different kind of building from an office, and nobody in either building thinks of the other one.**

---

The river courts are on the middle terrace, three streets off, and they are up four steps and through a door that is propped with a bucket, and inside there are about forty people and a long room with a board on the far wall.

**The board is not a notice-board. It has no number at the top of it and no district heading and no date in a hand. It is ruled in three columns and the third column is a column of hours, and the whole of it is a list of what people have sworn to, in open court, in this city, with the thing sworn against the name and the hour against the day.**

He read it standing up.

He read all of it. He has learned that in four counties and a man who reads three lines of a column is a man who has chosen three.

**About nine rows on the board, and the rows went back years, and the names were of every kind of person in the city: a woman who kept a ferry at the far end, sworn to the number of ropes on her boat and the hour she counted them; a man who swore to a debt and the day it was struck; a woman who swore that a boundary stone had not been moved; two men who swore to a horse.**

**And not one name on that board is a disgrace. That is the finding, and it took him about four minutes at nine feet, and it is the whole of the ninety-eighth.**

---

He went and found the clerk of the court, who was a man called **Coll Salkeld**, about six years in that hall, and who had a pen behind his ear and was not busy, and who did not want anything and did not have to be asked twice.

“**You are looking at the sworn board.**”

“**I have read it.**”

“**Then you know what it is.**”

“**I know what is on it. I do not know what it is for.**”

Salkeld took the pen from behind his ear and put it down again, which is a man buying himself about four seconds.

“**It is for reading, and I will tell you why in four seconds and then you can go and do whatever it is you came up here to do. A court is not an office. An office files. A court publishes, and the reason a court publishes is that a court is a room people are sent to, and a room people are sent to has to be a room they can be seen in. **We have a board on that wall because a man is a person and not a line, and if we kept it in a book then the only people who would know what he swore would be the four of us, and a thing four men know is a thing four men can be asked about later, and I would rather nine hundred people a week knew what my neighbour swore on a day I did not have to come in for.**”

He put the pen back behind his ear.

“**That is the whole of it and there is not a room above this one and there is no man in this city who decides what goes on that board. If you swear a thing in this room it goes on that board, and if you do not swear a thing it does not, and the only person in this kingdom who can stop a thing being on that board is the person who would otherwise have had to swear it, and that is me being careful and not clever.**”

---

**And Salkeld told him the other half, because Tomas asked it, and it is a question about a form, and it is the only question he had left.**

“**Can a person swear a thing here.**”

“**Any person. It does not want a standing. It wants three things and I will give you the three because you have asked me a question and there is nobody in the room.**”

“**Say them.**”

“**One, you say it out loud, in this room, in your own voice, and not in a clerk's voice and not in somebody else's. Two, you say it about yourself and not about a household and not about a man who is not in the room. Three, there is a person here who hears it and writes it down, and there is a clerk who enters it against the day and the hour.**”

**And then he said the thing that put a hand on Tomas Vale's arm where the sack had opened on the plank the day before.**

“**And there is no reason. There is no box for a reason on a court form. There is a box for the thing sworn, a box for the person who swore it, and a box for the hour, and that is all three of them, and the reason there is no reason is that a person swearing to a thing about themselves does not owe anybody a why.**”

---

**And Tomas Vale stood in a public room in a capital city and found out that there is a door in this kingdom with no reason-box on it, and it is four inches wide, and it is open, and it is a room on the middle terrace of a city of about nine thousand people, and it costs nothing and is over before a man at the rail has decided whether to stop at it, and he did not go through it.**

He did not go through it because he has nothing to swear to.

**He has thirteen documents and not one of them is about him. He has a claim in his own voice that he made in a market in front of about four hundred people, and he has the truth about a wire, and he has the truth about a column with nothing in it, and every true thing he has got to say is about a person who is not in the room or about an object he is not the keeper of, and a court wants a thing about yourself, and there is nothing there, and if he had gone through that door with a lie in his mouth it would have gone on a board in a public room and about forty people a week would have read his name against it, and it would have been true, and that is the part he thought about afterwards.**

> *There is a door in this kingdom with no box for a reason on it and it is four inches wide and it is a room on the middle terrace, and I stood in it and I could have had a name of my own on a board in about nine seconds, and I did not, and the reason is that I have not got a thing that is true and mine. **I have got thirteen things that are true and eleven other people's.** A court publishes and an office files and the difference is four inches long and it is the only thing anybody in this city has said to me that I did not already know and did not already want.*

**And standing in that public room at about the fifth hour, with a board of nine sworn names on the wall in front of him, he found out that he had a habit and not a principle, and the habit is this: whenever a building shows him a way of reading a person's own sworn word, he thinks about hers.**

He did not go and find it. He has not gone, in twelve days, and he is not going to on this trip, and nobody in this city has asked him for it, and he is not going to tell anybody it exists, because if a person in a capital learns it from a book in a hall four days behind him then the person will have learned it from a book, and that is a sentence about a person and not about him.

---

He came down off the middle terrace at about the seventh hour and went back to the flat and slept in the net-loft under a rope he did not touch.

**The frame in its cradle was thirty feet off under the tarpaulin with the four bolts made fast, and the cradle is a building-sized thing for a bell and there is no card and no clause that will let it be put inside anything, and all five of the clauses stand tonight, including the one that says he has no standing to speak for a household, which has now cost him a room and a bench and five sheets, and a notice nailed to a board, and a form he cannot fill in, and this morning it cost him the only thing he had, which was a true sentence of his own with a name to put at the top of it.**

He wrote the last entry of the ninety-eighth at about the ninth hour of the evening on the loft edge with the river under, and he filled the first and second columns and left the third and the fourth as they were.

> *The ninety-eighth. **A court publishes and an office files, and the difference is four inches long.***
>
> *I got up to the arch at the fourth hour with nothing to ask, because I spent the one question I came into this city with on the ninety-fifth and a man at that counter told me both halves of it for nothing and then shut the shutter, which is what a clerk does. **You cannot walk into a building twice with the same question.** That is the first thing I have learned here that I have not learned anywhere in four counties, and I learned it in a queue, standing up, and it took about four seconds.*
>
> *Second column, and the finding. **Three clerks behind a counter nine feet long and about nine in the room over the arch, and the difference is the three who answer and the nine who wait, and nobody has to be wicked in either building for that to be how it is.** A clerk repainted one word on the notice on that wall twice and I do not know what the word is and I am not going to guess at it. And at the end of the counter a man with nine years in an arch told me two things in the time it took me to get my hand as far as my coat and take it out again, and they are the two things I have been carrying for four days as though they were a wall: **a Crown notice about me is nailed up because it could not be served, and a form cannot be filled in for me and is therefore carried unfilled.** Both halves are the fifth clause and both halves are me, and neither of them is going to be different in the morning.*
>
> *And then the thing I did not expect to find and was not looking for. **The river courts keep a board on a public wall with what people have sworn to on it, and any person may read it, and about forty people a week do, and there is no number at the top of it and no district and no room above the room it is in and no man in this city who decides what goes on it.** A woman who keeps a ferry, sworn to her ropes and the hour she counted them. A man sworn to a debt. A woman sworn that a boundary stone had not been moved. **And not one name on that board is a disgrace, and that is the whole finding, and it took me four minutes.**
>
> *And a court form has three boxes: the thing sworn, the person who swore it, the hour. **There is no box for a reason, because a person swearing to a thing about himself does not owe anybody a why.** That is the only door in this kingdom I have found with no reason-box on it. It is four inches wide. It is open. **I did not go through it, and the reason is that I have not got a true thing that is mine — thirteen documents and every one of them is somebody else's, and I have a claim in my own voice about a matter that is not my own business, and a court wants a sentence about the man standing in the room, and there was no man in the room, there was a coat with thirteen papers in it.** And if I had gone through it with something false in my mouth it would have been true, and it would have been on a board, and about forty people a week would have read my name against it, and that is the part I did not expect to be the reason.*
>
> *And there is a thing in a county I have left that I have not gone to look for since the fifty-eighth, and that I have not been in that county in twelve days, and I am not going to on this trip, and I am not going to write down one word more about it in this book than that. **Nobody in this city has asked me for it and nobody is going to, and I am not going to hand it to a stranger because a stranger has been kind to me twice.** That is a thing I have not done in four counties and a city, and I am not going to start because a court has a board on its wall.*
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by anybody, by me.** Four bolts, the frame out of doors on the flat under a tarpaulin, not in a building since the thirty-third, and a tarpaulin is weather and not a room. The carrier in the sack untouched, twenty-one days, and I do not know where either of my two ringings went and nobody is going to tell me and I have not asked. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells. **Thirteen documents. A printed slip that is the Office's, a form I cannot fill in, a leaf on two boards I have not taken, and now a public board in a court with nine rows on it and not one of them mine.**
>
> *Which office is it. **I have got the name and the name is four hundred rooms and a format, and today I have found the other half of it, which is that a court is the same kind of room with the same kind of counter and the same kind of man, and the one thing it does that the other one does not is put it on a wall where anybody may come and read it, and neither building has ever heard of the other one, and that is four inches long and it is not a door and it is not a wall and I do not know yet what it is.** Four flights up from that arch there is a room with a counter and a book and a man and a shelf, and the list in it is open at the third hour on the hundredth, and a hearing is five days off, and I have got a form in my coat and no reason to put in it.*
