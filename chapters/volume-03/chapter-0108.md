# Chapter 0108 — A Notice In A Man's Own Name

He had known it was coming for about a day and a half and he had got it wrong, and the way he had got it wrong is in the book at about the seventh hour of the ninety-first, and it is this: he thought it would be an officer.

**An officer is a man who comes to you.** An officer has a satchel and a form and a number and he stands four feet off a thing in a yard and asks for the half he would rather not have written down. He has met four of them in six weeks and every one of them was correct and one of them was kind about it and one of them cost him a room.

What came instead of the officer was a sheet of printed paper with a drawing-pin through the top corner of it, on a board under a roof at the top of a market square, and Tomas Vale got to that square at about the fourth hour of the ninety-fourth day of the month on a market day with about two hundred people in it, and he stood in front of it with the barrow and read it, and it took about a minute, and the minute is on the page and it is the whole of the ninety-fourth up to about the fifth hour.

---

> *NOTICE. Public. To be posted and not carried.*
>
> *A hearing will be held at the upper room of the Quiet Office, Sennet, on the third day of the month following, at the second hour, in the matter of a claim made in public in the hundred of Wendle and Ashmoor by a person named below.*
>
> ***The person: VALE, of the bellhouse in Kells, in the county of Kells, being a man who has no standing entered in this district or in any district of the Crown, and who is therefore named here in a capacity assigned to him by the office for the purpose of this hearing only, which capacity is that of a person who has made a claim.***
>
> *The claim: that a certain child was taken. The claim is not made by a household and is not supported by a household and no household is to be treated as having made it.*
>
> *Any person having in his keeping a paper relating to the matter is to bring it to the hearing and produce it there. **A person having no such paper is not required to account for the absence of one, and nothing in his possession is to be taken from him on account of this notice.***
>
> *The office has not been able to serve this notice upon the person named, for the reason given above. It is posted.*
>
> *By order. A district of the Quiet Office. [number] [number]*
>
> *Dated this day of the month, in the hundred named above.*

---

He read it four times and then he stood away from the board and let about two hundred people read it, and he did not stand in front of it and he did not stand in front of it on purpose.

**And then he did the thing he has done at about nine counters in four counties, and he did it standing in a market square in the open with about two hundred people round him, and it took him about a minute and a half, and it is the whole of the ninety-fourth.**

He went to the edge of the board and read the last three lines out loud, in the ordinary voice, to anybody who wanted to hear it, and about forty of them did, and about nine of them read it out loud after him in pieces, and a woman at a pitch of butter said *posted* twice and did not know why she was saying it.

“**It is posted and not carried, and it is posted because of the line about standing, and I want that read out and I want it understood, because I am the reason it is on that board and I am going to be the reason it is on about nine hundred boards in four counties by the end of the month.**”

---

**And here is the whole of it, and it took him about an hour and a half on a wall opposite, and it is the finding of the ninety-fourth and the third time in three volumes that a clause he asked for has come back at him from a direction he did not think of.**

**Clause five of a receipt in a watch house in another county, signed at about the fifth hour of the morning on the thirty-third day of the month, at his own asking, because he was right, says that the holder has no standing to speak for a household in Kells or in any ward.**

**It has stopped a form entering a household. It cost him a room, a bench and five sheets in a town four days behind him. It is why there was no room in a form, and a clerk said that out loud in a street and said the same clause was the reason five sheets went out of that room, and neither of them knew why.**

**And this morning it is the reason there is a Crown sheet nailed to a board in a market square with his name at the top of it in a market of about two thousand people.**

Because a person with a standing in a district can be **served**. You take the sheet to the man. He signs for it. There is a date and an hour and two hands.

**And a man with no standing cannot be served anything, because a service is a thing one person does to another and it needs somewhere for the answer to go, and the whole of the fifth clause is *there is nowhere*. So the office posted it. And posting is the oldest thing there is. Posting is a nail and a board and four hundred people in a market and no hands at all.**

He wrote that down and then he wrote the next line and it is the one he underlined twice.

> *I asked for that clause because I was right. **A form cannot answer for a household because I have no standing to be a household, and a Crown sheet cannot be put in my hand for the same reason, and the same emptiness did both, and it has taken me ninety-four days to find out that the price of an empty form is that you also cannot be told anything quietly.** The fifth clause is not a wall. It is a hole, and I built it, and the Office put a nail in it and about two hundred people read my name this morning and there is not one of them who will be told anything about it again.*

---

**And the name at the top of it is a name he gave to about four hundred people in a market about a day and a half ago, and the Office has made a shape out of it.**

*A person who has made a claim.*

**And he has no document of his own to produce. That is the second half and it is the half he was not expecting and it went into him at about the sixth hour like a hand in a sill.**

A hearing is a room with a table in it and a man who asks questions, and a man who is asked questions produces paper. He has thirteen documents and every one of them is somebody else's: a return in a woman's own hand, four sheets from Fourmile, a deputy's answer, an auditor's own sentence, a clerk's sentence, a receipt for five sheets, a river-post receipt, a letter refusing him, a leaf, a page that is a household's and not his, and a question and an answer in two hands. **He has never in thirty-three years of being a maker had a document about himself, and he has had about eleven weeks of being a stranger and not one of the eleven is his.**

**And the notice says that a person having no such paper is not required to account for the absence of one, and that nothing in his possession is to be taken from him on account of the notice. And both of those sentences are true and both of them are the fifth clause again, and the Office has written his own emptiness into the notice as a protection, and the protection is a wall, and the wall is a nail and a board.**

He sat on a wall opposite for about an hour and a half and did not write anything and did not ring anything and did not ask one person a question, and at about the seventh hour a woman he did not know put a cup of something down on the wall beside him and did not say a word about it and went away, and he drank it, and it is in the book because he is not going to leave out the only kind thing that happened on the ninety-fourth.

---

He wrote the last entry of the ninety-fourth at about the ninth hour of the evening at the edge of a market of about two thousand people who had all gone home and taken a name with them, and he filled the first and third columns and left the second and the fourth as they were.

> *The ninety-fourth. **A notice in my own name, and the law that put it on a board is a clause I asked a clerk to write into a receipt in a watch house in another county on the thirty-third day of this month at about the fifth hour of the morning, because I was right.***
>
> ***A person with a standing can be served. A person with none cannot. There is nowhere for the answer to go, so it is posted, and posting is a nail and a board and about two hundred people in a market on a market day, and about nine of them read it out loud in pieces and a woman at a pitch of butter said *posted* twice.*** The fifth clause is not a wall. It is a hole, and I built it, and a Crown sheet has gone into it, and **the same emptiness that stopped a form entering a household has stopped a Crown sheet going into my hand, and it has cost me a room and a bench and five sheets and a door it is, and those are the same door and I have not been able to tell anybody which way round it is.**
>
> *And what it has made me: **a person who has made a claim.** That is the capacity. Not a witness, not an informer, not a keeper, not a man in a house. **A claimant.** And I have been a man making claims at about nine counters in four counties for about eleven weeks and not one of them had a name, and the office has given me one, and the office did not have to, and the office has got to invent a capacity when it wants to name a man with no standing, and it invented the one that lets it order me to produce.*
>
> *Second column, and it is the hard half and I am not going to separate it from the half above: **I have thirteen documents and not one of them is about me.** A woman's return in her own hand. Four sheets from a district office in another county. A deputy's answer. An auditor's sentence in his own book. A clerk's sentence on a hall's paper. Five receipts for five sheets that are not mine. A river-post receipt. A letter refusing me and giving me a date. A leaf of unknown hand in a stock book. A page that belongs to a household of five and is not mine. A question and an answer in two hands on an officer's paper. **Thirty-three years of being a maker and about eleven weeks of being a stranger and there is not one paper in this coat that anybody could put on a table and say *this man*.** And the notice says I am not required to account for the absence of one, and it is true, and a man who is not required to account for the absence of a thing cannot be asked to produce it, and a hearing is a room where a man is asked to produce things.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and I am merging nothing: **the hearing is on the third day of the month following, at the second hour, at the upper room of the Quiet Office in Sennet. I have no standing to be served, I have no paper to produce, and I have counted the days from the day at the foot of that board to the day at the top of it and it is nine, and I have counted them twice, and the count is the only thing on that board a man can check and I have checked it, and I have nine days to get a document of my own out of a kingdom in which nobody has ever been asked to write one.** And the office has written the reason onto the board in about four lines and the reason is me, and about two hundred people in a market have read it, and about nine of them will say my name in a different town inside a year, and a man in a market four days behind me put a clerk's name in nine hundred people's mouths on purpose and told me the price of it at a trough, **and I have now paid the same price from the other side and I did not get a choice of the side and that is what ninety-four days is.***
>
> *Which office is it. **The Quiet Office. It is on a board in about four-inch type and it is the name of about nine hundred rooms, and it has a hearing on the third of next month and it is going to be full of people who are right.** I am going to walk into it. I have been outside buildings in four counties for eleven weeks and I have not been refused once and I have been frightened of that for a day and a half and **a notice on a board is not a refusal and I am not going to be relieved about it.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing was rung, and nothing was taken, and no clause was broken by any of it.** Four bolts. The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third. The bell in the sack untouched since the seventy-seventh and I do not know where it went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents, unchanged, and a printed sheet nailed to a board in a market square that I am not allowed to be given a copy of, and I have not asked for one and I could not have carried it, and I want it written down that I did not want it, which is not the same as not wanting it.***
