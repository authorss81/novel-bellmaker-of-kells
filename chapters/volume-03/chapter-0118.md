# Chapter 0118 — The Man Who Was In The Room Does The Only Thing There Is

He went up to the top terrace at about the fourth hour of the hundred and fourth day of the month and he stood in the yard under the frame of the gate and looked at the bell for about four minutes, and the bell is about as big as a cartwheel and it has a bracket on the back of it and a socket and a wire going down the inside of the post, and it did not ring, and it is not going to ring today, and there is nobody in this city he could ask to ring it and no standing in any county that would let him ask.

**And four streets and four flights down, on a barrow on the flat under a tarpaulin, there is a frame in a reinforced padded cradle with four retaining bolts made fast and no clapper in it, and the receipt says in five clauses that no person shall furnish one, and one of those five clauses says the frame stays in its cradle for the carriage, and the second clause is the reason he has never been able to pay for anything since the thirty-third, and the third clause is the reason he cannot put it in a room, and he has checked all three of them this morning on purpose because it is the only bell in this kingdom he cannot have.**

---

**Nobody came out of the upper room.**

He went up at about the fourth hour and again at about the fifth and again at about the sixth, and he did not go in, and he had not gone in yesterday either, and he has written down that he does not intend to go in today, and the reason has not changed in twenty-four hours and he is not going to improve it.

**The yard is the same yard. The bench is under the gate frame and about nine people crossed it between the fourth hour and the seventh, and a man turned a plate over on a board at the sixth hour and about forty people read it, and the bell rang four times, and about two hundred people in the yard let it ring and made no noise, and he stood in the middle of that and was part of it and is not going to write a paragraph about it.**

---

**And nobody told him anything, and that is not because they are protecting anybody.**

He asked four people between the fourth hour and the sixth whether they had been in the upper room yesterday, and three of them said no, and the fourth said, in the ordinary way, “**I was in the yard at the sixth hour like everybody. That is not being in the room.**”

**And that is true, and it is the correct answer, and it is the answer a man gives, and there is nothing behind it.**

**Nobody in this city is keeping a thing from Tomas Vale. There is no door being held shut. There is a room, and the room happened, and the room has gone back to being a room, and there is no version of this in which a person has decided to keep him out of an account of it, because there is no account of it. That is the whole of the hundred and fourth morning and it took him about two hours to believe it.**

---

**And then, at about the seventh hour, a man he had spoken to once, in a public room, three streets away, on the ninety-eighth, came up the stair and went along the rail and did not stop, and Tomas Vale went with him for about forty yards because there was nowhere else for him to go, and the man said what he had come up to say and would not say it again.**

It was **Coll Salkeld**, who is a clerk of the river courts and has been in that hall about six years, and he had a sheet of his own in his hand with a copy of something on it, and he did not sit down and he did not stop walking.

“**You have not been in the room.**”

“No.”

“**Then I am not going to tell you what was said in it, and I am not being careful, and I want that said in the right order: I am not being careful. I would have told a man in a market in a fortnight. **A thing said into a column in a room belongs to the room. The same thing said at a rail belongs to the man who said it, and it is the room's copy that has a date on it and a hand over the column.** You did not ask me. That is the only reason I have said this much.**”

---

**And then he said the half that is the finding of the hundred and fourth day, and he said it walking, at seven feet, in about as long as it takes to cross a hall, in a corridor on the top terrace of a capital city.**

“**Here is what a hearing is, and I will give it to you in the right form, and then I am going back down the stair.**

“**A hearing is a room where one person's answer, once it is given, is written into a column, and out of that column it comes as the answer of the whole matter. Not the answer of the room. Of the matter. The thing the matter was about.** And it is entered in about four seconds, in the correct form, out of the correct column, by a man doing his job, and the man is not choosing, and the four seconds are not the point, and **I have watched that be done in a town of about two thousand and I have watched it be done here and it is the same hand and the same column and there is no difference whatever between the two except which building you are standing in.**

“**And that is all of it. I am not going to say a word more about who was in the room or what was said and I am not going to be asked and I will say *I am not going to say* if I am asked, once, and then I will not say it again either.**”

---

**And then he said the other thing, and it is the thing the hundred and fourth is for, and he said it standing at the top of the stair with a sheet in his hand, and Tomas Vale wrote it down that night in full.**

“**A man came into the river court hall yesterday at about the second hour of the morning, which is the hour that room is shut, so he had a key, and he put his hand against a line on the sworn board. Not a slip. **A hand, on the board, against a line, in his own name, in the open, in front of about eleven people who had come in to swear things they had not thought of swearing in front of anybody.**

“**His name is at the head of it. He is a clerk of the upper room on this terrace. His line says what he did yesterday morning, in about nine words, in the ordinary way, and it is true, and it is the only true thing on that board in about four years, and I have been a clerk six years and I have never seen anything on that wall that was written by a man about himself on the day he did it.**”

---

**And Tomas Vale asked the only question he was going to ask, and it is in the book, and it is the question a man asks who has spent a hundred and four days looking for a person.**

“**What did it cost him.**”

And Salkeld said, in about nine seconds, and this is the whole of what Tomas Vale got out of the hundred and fourth day:

“**About four days of his working life. He is not to be in the upper room and he is not to be at the counter and he is not to be in the corridor that runs about a hundred yards from this rail to the stair, and that corridor is his whole working day, and he will be at a desk in a room with no door on it until a district decides otherwise, in about four days' time, and the district has not decided otherwise and is not going to, and he knows that.**

“**And there is a paper about it in a room four streets down. It is a district's paper with four boxes, and one of the boxes is a day, and the reason is in the fourth box, and what is in the fourth box is *the district's business*, which is three words and not a reason, and it is the only reason anybody is ever going to have, and it is not a lie, and it is a door closing.**”

“**Does he know he has done it.**”

“**He knew before he put his hand down. He has known since the room shut. Nobody persuaded him. **I have been a clerk six years and in my experience a clerk who does a thing like that is not looking for a person to notice it, and if he was looking for one he would have done it where a district could not put a paper about it, and he did it in a public room on a public wall, which is the most expensive place there is, and he is not a fool.**”

---

**And then Coll Salkeld said the last of it, at the top of the stair, and he said it to Tomas Vale and not to a market, and he said it once and he went down the four flights and did not stop.**

“**And I am going to tell you why he did it and then I am going to say I am not going to say why, and both halves are true and you may have the first.**

“**He did it because he did it yesterday morning in a room with a lamp in it, in about four seconds, out of the correct column, in the correct form, as his job, and then he went down a hundred yards of corridor and had about an hour and a half with nothing to do in it and a piece of paper in his hand that he was about to put in a file, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man may say *I did that and here it is* except a court board and a court board takes no district's paper, so he put a hand on a line and wrote nine words and it cost him four days and a hundred yards of corridor, and the nine words are in his own name, and there is no copy of them anywhere but that wall.**”

“**And then you are not going to say why.**”

“**I am not going to say why he thought it mattered, because that is nine words on a wall in his own name and it is his, and he did not put it there for me to explain. **If he was wrong then it is four days. If he was right then it is four days. There is nothing in this kingdom that turns four days back into nothing, and there is nothing in it that turns four days into more than four days, and both of those are the answer and I am going down the stair now.**”

---

**And Tomas Vale did not ask, and he did not follow, and he was in the arch at the foot of the stair about an hour before that, four streets away, asking a clerk at a counter what a district's paper with a third hour on it is for, and he was told, in about nine seconds, and it was nothing, and he had no idea while he was being told it that a thing was happening in a public room three streets away on his account.**

**And that is the thing about the hundred and fourth day and he wrote it down at about the ninth hour of the evening and he has not been able to put it anywhere since, and it is the finding of the hundred and fourth and it is the best thing anybody has done to him in a hundred and four days:**

> ***I have spent a hundred and four days looking for a person who is right and doing something about it. I have found eight. A clerk at a counter, a smith at an arch, a man at a board in a village, a board-keeper under a roof, an officer in a market, a storekeeper behind a counter, a court clerk in a public room, and now a clerk of an upper room who put his own hand against a line on a public wall in his own name on his own account and cost himself about four days and about a hundred yards of corridor and was not asked to by anybody and will not be asked to by me.***
>
> ***And the act is the same act.** It is the same act as a yard on the seventy-ninth, where six men put their hands against a line in a counting house and were paid one at a time and said nothing and had to say nothing. **It is a chore. A chore is a thing a person does for himself, in his own yard, with his own hands, on his own day, and a firm is not a person and cannot do one, and a district is not a person.** He did a chore for himself. He did it in a room he chose, in a form that is the room's, out of the column the room uses, in his own name, and it is the only thing in this kingdom that has ever moved a line, and it cannot be done by anybody else, and it cannot be done twice by one man, and it cannot be done at all by nine hundred people.
>
> ***It is not scalable. It is not a network. It is not the thing I have been looking for and it is the only thing that has ever worked, and a man who says the second is the answer is the man who says a format is a room, and I have been that man for nine days and I am not going to be him tomorrow.***

---

**And he did not get to thank him and he is not going to be told it was worth it, and Tomas Vale wrote that down in the third column in his own hand and underlined it twice, which he has done about four times in a hundred and four days:**

> ***I am the reason there is a hearing. His name is at the head of his own line and it is there because a notice in my name went up on a board in a market four days back in a town two days' road behind this city, and because a clerk in a room invented a capacity for me so that he could order me to produce something, and because I had no standing and could not be served and was therefore posted, and because a sheet with my name on it came four hundred miles on a maintained road and a district wrote back and said there would be a hearing on the third day of the month following at the second hour at the upper room of this building. He read the sheet. He entered the column. He did his job in about four seconds. And then he paid about four days and about a hundred yards of corridor for it out of his own pocket, in his own name, in a public room, and nobody asked him to, and nobody is going to tell him it was worth it, and I am not going to be one of the people who tells him, and I have written that down in a book that nobody but me has read for a hundred and four days and I am not going to stop writing it down.***

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening he was in a yard on the top terrace with about nine hundred bells in this city and not one of them going, and a bell about as big as a cartwheel nine feet over his head that a man had turned four strikes on at the sixth hour in front of about two hundred people, and a rail, and a corridor a hundred yards long, and a bench.**

**There is a form in a file in a room four streets below that says a clerk is not to be in a room or a corridor or at a counter, and it has four boxes and one of them is a day, and the fourth box says the district's business, and the clerk's name is in none of the four boxes, and neither is the sentence he wrote on a public wall, and the paper in the file is about a man and does not say one word about what he did or why, and it is the most exact document in this kingdom and it is not about anybody.**

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and fourth at about the ninth hour of the evening on a step at the foot of the top terrace, with the frame in its cradle four feet off him and the bolts made fast, and he filled the first and second columns and left the third and the fourth as they were.

> *The hundred and fourth. **A hearing is a room where one person's answer, once given, comes out of a column as the answer of the whole matter. Entered in about four seconds, in the correct form, out of the correct column, by a man doing his job. The man is not choosing. The four seconds are not the point.** I have that in a corridor on a stair at about the seventh hour from a clerk of the river courts who has been in his hall six years, and he said it walking, in about as long as it takes to cross a hall, and then he stopped and would not say one word more about who was in the room or what was said in it and is not going to be asked.
>
> *And he said the other half and it is the half I have not stopped. **The same hand and the same column as a town of about two thousand, and there is no difference between the two except which building you are standing in. The machinery four counties found in a hundred and four days is the same machinery, and it has a room, and the room is not the point.** A format does not have a door and a format does not need one, and I have got to the end of that road and I am going to walk back down it.
>
> *Second column, and it is the finding and I am not going to improve it in the morning. **A man came into the river court hall yesterday at about the second hour with a key, because the room shuts at that hour, and put his hand against a line on the sworn board. Not a slip. A hand, on a public wall, in his own name, in the open, in front of about eleven people, and nine words about what he did yesterday morning, and it is the only true thing on that wall in about four years.** His name is at the head of it. He is a clerk of the upper room. **It cost him about four days of his working life, the upper room, the counter, and about a hundred yards of corridor, and he will be at a desk in a room with no door on it until a district decides otherwise, and the district has not decided otherwise and is not going to.** And there is a paper about it with four boxes and one of them is a day, and the reason is in the fourth box, and the fourth box says *the district's business*, and that is three words and not a reason, **and both of those things are correct and neither of them can be answered.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the whole of it. **I have spent a hundred and four days looking for a person who is right and does something about it. I have got eight, and this one did a chore, and it is the same chore as a yard on the seventy-ninth where six men put hands against a line and were paid one at a time and said nothing and had to say nothing. It is a thing a person does for himself, in his own yard, with his own hands, on his own day, and a district is not a person and cannot do one. He did not do it for me. Nobody persuaded him. He knew before he put his hand down, and if he had wanted a person to notice it he would have done it somewhere a district could not put a paper about it, and he did it on a public wall, which is the most expensive place there is, and he is not a fool.**
>
> ***It is not scalable. It is not a network. It cannot be done twice by one man and it cannot be done at all by nine hundred people, and it is the only thing in this kingdom that has ever moved a line. And I have been a hundred and four days looking for the other thing, and I am not going to be the man who says this is the other thing, because I said on the ninety-sixth that a format is a room and that was wrong, and this is the same mistake in a better coat.***
>
> *And the reason, in my own hand, underlined twice, which I have done about four times in a hundred and four days: **I am the reason there is a hearing. A notice in my name on a board in a market four days and four hundred miles back, and a capacity invented so that a man with no standing could be ordered to produce something, and a fifth clause that means he could not be served and was posted instead, and a district that wrote back and named a day and an hour and a room.** He read the sheet. He entered the column. He did his job in about four seconds, and he did not choose the column and he was not told to. And then he paid about four days and a hundred yards of corridor out of his own pocket, in a public room, in his own name, with nobody after him and nobody owing him anything, and **nobody is going to be told by me that it was worth it, and I have written that in a book that nobody but me has read for a hundred and four days and I am going to keep writing it down.***
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung by me and nothing rung by me anywhere in a hundred and four days.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been inside a building since the thirty-third, and a cradle is a building-sized thing for a bell and there is no card and no clause that will let me put it in a room, and a bell about as big as a cartwheel went four strikes over my head at the sixth hour in front of about two hundred people and I was in the middle of it and I could not have rung it if I had had the standing of the man who did.** The carrier in the sack untouched, twenty-seven days. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents, and a record of a hearing somewhere in this city that is not mine and is not in my hands and I have not asked for.**
>
> *Which office is it. **A room with a lamp in it, a column, and a man in it who is right, and a form in a file four streets below that says a clerk is not to be in a room or a corridor or at a counter and does not say one word about why, and about four thousand people a year in this city who never go up the stair at all.** A hearing has been had. A warrant is not a warrant, and a warrant is not a thing you can see, and there is a bell over a gate nine feet above me that nobody has rung and about nine hundred of them in this city that are not going to, and a hand on a public wall is not a bell and it is not going to be a bell, and I have to go and find out what happened in a room I did not go into, and there is a court on the middle terrace that publishes, and it opens at the fourth hour, and I have nowhere to be after that.*
