# Chapter 0141 — A Bell In A City Where Every Bell Is The Hour

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and twenty-seventh day and Tomas Vale was on the flat with the barrow, a length of bar, a spade, and about nine feet of rope that belongs to the net-loft and that he had not asked for and had not taken.

**He was going to build a mount. That is the whole of the hundred and twenty-seventh and it is the least sentence in a hundred and twenty-seven of them, and it took him until about the seventh hour, and it took that long because a mount is a post and a bracket and a thing to sit a bell in, and a man who has spent his life in this trade can make one in an afternoon, and there is no fitting in this kingdom that will do the job and he cannot buy one without an instruction and he is not going to ask for an instruction.**

The rope went round the post twice and the bar went through the eye of it, and he drove the post into the bank above the road with the butt end of the spade, because the bank is soft and the flat is hard and a post in hard ground shakes a bell off its bearing inside a winter.

**The four bolts on the cradle were made fast where they were made fast and the tarpaulin was over the frame and the frame was thirty feet off him, and he checked all four of them twice, and he has checked them every morning since the thirty-third.**

---

At about the fifth hour he had the post in and the bracket on it and nothing on the bracket, and he stood in the road and looked at it.

A mount is not a bell. A mount is a promise made out of iron to a bell that has not arrived, and every bellwright in this kingdom has stood in front of one at some point in his life with the same expression on his face, and the expression is not hope.

**The only bell in this kingdom he could put on it is in a sack against that wheel, and it is a plain carrier of fourteen pounds two ounces with no wax in it and no dressed groove and only a caster's mark and a day, and it has not been rung since the seventy-seventh, which is fifty days, and it is not barred, and the bar is on my own bell and not on it, and that is the whole of the law and it took me four days to find out and it did not help.**

The other one was in a cradle under a tarpaulin with nothing in it that could strike, and a receipt in five clauses that says no person shall put anything into that frame that could, and one of those five says the frame stays in the reinforced padded cradle for the carriage, **and the frame is the only bell in this kingdom I cannot have and it is thirty feet off me and it has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a bank above a road is not a building and a road is not a building and I want that on the page because I have been in two rooms since the hundred and ninth.**

---

So he left the post in the ground and went up.

He went up the middle terrace first, because that is where about nine hundred people a day walk past a wall and do not stop, and then the top, and he did not go into the top. He walked it. He counted what bells in this city are fixed to something, and he did it in the ordinary way, a man looking up at iron.

The answer took him about two hours and it is four words long.

**A bell in this city is on a gate, on a board, on a post or over a yard. And all four of those belong to somebody else's paper.**

There is a bell on the frame over a gate in a yard on the top terrace and a bracket and a socket and a wire going down the inside of the post, and the yard is a district's yard and the frame is a district's frame. There is a bell on a post above a board and the board is a notice and the bell is what the notice is for, and the man who decides when it goes is not the man who reads the sheet. There is a bell over a gate in a yard of packing cases at the foot of the top stair and the man who has that yard strikes it himself and has for nine years.

**And a bell over a door of a shop, and a granary gable bell that is not the wharf's, and a tide bell in a boatyard, and eight more between the wharves and the river, and a man in a boatyard who has been there nineteen years told me on the hundred and twenty-fifth that I was the fourth person that year to ask him about one, and he did not look up, and he said *it is a bell*.**

He went past the gate with the frame over it and the man who has the yard was in it, and the man looked at him and then at the post on the flat with nothing on the bracket thirty feet below him, and he did not ask him a single question, and Tomas Vale did not ask him one either.

**I have been in this city thirty-two days and not one person in it has ever once asked me what I am doing, and it is not kindness and it is not a door. It is that there is nothing here to be asked about.**

---

At about the sixth hour he sat on a step at the foot of the top stair with the post in the ground behind him and worked out the thing he has been carrying for ten weeks, and it took him about nine minutes, and it is the whole of the hundred and twenty-seventh day.

**A claim in metal cannot be edited. It cannot be added to. It cannot be taken back. It cannot have an hour put on it. And it cannot be shown to anybody on a page.**

He wrote it out flat in his own book because a man has to write down the things he works out or he will do them in a month and call them new.

> *Everything I make is a thing a man cannot edit, and that is the whole of why I make them, and it has been the whole of why since I was in a yard at nine years old. Not one hour in a hundred and twenty-seven days has gone by when I have not been able to look at a thing I have struck and know it is going to be exactly what I said it was on the day I said it.*
>
> ***And the instrument that would carry a true sentence out of me in one evening, and put it in front of every door in a city of about nine thousand people, is a bell. That is the only one. I have been refusing it since before we left Kells, and I have got the reasons written down in three counties and a city, and every one of them is true.***
>
> *And at about the sixth hour this afternoon I found out that none of the three is the reason.*

> *I have been angry at a yard for twenty-one days. A yard on the top terrace with about two hundred people in it at the sixth hour, and a bell about as big as a cartwheel went over a gate on the hundred and sixth, and there was nothing on the plate, and the man turned a plate over and struck it four times whether or not there was anything on it, and about two hundred people let it ring and made no noise and went in again.*
>
> ***And I have been angry about that yard since the hundred and sixth, and this afternoon I worked out that what I was angry about was that a bell in a city of nine thousand is an hour, and that a tenth bell in among them is one more hour, and that a true thing standing in that position is a false one — and that I have been carrying that exact objection in my own head for ten weeks and calling it a reason not to ring.***
>
> ***So I was not refusing the instrument. I was refusing the sound, and I was doing it for the same reason the man at that rail does his job, which is that I could not tell the difference either, and the reason I could not tell the difference is that there is no form in this kingdom that can tell the time off a bell that is telling you a true thing, and that absence is four inches long, and it is not anybody's doing.***
>
> *And I have found out one more thing this afternoon and I am going to write it down while it is fresh. **There is nowhere in this city to put a bell that is not somebody's.** Not a wall, not a gate, not a yard, not a post, not a market — a market is on the ground and I have got no right in a market and I have not got a keeper in any house in it. **I have a bar on my own bell, a frame I cannot furnish, a carrier I am not allowed to unroll in a town, thirteen documents none of them about me, and a post in the ground with an empty bracket on it, and I am going to be struck in the morning by nobody at all if I wait for a place to appear.**

---

He made the claim at about the seventh hour. It is four sentences and it took about nine minutes, the same as it took in a market ten days ago and the same as it took on a step in this city on the hundred and twenty-fifth, and the shape has not changed once in a hundred and twenty-seven days and he is not going to improve it this evening.

> *I know that a list of things was kept in a store four streets and four flights up and that a column on it wants to know what each thing is for, and that the column is empty from end to end, and that I do not know what any of the words in the list is for, and I have not pretended to for a hundred and twenty days.*
>
> *I do not know why a route that answers is a route the district does not have to argue with, and I have not found that out, and I am not going to guess at it in a market.*
>
> *I am the keeper of a thing I cannot show anybody, and I have been carrying it since the hundred and eleventh, and I have not made a copy of it, and I am not going to.*
>
> *And I do not know what that makes me or what I ought to do with any of it, and I am not telling anybody else what to do with it either, because I have no standing in any county in this kingdom and no keeper and no household and no second answer, and a man with one answer is a man talking.*

**That is the shape. It is the shape of every bell I have rung four times in my life and it is the shape the whole of this trade is, and it took nine minutes, and there is not one word in it that I have improved since the hundred and sixth, and if I had made it better it would have been worse.**

He did not write it out on a sheet. He did not say it out loud to anybody, and there was nobody within thirty feet of him, and it is in his own book in his own hand and it is not given to anybody and it is not going to be.

At about the eighth hour he unrolled the sack.

**He put his hand flat on the crown of a plain carrier of fourteen pounds two ounces with no wax in it and no dressed groove and only a caster's mark and a day, and it is not rung, and it has not been rung since the seventy-seventh, which is fifty days.**

**That is the fourth time. The first was on the hundred and sixth and the second was on the hundred and twenty-second and the third was on the hundred and twenty-fifth, and it is the same act four times now and a different reason each time, and a man who has put his hand on the same crown four times in a hundred and twenty-seven days is a man who has run out of reasons, and this is the last of them.**

**I put a post in a bank today with a bracket on it and nothing to go in the bracket, and I built the only thing I have wanted to build in ten weeks, and there is no bell in this city that will take a claim from me, and a man who has built a mount and has nothing to hang on it is a man who has finished doing that. So that is the fourth one and it is the last one and I know it is the last one while I am doing it, and I am not going to write down in this book what the last one was about, because I have not got to that, and because a man who writes down why he has stopped doing a thing is a man who has started it again.**

He rolled it up and made it fast.

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and twenty-seventh and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and twenty-seventh, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A post, a bracket, and nothing on it, and six hours, and the sixth hour is where the whole of a hundred and twenty-seven days turned over.** I have spent ten weeks refusing an instrument and I have had the reasons written down in three counties and a city and every one of them is true, and at about the sixth hour this afternoon I found out that none of them is the reason and that the reason is four inches long and it is that in this city a sound is the hour.*
>
> *Second column, and the finding, and I am not going to improve it. **About nine hundred bells in this city, and not one of them a keeper's bell, and not one a plain bell that somebody decided to hit, and every one of them the hour. Nine of them in a mile of river road and I asked three questions at each one on the hundred and twenty-fifth and the answer at all nine was the time it is.** I went up both terraces this afternoon and looked at what bells are fixed to and there are four answers and they are a gate, a board, a post and a yard, **and a bell on a gate is a gate's bell, and a bell on a post above a board is a notice, and a bell over a yard is a yard's, and a bell over a shop door is a shopkeeper's, and all four of those have an owner with a document and I have no document in any of them.**
>
> *So there is nowhere in this city to put a bell that is not somebody's. **I have a post in the ground with an empty bracket on it and I have got nothing to put in it, and the two things I would put in it are both impossible: my own bell is barred in another county and in a sealed yard, and the frame on the barrow has nothing in it that can strike and the receipt says in five clauses that no person shall put anything into that could.** The second clause is the reason I cannot pay for anything with the barrow and I paid cash for an hour of a room ten days ago, and the second clause has now cost me a fitting as well and that is six jobs in a hundred and twenty-seven days and it has cost me something every single time and nothing else has.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the thing I have been angry about for twenty-one days. **On the hundred and sixth a yard on the top terrace let a bell ring four times with nothing on the plate and made no noise, and a man at a rail with a book of his own and no office said out loud that he does not know whether he is to strike it and that there is nobody above him to ask.** He is right. I have been angry at him for twenty-one days and this afternoon I found out that I was angry at my own reason. **I have been refusing the instrument because a sound in this city is an hour, and I have been carrying that in a book as if it were a rule about paper, and it is not a rule about paper, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom that can tell a bell telling you the time from a bell telling you a true thing.** That absence is four inches long and it is the whole of it and nobody is wicked and nobody is hiding anything.
>
> *And the shape, four sentences, nine minutes, the same shape. **Names what I know, names what I do not know, names my own fault, and tells nobody what to decide.** I have made it in this city on a step on the hundred and twenty-fifth and in a market ten days ago and in a room on the middle terrace and I have never once improved it, and I have not improved it tonight, and I am not going to. I did not write it out on a sheet and I did not say it to anybody and there was nobody within thirty feet of me and **it is in my own hand in my own book and it is the fourth instrument and I have just made it the wrong one, because the only thing that could carry it is a sound, and a sound in this city is the hour.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had.** Four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a bank above a road is not a building and a market is not a building and a room over a shop is a building. **The carrier in the sack, not rung, fifty days counted in days from the seventy-seventh, and I put my hand flat on its crown at about the eighth hour and it is not struck and that was the fourth time and the last one.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about me, and a post in a bank with a bracket on it, and four sentences in my own hand in my own book, and nine bells in a mile counted in my own hand, and none of them is a fourteenth.** I have produced nothing today and asked for nothing and I have built one thing out of a rope that belongs to a loft, and I am going to have to give the rope back, and I have not thought about how.
>
> *Which office is it. **It is about nine hundred bells that are the hour and a gate and a board and a post and a yard, and a bracket with nothing on it, and an order that has been running for six days, and a yard of about two hundred people who came out and made no noise, and a man at a rail who is right and is not owed a visit from me.** I have worked out tonight that the thing I have been refusing for ten weeks is the only thing in this kingdom that could put a true sentence in front of nine hundred doors in one evening. **And that the reason it is refused is that it is a sound, and a sound in this city is an hour, and no form in this kingdom can tell those two apart, and that absence is four inches long and it is the whole of this hundred and twenty-seventh day.***
