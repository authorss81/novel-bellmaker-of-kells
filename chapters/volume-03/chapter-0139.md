# Chapter 0139 — What The Bell Would Say, And Why He Does Not Say It

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and twenty-fifth day and Tomas Vale was on the flat with the barrow and the tarpaulin over the cradle and the sack rolled against the wheel, and he was going to walk the Wharf Road end to end and count the bells on it, because he has known for about a month that he wanted to know whether this city could hear one bell differently from nine hundred.

**It is about a mile. There are nine bells in it. He counted them on the ninety-seventh and there were nine and none of them was sounding and none of them was broken, and the count of them is a figure nobody in this city keeps.**

---

## And he went along it and at every one of the nine he asked the same three things, and he got an answer at four of them and no answer at five, and the answer at all nine was the hour.

The first was over a gate in a yard and a man who had the yard looked at it and then at him.

“**Do you strike it.**”

“**No.**”

“**Who does.**”

“**I have never seen anybody.**”

“**What is it for.**”

“**It has been on that frame since the frame was new.**”

---

The second was over a door of a shop and the man behind the counter struck it himself, and he did it at the same minute every day and he could not have told anybody what it was for.

“**What time is that.**”

“**It is the time it is.**”

“**What is it for.**”

“**It is the time.**”

**And that is the whole of it and it took him about four seconds and the man was not offended and he was not a philosopher and he was not going to be thanked and he had about nine customers.**

---

The third was a granary gable bell that is not the wharf's, and the wharfman is Nevin Holt and has been on that flat thirty-one years and knows every bell on it, and he said the thing that made the whole morning.

“**That is not mine and I have never asked whose it is.**”

“**Does it ring.**”

“**It rings.**”

“**When.**”

“**When there is nothing else.**”

---

The fourth was in a boatyard and the man in it has been there nineteen years and did not look up.

“**That is the tide bell and I do not know who keeps it and I have not asked and you are the fourth person this year who has asked me about a bell.**”

“**What is it for.**”

“**It is a bell.**”

---

**And at the other five he got nothing at all, and the reason he got nothing at all is not that they are kept from him. It is that there is no answer to give, and a man who cannot give an answer cannot invent one, and I have spent five weeks finding that out in counters and rooms and this morning I found it out on a road, and a road is the cheapest place in the kingdom to find it out and it took me an hour.**

---

## And here is the finding, and he did it on a step by the river at about the sixth hour with nine bells in a book, and it is the whole of a hundred and twenty-five days in one sentence and it is not new and he has known it since the hundred and fourth and had it in his own hand on the hundred and sixth.

**A city of about nine thousand people has about nine hundred bells in it and not one of them is a keeper's bell and not one of them is a plain bell that somebody decided to hit, and every one of the nine hundred is the hour. A man in a town of about two thousand walks into a market. A man in a city of about nine thousand comes out of his own door at the hour.**

**And a bell they have heard every day of their lives is not a signal to them. It is the hour. If I struck one this evening — not one of these nine, one of my own, in a place nobody has heard it in — then about a few hundred people in this city would come out of their doors, out of habit, in the ordinary way, the way two hundred people came out into a yard on the hundred and sixth and let a bell ring four times and went in again and made no noise. Not one of them would be an answer. Every one of them would be a person who heard a bell.**

---

## And the shape of the thing he would be saying is the shape of everything he has ever made, and he wrote it out flat on a step, and it is four lines and it took him about nine minutes.

> *A warning names what it does not know. It names the maker's own fault. And it tells nobody what to decide. **That is the shape and it is the shape of every bell I have rung four times in my life, and it is the shape the whole of this trade is, and if I make a fifth it will be that shape or it will be nothing.**
>
> *And I have one keeper, which is me, and one carrier, which is in a sack against a wheel, and one answer, which is me. **A bell one man put up and one man struck is a man talking, and the whole of the difference between that and a warning is that anybody can walk away from a warning.** And I am one of the three answers and I cannot be two, and the second one I am, the first one stops being an answer and becomes a man saying the same thing twice, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man could be two. **If there were three of me.**

---

## And then the thing he cannot unhear, and it is the hundred and sixth and a yard on the top terrace and a man at a rail with a book of his own and no office, and he is going to write it out and then he is not going to write it out again.

> *On the hundred and sixth, at about the sixth hour, in a yard on the top terrace, a bell about as big as a cartwheel went over a gate about nine feet high, and there was nothing on the plate, and about forty people read nothing and went in again, and about two hundred let it ring four times and made no noise, and a man turned a plate over and struck it four times whether or not there was anything on the plate.
>
> ***And then that man said out loud, to a yard, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know whether he is to strike it, and that there is nobody above him to ask.** He has stood at that rail six years. He has a book of his own and no office. He is not a coward and he is not a villain and he is exactly right.
>
> ***That is what a schedule sounds like from underneath.** I have heard it now and I cannot unhear it, and I have been carrying a shape for six days — a plain claim, naming what is known, naming what I do not know, naming my own fault, telling nobody what to decide — and I thought the difficulty was the form. **The difficulty is that a shape is a sound, and a sound in this city is an hour, and about nine hundred of them go at an hour appointed by somebody else's paper, and a man who adds a tenth and expects a different ear is a man who has not listened to a yard.***
>
> *And I have no keeper and no bar and no third answer and no fourth. **I have a frame in a cradle four feet off this step with nothing in it that can strike and a receipt that forbids any person from putting anything into it that could, and the second clause says the frame stays in the reinforced padded cradle for the carriage, and that clause is the reason I cannot pay for anything in this city with the barrow. The bell is in a sack. And about nine hundred bells in this city go at an hour appointed by somebody else's paper, and I have found out what that is from underneath, and it is a man at a rail saying he does not know whether he is to strike it and that there is nobody above him to ask.***
>
> ***If I put a sound into about a few hundred people in this city this evening, I have nothing to say that would survive being said by a stranger in four towns. And the instrument that would carry a true thing out of me in one evening and put it in front of every door in this city is the one instrument I have been refusing since before we left Kells, and it is the wrong shape for it, and both of those are true at once and I have not been able to get round either.***

---

## And a bell is not a document and a document is not a bell and he was holding the difference in his two hands on a step by the water, and it is the only thing he has got that is worth the walk.

> *Everything I make is a thing a man cannot edit, and everything I write is a thing a man can produce against me by a stranger for forty years, and the difference between the two is not a matter of craft and it is not a matter of quality. **A claim in metal cannot be shown to anybody on a page and cannot be read by anybody who was not in the reach. A sentence on paper is read by every clerk who comes into a room after me, and it does not need me in the room, and it can be produced by a man I have never met and will not meet.** I proved that on the hundred and second in a market and I paid it on the hundred and fifteenth for fourpence in a public room and I have been carrying both halves of it in a coat for ten days and I am not going to put one of them on a wall to make the other one feel better.
>
> ***And there is nothing I can do with a claim in metal and nothing anybody can do against me with one, and there is nothing I can do with a sentence on paper and anything at all can be done to me with it, and I have got one of each, and the one I can do nothing with is mine and the one anybody can use is the only sentence I have ever managed to get about myself.***
>
> *That is the difference and I am holding it in my two hands and I have been holding it for ten days and I have not found a form for it, **and there is no form in this kingdom for saying that a thing you cannot prove is the only thing you have, and if there were one then somebody with a column would ask me what I wanted it for.**

---

## And at about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step by the river with the tarpaulin over the cradle and the four bolts made fast, and the sack rolled against the wheel, and he unrolled it and put his hand flat on the crown of a plain carrier of fourteen pounds two ounces with no wax in it and no dressed groove and only a caster's mark and a day, and it is not struck, and he rolled it up again and made it fast.

**That is the third time. The first was on the hundred and sixth at about the eighth hour and the second was on the hundred and twenty-second, and the two of them were for different reasons and it is the same act three times now, and a man who has put his hand on the same crown three times in a hundred and twenty-five days is a man who is thinking about it, and I am not going to write down in this book what about.**

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and twenty-fifth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and twenty-fifth. **Nine bells in a walk of about a mile and I asked three things at every one of them and I got an answer at four and nothing at five, and the answer at all nine was the hour.**
>
> *Second column, and the finding, and I have had it since the hundred and fourth and this is the day I walked it. **About nine hundred bells in this city and not one of them a keeper's bell and not one of them a plain bell that somebody decided to hit, and every one of them is the hour.** A man in a town of about two thousand walks into a market. A man in a city of about nine thousand comes out of his own door at the hour. **If I struck one this evening then about a few hundred people would come out of their doors out of habit and not one of them would be an answer, and every one of them would be a person who heard a bell, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a person hearing a bell is a thing that got entered anywhere.**
>
> *And the shape, which is four lines and takes about nine minutes and is the shape of the whole trade. **What I would be making has to admit what it does not know, has to carry my own fault in it, and must not tell a single listener what to do about any of it, and if I make a fifth it will be that shape or it will be nothing at all.** Underneath that there is one keeper, and it is me, and one carrier in a sack, and one answer, and it is also me. **And a bell one man put up and one man struck is a man talking, and a man talking is the one thing a warning cannot be.** I am one of the three answers and I cannot be two and there is no form on which I could be two. **If there were three of me.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and why this is a different entry from a road with nine bells in it. **I have known since the hundred and fourth that a city of nine thousand is not a market of nine hundred, and I went up and down a mile of this flat this morning to find out what the difference sounds like from underneath a claim, and the answer is that it does not sound like anything at all.** On the hundred and sixth a bell went over a gate in a yard on the top terrace with nothing on the plate and the yard let it ring and went in again, and the man who struck it told the yard in plain words that he does not know whether he is to and that there is nobody to ask. **Every one of the nine hundred in this city is that yard. So a tenth is not a signal, it is one more hour, and a true thing standing in that position is a false thing, and I would be doing in an afternoon with something out of my own hands exactly what I have been reading about for six weeks.** He is right at that rail and I am not going to tell him and he is not owed a visit.
>
> *And the frame is four feet off this step in a reinforced padded cradle with four retaining bolts made fast, and there is nothing in it that can strike, and the receipt forbids any person from putting anything into it that could, and the second clause says the frame stays in that cradle for the carriage, **and that clause is the reason I cannot pay for anything in this city with the barrow and I have used it to be a man who pays cash for an hour of a room over a shop.** I cannot put a bell up anywhere in this city and I have not tried and I am not going to.
>
> *And a bell is not a document and a document is not a bell. **A claim in metal cannot be edited and cannot be read by anybody who was not in the reach. A sentence on paper is read for forty years and produced against you by a stranger.** I have got one of each, and the one I can do nothing with is mine, **and the one anybody can use is the only sentence I have ever managed to get about myself, and I put it there on purpose for fourpence in a public room, and the instrument that would carry a true thing out of me in one evening and put it in front of every door in this city is the one I have been refusing since before we left Kells, and it is the wrong shape for it, and both of those are true at once and I have not got round either.** Nobody in this kingdom has a place to put what I am holding, and anybody who built one would want to know the use of it, and I have four clauses about a carrier and not one word about a sentence.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no claim made in a bell, and no perception attempted and none had.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it has been in the open every hour of this day and a boatyard is not a building and a yard is not a building and a road is not a building. The carrier in the sack untouched, forty-eight days, and I have put my hand on its crown three times now in a hundred and twenty-five days and I rolled it up again and made it fast and it is not struck. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Her bell is in her bag.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about me, and nine bells in a mile counted in a book in my own hand which is not a document and is nobody's, and a plate, and a wall, and a cart.** None of them is a fourteenth and I have produced nothing today and asked for nothing and I have written down three things that I asked a man in a yard and a man behind a counter and a wharfman and a boatyard about, and not one of them was a reason and not one of them is going to be in a form.
>
> *Which office is it. **It is about nine hundred bells that are the hour and a rule nobody wrote this year and a plate two streets off that cuts about nine hundred forms a year and a schedule that a man at a rail carries out six days a week without knowing whether he is to.** I have worked out today that the thing I have been refusing since before we left Kells is the only instrument in this kingdom that could put a true sentence in front of nine hundred doors in one evening, **and that it is the wrong shape for it, and that the reason it is the wrong shape is that it is a sound, and in this city a sound is an hour, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom that can tell the difference between a bell that is telling you the time and a bell that is telling you a true thing, and that absence is four inches long and it is the whole of it and nobody is wicked.***
