# Chapter 0122 — A Counter Where Nothing Wants A Reason

It was about the fourth hour of the hundred and eighth day and he went to the arch at the foot of the top terrace first, because it is on the way and because a man who has been refused at four counters in four days learns to be refused cheaply.

Perrin Lowe was there and the counter was nine feet long and there were three clerks behind it and about nine in the room over it, and the notice on the wall said **any person may bring a paper to this counter and ask what it is**, and the paint was the same colour it had been on the ninety-eighth and had begun, in ten days, to look like paint.

“**I have not got a paper.**”

“**Then you have come to the wrong end again and you have come to it the other way up this time, which is worse.**”

“**I have a number. Four one eight two.**”

Lowe wrote nothing down and looked at him for slightly longer than he had on any previous occasion.

“**That is not a paper. If you had brought me a paper with that number on it I could have told you what it was in about the time it takes to look at it, and I have told you four times now that there is no box on anything that comes out of this building for a question, and I am not going to tell you a fifth time on a morning when I have a queue.**”

He put his thumb on the notice.

“**It is the same notice. You may read it as often as you like. It says what it says and it has said it for nine years and it is not going to say more because a man has learned to read it.**”

---

**And then Tomas Vale went up four steps and through a door propped with a bucket, on the middle terrace, with a barrow left in the street below the steps and the four bolts on the cradle made fast, and he asked his question in a public room, and this time he was not asked for anything first.**

**The question was the same one he had asked in that room on the ninety-eighth and it was the only one he had. Not *what is this document*.**

“**Can a person read this.**”

The man who was the clerk of that hall had a pen behind his ear. His name was **Coll Salkeld** and he had been in that hall about six years, and he put the pen down and picked it up again, which is a man buying himself about four seconds.

“**Which thing.**”

“**A record. Of a matter. Belonging to a district I have never been in.**”

Salkeld looked at him for longer than four seconds.

“**That is a better question than the one you asked me on the ninety-eighth and I am not going to tell you that, because you did not ask me to and because I am not going to be a man who improves a stranger's mood at a counter. Sit down or do not sit down. **Yes.**”

---

## And he said it, and then he said the other half of it, and the other half is two documents instead of one.

“**Any person may read the record of any matter in that room, at any hour it is open, and the record is a public document and it belongs to the court and not to anybody in it, and the person who swore it does not own it and neither does the district.**”

“**And the file.**”

“**The file is a different object and you have just said the whole of the business in one word, so I will take it apart for you, because you have not got the thing straight and almost nobody does. **The record of a matter is the court's. The file of a matter is the office's.** They are two documents about two different subjects. The record says what the court did in a room with a door. The file says what the district did about a thing afterwards, and the file is not published and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom that says who may read a file, and there is not one either for a record.**”

He put the pen behind his ear.

“**A person may read the first without ever touching the second and I could not tell you who is allowed to read the second if you asked me, because it is not my business and I have never been asked.**”

---

**And that is the whole of the second half, and Tomas Vale stood in a public room and understood that he had been carrying two words in one bag for a hundred and eight days, and that a court publishes and an office files, and that the difference between the two words is four inches long, and that he had thought the difference was a building.**

Then he said the thing about the absence, and it came out of the man in the ordinary voice, unasked, as it had on the ninety-eighth.

“**And there is no box for a reason on that form. There is a box for the thing sworn, a box for the person who swore it and a box for the hour, and that is the whole of it, and there is no fourth box and there has never been a fourth box in the nine years I have been in this hall, and if there were a fourth box on it asking why a man swore a thing about himself then nobody would ever swear anything.**”

“**You have said that to me.**”

“**I said it to a man in this room ten days ago and I have not thought about it since and you have come back and asked me the same question with a better sentence on the front of it, and I have given you the same answer, and that is the whole of what has happened in this hall in ten days.**”

---

**And then he said the part that turned the hundred and eighth over, and he said it while walking, the way he said the part about a hearing, in about as long as it takes to cross a hall.**

“**Why did you not walk through that door on the ninety-eighth.**”

“**I did not have anything of my own to swear.**”

“**No. You did not. But that is not why, and you know it is not why, and if you go on writing that down as the reason you will be writing down a reason for thirty years and none of them will be this one.**”

He stopped at the end of the long room.

“**You did not walk through it because a reader is not a swearer. **A man who reads has to give nothing and gets nothing and is asked nothing, and a man who swears has to give a sentence with his own name at the top of it, and I have watched about four hundred people a year come in here and put a true sentence on that wall for fourpence, and it moves nothing, and it is on that wall for forty years, and not one name on it is a disgrace. **A reader wants no reason and gets none. That is the whole of the door and it was open the whole ten days you were standing in it.**”

---

**And that is the finding of the hundred and eighth, and it cost him about four minutes to get and about a hundred and eight days to be ready for, and he wrote it on a step by the river at about the sixth hour with the barrow above him on the bank.**

> *The door with no reason-box on it has been open the whole time. **The reason I could not walk through it on the ninety-eighth was not the form and not the clerk and not the room. It was that I had nothing true and mine to swear about anything, and a reader is not a swearer, and a reader wants no reason and gets none.**
>
> *A record and a file. **I have been carrying those as one word for a hundred and eight days and they are two documents about two different subjects, and the court publishes one of them and the office keeps the other, and there is no box anywhere in this kingdom that says who may read a thing, and the absence of that box is the whole of the coercion, and it is in a form and not in a room.**
>
> *And the difference between the ninety-eighth and today is a building and not a man. **Nothing in that hall changed. A man in it said the same thing twice, and the second time it landed, because the first time I heard a door and today I heard a shape.**

---

**And at about the seventh hour he asked a man at a counter in that hall to walk him down a corridor to the second room, and the man decided to walk and did not say so, and it is three doors along, and it is a room he has never been in, and it is not a large one.**

There is a rail. The rail is about nine feet and it is against the wall and it is a rail for a book, not a counter.

On it there were four or five volumes, and a desk with a lamp on it, and a board on the wall with a list on it.

**And the list was about nine hundred deep, and it was a list of matters, and the columns of it were ruled the same five ways as the columns of a store's book in a room four streets and four flights up.**

“**I have a number,**” Tomas said. “**Four one eight two. The district is Netherby and the day is the eleventh.**”

The clerk looked up. He was called **Corvin Hale** and he had been in that room about four years and he had a lamp and a list and no queue.

“**Then the board and not the volumes. If you want the volumes I will not let you, and I will not tell you why, because I have not got a reason and I am not going to invent one at a quarter to the seventh hour on a day with nothing on the plate. If you want the board you may stand in front of it as long as you like and I will not ask you for a thing.**”

He put his hand flat on the desk.

“**And if you have come about the file, which you have not asked about and I am not going to assume, then I am going to tell you now so that you do not waste a morning. The file is not mine. It is the district's, and the district will not show a file to a person with no standing in it, and it is right, and I have read the paper that says so and there is no arguing with it and I am not going to try. You have no standing in Netherby. Neither have I. I keep the door.**”

“**Who opens the file.**”

“**I do not know, and that is not a thing I am permitted to say, and I notice that I have said it anyway, and I would like to go back to my lamp.**”

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step by the river with the tarpaulin over the cradle, four bolts made fast, and the sack against the wheel, and Tamsin Rook nine feet off his left shoulder with a bell in her bag, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and eighth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.**

> *The hundred and eighth. **A court has a second room, and in it a rail and a list about nine hundred deep, and I have a number, and I have not been refused at either.**
>
> *I asked a man at the arch first because it is on the way, and he told me that a number is not a paper, which is the third time he has told me the truth for nothing in ten days, and he read the notice over my head at me, and he is right about the notice and I have read it about nine times now and I am not going to ask him a fifth question in this city.*
>
> *Second column, and it is the whole of today. **Can a person read this.** I asked it in the same words I asked it on the ninety-eighth and this time it was not a wasted question, and the reason it was not a wasted question is that this time it is the whole of what I need. **The record of a matter is the court's and the file of a matter is the office's. Two documents about two different subjects. I may read one of them without ever touching the other and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom that says which, and there is not one for either.** And a man in a hall of about forty people told me that a reader is not a swearer and that a reader wants no reason and gets none, **and that the door was open the whole ten days I was standing in it, and that what stopped me was not the form and not the room and not the clerk, it was that there was nothing of mine in my mouth.**
>
> *I have written *if there had been one true thing of mine I could have said, I would have gone through it on the ninety-eighth* and that is a sentence I am suspicious of, because I did not want to go through it, and I have wanted a document of my own for about twelve days. **So the true reason is the one I wrote on the ninety-eighth and it has not changed: thirteen documents and not one of them is about me. It took a second visit to hear the rest of it and the rest of it did not make me braver, it made me smaller, which is what usually happens.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the thing I have got. **A board on a wall in a room I have never been in, with about nine hundred matters on it going back years, and one of them is four one eight two, and the district on the line is Netherby, and the day is the eleventh.** And the five columns on that board are ruled the same five ways as the five columns in a store's book four streets and four flights up, which I noticed in about two seconds standing nine feet off it and which I have not got the bottom of. A clerk walked me down a corridor and told me I could stand in front of it all day and would not be asked for a thing, **and nobody in this city has ever asked me for a thing, and I have been let into every room in this city that I have walked into in thirteen days, and there is not one villain in any of them and there is not one door.**
>
> *And the file. **The record of a matter is the court's and the file of a matter is the district's, and a district will not show a file to a person with no standing in it, and the clerk who told me that is right, and I have no standing in Netherby and he has no standing in Netherby and he said so himself and said he keeps the door. Nobody in this kingdom will tell me who opens it and the man who keeps the door told me he is not permitted to say, and then said it anyway, and then asked to go back to his lamp.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung.** Four bolts and the frame out of doors on the bank above the step, and it has not been in a building since the thirty-third and I have been in two today and it makes no difference to the frame. The carrier in the sack untouched, thirty-one days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells. **Thirteen documents, and a board that is the court's, and a list of nine hundred matters that is the court's, and neither of them is mine, and a man who carries a court list in a coat has mistaken a thing he may read for a thing he has got.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a format, and the format has a second room with a rail in it, and in that room there is a board with nine hundred matters on it and about nine hundred matters a year going into it, and I am stood in front of it with a number in my head and no paper in my coat, and tomorrow I am going to read all of it standing up, and there is nothing in this kingdom that can stop me doing that.**
