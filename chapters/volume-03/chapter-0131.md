# Chapter 0131 — The Name Of A Thing On A Board Is Not A Person

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and seventeenth day and Tomas Vale went down off the middle terrace with the barrow left at the foot of the top stair, and there were about nine hundred people in that market and about two hundred of them were going past a board on a public wall in about nine minutes' worth of a morning, and one of the sheets on that board was his.

**He stood at the edge of it and he did not go in, which is what he did in a market on the hundred and sixth and is a habit now and not a principle.**

The sheet was at the right-hand end of the middle board, under about nine others, and the two nails were about four inches apart at the top corners and one of them was not straight and had been straightened with a thumb.

He read it standing up, the fourth time in three days, and it took him about four seconds.

> *I am the keeper of a paper or instrument of a kind used to repeat an answer, being of the kinds entered in the store list of the district. I have been in a store and read the list. The list does not say what any of the ninety words in it is for. And a column headed what the thing is for is empty from end to end, and I know a man who will not fill it in and I do not blame him for it.*

Four sentences. About ninety words. **It is the truest thing he has ever put in a document. About two hundred people read it last night in about nine minutes, and nobody has counted them since, and nobody is going to, and I am not going to either.**

She had a tray and about nine dozen needles in it and a bit of string, and she had been at that pitch long enough to have a worn place in the ground.

“**Is that yours.**”

“**It is.**”

“**I did think it was a handbill about a horse until I got to the third line.**”

“**It is not a handbill about a horse.**”

“**No. It is not.**” She put the tray down on her hip. “**Well. It is a fact.**”

**And that is what a market is. It is about nine hundred people and the one who comes round the edge of a crowd and asks a stranger a question is a woman who has been selling needles here for years and has no interest in him whatever, and she will tell two hundred people over the next four days that the man in the sheet is a man who will say so to your face, and she will not be thanked for it and will not know there was anything in it.**

He had said his own name out loud, in a market, to a stranger, in the ordinary voice, and it cost him nothing at all.

**That is the price. A man says his own name in a market and it is worth about the same as a handbill about a horse, and what is not worth about the same as a handbill about a horse is the nine of the two hundred who will take it out of here in a different town inside a year.**

What he had come down for was to find out what a district is required to do with a sworn answer once it has got one. He got the answer off a woman with a tray of pins and a hammer before he had asked anybody in a room.

She was at the next board along. She had a bag with pins in it and a hammer in the front of her apron and eleven sheets folded under her arm, and she had a face of a woman doing about four hundred small jobs a week and not looking at any of them.

He stood beside her for about four minutes and watched her do two sheets, and then he asked her the cheapest question in the kingdom, which is not the question he asks clerks and is a better one, because she was not a clerk and nobody had told her anything.

“**Do you know what they say.**”

“**No.**”

“**Do you want to know.**”

“**No.**” She straightened a nail with her thumb. “**I have been doing the boards along about two years and I get them from the cart and I nail them and I go on down the row. There is about four hundred of them a year and I am paid by the sheet and I am paid the same for one as for the other.**”

“**The other.**”

“**A handbill about a horse is a handbill about a horse and I get two a week off that board and they are the same price as the Crown ones and they weigh the same and if you stood at that end all day you could not tell which was which unless you could read, and about two hundred people a day go past and not one of them stops.**”

**And she had said the whole of it, unasked, in about four seconds of her own work, and she did not know that she had said anything at all, and she is not a villain and she is not part of anything and she is not going to be thanked by him or by anybody.**

The finding is four inches long, and he worked it out at about the sixth hour, standing at a board with a bag of pins and a hammer about four feet off him.

*What a district is required to do with a sworn answer is send it round. What it is required to do with the reading of it is nothing at all, because there is no reading, because nobody is unrolling anything.*

**About eleven thousand lines are made in a district in a year. There is no form for a list. There has never been a form for a list. And there is no form anywhere in this kingdom by which a district can be asked to take a thing back off a board, and the reason there is not is not secrecy and it is not a wall — it is that a district has never once had to un-say a thing, because every one of the eleven thousand is a thing somebody wanted to be true.**

And the other half, which he had been carrying since the hundred and sixteenth without knowing it:

*He went and stood at the end of that board and tried to find the machinery that would take his four sentences down, and the machinery is a woman with a bag of pins and a hammer and about eleven sheets under her arm, and she is paid the same whatever is on them, and there is nothing above her in that arrangement and nothing below it, and if she were to take one down there is nobody in this kingdom who would ever know.*

**That is not a conspiracy and it is not a comfort. It is a price list.**

**And at about the sixth hour he stood at the end of it and said nothing to anybody for about two hours, which is what a man does in a market of about nine hundred people. It is also what about nine people an hour do at a rail in a court hall four streets off, and about four hundred people a year go into that hall and have a true sentence of their own written on the wall for fourpence, and none of the three is about him.**

**He did not go back to the court and ask for the sheet to be taken down. He has known for a day that he cannot and he went and looked for the way round it and there is not one, and the reason he did not ask is four inches long and it is a habit and not a principle and he has had it since the ninety-eighth:**

> *Whenever a building shows me a way of reading a person's own sworn word, I think about hers.*

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step at the foot of the top stair with the tarpaulin over the cradle and the four bolts made fast where they were made fast at the fourth hour, and the sack rolled against the wheel, and the frame had not been in a building since the thirty-third, and it was thirty feet off him and it makes no difference to the frame that a woman with a bag of pins gets paid the same for both kinds of sheet.**

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and seventeenth, and filled the first and second columns and left the third and the fourth as they were.

> *The hundred and seventeenth. **The sheet went up last night and about two hundred people read it in about nine minutes and I stood at the end of that board this morning and worked out that I cannot get it down, and it took about two hours, and the answer is a woman with a bag of pins and a hammer who is paid by the sheet and is paid the same for a Crown sheet as for a handbill about a horse.**
>
> *Second column, and the whole of what I got for it, which is the answer to the thing I said I was going to go and find out. **A district is required to send an answer round and it is required to do nothing whatever about the reading. About eleven thousand lines go out of a district in a year and there is no form for a list and there never has been, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom for asking a district to un-say a thing, and the reason there is not one is that a district has never had to.** Nothing in that is a room and nothing in it is a man and nothing in it is a door. **It is a price list and the price of a Crown sheet and the price of a handbill about a horse are the same, and I stood at that wall for about two hours and about two hundred people went past it in the ordinary way and not one of them stopped and that is not because of the Office and it is not because of me.**
>
> *And a woman selling needles put her head round and asked me if it was mine and I said it was, in a market, in the ordinary voice, to a stranger, and it cost me nothing whatever. **She is not a villain and she has no interest in me and she will tell two hundred people over four days that the man in the sheet will say so to your face, and she will not be thanked for it and will not know there was anything in it.** That is what a market is. About nine hundred people and one of them comes round the edge of the crowd and asks, and I have paid fourpence for a sentence and I have just been given the other half of it for nothing in front of a woman with needles.
>
> *I did not go back to the court. **There is no form in this kingdom for asking a district to take a thing off a board, and I spent a day finding that out the slow way instead of the way that takes a clerk about four seconds, and the slow way cost me two hours in a market and the four seconds would have cost me the rest of my life with a man at a rail remembering me.** That is not a rule. It is the habit I wrote out flat on the ninety-eighth the first time, and the third time I have set it down, and I have not improved one word of it in twenty days and I am not going to, **because a habit is cheaper than a rule and I have got extremely good at them in a hundred and seventeen days, and a man who is good at habits is a man who has stopped deciding.**
>
> *And I stood in that market for about two hours and said nothing to anybody. **Nobody in this kingdom has to have a reason for standing in a market and nobody has to have a reason for standing at a rail either, and it turns out that both of those are the same door with the reason-box taken off it, and I have spent five weeks walking up to counters and being asked what I want, and this morning I found a counter in this city that does not ask.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and no sheet taken off a board.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it has been in the open every hour of this day. The carrier in the sack untouched, forty days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. Her bell is in her bag.
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about me, and a sworn sentence that is the court's and is on a board on a public wall, and a copy of it on a district's cart and now on a board in a ward, and eleven lines in a fold on a rail that nobody may copy and that I did not copy, and a sheet on a board on the top terrace with my sister's name at the foot of it that I did not take off.** Thirteen, and none of them is a fourteenth, and the only two things in this kingdom that are about me are on walls where anybody may read them.
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a price list.** About eleven thousand lines a year go out of a district and nobody unrolls them, and about four hundred of them come to one woman in a market in a year, and she is paid the same for a Crown sheet as for a handbill about a horse, and there is nobody above her and nobody below her, and if she took one down there would be nobody in the world who ever knew. **It has no face, it has no room, and it is not wicked, and I have been angry for eight weeks and could not be, and the reason I could not be is that I was looking for a face and what is in front of me is a woman being paid about the same for two kinds of paper.***
