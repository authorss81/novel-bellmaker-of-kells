# Chapter 0105 — A Leaf On A Board

There is a market on the Crown's maintained road at about the twenty-eighth mile with about nine hundred people in it, and it has a board, and the board has a roof over it, and on Day 91 Tomas Vale got the barrow to it at about the fourth hour and put the bolts in and stood and looked at it, and that is the whole of the first half hour of the ninety-first.

The board was a Crown board. **Four sheets, all printed, all with a district heading and a number at the top of them, and a handbill about a horse, and one more thing.**

The one more thing was a leaf of paper about the size of a sheet of a day-book page, folded once, and pinned at an angle under a drawing-pin, and it was the only thing on that board that anybody had put there with their own hands.

---

He did not touch it for about four minutes.

He read the four printed sheets first, standing up, because he has read every sheet in this kingdom that has come in front of him since the thirty-second day of the month and he is not going to stop on a road because one of them is different.

**One: a notice of a work inspection at a mill, on a day eleven days forward, with a district number.**

**Two: a notice of a hearing in a matter of a boundary, on a day nine days forward, with a district number.**

**Three: a list of about two hundred and forty things entered for district duty, being the sheet he had stood in front of yesterday, the one with nineteen bells in the first column, and the water bell with nothing in the keeper's box, and he looked at the line and it was the same line.**

**Four: a notice that the wire to a certain crossing is to be cut for repair on a named day and that a bell will not sound on that day, and the district asks that households will keep their own means of telling the time for one day.**

He stood in front of the fourth sheet for a bit longer than the other three and the reason is in the book, and the reason is that **a notice whose whole content is *there will be no bell tomorrow* is the only one of the four that tells a person anything they can act on, and it is the one that has a number at the top of it and a district's name under it and a man about eleven miles off who decided on a day.**

Then he went back to the leaf.

---

**It was hers and he confirmed it by comparison and not by anything else.**

He had a hand in his head and he had had it in his head for six years and he had looked at it in a bellhouse in a yard in a hurry, on a bench, in a market court, and in a room over a draper's shop in a town four days behind him, and it is the one piece of paper he has never had to look at twice to know.

**The t, which she does not cross. Everybody in that trade crosses their t. She does not.**

**A full stop, and then a space, and then the next sentence starting a little to the right of where the last one finished, as though the two were set on a bench by different people.**

**And a downstroke on the g that stops and starts.**

That is the whole of the reading and it took him about four minutes at a board under a roof in a market of about nine hundred people, standing still, with a barrow behind him, and he would have liked nine minutes and did not get them, because a man with a barrow in a market is a thing people look at.

---

**And there was no copying wax on it.**

He looked for that first, before the hand, because he has been looking for it for fifty-one days, and the wax is what the state of this business has been calling the evidence since the first day of this month, and there was none.

It was a leaf of paper and a drawing-pin and a hand. That is all it is. **And the leaf in Amos Trewin's stock book four counties away is a leaf of unknown hand and it is going to stay a leaf of unknown hand, because the wax on that one is a mark the size of a thumbnail and a mark is not a hand, and I have been carrying a wax for nine chapters of my own life as though it were a signature, and it is not one.**

He wrote that down at the board and he wrote it down hard, and it is in the second column, and it is the second entry in his whole book in ninety-one days that he has wanted to be wrong about.

---

**Five words.**

> *Ask the board. Not me.*

He read it four times. He did not unfold it further because it was not folded further; it was folded once and the fold was the other way, and the five words were the whole of the inside, and the back of the leaf was blank.

And he stood in a market of about nine hundred people and had the first thing anybody in ninety-one days had put in his hand that was not a document, and it took him about four minutes to get to the bottom of it, and the bottom of it is this.

**It is not an answer. It is not where she is, and it is not whether she is alive, and it is not a name and it is not an office and it is not a date. It is a direction, and the direction is the emptiest one in this kingdom, and she has known that for six years and she has put it on a public board anyway.**

**Because a board has no standing. You may pin a leaf to a board in a market without a form, without a reason, and without anybody owing you anything, and the man who reads the board in the morning has to read it because that is his job. There is not one door in this kingdom she could knock on that would not have wanted something.**

He wrote that down and then he wrote, immediately after it, in the same hand:

> *And she is inside it. Whatever the thing is that she is inside, she is inside it, and she is putting five words on boards in market towns, and it is the only way anybody who is where she is can be said to have said anything at all, and she has worked that out and I have not.*

---

**He did not take the leaf off the board.**

That is the whole of the ninetieth decision that the ninety-first turned on and it took him about a minute and a half and it is in the book at about the seventh hour, and the reason is not noble and he has written it flat.

> *I stood there about a minute and a half with my hand up at about the height of a drawing-pin and I did not take it.*
>
> *And the reason is Miremarket, on the eighty-first, at about the sixth hour, in a room over a draper's shop, where a man with a form and a satchel took five sheets off a table in about three hours and receipted them one at a time and it was entirely lawful, and I stood there and let it happen, and the law that let it happen was a clause of a receipt I asked a clerk to add to in a watch house in another county because I was right.*
>
> ***A paper off a public board in a Crown town is the same act with a different name. If I take that leaf then I am a man in a market with a paper that is not his, in a town where I do not know a single person, on the ninth day after a Crown thing in his cradle went four counties, and there is no clerk within a day's road who would give me a form, and there is nobody in that market who could say one word in my favour, and the whole of eight weeks has been me learning that a habit of carrying is not a right.***
>
> *And I have wanted to take it. I want it in the coat more than I have wanted anything since the seventy-seventh, and it is five words and it is on a board in the open and it has been there nine days and nobody has taken it down.*

---

The woman who kept the board came out at about the seventh hour, and she was not a clerk and she had no office and she had been doing it about nine years.

“**That one is not ours.**”

“I know it is not.”

“**Then you are not going to take it, and I am going to tell you why you have not, and it is not because I asked you. A Crown sheet comes off at the end of a period and goes back in a barrow, and there is a number against it. A leaf does not come off. A leaf stays up until somebody takes it down, and I have had that board nine years and I have had about nine leaves on it in nine years and I have taken five of them down and put four back up again in about the same place.**”

“Why the four.”

“**Because somebody wanted them read by the next man and not by me.**”

She looked at the leaf for a moment.

“**It has been up nine days. It is read every morning before the market, by me and by about four other people, and it has not been taken down, and you want me to tell you why, and I am going to tell you, and it is not that nobody has seen it.**”

“Say it.”

“**Because it is a leaf and not a sheet. A sheet has a number on it. A leaf has a drawing-pin on it and nothing else, and there is no form on which anybody may be charged with a leaf, and if a man comes off a board with a leaf in his hand in this town then the man with the leaf is the man with the leaf, and we have all four of us read it and not one of us is going to say a word about it to anybody, and you may rely on that as far as this board goes and not one inch further.**”

He did not say the name. He had it behind his teeth at about the seventh hour and he did not say it and there were about four people within nine feet of the board and he has written down in the book that he did not say it and why, and the why is that a name said on a Crown board in a market town is a name in four mouths by the fourth hour, and he has made four records in eight weeks about persons who did not ask him for one.

---

**And he did not go in anywhere to ask after her.**

That is the rest of the ninety-first and it is the half that matters and it is the half nobody would have believed from a man who has walked four days up a road with a bell in a bag.

There was a thing he could have done. It was on the fourth sheet of the Crown board and it was the first one he had read: a work inspection at a mill, at a named place, on a day eleven days forward. **And in this town there was, by his own count on the board, one Crown thing and no Crown thing, and a man who travels on Crown business came and went on Crown business, and a name is a thing a man is read by, and a man can be asked about a name.**

He wrote that out in the book as an option, in full, with the arithmetic, and then he wrote underneath it:

> *And I have a receipt in five clauses in this coat and the fifth of them says I have no standing to speak for a household of this ward and no standing in any ward, and the reason there is no room for a household in a form is that receipt, and I have been glad of that clause in a street four days behind me over a room and a bench and five sheets.*
>
> *And the whole of what I would be doing up there is asking a man in a uniform whether a person is in a building, for me, about a woman who has put five words on a board and told me to ask the board, **and I would be doing it in order to be able to say I did not go and ask after her. That is the worst of the three reasons and I found it in about four seconds and it is the reason.***

He did not go. He went and put the barrow against a wall at the edge of the market and sat down on it and stayed there until about the eighth hour, and he did not write one word in the book for about an hour, which is the longest he has gone in ninety-one days.

---

The last entry of the ninety-first was at about the ninth hour of the evening, sitting on the barrow in a lane behind a row of pitches, and he filled the first and second columns and left the third and the fourth as they were.

> *The ninety-first. **Five words on a board.**
>
> *She is inside it, and she is putting leaves on public boards in market towns, and it is the only way anybody in that position can be said to have said anything at all, and she worked that out before I did and I have been walking four days up a road with a barrow looking for a room.*
>
> *The words are: **Ask the board. Not me.** Five. And it is not an answer and it is not where she is and it is not whether she is alive, and she has given me the emptiest direction in this kingdom and she has given it on the one surface in this kingdom that has no standing attached to it, and I have been glad of that surface for eight weeks and I did not know until this afternoon that it was also the only place she could put anything.*
>
> *And I want both halves of this in one paragraph and I am not going to separate them: **she is the only person alive who can say a thing to me without owing me anything, and she has spent the whole of it refusing, and I have spent eight weeks learning how to ask a person and be refused and this is the first time I have been refused by somebody who put herself in a position to refuse me and then put the refusal where I would find it in the open, and I am not going to treat that as a sign.** I know what signs are. They are the things I make out of two empties in a column and a leaf with wax on it.*
>
> *Second column, ruled apart, and the two lines in it are the two things I got wrong this month and had to put right in my own hand: **there is no copying wax on it and I have been carrying a wax for fifty-one days as though it were a signature, and a mark the size of a thumbnail is not a hand, and the leaf in a smith's stock book four counties away is a leaf of unknown hand and it stays one.** And the hand is hers — the t she does not cross, the space she leaves after a full stop, the downstroke on the g that stops and starts — and I have been wrong about the wax twice and I have been right about the hand once and I am going to be more careful about which of those is which.*
>
> *And the thing I did not do, and it is the first thing in ninety-one days I have refused that I wanted, and it is in the second column and it is a decision and not a feeling: **I did not take the leaf off the board, and a man in a form and a satchel took five sheets off a table in a town four days behind me in about three hours and I stood there and let it happen, and a paper off a public board in a Crown town is the same act with a different name.** And a woman who has kept a board nine years told me it was read every morning by about five people and not one of them will say a word about it, and I have been told that by a stranger for nothing and I am not going to thank her for it because she told me not to be thanked for things and I have four of those in four counties now and I am not going to make it five.*
>
> *And I did not go in, and there is a Crown work inspection in this town on a day eleven days forward and a name is a thing a man is read by and I could have asked, and the fifth clause of my own receipt is the reason I had no standing to ask with, and I have been glad of that clause twice in four days, and the third of the three reasons is that a man who goes and asks after her so that he can say he did not go and ask after her is doing the thing for himself and calling it for her.*
>
> *Which office is it. **I have got a name now and it is the wrong shape. It is not a room and it is not a man and it is not a form. It is about nine hundred rooms and a set of forms and about eleven thousand lines a year, and there is a woman inside all nine hundred of them writing on boards, and I have got a name for it out of a book about a bell and I am not going to write the name in this book on a road because a name in a book on a road is a name in four mouths by the fourth hour and I did not say hers this afternoon and I am not going to start now.** Sennet is four days up this road. **I am going to stand in a street in it and look at a building, and I have been standing outside buildings in four counties for eight weeks and not one of them has refused me, and that is what I am frightened of and I am not going to improve the sentence.***
>
> *All five clauses stand. Nothing was rung. Four bolts, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third. The bell in the sack untouched since the seventy-seventh and I do not know where it went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Thirteen documents, and a leaf on a board in a market town that I have not taken and am not going to go back for, and it is the only piece of paper in ninety-one days that has been given to me for nothing by somebody who does not love me, and I have written that down flat and I am not going to do anything about it.***
