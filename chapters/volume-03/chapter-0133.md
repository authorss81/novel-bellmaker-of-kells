# Chapter 0133 — She Chooses Once

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and nineteenth day and Tomas Vale went up four flights with the barrow left at the foot of the stair, as he leaves it two days out of three, and on the top terrace there is a public wall with nine Crown sheets and a handbill about a horse on it, and between the ninth sheet and the handbill there was still the sheet of paper with four drawing-pins in it, one at each corner.

**It has not moved. It is not dusty in a way that would tell him anything and it has not been taken down and it has not been added to, and about two hundred people a day walk past that wall and not one of them stops.**

Two nails in it, about four inches apart at the top corners, and one of them not straight.

At about the fifth hour he was still on the top terrace and he read the next board along.

**It is a district's paper about a person in a market. Four fields. One of them is a day.**

He read it standing up in about a minute and a half, and the second field is the market, and the third field is the day, and the fourth field is the ground and it says *the district's business* in three words.

**And the name at the head of it is not his name, and it is a woman's, and he has read about nine hundred lines of boards in five weeks and he cannot tell whether it is hers, and he is not going to decide.**

> *I have got a rule and I have written it three times and this is the fourth thing it applies to. **Two things I have seen myself, inside one day, in one city, is a man arranging a thing.** A name at the head of a sheet on a board is not a person and I have had that on the page for two days. And if I go up four flights and look at a woman's name on a district's paper and decide that it means my sister, then I have taken a name off a sheet and put it in my own head where it will be for the rest of my life, **and there is nobody in the world who could ever check me on it, and I have spent a hundred and nineteen days learning that a thing nobody can check me on is a thing I must not do.**

He did not take it off the board. That is the fourth time and it is the same decision.

At about the fifth hour he came down off the top terrace and went along to the river courts, up four steps through a door propped with a bucket, and there is no form in this kingdom that says whether he may read the top row of a public board in a public room or whether he may not.

The long room had about forty people in it in the ordinary way and the board on the far wall was in three columns and the third was a column of hours.

**There was a new row at the top. It was the top row. The ink on it was not dry, which meant it was a day old, which meant the hundred and eighteenth.**

Coll Salkeld had a pen behind his ear and did not look up, and said it in the ordinary way, as a man reads the top of a board to himself while he is doing something else.

“**That is the ninth this month.**”

“**I can count.**”

“**Then you can count and I can go back to this.**”

“**I am not going to read it.**”

Salkeld put the pen behind his ear and then took it out again and put it back, which is a man buying himself about four seconds.

“**Say why, or do not say why, and understand that I am not going to be the man who stops you.**”

“**I am not going to say why.**”

“**Then read it.**”

“**No.**”

> *She published a standing instruction in her own name on a public board on the hundred and fourteenth and said she will not be found and will not answer and will not be sent for, and that a person who wants a thing from her may go and ask a board. **Not me. A board.**
>
> *And a board is not a question and a board does not answer. It is the only surface in this kingdom that a person owes nobody a why on, and that is exactly why she put it there, and it is exactly why I have not been able to read one line of it as being for me since the day she wrote it.
>
> *And if I stand at that rail this morning and read the top row, then she has answered. **She put a true sentence in a public room in her own name about a person and a clerk wrote it down and fourpence and nobody asked her why, and the reason she did it was that it would otherwise be published for her, and the moment I read it I have turned the most expensive free thing anybody has done in this city into a thing that was for me, and there is no version of that in which it is still hers.**
>
> *A row on a public board is a thing she put there for a clerk and for the forty people a week who go through that door. **If it is also for a man with a barrow then she put it there twice, and she did not say she would do that, and I have not got a right to it, and I asked a clerk on the hundred and fifth to write a clause into a receipt for me and he was right to and I would do it again.**

He stood nine feet off that rail for about a quarter of an hour and did not read the top row and Salkeld went back to his pen and about nine people came in and out of the door and not one of them looked at the man standing nine feet off not reading.

**The reason is four inches long and it is not care and it is not a rule, and he worked it out standing at that rail with his hand on it.**

**And nobody in that room asked the woman who swore it why, because there is no box on that form for a reason, and a person swearing to a thing about herself does not owe anybody a why, and that is the cheapest thing that has ever happened in this city and it cost her fourpence and it moved nothing.**

**There is a person in this city of about nine thousand who is a stranger to him and who has done a thing this week, on her own account, in the open, in a room with a clerk in it, that is about the arrangement of a man who is not the clerk of it. And she did it and told nobody and asked for nothing and he cannot meet her and cannot write to her and cannot thank her and cannot be seen to know.**

> *A person can do a thing for you on your account and refuse to be thanked and refuse to be met, **and the refusal is not a wound. It is the shape of the person.** I have been carrying her for six years as a thing I lost and I have written that down in this book twice and it is the wrong sentence, and I have known it was the wrong sentence since the hundred and fourteenth and I have not been able to correct it because correcting it would be a thing I did about myself.
>
> *And a thing she did in a public room about a person is not about that person. **It is about a column and a duty and whatever it is she has been doing that she has never told me, and I am not going to be the man who works it out of a row on a wall and calls it finding out.**

Hob Kersey is in a room with a form in it. He cannot say what put him there.

**And Tomas Vale cannot say what put him there either, and the reason neither of them can say it is the same reason and it is four inches long:**

*Two true things make a story. And there is no form in this kingdom for a story.*

*One true thing: a man read a sheet on a wall. Two true things: he read a sheet on a wall and then he said his own three things about his own pitch in a market and then somebody said them back to him at ten feet. **Put those two things end to end and you have a man with a grievance and a man with a sentence, and a man with a grievance is exactly the shape a district is looking for at about the fourth hour of a round.** Two true things make a story and a story is not a form and a story cannot be entered and a story dies with the man who tells it and the man who hears it, and there are about nine people in that market who heard it and I do not know one of them and I have not asked and I am not going to.*

**So the man on the bench cannot say it, because he does not know who said the four inches. And I cannot say it, because if I say the first half I am a man with a grievance and if I say the second half I am the man who put a name at the head of a wall, and neither of those is true and both of them are the half of it that a form wants.**

**And that is the most expensive thing I have found out in a hundred and nineteen days, and it is not a thing anybody can be thanked for or told or written down, and I have got exactly one instrument in this kingdom that cannot be taken off me, and it is a head, and I cannot give it to him, and he is on a bench because of it.**

At about the seventh hour he wrote the three reasons out flat in his own book, and they are the reasons he wrote out flat on the ninety-first, and the third of them is the one he is using.

> *There are three reasons I have not gone and not written, and the first two are good and the third is the one I am using.*
>
> *The first is that a paper off a public board is the same act with a different name as five sheets off a table in a room, and I have refused it three times and I am not going to find a new name for the fourth.*
>
> *The second is that if a person in a capital learns a thing about me out of a piece of paper then the person has learned it out of a piece of paper, and that is a sentence about a person and not about me.*
>
> *And the third is the worst and I am not going to improve it in a month: **a man who goes and asks so that he can afterwards say he did not go is doing the thing for himself.** There is a sheet on a public wall in this city that has told me where she is and I have not used it, and there is a row on a board in a court four streets and four flights below that I have not read this morning on purpose, and I am not going to go up four flights and look at a district's paper with a woman's name on it and decide that it means her. **If I go up, then the only new thing in this book is about me, and it would be the fifth entry about me, and I did not make the first four.**
>
> *So I am not going. And I did not come back up. I was at the top of the stair at the fourth hour with the barrow at the bottom of it and there is a wall four streets and four flights above my head with a woman's name at the foot of one sheet and a district's paper with somebody else's name on another, **and I have been down here all day, and I am not going to be the man who stands under his own rule and watches it get soft.**

He wrote the last entry of the hundred and nineteenth, and filled the first and second columns and left the third and the fourth as they were.

> *The hundred and nineteenth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **She chose once, on her own account, and I am not thanked for it, and I cannot thank her, and I did not read it, and I am not going to find out what she said.**
>
> *Second column, and what I know, which is four sentences and no more. **A woman swore a true thing about herself in a public room in this city on the hundred and eighteenth, at a counter, in her own name, for fourpence, and a clerk wrote it down in a column with an hour against it, and nobody asked her why, because there is no box on that form for a reason.** She did it because it would otherwise be published for her. **That is the whole of what I have and I am not going to improve it and I am not going to go and look.**
>
> *And the other thing on the wall on the top terrace. **A district's paper about a person in a market, four fields and one of them a day, two nails about four inches apart, up two days, with a woman's name at the head of it that is not mine.** I read it in about a minute and a half and I cannot tell whether it is hers and I am not going to decide, **because two things I have seen myself inside one day in one city is a man arranging a thing, and a name at the head of a sheet is not a person, and I have both of those on the page already and I wrote the first one down three times.** I did not take it off the board. That is the fourth time and it is the same decision and I have not found a new name for it and I am not going to.
>
> *And the top row of the sworn board, and I am not going to describe it. **It was the top row, the ink was wet, a clerk told me it was the ninth this month without being asked, and I stood nine feet off a rail with my hand on it and did not read one line.** There is no box in this kingdom that lets a man not read a public board, so a man who leaves one carries a reason round with him, and I am not going to produce mine in that room. **The reason is that she has put a thing in a public place saying she will not answer, and a man who goes and reads the one thing she put in the open has made her answer him.** She wrote it for a clerk and for the forty people a week who come through that door. If it is also for a man with a barrow then she put it there twice and she did not agree to that, and I am not going to be the one who made her.
>
> *And the thing I cannot get round, and it is a man on a bench and it is nine days. **Neither of us can say the true thing and both of us know it.** The chain is a man reads a wall, then a man speaks in a market, then a stranger speaks to him, and about nine people heard the second of those and I do not know one of them and I am not going to ask. **Two true things make a story, and a story is not a form, and a story cannot be entered anywhere, and a story dies with the two men who are the only ones who can tell it, and that is why the whole of what happened to that man is sitting in four boxes with a day in one of them and the true thing is in none of the four.** He cannot say it and I cannot say it and I put one end of it into the world on a wall for fourpence and there is nobody in this city who is ever going to be able to tell him that.
>
> *Nobody has been thanked and nobody has been rescued and nothing has been resolved and nothing has been taken off a board. **A person can do a thing for you on your own account and refuse to be thanked and refuse to be met, and the refusal is not a wound, it is the shape of the person, and I have been carrying her for six years as a thing I lost and that is the wrong sentence and I have not been able to correct it because correcting it would be a thing I did about myself.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, and nothing bound, and nothing asked of anybody.** Four bolts, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it has been thirty feet off me at the foot of a stair all day and it was in the open on the top terrace this morning for about an hour and a half and neither of those is a building. The carrier in the sack untouched, forty-two days, and I do not know where either of my two public ringings went and I am not going to find out. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it. **Her bell is in her bag.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about me, and a sworn sentence that is the court's and is on a board on a public wall, and a copy of it on a district's cart and on a board in a market, and a district's paper on a board on the top terrace that is a district's, and eleven lines in a fold on a rail that nobody may copy.** None of them is a fourteenth. **And there is a row on a board in a court four streets and four flights below that I did not read this morning and that is the only thing in a hundred and nineteen days that is mine and is not about me and I have left it exactly where it is.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a counter with a lamp on it and a form with three boxes and no reason in it, and about four hundred people a year use it, and a woman used it this week about herself, and a clerk wrote it down and did not ask her anything, and a man stood nine feet off a rail and would not read it. **I have spent a hundred and nineteen days learning that a form is the Office's and can only be filled in by a person with a standing in a district, and there is a row on that board with a woman's name in it and I have got no standing in anything, and she used the one door in this kingdom that has no box for a reason on it, and she used it about herself, and she is not going to be thanked for it and she did not do it for me.***
