# Chapter 0190 — Rafe Mear Laid a Coil and Named Three Places

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and seventy-sixth day and Rafe Mear was laying a coil of hawser on the flat boards at the head of a staging, and he was doing it at the fourth hour because tar is worse in the heat and he had three days of it in a pot and about nine days' work in him, and the second bench had eleven fathoms to have by the water turning and he could not make eleven fathoms he liked in nine days and he was going to have to make eleven fathoms he did not like, and he had known that since the day before yesterday.

The barrow stood on the boards at the edge of the flat with the four bolts made fast and it is a hundred and forty-three days since the frame has been in a building, and boards at the head of a staging are not a building.

He coiled it the way a man coils a thing he has coiled a great many times, which is without looking at his hands, and he had a boy behind him turning the coils over as they came off, and Rafe Mear did not explain anything twice, and the boy had not laid a coil yet.

"What is it," Tomas said, at about the fifth hour. "I have not asked anybody a question for two days and I have one and I am going to spend it on you, because you are the only man I have met on this river who has not told me about a form."

"I know what it is," Rafe Mear said. "I have had four men in two days and two of them wanted to know about the water and one of them wanted to know about the road. Go on then."

He kept working while he said it, which was the whole of how he said it.

"There were four places on this reach where a sheet was read out at a landing. I laid the rope for it. That is the whole of what I do about it. A man lays a length of rope round a reach so that the crews going along the reach have a line to haul against, and the rope is ordered by whoever is working the water, and the rope is mine and the ordering is not. There were four lengths on this reach and there is one, and the one that is left is at the top landing and it is the worst of the four because it is the one nobody wants."

"Where are the other three."

He turned the coil as he said it, which is a thing a man's hands do.

"The first was at a byre, and there is a byre there, and it was the second best of the four. The second was at a gate, and the gate is a different gate. It was not a gate then, it was a rope's end on a stake and there was a hut." He put his thumb down and turned the coil again. "The third was where a wall is, and the wall is not old, and it was a wall of a different kind about nine years ago and I would not want to say what the wall is now and I have not looked at it in about a year."

"That is three."

"That is three."

He stopped.

Tomas stood on the boards and did not ask him the fourth, and there was about a minute and a half in which the only noise was the water against the staging legs and a gull somewhere, and the boy stopped turning coils as well, which is how a boy knows.

"Well," Rafe Mear said.

"Go on then," Tomas said, and he was not asking, and it was the only time in a hundred and forty-three days he had said a man's own words back at him as an order to himself and not to the man, and he heard it in his own mouth and did not do it again.

"Not now," Rafe Mear said. "Turn the coil. And I am not going to finish that, and you have not asked me to, and I would have finished it about four days ago for a man who had a bottle on him. There is the fourth place and I know where it is and I have not been up it since the spring, and that is all you are getting off me and it is more than the second bench got this morning and considerably more than the man in the coat got in the spring."

---

He coiled for a while, and then he said, without stopping and without any weight on it, "The rope is a rope and it goes when the ordering stops and nobody writes down that a rope is not a document. You can have that for nothing, it is not worth a sheet. Turn the coil."

---

At about the sixth hour the four of them were on the flat above the staging with the water about nine feet down, and it was Mab Sarter's turn to say the thing she had come up the road to say, which was a number and which was not finished.

"Two hundred and fifty-eight," she said. "Ditch and fence together, and I have started the wall at the second bench and I have not finished that either, and the light was different at the far end yesterday afternoon from what it was in the morning and the number I gave the man is the number I have, and I have not told anybody I was afraid of it being wrong and I am not going to now, because it is the number I had."

Pell had gone down at the third hour with a load of chain for a man at the sixth bench. Ned was at a soft place and Isa was on a flat with a barrow of lime, and the four of them had not walked together for two days and nobody had arranged it and nobody had said anything about it.

At about the seventh hour Tomas Vale sat on a coil of rope on a staging flat with a barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and did the ten days.

---

Four tables, four trades, four men who owed him nothing. A carter who would not touch paper he had not been asked about and gave up a quarter of an hour of a morning he did not have. A weighmaster who would not put a thing in a book he did not know and held up a bridge for a third of an hour to find out that a corner was heavier than a crease. A lime seller with six boards and a heap and a price and no ledger, who could tell one thing from another in four seconds out loud and had nowhere to put it. A bench clerk who would not carry a figure to another bench, and who had that figure in a rate book six years and never joined the two.

Not one sentence. Not one document. A crease, a corner, a price, and a figure at the head of a column. And the paper itself had told him two things he had not had: that a draughtsman in this kingdom cannot see a place where the drawing stops, because his whole trade is a place where the drawing stops; and that the figure nearest the top of the sheet is the same figure as the sheet's number and is in the same hand, and that a man has had it in the corner of a rate book for six years and cannot join them, and that joining them is not his work.

The two questions were narrower by three lines and no nearer an answer. The rectangle was not explained, and it was not going to be explained by a man with a barrow. A box was on the hinge of a gate at the cart bridge and nobody knew what it was or who had put it there, and the man who shut the gate could no longer open it from the side he shut it, and there was no form on which anybody could ask him about that either.

A keeper of a line was asked to go on holding it, and could not refuse, because a refusal is an answer and an answer is a reason and he had no standing to put a reason in a box. That was the second of the three partial answers and it was the price of the thing and not the thing. The first had come off a clerk's mouth while he ruled a line four days ago, and it was the reason there was nothing to refuse. The third had come off a man coiling a rope an hour ago, out loud, while doing something else, and it was an account and not a finding, and it named three places and did not name the fourth, and it closed nothing.

The road went on. The map was still in the inside pocket of his coat, folded in three, with a rectangle about two hands together in it, about a third of the way along a run, and the rules stopping short of it and the numbers going on either side without a break, and it was a district's, and part of a paper, and put into his hands by a man in a coat who was not asked to do it, and he was not going to hand it back, because there is no form in this kingdom that lets a district keep a man.

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a flat of beaten earth above the water with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and the staging legs went down behind him into the dark, and the one remaining length of rope on that reach was at the top landing where nobody wanted it, and a man with a barrow had asked a rope-maker to finish a sentence and had not asked, and the third partial answer of three was on the page and it closed nothing.

> *The hundred and seventy-sixth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. A staging, boards, a coil of hawser, a boy turning coils, eleven fathoms owed by the water turning, a flat.*
>
> *Second column, the account. **Four places on this reach where a sheet was read out at a landing, and he laid the rope for all four, and the rope is ordered by whoever is working the water. There were four and there is one, and the one left is the worst of the four because it is the one nobody wants.** A byre that is a byre. A gate that was a stake and a hut and is a different gate. A wall that is not old, that was a wall of a different kind nine years ago, and he would not say what it is now. **Three, and then he stopped, and there was about a minute and a half, and the boy stopped turning coils, which is how a boy knows.** He said not now, and that he would have finished it four days ago for a man with a bottle on him, and that it was more than the second bench got this morning. Then: **A rope is a rope, and it goes when the ordering stops, and nobody has ever written down anywhere that a rope is not a document.** I said go on then and I was not asking, and it is the only time in a hundred and forty-three days I have said a man's own words back at him as an order to myself. **He did not name a line, did not say when it stopped and did not say why, and that is an account given out loud while he was doing something else, and it closes nothing.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. Three partial answers and no conclusion. **First, off a clerk's mouth while he ruled a line: a house that is answered for is a house with a keeper, and a district cannot get keepers, so there is nothing to answer for, so there is nothing to refuse. Second, off a man with a scraper on a lock step: a keeper is a person who can be asked, the asking is free, the price is four doors up a bank, and a man cannot refuse it, because a refusal is an answer and an answer is a reason and he has no standing to put a reason in a box. Third, off a man coiling a rope: a thing that was worked on this reach and then was not, named as three places and not a fourth.** A reason, a price, and an account, and none of the three is an answer. The question is what a person who holds a line is obliged to do about it, and it is not answered here and it is not answered on this river, and I am not going to write down that I know what it is, because I do not, and a sentence I have not got in me is a sentence somebody else will have to pay for.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and the warrant stands, and a fitting is not called anything it is not and it is on a gate at the cart bridge and nobody in this kingdom knows what it is or who put it there.** Four bolts made fast, and **the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and forty-three days, and boards at the head of a staging are not a building and a lock office is a building and I went into it for two hours and there was nothing in it I could ring.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, ninety-nine days from the seventy-seventh, and I have not put my hand on it.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour today, including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, nobody was thanked on any of the ten days, and I entered no name anywhere and there is no form on which I could. A sheet of paper that went onto four tables is not a fourteenth: it was in the pocket it was in on the hundred and thirty-fourth and it is in the pocket it is in now, and there is no form in this kingdom that lets a district keep a man and I am not going to hand it back.**
>
> *Which office is it. **Four tables that did not produce a sentence, a rectangle nobody could see, a figure at the top of a column, a box on a gate frame, four boxes and a fifth space with a line under it, four doors up a bank, a coil of rope on boards, and three places out of four.**
