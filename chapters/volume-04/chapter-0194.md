# Chapter 0194 — A Rope and a Post in a Yard of About Forty People

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and eightieth day and Retha Vask was standing in a yard of about forty people with a rope round her neck and a stick in her hand, in a yard behind the steps and down a passage from the low quarter and it was two hundred yards long and forty foot wide with a fire in the middle of it and about nine hundred small things against the two long walls, and she had been in the yard since the second hour and had done two rounds already, which is what she has done every morning of her life in it, which is to go down the length twice and put her hand on every post.

There were nine posts down the length of that yard and she touched all nine, and Tomas Vale came in at the top end with a barrow and stopped, because a woman with a rope round her neck going along a wall touching things is a woman doing a count and there is no way to help with a count without becoming part of it.

""You are not going to be able to help," she said, without turning round, at the fifth post. "Nine posts and about forty of us, and everything anybody in here owns is against one of those two walls or it is not here, and if a thing is not in the yard then the person who owns it is standing in front of me telling me about it, and that is the system."

"How many are standing in front of you telling you about it."

"Nobody, this morning, and that is not a good sign and I am not going to say why it is not a good sign." She came round the last post and looked at him. "You are the man off the flat. I have had the words. A carter said them at the top of my passage on the day before yesterday and he said them the same way twice and he said he had got them from a man with a barrow, and I have not sent for you and I am glad I did not have to, because I could not have sent for you, there is no form on which anybody in this yard sends for anybody."

"Is anybody in this yard frightened."

"No," she said. "Forty people and a fire and nine foot of bank with the water over the top of it, and every one of us was born a foot above this ground and has watched this happen. What we are is short of time. A frightened person runs and a person short of time carries, and a person who runs leaves what he is carrying."

She turned the stick over. The yard was dry and about a hundred and forty foot above the low quarter's ground, which was a foot above the river.

---

At about the fifth hour two people came down the bank who had not been down it before, and neither of them came to the yard, and both of them stopped forty yards short of the passage.

Mab Sarter was sitting on a wall with her back to the low quarter and a piece of chalk, and she had been counting since the fourth hour of the day before, and she had counted the run at the market ground twice, which was a thing she had never done.

"Two hundred and ninety-one the first time and two hundred and ninety-four the second," she said, before he was anywhere near her. "Ditch and fence together, and the run at the top is a stone wall and I counted the stones, which I should not have, and the three are stones. I am going to keep the two hundred and ninety-four and I am not going to tell anybody about the two hundred and ninety-one, and I am aware that is the kind of thing a person decides about themselves at about the fourth hour on a morning when the water is up."

"You have not finished the wall at the second bench."

"I have not finished the wall at the second bench and I will not finish it this week, and the wall is not the thing I came about." She put the chalk in her pocket. "The passage is under two foot at the top and I have been standing over it for about an hour and I am not going down it, and that is not because I am frightened. It is because I cannot get a number down there."

Ned Adley was at the mouth of the passage with a hod over his shoulder and had not gone into it either, and he was the man who fills the soft places in a road and who has filled one and will not fill it twice.

"I have got nine foot of new stone in my own yard about a mile up the bank," he said. "Six foot of it paid for and three foot of it not paid for until the ninth, and a flood is a thing that does not care about the ninth. That is my own and I am telling you at a standstill so that you have got it, the way I told you about the cart bridge, and I am not going to be asked about either. I am not asking you for anything. I am telling you the ninth is a real day and that my yard is a real yard and that I will be in it at the sixth hour this evening with a hod and a barrow, and if there is nine foot of water where the yard is at the sixth hour then the three foot of unpaid stone is going to be in the river, and so are about four hundred pounds of my own work, and I have been in this trade a long time and I have had a soft place go once and I did not lose that much, and this is not a soft place."

"The yard behind these steps," Tomas said. "About forty people. Is anybody going to try to get them out today."

"Somebody will," Ned said. "Not me and not from this end. There is a way out of that yard at the far end over a plank and into a boat-shed, the plank two foot under, and the door in that shed a man has to get through backwards. I know that because I have carried stone into that yard and been told to carry it back out the same way, and I asked why and was not told. I am not going to stand at the top of that passage and send a woman of forty through two foot of moving water into a shed I have never been inside of."

---

At about the sixth hour the four legs of the warning were put together again on the flat, and it is worth saying that none of the four of them was asked.

Mab Sarter carried leg three, from the fourth house to the top of the passage, and she said the words at the top of the passage to a man standing there with a barrow and a bad coat, and the man said the words back to her and got them right, and she did not go further, and nobody told her to go further. Ned Adley carried leg two, from the flat to the top of the low quarter's ground, and he said the words at four places and two of the four people were not in the yard and were not on the ground and were inside and he said them at a door. The man at the upper landing with the boat had his boat up two days and his leg was dry and a hundred yards of it and four minutes of work.

And Tamsin Rook was in that yard from the sixth hour and nobody asked her anything at any hour, including him.

She was at the ninth post when he came down the passage with a barrow he could not get down a step and did not try, and she had her hand flat on the post and her bag on the ground at her feet and her boots on the flags, and the post was nine foot of oak set in a socket of brick, and it was carrying nine hundredweight of the two long walls on one side and nothing on the other, which is a thing you can find out in four minutes with a hand and cannot find out any other way that does not cost money.

She went down the length of the yard and came back and touched the eight others, and then she stood at the fifth post with her hand on it and her face turned to the wall, and Tomas Vale stood nine feet off her and said nothing at all, because he had been on this road with her forty-four days and had not asked her once what the road was for and was not going to.

What she wrote in her own book that night was the post and the brick socket and the weight on one side, and the four columns, of which the fourth has two lines in it and the price side of the board is the other way up and the count is eight, and she did not improve any of it, and she did not write the number of people in the yard down anywhere, because she was not doing a count.

---

At about the seventh hour he was at the top of the passage and the passage was under two foot and rising, and Retha Vask came up out of the yard and stood at the top of her own steps and looked at him.

"You have got a number," she said.

"I have got a number."

"And you have got four people to say it and one of them is a girl on a wall and one of them is a man with a hod and neither of them has said it in my yard, and one of them is a carter who said it at the top of these steps on the day before yesterday, and I have got it, and so have nine other people in here, and thirty in this yard have not got it and are not going to." She put her hand on the top post, which was the same post, the one at the top of the steps, which was why she touched it twice a day. "I am not asking you to do anything. I have got a fire and nine foot of bank and eleven years of a rope, and when the water comes up to that step I am going to do what everybody in this yard has done four times in my life, which is put the small things on the plank and the plank is two foot under and the plank is a thing I have never been able to lift on a day when the water was up, and the big things stay, and the big things have always stayed."

"That is your yard."

"That is my yard and I have been in it eleven years and it is nine foot of bank above this town because a river went round here once in a time that is not in anybody's book." She let go of the post. "Thirty people is not a number I am going to lose on a man with a barrow, and I have not said that to be cruel to you, and if I had said it to be kind to me I would not have said it either."

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on the flat with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and the water was nine inches over the highest iron and holding, and thirty people in a yard a hundred and forty foot above the low quarter did not have the number, and he had three days in front of him and four legs and no fifth leg, and he was not going to write one.

> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and the warrant stands, and a fitting is not called anything it is not.** Four bolts made fast, and **the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and forty-seven days, and a yard with a fire in it and nine posts in a line is not a building, and I did not go in under a roof today.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, a hundred and three days from the seventy-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. **Her bell is in her bag and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour today, including me.**

> *The register is thirteen. **I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and nobody of the four was asked and I did not thank the woman who told me thirty.** I was on this road with her forty-four days today and I did not ask her what it was for. She was at the ninth post with her hand on nine foot of oak in a brick socket and I stood nine feet off and said nothing, and nine feet off with nothing said is not a question.

> *Which office is it. **Nine posts, a stick, a rope, a fire, nine hundred small things, and a yard that is nine foot of bank because of a river that is not in anybody's book.**
