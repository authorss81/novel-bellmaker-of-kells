# Chapter 0189 — Arley's Office at the Lock and the Sheet With Four Boxes

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and seventy-fifth day and Cobb Arley was in the lock office with a sheet of paper on the sill of the open window, and Tomas Vale came up the step with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast in the yard where there was about nine feet of gravel between the bench and the wall, and it is a hundred and forty-two days since the frame has been in a building, and a lock office is a building, and he stood in the yard and did not go in for the first hour.

After the first hour he went in, because Arley put his head out of the door and said, "If you are going to stand in that yard all morning you can stand in here and I cannot hear you any better either way."

The office was one room with a stove, a table, a stool, a barrow-load of chain in the corner and a window facing the water, and a lantern on the table that was not lit at that hour. The small bell was on its bracket outside the door and it was not rung.

"That came yesterday," Arley said. "Not the man. A sheet, under the door, and it is a different shape from the one he came with in the spring."

Tomas read it standing up, which was not politeness. It was that he could not sit down in a room where a man had not offered him a seat, and the fifth clause of a receipt in five clauses, all five standing, was four inches long and had something to do with it.

The form had four boxes. Ward. Day. Number. Ground. And under the four, ruled across the page, a fifth space with a line under it and nothing printed over it, and in the margin in a small hand the words for a reason, and a person with a standing to fill in.

"The same form as the spring one," Tomas said.

"The spring one was words. This one is a form." Arley put his thumb on the bottom margin. "I have had it since yesterday. I have not filled it in. I could not fill it in this morning with a pen in my hand and an hour, and here is why, and I have been in this room since the second hour working it out."

"The fifth space wants a person with a standing and there is not one in a lock."

"That is the easy half." Arley took his hand off the page. "The hard half is what it is asking. It wants to know whether the four households up that bank are answered for. Not whether they answer. Whether they are answered for. And there is no box for that and there is no ward for it, because a bank is not a ward and I am not a district and four doors are not a ward either, they are four doors, and if I had a pen and a standing I could not put that in the ground box because the ground box is for a place and a bank is a place, and I could put the four doors in the ground box and I could put a day in the day box, and then there would be a form in this kingdom with four doors on it, and if I did that once it would be in a book somewhere in about nine years and I would be a man who has put four doors into a form."

"So you have not filled it in."

"I have not filled it in and I cannot tell you that I have not filled it in, and that is the part that has been sitting on me since yesterday." He pushed the sheet about an inch towards the middle of the table. "That is what is true. I have not been asked whether I have filled it in, there is nobody to ask me, and there is no form on which anybody asks me, and the form has got a day on it and the day was yesterday. So in a fortnight a man in a coat will come up this step and take it away and say nothing about it, and it will go on a shelf somewhere, and I will not be able to find out what is done with a form that was not filled in, and nobody has ever explained that to me and I do not correspond with anybody."

"Then what is the question that I can answer."

Arley looked at him. "You have been standing in my yard for an hour. You are a maker and you have a paper you cannot read, and you have just told me a form has four boxes and a fifth space, and you read it in about nine seconds standing up, and you read forms. So ask it."

"Is there a form anywhere in this kingdom that says what happens to four households if the man who holds their line stops."

"No."

"You are certain."

"I am certain and it took you nine seconds and it will take me the rest of my life." Arley sat down on the stool. "And here is the thing I have not been able to get round, and you can have it because you cannot do anything with it. I was asked a question. I cannot refuse it. **A refusal is an answer, and an answer is a reason, and I have got no standing to put a reason in a box, and so I cannot refuse it, and a thing you cannot refuse is a thing you are holding.** I have not gone up that bank. I have not told them. I have not said no and I have not said yes and I have not stopped and I have not started, and I am a man who holds a line, and I do not know whether that is because I have chosen to hold it or because I have not gone up four hundred yards, and neither does the man in the coat, and the form is going to get older on my table either way."

Tomas did not have an answer, and he had one thing he could have done with a hundred and forty days, which was to tell this man that he was right. He did not do it, because a true sentence said to a man who is holding something is a thing the man carries and cannot put down, and he had learned that in four counties and a city.

He said instead: "There is a thing about a quarter of a mile down that bank at a cart bridge that I am not going to tell you about."

Arley waited.

"There is a box on the hinge of a gate at the cart bridge," Tomas said. "A man came in the spring with a sheet and put it on and said it was for the gate, and I do not know what it is, and I looked at it yesterday for about a minute and had nothing. I am not going to follow the wire and I am not going to find out who put it there, and I am not going to hand you a fifth thing to carry up a bank, and I am writing down here that I did not tell you and why, so that a stranger in forty years can find the fact and not the man."

"That is the first sensible thing anybody has done on this reach in my time," Arley said. "If you had told me I would have gone and looked and not been able to stop, and then I would have had to decide what to do with it, and I have four doors and a form with a day on it and I have not got room."

Tamsin Rook was outside the door the whole of that, with her hand on the oak jamb and her boots on the step, and the lock was filling and she was not in the room and nobody had said a word to her and nobody was going to. She had been on that step since before Tomas came up the yard, and she had written it down that morning in her own book, in her own hand.

---

He went up the bank.

It is four hundred yards and there is a lip on the inside of the turn about a foot down that he put his heel into twice. At the top there is a hedge and a wall, and along the wall four doors at about ninety yards apart: one with a step worn hollow, one with washing out, one with nothing in front of it, one with a board nailed across the bottom half.

He did not knock at any of them.

He stood about thirty feet off the first one for a while, then walked the whole length and stood about thirty feet off the fourth, and a woman came out of the third with a bucket and looked at him and went in, and that was the whole of what happened in four hundred yards.

A stranger with a barrow standing at a door is a man asking a woman what a bell does, and she would have no copy of the answer, and nobody on this bank holds a copy of anything.

Then he came back down, because the light was going, and about a hundred yards above the lock yard the ground has a ditch along it for the water that comes off the bank, and in the ditch, in the wet, there was a wire. It comes out of the ground about a foot from the ditch's edge and goes along the bottom under about four inches of silt towards the bridge, and it is not a wire anybody has put there for anything to do with the bank, because it is not on the bank and the bank is above it.

Tomas Vale stood in the ditch with the light going and looked at about four feet of it and did not put his hand on it, and did not follow it, and did not find out where it goes under, and then climbed out and made the four bolts fast on the barrow where it was standing in the yard.

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a flat above the lock yard with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and the lock office window was lit and there was a light burning in a building he had been in for two hours, and a form with a day on it was on a sill inside it, and a small bell on a bracket outside it was not rung, and nobody in this kingdom knew what was on a gate four hundred yards down the bank or who had put it there, and he knew that he had just been in the room where the question of four doors was asked and had not answered it, and he was thirty-nine days on this road with her and had not asked her once what the road was for.
