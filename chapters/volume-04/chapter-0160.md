# Chapter 0160 — A Man Who Was Born At The End Of The Road And Has Never Been Asked

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and forty-sixth day and Tomas Vale came out of a lane into a village of about two hundred people, and the barrow was behind him and the four bolts were made fast on a bank at the top of the lane, and he had walked the last mile of nine at walking pace, and he was on the tenth day of it.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and forty-six days, and a lane and a bank and a village street are not buildings and I have not been under a roof in this county except a smithy door and a bench room with a barrow on the landing forty feet away.**

---

The man was mending a gate at the end of the street and he was the fourth person Tomas Vale had spoken to that morning, and Tomas Vale asked him the question the way he asks. His name was Wend Dreeve, and he had been born in the last house on that street, and nobody had ever asked him anything about it.

“**What do they call this road.**”

The man stood up off the gate and looked at him for about four seconds.

“**Nobody has ever asked me that.**”

“**I am asking you now.**”

“**I know you are. That is what I have just said.**”

He put the hammer down.

“**It has got four names and I am not going to choose one for you and you are not going to get one out of me in a day, and if you wanted one I would have to lie to you to give it to you, because all four of them are used and I use three of them myself depending on what I am talking about.**”

“**Say all four.**”

“**The cart road, because a cart from this staging comes up it four days a week and a cart from the inn comes down it four days a week and both of them think of it as theirs.**” He held up a finger. “**The ford road, because that is where it starts if you are walking it from the water and everybody who walks it calls it that for the first two miles and then forgets.**”

“**That is two.**”

“**The long pull, and that is mine, and I have used it since I was in a position to use anything, and I do not know where it came from and I have asked nobody.**” A second finger. “**And the toll road, which nobody uses any more, because the bar was at the fourth mile and it was taken down before I was born, and it is the oldest of the four and it is dead and about half the people in this village will still tell you it is the toll road if you ask them at a door.**”

“**The toll road.**”

“**The toll road.**”

**Four names for nine miles and all four of them correct and not one of them on any sheet, and two of them are the same two words a woman at a pitch at the ford gave me eight days ago, and that is a fact about people carrying words along a road and about a word outliving the thing that made it, and it is two, and I am not going to make a shape out of two names because a name is a word and a word travels on its own.**

**And there are four names for one road and one of them belongs to a man with a bar and a hand about nine feet off my left shoulder, and about four in five of the people I have asked on this road would not know the other three, and that is not a fault in them. It is that a road is not a thing with a name on it. A road is a road because people walk it.**

---

At about the fifth hour he asked the question he had carried in his coat for ten days, and he put his hand flat on the paper through the cloth without taking it out, in the lane, and the man watched him do it and said nothing about it.

“**Is this the road that goes to the coach inn above the ford.**”

“**Aye.**”

“**All the way, with nothing in the middle.**”

“**There is a thing in the middle.**” The man went back to the gate. “**There is a place at about the third mile where the road goes along a wall on your right for a bit. And there is a low place at about the fifth mile that is not a ford and has never been a ford and everybody calls it one. And there is a hedge on the left at the third mile that a man walking is in and out of. None of those is the road stopping. It is the road being a road and having things about it, and if you have come up here to find a piece of the road that is not there then I am very sorry for you and it is not going to be here.**”

He had it in the same four seconds and in the same order as the bench, and he did not improve it, and he wrote it down the same way.

---

At about the sixth hour the man sat down on his own gate and told him two things, and he told them because he was not asked and not because he wanted anything.

“**The district had the road open in the spring and there is a notice on a stake at the top of the bank and it says *wires*, and it has said *wires* since before the water went down, and I have not seen anybody up there.**”

“**You have not read what is on the stake.**”

“**I have not read what is on the stake. It says *wires*. I know that because it is a stake with one word nailed to it and everybody within four miles knows that word, and there is nothing else on it, and nobody has ever asked me to go and look.**”

And then, without any change of voice at all, and without looking up off the gate:

“**My father's line came up through the bottom of a wall in this village. Once. It came up through a stone in the bottom course of the wall of that house — **” he nodded at a house with about nine feet of front and a door that had not been painted — “** and it came up, and it stopped, and my father took the bottom of that wall out with his own hands in a week in a dry summer and he put it back the way it was, and I was in the house, and I was not old enough to be asked anything about it.**”

He picked the hammer up.

“**He never told anybody what he found and he is not here to tell me and I have never told anybody what I found, and I did not find anything, I was a child in a house, and I am not going to now, and you can go up the road and write it down however you like and it will be the second man in fifteen days who has told you about a line in his father's time and I am not going to be the one who says what that is.**”

Tomas Vale stood in the street with his hand flat against a paper in his coat and did not say one word for about four seconds.

Then he said: “**I am not going to ask you what you found.**”

“**No.**”

“**And I am not going to put that next to another one.**”

“**Good. I have been told I am the second one. A man with a barrow said so to me about two years ago and I did not know what he meant then either and I am not asking you what you mean now.**”

**That is the second time in fifteen days and it is the second time I have been handed a man's father by a man who owed me nothing, and it is on the page, and I am not going to put two of them in a row. A man who joins two men talking about their fathers is a man arranging a thing, and arranging is the damage, and I have written that sentence three times in three months and it has been one sentence every time. I have got a rectangle about two hands together in a drawing in my coat, and a notice on a stake that says *wires*, and a man who was in a house, and that is three things in three counties and I am not going to make a shape out of them, and it is the second time this month I have stood in a place and not done the one thing I wanted to do.**

---

At about the seventh hour he worked out, standing in the street with the barrow thirty feet off, the one thing the ten days had produced, and it took about nine minutes and it is the finding of the hundred and forty-sixth.

**A road is a road because people walk it. A schedule is a drawing of a road. And this kingdom has one word for both of them, and has had it for so long that nobody has a second word, and I have been trying for ten days to get the drawing to do the walking's job and it is not a bad instrument and it is not a good one. It is a different instrument and I had it in my coat the whole ten days and I was talking to it about directions.**

**And the whole of what I have got out of ten days and nine miles is this. Four names for one road, and a word on a stake, and a burr under a cut that a tool leaves and not a man, and a store four days' walk west with a counter and a book and a number in it, and two people who said yes to a day, and a man who was born at the end of it and had never been asked what it is called.**

**That is the list. I have had nine counties and a city to make a list with and I have used four of them and one day in ten of the last month and there is a rectangle in a drawing in my coat that I walked over on the first day and it is still exactly where it was and I still cannot read a run and I still cannot say what is in it, and the ground under it is exactly like the ground either side of it, and that has been true for ten days and it will be true tomorrow and nobody is going to restore anything for me and nobody is going to be thanked.**

He put the schedule back in the inside pocket, and the eleven lines on the leaf of his own book are in the other pocket of the same coat, and he did not take either out, and he did not put them next to each other.

---

And then he noticed, at about the seventh hour, standing in the street, that he had been on this road for ten days with a woman nine feet off his left shoulder and had not once asked her what she was walking for, and he noticed it and he did not ask.

**Ten days. I have not asked her one question about what this is for, on any of the ten, and I have asked everybody else on this road about nine times a day, and the reason I have not asked her is not that I do not want to know.**

**The reason is the fourth clause of an arrangement I made out loud in a room I paid for, on the hundred and twentieth, and it is the whole of the reason, and it is four inches long: I will not ask either of them for anything, and if either of them does a thing I will not ask what it was for and I will not write it down and I will not say thank you.**

**And the not-asking today is mine. It is not a thing I did to her and it is not a thing she did and it is not going to be a wound and it is not going to become one, and she has said out loud in her own book that the correct arrangement is that I do not ask and she is right, and I have discovered this morning that I have not asked for nine days without deciding to and I am not going to decide anything about it now either, on a street, in front of a man with a hammer, because a man who fixes a reason on a thing at about the seventh hour is a man building a case and I do not have a case, I have a road and a barrow and a woman nine feet off my left shoulder.**

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step by the water at the end of the nine miles with the barrow on the bank behind him and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and forty-sixth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and forty-sixth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A village of about two hundred, a gate, a hammer, a house with nine feet of front, a lane, a bank, nine miles in ten days, and a step by the water at the end of it.**
>
> *Second column, and the four names. **The cart road, because a cart comes up it four days a week and a cart comes down it four days a week and both of them think of it as theirs. The ford road, because that is where it starts if you are walking it from the water. The long pull, which is his and which he has used since he was in a position to use anything and cannot account for. And the toll road, which nobody uses, because the bar at the fourth mile was taken down before he was born, and about half the village still says toll road at a door.** Four names, nine miles, all four correct, none of them on a sheet, and two of them are the same two words a woman at a pitch gave me at the ford eight days ago. **That is a fact about words travelling along a road and not a shape, and I am not going to make a shape out of two names.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the whole of the ten days. **A road is a road because people walk it. A schedule is a drawing of a road. This kingdom has had one word for both of them for so long that nobody has a second word, and I have had the drawing in my coat for ten days talking to it about directions.** The list is the list and it is a short one: **four names for a road, a word on a stake, a burr under a cut that a tool leaves and not a man, a store four days' walk west where a man will be kind to me and give me a number, two people who said yes to a day, and a man born at the end of it who had never been asked what it is called.** That is what four counties and a city and ten days came to, and I am not going to improve it and I am not going to add to it tonight.
>
> *And the rectangle. **It is in the same place. It was in the same place on the first day when I walked over the top of it and the ground under it was exactly like the ground either side of it, and it is in the same place tonight, and I cannot read a run, and it has not moved and it has not been explained and nobody in this county has offered to explain it and I did not ask.** I am going to write the question down tonight, which I have not done in ten days, and it is two questions and not one: **what is in the rectangle, and is it a thing done to every copy or a thing done to the plate.** I have not got either answer and I am not going to get either answer this month, and the name of the district above that inn is not going in this book, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man with a barrow may put a drawing in front of a stranger and call it evidence.
>
> *And a man and his father, twice in fifteen days, and I am not going to put them together. **His father's line came up through the bottom course of a wall in this village once and stopped, and he took the wall out with his own hands and put it back, and he has never told anybody what he found and he is not going to now, and he said out loud that he knows he is the second and that he is not going to say what that is.** A rectangle in a drawing, and a word on a stake, and a man who was in a house. **Three things in three counties and I am not going to make a shape out of them.** I have a man with a hammer telling me about a wall and a girl on a stone wall counting three hundred and eleven fences and a woman upstairs with nine hundred names in a book, and none of them has asked me for anything, and I am not going to go and ask any of them what it was for.
>
> *And the not-asking, which is mine. **I have walked this road in ten days with a woman nine feet off my left shoulder and I have not once asked her what she is walking for, and I noticed it at about the seventh hour and I did not ask, and it is the fourth clause of an arrangement made out loud in a room I paid for on the hundred and twentieth, and it is four inches long, and it is not a wound and it is not going to become one.** It is not a thing I did to her and it is not a thing she did. I am not going to decide anything about it tonight on a step by the water, because a man who fixes a reason on a thing at the seventh hour is a man building a case, and I do not have a case. I have a road, a barrow, four names, a word, a burr, a store, two yeses and a man with a hammer.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a lane, a bank, a street and a step are not buildings.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, sixty-nine days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to, and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am barred from any public place in that county and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and I am not going to it, and it does not travel, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and forty-six days, including this one, and including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one. Four things came to me in ten days and not one of them is a fourteenth: four names for a road, a word on a stake, a burr under a cut, and a man who was born at the end of it. Two yeses and a man with a hammer are not things that came into my hands and I have not written a list of the nine who said no, and nobody was thanked today, and a man with his head against a wall would have been embarrassed, and a woman asked for a sentence to be entered in a book she does not have a copy of and nobody entered it.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a bench that will not carry a sentence nobody can certify, a lodging book over a landing of about nine hundred names, a hire book nobody has ever been asked to produce, a store with a counter and a number, a notice on a stake that says *wires*, and a drawing with a rectangle in it in the same place that says nothing about a road at all.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A road is a road because people walk it, and a schedule is a drawing of a road, and a man at the end of nine miles has four names for it and has never been asked and nobody in this kingdom has a second word, and the next thing I have to find out is the two questions at the bottom of this page and I am not going to get them this month and I have not thanked anybody in ten days and I am not going to start.**
