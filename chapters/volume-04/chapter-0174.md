# Chapter 0174 — The Landing Gang

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and sixtieth day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on mud at the top of a landing, and below him a hundred feet of plank ran out over black mud to the water, and a barge sat low against it.

The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and twenty-seven days, and mud and plank and water are not buildings.

---

At about the fifth hour the gang worked the barge. Eight men on the planks, a chain through a ring in a post, sacks coming up hand to hand. A woman at a tally board chalked each twentieth sack and wiped her hands on her apron.

Tomas stood clear with his barrow. Tamsin Rook went down to the edge of the plankway on her own and stopped where the mud turned to wood, and put her boots square on the hard edge and her hand on the post at the corner.

She stayed there a long time. The post shivered each time the chain took the load. She kept her palm flat against it and her eyes on the men's feet, reading the work through the wood. The tally woman glanced at her once and went back to her board.

At about the sixth hour the tally woman came up for water and stopped near Tomas.

"You are the barrow," she said.

"I am one of two," Tomas said.

She drank. "Road above is soft. Keep to the stones past the shed."

"How many live here," he said, because the shed behind showed bedding, a fire, pots, children playing with rope ends.

She wiped her mouth. "Gang is eight. With the shed crew, near forty. We load and unload. District pays by the sack landed."

"And when there is no barge."

"Then we mend nets and wait." She picked the pail up. "That is the work."

At about the seventh hour Tamsin came back up the slope and stood about nine feet off his left shoulder, the way she always did, and looked at the water without looking at him. The wind moved her coat. Her hand was still shaped from the post, fingers slightly curled.

Tomas did not ask her anything. He had not asked her what any of this was for on any day since the road began, and he did not ask now. He set his barrow straight and checked the sacking over the carrier instead, giving her the quiet to stand in.

Ned came up with mud to his ankles. "Planks sound," he said. "Posts set true. That landing will hold a winter. Whoever set it knew mud."

Pell counted sacks the way he counted everything. "Eighty landed since noon. That is wages for eight and food for forty. Good rate."

Mab said, "No fences here. Water instead. I am not counting water."

Nobody laughed, but Isa nodded as if that settled something.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on mud above the landing with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and she was against the shed wall with stone under her boots and her hand on the corner post, where the ground changed and she could work out most of a room.

> *The hundred and sixtieth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. Mud, a landing of plank over it, a barge, a shed, a fire, a flat.*
>
> *Second column. Eight on the gang and near forty with the shed crew, living by sacks landed. The tally board chalks each twentieth. The district pays by the sack. Nets mended between barges.*
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. Ruling: forty people by a landing are paid for what crosses the plank and not for sleeping behind it.*
>
> *All five clauses stand. Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and the warrant stands. Four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third. The carrier in the sack, not rung, eighty-three days from the seventy-seventh, and I have not put my hand on it and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour, including me.*
>
> *The register is thirteen. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today.*
>
> *Which office is it. It is a post with a ring in it, a tally board, and a shed with forty behind it.*
