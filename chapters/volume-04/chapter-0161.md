# Chapter 0161 — Two Barrows On The Same Nine Miles And A Figure That Is Not A Rate

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and forty-seventh day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on a flat at the top of a lane, and went on up the road past the yard of the coach inn without going into it, and about a mile above the inn he came round a bend and a man with a barrow was coming down.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and fourteen days, and a flat and a lane and a road and a yard I did not go into are not buildings, and I have not been under a roof in this county except a smithy door and two rooms over a landing, and the barrow went to neither and the barrow is not going to anything today.**

He had come nine miles in ten days and there was no coach road after the inn that anybody had ever drawn for him, and so he had gone on, and going on was the only thing left that did not require anybody's permission.

---

At about the fifth hour a man with a barrow came up the road behind him and then came past him and stopped, and it was Pell Okell, and he had a load of nothing under a rope and he was not going anywhere in particular, or he was going to the inn and would not say so.

“**You said you would say no to me for the rest of the week if I asked you twice.**”

“**I did.**”

“**It is the fifth day.**”

“**It is, and I have not said no and I am not going to, and if you had asked me again I would have said no, and you did not, and I noticed that about nine minutes after I saw the barrow and I want that in whatever you are writing.**” He shifted the barrow. “**I am going up to the inn for a load and I am going up in a day and coming down in a day with it, and I am doing it in one day each way because a load of sacking waits for nobody and I have four days a week at the inn and four here and the four are not mine, they are the inn's.**”

“**Then you are not walking above the inn for me.**”

“**No.**”

“**You are walking above the inn for a load of sacking.**”

“**Nine miles is a day with a barrow and I have known that for about nine years and I did not learn it here, and that is the whole of what I know about this road, and it is yours if you want it.**”

---

At about the sixth hour he asked the question, the way he asks, the same words in the same order.

“**What do they call this road.**”

“**They do not call it anything.**” Pell Okell took the rope off his load and put it back. “**You have been asking that for ten days and you have been getting four answers and you have been writing all four down, and I am going to give you a fifth, and mine is not a name, it is a haul. There is a staging at this end and there is an inn at the top and between them there is nine miles that I go up four days a week and down four days a week, and neither end has ever given that nine miles a name in my hearing, and neither have I.**”

“**Then what do you call it.**”

“**I call it the pull, and so does the man who comes down behind me, and if you asked him he would say the same, and that is the only reason I use it, and it is not a fact about the road.**”

“**A word two men use because they both use it.**”

“**A word two men use because they both use it.**” He picked the barrow up again. “**The long pull on that stretch is a thing about a man with a cart, and you asked a man with a hammer what the road was called on the last day of it, and he gave you four, and he was not wrong, and I am not going to argue with a man about words when the words are the only thing he gets paid in either.**”

**And that is the fifth man in five days to tell me that a word is a word, and I have got five now and four of them are right and none of them is on a sheet, and I am not going to write the fifth one on the same page as the other four, because a man with a barrow of sacking has told me what a word costs him, which is that it is the only currency he has, and I have no right to spend somebody else's word.**

---

At about the seventh hour he worked out, standing in the lane with two barrows in it, the thing the second barrow is worth, and it took him about nine minutes, and it is the finding of the hundred and forty-seventh.

**One barrow is one reason for a man to stop. That is all it has ever been and I have been calling it a road.**

**I came up this road with a barrow and I got about one question a day out of nine miles, and a man with a cart of lime stopped on the first day and a man with a hammer stopped on the ninth and a man with a gate stopped on the tenth, and that is three strangers in ten days, and every one of them stopped because of the barrow and not because of me, and the woman at the pitch stopped because I stopped, and the only real difference between that woman and a man is that a barrow does not say sorry when it stops you.**

**And there are two barrows in this lane now, and I counted it, because Pell Okell told me without being asked. Six strangers in a day instead of one, and about four in ten of what any of them says to me on a day is right, and about two of those six are going to tell me something about this road that is not about a barrow, and that is two true things a day where there was one, and I did not do it and I cannot pay him for it and there is no form on which I could.**

**And the honest arithmetic, which I have now got to correct, because I have been carrying it wrong since the hundred and forty-first. Four days and four miles is a mile a day with a barrow. Nine miles in ten days is a different figure and it is not a mile a day, and I have not got a figure for what any one of the ten days was, because nine of them were not a whole day of walking and I am not going to invent a fraction tonight. Those are two figures and a barrow is the whole of the distance between them, and a road does not have a rate, and a man who divides one figure by the other is counting himself.**

Pell Okell got about forty feet up the lane before he turned round.

“**You have gone quiet.**”

“**I have been counting.**”

“**Count what.**”

“**People.**”

“**That is not a trade.**”

“**No. I have been trying to find one for a hundred and forty-seven days.**”

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a flat above a lane that comes down to the road about two miles above the inn, with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the first entry of the hundred and forty-seventh and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

Then he noticed, standing there, that it was the twentieth day and that he had not asked her what this was for on any of the twenty, and he noticed it and he did not ask, and the reason is four inches long and it has not changed since the hundred and twentieth.

> *The hundred and forty-seventh, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A flat at the top of a lane, the yard of an inn he did not go into, a bend above it, two barrows, a man with a load of nothing, a lane that comes down to the road two miles above the inn, and a flat.**
>
> *Second column, and the man. **Pell Okell said he had noticed I had not asked him twice and he wanted that in whatever I am writing, and he is right and I am putting it in.** He is going up to the inn for a load and down again in a day, and he told me without being asked that nine miles is a day with a barrow and that he has known it for about nine years and did not learn it here. **And he told me that the nine miles between the two ends has never had a name in his hearing, and that what he calls it is a word two men use because they both use it, and that the word is the only currency he has and I have no right to spend it.** Four names in nine days from four people and a fifth today that is not a name, and all five right, and **I have not put his on the same page as the other four and I am not going to.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the whole of the finding. **A barrow is one reason for a man to stop, and I have been writing that down as a road for ten days, and the road was the barrow and the man was the reason and I was the cargo.** One barrow gave me about one question a day. **Two barrows gave me six people in a day, and Pell Okell counted them for me without being asked, and about four in ten of what any of them says is right, and two of those six told me something about this road that was not about a barrow.** And the arithmetic, and I have been carrying it wrong since the hundred and forty-first and only got it straight standing in a lane with a second barrow in it. **What I have got is two figures and I have been treating one of them as a correction of the other, and it is not a correction.** The first is a whole day and it is the rate of a man with nothing on him. The second is ten days, and there are days in it that were not whole, and I have not got a figure for any of those and I am not going to invent a fraction tonight. **The thing sitting between the two figures is a barrow, and there is no figure for a barrow anywhere in this, because a barrow is not a rate and I have been treating it as one since the hundred and forty-first.** Two figures, a wheel between them, and a road with no rate on it at all. Put them one under the other and leave them there.
>
> *And the second man. **I have got a second man and he is unpaid and I did not ask him twice, and the second man is worth about three questions a day, and I cannot pay him for one of them, and there is no form in this kingdom on which I could, and being truthful about it is not a currency and has never been one.** A man with a barrow of nothing in it was worth more to me in one morning than a man at a board was in nine years, and I am not going to say that out loud anywhere but here.
>
> *And the not-asking, which is mine. **Twenty days, and I have not asked her once what this is for, and I noticed it at the seventh hour and I did not ask, and it is the fourth clause of an arrangement made out loud in a room I paid for on the hundred and twentieth and it has not got longer in twenty days and it is not going to get longer this month.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a flat, a lane, a road and a yard I did not go into are not buildings.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, seventy days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to, and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am barred from any public place in that county and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and I am not going to it, and it does not travel, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and forty-seven days, including this one, and including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one. A man with a barrow came up a road because of a load of sacking, a word two men use because they both use it, and six strangers in a day instead of one, and not one of the four is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and I did not ask him twice, and nobody was thanked today, and he did not do it for me and he is going up for a load of sacking.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a haul between two ends that have never given the middle a name, a barrow that is a reason for a man to stop, a yard he walked past, and a man with a cart who has four days a week at an inn and four here and the four are not his.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A road is a road because people walk it and a barrow is a reason for a man to stop, and I have had both in one lane this morning and I could tell you which one has been talking to me for ten days, and it is not the road.**
