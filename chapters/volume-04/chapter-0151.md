# Chapter 0151 — A Road With Nine Miles Of Copper Under It And Nothing To Read

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and thirty-seventh day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on a bank above the water about four miles west of Sennet, where they were made fast at the fourth hour, and put the tarpaulin over the frame, and rolled the sack against the wheel, and then stood on the bank and looked west along nine miles of road and worked out, standing up, the way he works out everything, that he was going to walk them.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and four days since the thirty-third and a barrow is not a building and a bank is not a building and a tarpaulin is not a roof. The second clause says the frame stays in the reinforced padded cradle for the carriage and that is where it is, and the second clause is also the reason I cannot pay for anything with it, and I have not paid for anything with it since the thirty-third.**

**A post I drove into a bank on the hundred and twenty-seventh with an empty bracket on it is a city behind me now, and a mile of flat, and a river. I did not take it out. It is not mine and I left it anyway, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man may ask a district to take a thing out of the ground for him, and nobody is going to be told about it by me.**

He got the schedule out of the coat and unfolded it against the cart board of his own barrow, which is the only flat thing he owns, and it is folded in three and about the size of two hands together and it came out of a bundle undone on a table in a corridor in a city he is not going back to.

There are about nine hundred lines in it. Numbers, wards, days, columns ruled, and a scale in one corner.

**I can see wards and numbers and a scale. I cannot see a run. I have never been able to and there is no trade in it and reading metal is the whole of what I can do.**

---

At about the fifth hour he was two miles along it and the road was doing what a road does, which is being a road because people go along it.

A cart came by with lime in it and the man on it slowed down.

“**You have a barrow.**”

“**I have.**”

“**Where are you taking it.**”

“**West.**”

“**That is nine miles to the crossing and there is nothing at the crossing.**”

“**I know there is nothing at the crossing. I want to see the nine miles.**”

The man looked at the tarpaulin and then at him and did not ask what was under it, and he said a thing that Tomas Vale wrote down that night and did not improve:

“**Nine miles is a long day with that on it. I do four of them and I have a horse.**”

**A man with a barrow stops people. A man on foot does not. I have been on foot for a day and I have been asked one question by a stranger and it was about my own business, and I have had the same nine minutes in a market in a city of nine thousand people and it moved nothing, and one question on a road is worth about the same as one question in a market, which is nothing, and I got a true thing out of it for nothing, and the true thing is about his cart.**

---

At about the sixth hour he stopped, and sat down on the low wall that runs the whole length of this stretch, and took the schedule out again, and tried to do the thing he had been doing for three days with the parcel of a paper in a corridor, which is to make the drawing say where he is standing.

He tried it for about nine minutes.

**And here is what a schedule is. It is a drawing of nine miles. And a road is nine miles. And the road is under my boots, and I have been walking on top of the thing the paper is a picture of, and I have been asking the picture which way to go, and the picture does not know.**

**Not torn. Not cut. The paper is smooth and uncreased in a rectangle about the size of two hands together and the column rules that rule the panels either side stop short and do not cross it, and the ink in the panels either side is a slightly different black, and the numbers either side of it go on without a break, and it is in the same place in the middle of a run.** He put his thumbnail in it. **And the ground under it is exactly like the ground either side of it, and I have now walked over the place where it is not and it is there, and that is the whole of what I have learned about it in four days and it is a thing about paper and there is no form for it.**

**I do not know whether that is a thing done to every copy or a thing done to the plate. I have not found out and I have not written it down and I am not going to join it to anything I have seen in three counties, because three empties seen in three counties is a man arranging a thing and I have had that rule since the ninetieth and I am not going to break it on a low wall with a barrow in the lane.**

---

At about the seventh hour he worked out the difference between the two things in his coat, and it is four inches long, and it is the only thing in a hundred and thirty-seven days he has got that he can put in a sentence.

**A claim in metal cannot be edited. It cannot be added to, it cannot be taken back, it cannot have an hour put on it, and it cannot be shown to anybody on a page. A sheet can be edited, and it can be dated, and it can be produced by a stranger I have never met forty years from now against a thing I said once in a market.**

**That is the whole of the difference between the only instrument I have ever trusted and the thing I am carrying in the inside pocket of this coat, and it is four inches long, and I have known the four inches since I was a boy in a yard and I have had a drawing in my coat for three days and have been treating it like the other one.**

He put the schedule away and got the barrow moving again.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was in a lane off the road with the barrow on the flat ground at the top of it, and the four bolts were where he had made them fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and thirty-seventh and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and thirty-seventh, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **Four miles of road west of a city I am not going back to, a low wall the whole length of it, a lane, and a barrow on flat ground. A man with a cart of lime slowed down and told me his cart does four of the nine miles and I have not asked him one question about anything else.**
>
> *Second column, and the thing I have been carrying. **A schedule is a drawing of a road. I can see wards and numbers and a scale and I cannot see a run and there is no trade in it, and I have been asking a picture which way to go for three days and the picture does not know.** Four miles of the nine are walked and the ground under the place where the rectangle is, is there, and the rectangle is a fact about paper and not about the ground. **I have not written down whether it is a thing done to every copy or a thing done to the plate, and I am not going to join it to a thing I have seen in three counties.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the finding I did not want on the hundred and thirty-seventh and have written down anyway. **I have been treating a drawing as an instrument and it is not one, and a road is a fact about the people who walk it, and a schedule is a drawing of one of those, and I cannot read it and I am not going to be able to.** I have four days of this road in front of me and four of the nine miles behind me and I have learned nothing whatever about the rectangle and that is the honest state of a man with a barrow. I do not know yet what a man who cannot read a thing is supposed to do instead, and I am not going to write down a plan tonight, and a man who writes down a plan on a low wall has usually not got one.
>
> *And the four inches, which is the only new arithmetic in a hundred and thirty-seven days. **A claim in metal cannot be edited and a sheet can be, and the difference between the two instruments I own is four inches long, and the eleven lines on the leaf in my own book are in the same coat as the drawing and I have not taken them out and I am not going to, and one of those two is a copy of my mother out of my own head and the other one is a picture of nine miles, and I cannot tell anybody which is which from the outside and I am the only man in this kingdom who can tell at all.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and no document produced, given or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it has been on a bank and a road and a low wall and in a lane and none of those four is a building.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, sixty days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and she has not taken it out since a market of about nine hundred people on the ninety-second, and nobody has asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and thirty-seven days including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and one true thing a carter said to me for nothing, and not one of them is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing today and I have been given nothing and I have taken nothing off anything.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a post with an empty bracket on it in a bank in a city I have left, a road of nine miles with copper under it, a sheet folded in three with a rectangle out of it in the same place, and a man with a barrow who cannot read either of them and is asking them both for directions.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A road is a road because people walk it and a drawing of a road is a drawing, and I have walked over the top of the one for four days and I am going to go on walking it, and the section I am looking for is not a section of road.**
