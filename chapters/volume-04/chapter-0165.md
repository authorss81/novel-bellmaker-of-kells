# Chapter 0165 — The One Thing Four People Agree About Is A Bridge, And It Is The Only Thing They Can Check Themselves

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and fifty-first day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on a flat above the old road about a quarter of a mile below the bypass, and the bridge was there, and it was three boards nailed across the near side and a hand's width of daylight on the far side of them, and the water under it was about nine inches and moving.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and eighteen days, and a flat, a bridge and three boards are not buildings, and I have not been on that bridge and I am not going to.**

---

At about the fifth hour Pell Okell put his barrow down at the third board and looked at the daylight and then at the water, and told Tomas Vale what a boarded bridge is, which is a thing with a price on it.

“**Two stringers and a deck.**”

“**That is four words.**”

“**That is the whole of it and I will tell you the rest because you are not going to get it anywhere else. The bypass has a return in it, because a bypass is a new road and a new road is a thing a district can put a line against and say *this is where the traffic went*. A bridge is a repair. A repair has no new line. And the timber is somebody's, and the somebody is a man who cut it about nine years ago and put it in a yard and forgot about it, and I have seen the yard.**”

“**So nobody owns the timber.**”

“**Nobody owns the timber and the district will not buy it and will not cut its own, and so it stands.**” He looked at it again. “**And I will tell you the other half, because you will not get that either: a man with a dog has been up this road and told you it is dangerous, and he is right, and it is dangerous, and being right about that does not make it a decision anybody has to make. Four boards and a hand's width, and a man going over it in the wrong light goes in the water.**”

“**Have you crossed it.**”

“**Crossed it a hundred times when it had a deck. I do not cross it now and I have a cart, which is the only reason it is still standing, because a cart on a bridge like that is a thing a man hears about.**”

---

At about the sixth hour a woman came up the old road with a barrow of lime and a yoke and no horse, and she was called Isa Trevail, and she had not asked anybody for anything, and she stopped about nine feet short of the boards because she had seen them and worked out what they were.

“**That is the fourth mile.**”

“**It is.**”

“**Then I have got to go round and I have got a barrow of lime and no horse and about a mile round, and that is the last thing I needed today, and I am saying it out loud so that nobody can tell me afterwards that I did not say it.**” She set the yoke down. “**You are three people and a barrow.**”

“**Four and a barrow, and the girl is not carrying anything and will not let me.**”

“**Then I will walk round with you, and I will say now, in front of you all, that I am walking round because there is a mile of road and I have a barrow on it, and not because anybody asked me, and I would be walking round if there were four of you or forty.**”

“**You are.**”

“**Good.**”

Then nobody said anything for about four minutes, and the four of them stood on the old road about nine feet apart, and each of them, separately and without being asked and without looking at anybody else, said a thing about the bridge.

Pell Okell: “**That is a repair and a repair is a thing a district does when it has a new road to point at. It has got the new road. Put the timber in and the bridge is finished and the new road has an old thing at the bottom of it that looks like it was planned.**”

Mab Sarter, without stopping counting: “**I have counted the fences on the top of that bank and there are more of them on this side than on the other, and somebody has to come to this side to mend a fence, and nobody is going to come to this side, because the road they are paid for is on the top.**”

Isa Trevail: “**I have come up this road with a barrow eleven times and I have had to go round all eleven, and I never once said anything about it to anybody because there was nobody to say it to, and I am not a person who complains about a bridge.**” She picked the yoke up. “**And I would like it open. That is all. I would like it open and I have said why.**”

And Tomas Vale did not count them, did not improve them, did not ask a fourth person, and did not write any of the three sentences down in the same book as the other three, and he said so out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, and none of them agreed with him about that either.

**A bridge is the only thing on this stretch of road a man can check himself, and that is the whole reason the four of them said the same shape of thing in about four minutes without anybody asking, and it is also the reason it is the least useful thing the four of them could have agreed about. I could not get one of these three people to say the same sentence twice about a road, about a line, about a stake, about a bypass, or about a drawing in my coat, and I have been at this for ten days and I know exactly why: because none of those can be checked by the man who is told about them, and this one can, and a person will agree with a plank about as far as a plank.**

**And a bridge is not a route. A route is a person on foot and it costs a day, and it cannot be edited, and I have four people and they agree about a bridge, and I did not arrange that and I am not going to claim it and I am not going to put it in a book as though I had.**

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a flat by the third board with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and fifty-first and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was, and it is the shortest one he has written in four days and he is not going to make it longer.

> *The hundred and fifty-first, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A flat above the old road, a bridge, three boards, a hand's width of daylight, nine inches of moving water, four people, and a flat.**
>
> *Second column, and the bridge. **Two stringers and a deck. The bypass has a return in it because a new road is a thing a district can put a line against and say where the traffic went. A bridge is a repair, and a repair has no new line, and the timber belongs to a man who cut it about nine years ago and put it in a yard and forgot about it.** The district will not buy it and will not cut its own, and so it stands. **And a man with a dog told me on the road that a man going over it in the wrong light goes in the water, and he is right, and being right about that is not a decision anybody has to make.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the only thing. **Three people, unasked, in about four minutes, in three different vocabularies, said that the bridge should be open, and nobody looked at anybody else while they said it.** A repair is a thing a district does when it has a new road to point at. A man with a barrow of lime has gone round it eleven times and never said anything because there was nobody to say it to. There are more fences on the top of that bank and nobody is coming to this side. **And I did not ask a fourth person and I did not improve one of them and I did not put the three in the same book as each other, and I said so out loud once and none of the three agreed with me about that.** A bridge is the only thing on this stretch a man can check himself and that is the whole of why it is the only thing they agree about, **and it is also the whole of why it is the least useful thing the four of us could have agreed about. I cannot get one of them to say the same sentence twice about a road, or a line, or a stake, or a bypass, or a drawing in my coat, and I know why: a person will agree with a plank about as far as a plank, and a road has no plank in it.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a flat, a bridge and three boards are not buildings, and I have not been on the bridge.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, seventy-four days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to, and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am barred from any public place in that county and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and I am not going to it, and it does not travel, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and fifty-one days, including this one, and including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about them except the oldest one, and three sentences about a bridge said out loud in a lane by three people who were not asked and did not know they were agreeing, and not one of them is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and the woman with the barrow of lime said out loud in front of all of us that she was walking round because there was a mile of road and not because anybody asked her, and she was right to say it and I am not going to be the man who improves it.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a return with a new road in it, a yard with nine-year-old timber in it, four boxes and no line for a repair, and a bridge with three boards on it.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A bridge is the only thing on this road a man can check himself, and that is why four people say the same thing about it, and it is the least useful thing four people have ever said the same thing about, and I have not got anybody to say the same thing with me about anything else and I did not arrange this and I am not going to.**
