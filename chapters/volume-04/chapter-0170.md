# Chapter 0170 — Four People, One Bridge, Twenty Days Of Not Asking, And A Road That Goes On Whether He Can Read It Or Not

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and fifty-sixth day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on a flat at the top of a gate lane above the street, and the four of them were on the road above the flat, and the road went on up past a turning and there was nothing at the top of it that he knew of.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and twenty-three days, and a flat, a gate lane and a road are not buildings, and I have not been under a roof in this county since a smithy door and two rooms over a landing, and the barrow went to neither and it is not going to anything today.**

---

At about the fifth hour he said the thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, to the four of them, and he had worked it out on the flat and he did not improve it.

“**I am going up this road. I have a barrow I cannot pay anybody out of and a paper I cannot put in front of anybody and no standing in this district or in the last three, and I am going up it on my own account and I am not going to ask any of you for anything, and if you walk it, that is yours and I will not say thank you for it, and if you go home, that is yours and I will not ask you why.**”

Nobody made a sentence of it.

Pell Okell: “**I have to fetch a load and go down in a day, and I have been four days up and I have not fetched it, and I am going to be four days late, and that is my business.**”

Mab Sarter: “**I have two hundred and six and a bit and I have not finished, and I am not going to go home and finish a number I have not got to the end of.**”

Isa Trevail: “**There is a mile of road with a barrow on it before the bridge and I would like that mended and I would like to be somewhere it is not.**”

And Ned Adley said nothing at all, and picked the shovel up, and went up the road, and Tomas Vale did not ask him why and did not thank him and did not write down anything about it.

---

At about the sixth hour one of them asked him a question that he could answer, and it was the only question anybody has asked him in ten days that he was able to answer inside a minute, and it came from the man with the shovel, from about nine feet behind, in the ordinary voice of a man who is walking and thinking.

“**What happens if the timber is never bought.**”

“**It stands.**”

“**And if nobody puts a return against it.**”

“**It stands, and it will be three boards in another nine years, and there will be a hand's width of daylight, and about nine people a year will be right about it and about nine people a year will go round it, and neither of those two nines will ever be written down next to each other, and nobody in this kingdom will be able to say afterwards whether the bridge was shut or abandoned, because those are the same word for a bridge.**”

“**That is a bad answer.**”

“**It is the true one and it took me about nine minutes and a half of a day to get to it and I have not improved it.**” The shovel went over a stone. “**You wanted me to say somebody ought to do something about it.**”

“**I wanted to know what happens.**”

“**What happens is that a district has a form with four boxes in it and a bridge is not one of the four, and that is not anybody's villainy, and I have now asked four days' walk of people in this county about four different things and every single one of them has told me the same sentence in a different accent: the form takes a thing they can put a line against.**”

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, and then a girl with a basket said: “**Then the bridge is the only thing on this road you cannot get into a column, and that is why it is the only thing we all said the same thing about.**”

And that was true, and he wrote it down, and he did not put it next to anything.

---

At about the seventh hour he stood on the road with the barrow on the flat behind him and worked out the whole of ten days and it took about nine minutes, and it is the finding of the hundred and fifty-sixth.

**Ten days. Four of nine miles behind me, then two miles past the inn, then five more, and a place of about forty houses with a fire going in it, and four people on a road who agree about a bridge. And what I have actually got is: a barrow is a reason for a man to stop, and two barrows stop three times as many; a bypass is a road and a road does not know who is standing on it, and the whole of what it takes off a place is the man who used to come past; a form with four boxes and a fifth for a reason that a man with a barrow cannot reach; a man who does the worse work twice so that a stranger can point at the worse one; and three words for a place, none of which I can match to a sheet I cannot read.**

**And the instruments, and none of them is mine. A thing said at ten feet in the open moves about four in ten and dies with the people who heard it over about nine years. A thing written down is read by every clerk who comes after for forty years and can be produced against you by a stranger you have never met. A route is a person on foot, it cannot be edited, and it costs a day, and the day belongs to whoever gives it and not to me. Those three are the whole of what I own and I have had all three for a hundred and fifty-six days and the first two have never once gone anywhere.**

**And a true sentence is the smallest thing anybody in this kingdom can carry, and I have four people, and I could not get one sentence out of any of them without putting something in it first, and the one man who would have carried one wanted a form, and I have no form, and he is right and I am not able, and those are the same fact and it is the whole of the hundred and fifty-sixth.**

And then he noticed it, standing on the road, and it is the last thing on the page and it is the only thing he is not going to write a rule about.

**Twenty days. I have not asked her what this is for on any of the twenty, and I have asked everybody else on this road about nine times a day, and I noticed it on the first of these ten days and I wrote it down and I have not asked on any of the nine since, and the reason has not changed and has not got longer and is four inches long, and I am not going to ask her today on a road with four other people in it, and I am not going to decide anything about it at about the seventh hour on a bank, because a man who fixes a reason on a thing at the seventh hour is a man building a case, and I do not have a case, I have a road, a barrow, four people and a bridge.**

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a bank above the road with the barrow on the flat below and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and fifty-sixth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and fifty-sixth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A flat at a gate lane, a road that goes on past a turning, four people, a shovel, a bridge three miles behind, and a bank.**
>
> *Second column, and what I said out loud on a flat at the top of a gate lane, because I said it and four people heard it, and I am setting down what a man said out loud on a flat rather than what he said to four people. **I am going up this road, and the barrow is not money, and the paper is not authority, and I have neither standing here nor in the last three districts, and I am not going to ask any of the four of you for anything at all.** And then I gave them the two halves one under the other and not joined, and I have written them so, because they are not one thing and I will not have them read as one thing: **if a person comes there is nothing at the end of it to be thanked for, and if a person does not come there is nothing at the end of it to be asked.** Nobody made a sentence out of it. **One of them is going to be four days late fetching a load. One has not finished a number. One has a barrow and a mile of road she would like not to be on. The fourth said nothing at all and picked the shovel up and came, and I did not ask him why and I did not thank him and I have not written down one word about it.** That is the whole of it and it took about nine minutes to say and about a second to be said back.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the ten days. **Four of the nine miles I came up are behind me and about seven are in front, and somewhere in the seven there is a street of forty houses with a fire in it, and on the road above it there are four people who have all said the same shape of thing about a bridge without being asked.** And what I have actually got, in the order I got it: **a wheel is a reason for a stranger to stop, and two of them stop about three times as many.** A new road on the top of a bank, and the man who used to come past underneath it, and what is missing where he was. **Four boxes and one more that a man with a barrow cannot get a hand into.** A man with a shovel doing the worse of two jobs so that the better one can never be pointed at. **And three words for one place and not one of them matched to a sheet I cannot read.**
>
> *And the three ways there are of moving a thing, which I have had for a hundred and fifty-six days and none of which is mine. **Out loud, at about ten feet, and it holds about four times in ten and it goes with the last ear that heard it, somewhere around nine years. On paper, and it is in a drawer, and a stranger can have it out of that drawer in forty years whether I am alive or not. On a person's feet, and it cannot be touched once it has left them, and it costs a whole day of somebody's life, and the day is theirs and not mine.** The first has never once gone anywhere. The second has never once gone anywhere. I have four of the third and not one sentence for them to carry, **and that is the arithmetic of a hundred and fifty-six days and I am not going to improve any of the three of them tonight.**
>
> *And the sentence, which is the smallest thing anybody in this kingdom can carry. **Four people, and not one of them will say a thing to me without something put in first, and there is a man on that road who would have carried a true one out of here for nothing and who asked for a form as his price, and there is no form in this kingdom that I could put in his hand, and he asked for it in advance and I told him I could not and he said yes after he knew.** He is right about the form and I am not able to give it and those are two ends of one fact and I am not going to stand here and improve it. I did not ask anybody to agree to anything. I have not taken the same words to two people and come back with two the same. **I am not the clerk of four people on a road and there is no paper in it and there is not going to be.**
>
> *And the not-asking, which is mine and has been mine since the hundred and twentieth. **Twenty days, no question, and the count is the only new thing in it: I noticed on the first of these ten and I wrote it in, and I have not gone near it on any of the other nine, and it has not got longer and it is not going to.** Not today, and not on a road with four other people standing on it, and not at the seventh hour on a bank, **because a man who settles a thing at the seventh hour is building a case, and I have not got a case, and what I have got is a road, a barrow, four people and a bridge.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a flat, a gate lane, a road and a bank are not buildings.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, seventy-nine days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to, and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am barred from any public place in that county and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and I am not going to it, and it does not travel, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and fifty-six days, including this one, and including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and four people and a bypass and a bridge and a man with a shovel and three words for a place of about forty houses, and not one of the six is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked in ten days, and not one person in this county was thanked by me, and one of them told me to go up my lane and I went.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a form with four boxes and a fifth that a man with a barrow cannot reach, a heading with a number on it, a heap of bricks about a hundred yards up a lane, a wood four miles over with no house in it, a return with a new road in it and no line for a repair, a yard with nine-year-old timber in it, a man at a fire, and a bridge with three boards on it that nobody is going to be told about in a column.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A true sentence is the smallest thing anybody in this kingdom can carry, and I have four people and not one sentence, and a route is a person on foot and it costs a day and the day is not mine, and the road goes on whether I can read the drawing of it or not, and it always has, and I have a hundred and fifty-six days of that and I am not going to spend the next ten pretending to have found something.**
