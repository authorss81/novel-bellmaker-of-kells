# Chapter 0155 — The Thing He Is Carrying Is A Picture And He Cannot Read It

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and forty-first day and Tomas Vale left the barrow on the flat above the river with the four bolts made fast and went up a bank with the schedule in his coat, and then went back down for his own book, and then went up again, because he had four hours of it and he is a man who works out things with his hands and there was nothing else to do with his hands.

The wall at the top of the bank was about nine feet long and dry on the south side and it was the only flat thing for a mile.

**The schedule came out of the coat and went flat on the wall, and the wall is stone and the paper is smooth in a rectangle about the size of two hands together, and I put my thumb in that rectangle again because I have been putting my thumb in it for four days and it is smooth and it is not torn and it is not cut and there is nothing under my thumb but stone.**

He stood back about four feet and looked at it.

About nine hundred lines. Numbers, wards, days, columns ruled and a scale in one corner.

**I can see wards and numbers and a scale. I cannot see a run. I have never been able to and there is no trade in it and there is no trade in it anywhere in this kingdom that I have heard of, and the trade I do have is reading metal, and a sheet is not metal, and I have had that in my coat for four days and asked it about a mile four times a day.**

---

He did the scale first, because the scale is the one thing on the paper he can use.

The corner said that a thing on the paper was so long, and he put his own thumb on it, and then he put his own foot beside it, and he worked out in about nine minutes that the whole of the nine miles of drawing on that sheet would be about so many inches of wall if it were all laid out flat, and that it was laid out in panels, and that the panels were about so big, and that there was a run of them going west.

Then he paced the wall, because a mile is a thing he has paced before and knows.

**A mile of good road is about nine hundred yards and I have paced enough of them to be wrong by about forty at worst, and I can be inside a hundred yards of a place in a day if I have walked it before, and I have not walked this one before, and it is nine miles long and I have been on four of them in four days.**

He took out his own book and wrote the numbers down on a page.

**Nine inches of drawing is a mile, and the mile I am standing in is somewhere on it, and there are about nine panels of it from this end to that end, and every one of the panels has a name at the top of it in a small hand and I cannot tell a ward from a number from a name, and the panels do not have names on the ground.**

**I have been carrying a drawing of nine miles in my coat for four days and asking it where to put my feet. And the ground is nine miles and the feet are on the ground, and those are two objects, and a maker's whole life is work where a thing is. A schedule is a picture of a road. I have been carrying a picture.**

---

At about the fifth hour he did the honest arithmetic, standing at the wall, and it took about four minutes, and it is the reason the hundred and forty-first is worth a page.

**Four days. Four miles. A mile a day with a barrow.**

**And a man with a barrow stops people, and a man on foot does not. I worked that out on the flat above the water in a city of about nine thousand people on the hundred and thirty-first day with a pair of hands on a frame and a woman on a sill, and I have been walking on foot for four days with a barrow behind me, and it is the barrow that stops them, and the reason I have had one question asked of me by a stranger in four days, and a carter and a woman at a pitch and a man at a board and a smith, is that a carter slows down for a barrow.**

**And I cannot leave it. Not in a lane and not in a yard and not under a roof. There is a receipt in five clauses and the second one says what, and the second one does not care why I want to set it down, and it has been right every morning of a hundred and forty-one days including this one, and it is the reason I cannot pay for anything as well as the reason I cannot put it down.**

**So: I am on foot and I am not a man on foot, and the difference is a frame in a cradle on a wheel, and I have learned nothing whatever about the rectangle in four days, and I am not going to learn anything about it at this rate, and the man who built the road did not build it for me.**

---

At about the sixth hour a man came up the bank with two sheep and stopped, because a barrow is a barrow, and Tomas Vale asked him one question and got a true answer and did not argue with it and did not thank him, and the answer was that there was a place about two miles west where the road used to go and did not go now, and that the road that goes now is longer, and that the road that used to go is not a road, it is a bridle way, and that his father used it.

Tomas Vale did not write it down in the same book as the wall.

**Two miles west. A thing said once, at a gate, by a man with two sheep, and it is the fifth thing I have been told in five days about the same nine miles, and I have got five of them in five places and about four in ten of any one of us is right, and I have not got a rule that lets me hold them together. The rule I have got is the one I have had since the ninetieth: two empties in one day in one building may be joined and three empties in three counties may not.**

**A rectangle about two hands together in a sheet, and an empty fourth box on a form in a printer's room two streets off in a city I am not going back to, and a keeper's field ruled past the end of a line on a water-bell return in a county I have left. Three. And I am not going to join them, and I wrote that down on the hundred and thirty-fourth on a stair landing, and I am writing it again on a wall on the hundred and forty-first because I am the kind of man who writes it again, and this time it is on a wall outdoors and it is still three and not a shape.**

**I still do not know whether that rectangle is a thing done to every copy or a thing done to the plate. I have not found out. I am not going to find out in a week with a barrow and no standing, and I have not written the question down.**

---

At about the seventh hour he sat down on the step at the top of the bank with the sheet in his hands and did the thing he came up the bank to do, and it took him about nine minutes, and it is the finding of the hundred and forty-first day, and he wrote it in his own hand and he is not going to improve it.

**A schedule is a drawing of a road and a road is a fact about the people who walk it and those are not the same kind of thing, and this kingdom has no word for the difference and neither do I.**

**I have been carrying a picture and asking it for directions. There is nothing in the picture. A rectangle about two hands together is not a gap in a road, because I have walked over the top of it, and the ground under it is exactly like the ground either side of it, and the drawing has nothing there and the ground has everything there, and both of those are true, and I have been standing in the middle of it for four days asking a piece of paper to tell me about a piece of ground.**

**And the way forward is not better at it. There is no better at it. Four counties and a city and a capital with a printer's room in it two streets off and about nine hundred lines in a bundle, and not one person in any of them can read one, and the reason is not that they are stupid, and I have asked three of them, and the reason is that there is no form in this kingdom on which a man may be taught to read a map, and a thing with no form on it is a thing nobody is paid to be right about, and the man at the board under a roof has wanted to rule a column for himself for nine years and has not, and he is the most capable man I have met in four days.**

**So it is four other people. It is not a lesson and it is not a form and it is not going to cost me nothing. It is four people putting their eyes on a drawing in my coat, one at a time, and each of them telling me what they can see, and every one of them telling me something I cannot see, and none of them being able to tell me where I am standing either, and the whole of it being worth exactly what four strangers think is worth looking.**

**And I have no standing to ask. I have no standing to ask any of them for anything. I have a barrow I cannot pay for, and a piece of paper I cannot give back, and a warrant behind me that nobody in this county has heard of and that is standing up in a room off a lane in a city four days' road east.**

He looked at the sheet on the wall for a while longer, and then he folded it in three, which is the way it is folded, and put it back in the inside pocket of the coat.

**The thing I am best at is knowing what a thing is made of. I have learned that faster than anybody I have met since I was nine and I have got four counties and a city out of it. And there is no version of that which reads a drawing, and I have never once been able to, and I have just written it down in my own hand on a wall in the sun, and it is not a defeat, and it is a shape, and the shape is that a man with one instrument walks a road until somebody else shows him what his own thing looks like from the outside.**

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on the step at the top of the bank with the barrow on the flat below and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and forty-first and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and forty-first, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A bank above a river, a wall about nine feet long, a sheet of paper flat on the stone with a thumb in a smooth rectangle, four days of road, four miles, and a step.**
>
> *Second column, and the scale. **Nine inches of drawing is a mile. I paced the wall and the arithmetic came out at about so many inches for the whole of the nine miles, in panels, each with a name at the top of it in a small hand, and the panels do not have names on the ground.** The drawing is about nine panels long. **I have been on four miles of it in four days and I cannot find which panel I am standing on, and there is nothing on the paper that says.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the whole of the hundred and forty-first. **Nine inches of drawing is a mile, and the mile I am in is on it somewhere, and there are about nine panels of it from this end to that end, and I cannot tell which one I am standing on and neither can the paper.** I walked over the rectangle on the first day and I have been over that ground four times since and there is nothing there and everything there and the two of those are not in conflict. And the honest arithmetic, which I have not written down before: **four days and four miles is a mile a day with a barrow. A man with a barrow stops people and a man on foot does not, and I worked that out on the hundred and thirty-first, and I have been on foot for four days with a barrow behind me, and it is the barrow that stops them, and the second clause of a receipt in five, which has been right every morning of a hundred and forty-one days, is the reason I cannot set it down.** I am on foot and I am not a man on foot. **The man who built the road did not build it for me.**
>
> *And the way forward, which is not a skill. **There is no form in this kingdom on which a man is taught to read a map, so there is no version of being better at it, and I have asked three people in four days, and the reason is not that they are stupid, it is that nobody is paid to be right about a thing with no form on it.** A man under a roof with a book has wanted to rule a column for himself for nine years and has not, and he is the most capable person I have met since I came up out of a city. **So it is four other people's eyes, one at a time, on a sheet in my coat, and not one of them will be able to tell me where I am standing either, and I have no standing to ask any of them for anything.** The whole of it is worth what four strangers think is worth looking, and I have got nothing to pay them with except being truthful, which is not a currency in this kingdom and has never once been one.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a bank, a wall, a step and a flat are not buildings, and I sat on a step for about nine minutes with a sheet on my knees and the frame was thirty feet down the bank the whole time.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, sixty-four days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to, and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag, and she was on the flat about nine feet off my left shoulder all day, and I have not asked her what this road is for on any of the four days, and I am not going to today, and nobody has asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and forty-one days and I have not asked her for one either, and that is four inches long and I am not going to describe how hard it was.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and one true thing from a man with two sheep about a bridle way two miles west that I have not written down on the same page as the wall.** Not one of them is a fourteenth. **I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and I have not joined the rectangle to the empty fourth box on a form or to the keeper's field ruled past the end of a line, and that is the second time I have written that down and it is still three and not a shape.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a form in a printer's room, a column under a roof, a bundle of about nine hundred lines, a plate, and a drawing in my coat with a rectangle in it in the same place, and the whole of the difference between the office and this wall is that the office has four boxes and a reason and a clerk who can be asked a question, and this wall has a smooth rectangle in it and a man who cannot read it.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A schedule is a drawing of a road. A road is a fact about the people who walk it. I have been carrying a picture and asking it for directions, and it is a hundred and forty-one days and I have had one instrument the whole of them, and it is a road, and I could not see that until I stood on a wall and gave up for nine minutes.**
