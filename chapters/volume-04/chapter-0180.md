# Chapter 0180 — The Bank Above The Ninth Mile

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and sixty-sixth day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on a bank above the ferry road, and below them the river bent and the nine places lay behind in smoke and work, and ahead the road went on.

The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and thirty-three days, and a bank and a ferry road are not buildings.

---

At about the fifth hour Corrie Vane left them at the ferry turning, hefting her spade, and went down to the next cut without looking back, and there were four again.

Pell counted the road ahead the way he counted loads. "Two days to the next store. After that, ground I do not know."

Isa settled the yoke. "Lime holds. I hold. That is the whole of it."

Mab said, "Two hundred and forty-four. Ditch and fence together now. I have not finished."

Ned looked at the soft edge of the ferry road. "Keep left at the culvert," he said. "Lip there. I would mend it but the return wants feet and not edges, and I have mended my own once and will not do it again."

At about the sixth hour Tomas stood with his barrow and looked back at the smoke. Nine places, each with people, each with a market or a ford or a landing, each in the district's fair hand as work and mouths fed. Paid coin and meal. Counted in a return about a thing in a ward. No box for a household anywhere.

He had not entered a name. He had read a document the district made and left it where it was. A true sentence had died in four mouths in a day. A document would live forty years.

At about the seventh hour he wrote nothing down but the day, because the finding was too big for a column and the third column would have to hold it as a ruling and not as a sentence. The bridge stood three boards and nine years of timber behind them, unbuyable, unmended, agreed about and useless. The road went on whether he could read the drawing of it or not.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on the bank with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and she was nine feet off with stone under her boots.

> *The hundred and sixty-sixth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. A bank, a ferry road bending, smoke behind, a road ahead, a flat.*
>
> *Second column. A fifth walker gone to her next cut at the ferry. Four again, two days to the next store, ground unknown after. Nine places behind as work and mouths fed, no household box.*
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. Ruling: a road walked ten days is still not a paper, and a paper read ten days is still the district's.*
>
> *All five clauses stand. Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and the warrant stands. Four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third. The carrier in the sack, not rung, eighty-nine days from the seventy-seventh, and I have not put my hand on it and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour, including me.*
>
> *The register is thirteen. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today.*
>
> *Which office is it. It is smoke, work, mouths fed, and a bank above them.*
