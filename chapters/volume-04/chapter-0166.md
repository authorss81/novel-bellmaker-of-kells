# Chapter 0166 — A Man Who Has Mended His Own Work Once And Will Not Do It Again, And Nobody Is Going To Thank Him For It

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and fifty-second day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on a flat by a heap of stone, and a man was filling a soft place in the old road with a barrow-less hod and a shovel, alone, at about the pace of a man who is not in a hurry because he is the one who will have to come back.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and nineteen days, and a flat, a heap of stone, a shovel and a soft place in a road are not buildings, and he did not look at the barrow when the barrow came up and I did not take the bolt off it for him because he did not ask me to.**

---

At about the fifth hour Tomas Vale asked him what he was doing, and got an answer about a trade, and did not get the second half of it for about nine minutes. The man was called Ned Adley and he had a shovel and a hod and no barrow, and Tomas Vale learned nothing else about him that he used.

“**What is that.**”

“**A place where the ground has gone.**” The shovel went in and came out. “**You put stone in the bottom and you pack it and you put more stone on the top of it and you leave it about a foot proud so the water runs off, and then you come back in about a fortnight and do it again lower, and then you come back in a year and do it again, and that is the whole of the trade and there is nothing in it that is clever.**”

“**Who is it for.**”

“**The people who go over it.**”

“**That is not a person who pays you.**”

“**No.**” He drove the shovel in again. “**I am paid for the district's new road, once a year, on a day that is not mine, and I am paid for it by somebody I have met twice and neither time in this county. Everything else I do, I do because it is soft.**”

---

At about the sixth hour he asked the question he had come with, which was about the man in a forge four days' walk back, and the man with the shovel answered it in about four seconds and in a different shape.

“**I have heard of a smith who mended his own.**”

“**I have not.**”

“**He said he will not do it again and that he called it the best work he has ever done.**”

“**Then he is right and I hope he keeps it.**” The shovel went in. “**And I have done mine once and I am not doing it again and I do not think of it as the best work I have ever done, and I would like to explain the difference, because I think a man who has not done it thinks it is pride and it is not pride at all, it is a column.**”

“**Go on.**”

“**I filled a hole in the wall of my own yard, in the street I live in, with stone I bought myself, in about a day and a half, and it is the only work I have ever done that I could not point at a week later, and I could not do it again, because a fault nobody can see is a fault somebody turns up one morning with a paper in their hand. And there is nowhere in this kingdom I can stand at a counter and say *I have mended my own and here it is*. So if I do it, the next man along who finds my handiwork and does not like it has nothing to complain of, and if I say it was mine then I am a man in a column, and a thing in a column is not a thing anybody owns after that.**”

“**So you will do it worse.**”

“**So I will do it twice.**” He drove the shovel in and packed it with the back of it. “**Once properly, which nobody will ever see, and once badly, with the stone a foot proud and a lip you could catch a heel on, which anybody can point at from the road. And the second one is what I will be remembered for and it is the one I will get told about, and the first one is the one that will still be holding up a wall in ten years. And nobody is ever going to ask me about the first one, and I have got about as much work left as I have had for the whole of my life, and I am not going to spend it being a man who is praised for the worse thing.**”

**A mend is the only work in this kingdom that cannot be edited and cannot be produced against you, and that is the whole of why it is done twice, and I have been carrying the other half of that sentence in my coat since the thirty-first and I did not have these two halves next to each other until this morning. A claim in metal cannot be edited and cannot be shown to anybody on a page. A mend cannot be edited and cannot be put on a page at all. They are the same instrument pointed at two different trades and one of them is a bell and one of them is a man with a shovel, and neither of them is ever going to be worth anything to anybody who is not standing in front of it.**

---

At about the seventh hour he said the thing nobody asked him for, and asked for it to be left out, and Tomas Vale wrote it down.

“**I have not been paid for this road and since the ford I have been the only man who has put stone in a road he is not paid for, and I am doing it because it is soft, and I would like it out of whatever it is you are keeping.**”

“**Why would you like it out.**”

“**Because a man who is paid for one road and does another one for nothing is a man somebody will want to pay for both of them in a year, and then he is on the district's list, and then he is on a list, and then there is a day somebody comes with a sheet and my name is on it for a thing I did for nothing on my own account.**” He straightened up. “**And that is the whole of it and it is not honour and it is not humility. It is that a thing nobody can produce is the only thing I have ever had that is mine, and I am not going to hand it to a book.**”

“**I am going to write it down.**”

“**I know you are.**”

“**You know why.**”

“**Because you are the only one who has it and there is nobody else to give it to, and because in about forty years a stranger will take your book out of a drawer and read it out in a room and I will be a name in it that a man put in for me.**” He picked the shovel up. “**I am not asking you not to. I am telling you what it is, and you can do what you want with it, and if you come back in a year and ask me whether it was worth it I will tell you it was not and I will not be sorry.**”

**And I wrote it down, and he knew I would before he said it, and he said it anyway, and he said it for himself and not for me. And nobody thanked him, and I am going to set this down plainly because nobody in this kingdom has ever had it set down plainly: a man told me the truth about a piece of work he had done for nothing and asked me to keep it out of a book, and I kept it in, and the reason I kept it is that there is no form in this kingdom on which a man with a barrow may be asked to leave a thing out, and that absence is the whole of the trade I used to be in, and I have spent a hundred and fifty-two days learning it and I am not going to pretend today that learning it makes me a person who would leave it out.**

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on the flat by the heap of stone with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and fifty-second and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and fifty-second, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A flat, a heap of stone, a shovel, a hod, a soft place in an old road, a foot of stone proud of the ground, and a flat.**
>
> *Second column, and the trade. **You put stone in the bottom and pack it and put more on the top and leave it a foot proud so the water runs off, and you come back in a fortnight and do it again lower, and you come back in a year and do it again, and there is nothing in it that is clever.** He is paid for a district's new road once a year on a day that is not his, by somebody he has met twice and never in this county. **Everything else he does, he does because it is soft.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the two halves. **He has done his own mend once, in the wall of his own yard, in the street he lives in, with stone he bought himself, in about a day and a half, and he is not doing it again, and he does not think of it as the best work he has ever done.** The reason was not pride and he said so before I could get near it. **A mend you cannot see is a mend that comes back at you one morning with somebody else's paper in their hand, and there is no counter in this kingdom where a man may put his own work down and say *I have mended my own and here it is*, and the moment he says it he is a line in somebody's return and it stops being his.** So he does it twice, and the shape of the two is the whole of his argument and I am not going to describe it again here because I have described it once today. **The one nobody will ever see is the one that is holding the wall up in ten years.**
>
> *And the two halves, which I have been carrying in two different pockets since the thirty-first and have not had side by side until this morning. **One of them is the instrument I trust: a claim in metal cannot be edited, cannot be added to, and cannot be put in front of anybody on a page.** The other is a man with a shovel doing the same work in stone, and it has the same two properties and the opposite of them as well, because that one can be pointed at from the road. **A bell and a shovel, one sentence, two trades, and the only person who can get anything out of either of them is the one standing in front of it looking at it, and that has been true since the thirty-first and I did not have it until a man with a shovel told me about a yard he lives in and a stone he bought himself.**
>
> *And the thing he told me and asked me to leave out, and did not. **He is not paid for this road and since the ford he has been the only man who has put stone in a road he is not paid for, and he did it because it is soft, and he asked for it out of my book, and I wrote it in, and he said he knew I would before he said it and he said it anyway.** The reason he wanted it out is not honour: **a man who is paid for one road and does another for nothing is a man somebody will want to pay for both in a year, and then he is on a list, and then there is a day somebody comes with a sheet and his name is on it for a thing done on his own account. And a thing nobody can produce is the only thing he has that is his, and he was not going to hand it to a book.**
>
> *And I wrote it in anyway, and **there is no form in this kingdom on which a man with a barrow may be asked to leave a thing out, and that is the absence I have been measuring for a hundred and fifty-two days, and I am not going to pretend tonight that measuring it makes me a person who would have left it out.** Nobody thanked him. He said if I come back in a year he will tell me it was not worth it and he will not be sorry, and I am not going to come back in a year, and he is right about that too.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a flat, a heap of stone and a shovel are not buildings, and I did not take a bolt off for a man with a barrow-less hod because he did not ask me to.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, seventy-five days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to, and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am barred from any public place in that county and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and I am not going to it, and it does not travel, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and fifty-two days, including this one, and including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and a man's own mend in a wall in a street I have not been to, told to me by him and not by anybody else, and not one of that is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and he did not do it for me and he told me before I said anything that he was telling me for himself.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a column, a counter, a sheet with a name in it, a list a man gets put on by being useful, and a hole in a wall about nine years old that nobody has ever asked about.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A mend is the only work in this kingdom that cannot be edited and cannot be produced, and that is the whole reason it is done twice, and there is nowhere to stand and say so, and he has just told me the price of that out loud and asked me to keep it out of a book, and I have kept it in, and I am not going to be the man who pretends otherwise.**
