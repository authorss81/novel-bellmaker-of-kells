# Chapter 0162 — A Number She Said In A Market And A Number She Said On A Wall

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and forty-eighth day and Tomas Vale had the barrow on a flat above a low wall with the four bolts made fast, and a girl was sitting on the wall with her feet off the ground and a basket beside her that was not empty and not heavy, and she had been sitting there long enough that the dew had come off the stone and dried on it again, and she was about a quarter of a mile above the yard of the coach inn and had come up from it at the fourth hour and had been counting as she came.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and fifteen days, and a wall and a flat and a dew-dried seat are not buildings, and she has not asked me once where I am sleeping and I have not told her, and that is the correct arrangement between two people who are not asking each other anything.**

---

At about the fifth hour she got off the wall and picked up the basket and said the thing she had come to say, which is that she had counted them again on her own time and not for him.

“**Two hundred and six.**”

“**You said three hundred and eleven.**”

“**I said three hundred and eleven in a market of about two thousand on the day before the day before yesterday, and I counted that standing on the yard of the staging at the water end, all nine miles, and I said out loud in that market that I would tell you when I got to a number, and I have got to one.**” She put the basket down again. “**This morning I started in the yard of the inn and I have four miles done and two hundred and six on them, and those are two different numbers about two different pieces of ground, and if you write them in the same hand one under the other you will have made a thing that is not there.**”

“**There is a gate where a fence was.**”

“**There is a gate at about the second mile of the nine, and it is on ground I counted five days ago, and the man who put it up put it up well, and I am not going to walk back down and count it out of a number I have said in a market, and I am certainly not going to tell the man who wanted a gate that his gate has come out of a count.**”

“**The man who wanted a gate.**”

“**A different man, and I have never spoken to him, and he did not pay me and did not ask me to, and I am counting his fences because I would rather count a fence than not count one, and you are not the reason and I would like that on whichever page you keep me on.**”

---

At about the sixth hour he asked her why she was out this side of the inn, and she told him, and then she asked him one, and he answered her, and she told him what she thought of the answer.

“**You have gone past the inn and you have not stopped.**”

“**I have not.**”

“**Why.**”

“**Because there is a road above this inn and nobody has ever drawn it for me, and a road that nobody has drawn is the only kind left.**”

She looked at him for about four seconds.

“**You have told me that because it is true and not because it gets me to do anything, and I would rather have had the other reason.**”

“**I know.**”

“**Then you have not got the other reason, and neither has anybody else, because there is not one, and I have watched about nine men on this road try to make a true thing into a reason and I have never once seen it work and I am not going to be the one it works on.**”

“**You said you would walk it with me.**”

“**I said I would count them and I would not go off by myself, and I have not, and you have asked me nothing since the market except how many fences there are, which is the only question a person is ever really asking anybody on a road.**” She shifted the basket. “**And I am not carrying anything up that bank and I am not carrying anything down it, and if you put your hand on this basket I will put it down and I will not pick it up again until you have gone past me, and I am not being proud, I am telling you where the line is so that you do not have to guess and so that I do not have to be rude about it later.**”

He did not touch the basket. **And the reason she gave is the fourth clause of an arrangement I made out loud in a room I paid for, in different words, in a different county, to a different person, and nobody in this kingdom has a form for it and I have watched it arrive at a gate in a different shape this morning and I am not going to tell her that, and I am not going to write it down as a pattern either, because she told me in one sentence where the line was and I did not cross it and that is a day, not a discovery.**

---

At about the seventh hour a man came up the road with a dog and a gun and stopped about nine feet off, and the dog was not interested in any of them, and Tomas Vale had asked this man a question five days ago and had been told no and had not written down the reason.

Rodge Ollis did not say hello and he did not wait.

“**No.**”

“**I have not asked you.**”

“**You are going to.**” The dog put its nose into a hedge. “**And I am giving you the answer first this time so that you do not have to work out how to ask it. I said no to you in a market and I am saying no to you on a road, and it is the same no, and it has not got a day on it.**”

“**Will you tell me the reason this time.**”

“**No.**”

“**You said you would.**”

“**I said I would not say why, and I did not, and you wrote that down, or you did not write it down, and either is correct and I have not been waiting five days to find out which.**”

“**I did not write it down.**”

“**Then you have got no list of nine men and I have got my morning.**” He shifted the gun. “**And I will tell you why I said you would want to be back, since you have not asked and I am in the mood. There is a bridge at the fourth mile and it is boarded and has been boarded a while, and a man who walks past a boarded bridge in the wrong light goes over it, and I have picked a man up off the water at that gap once and he was not you and he is not going to be you, and that is the whole of my knowledge and I have never sold it and I am not selling it to you.**”

He went up the lane. The dog came last.

**He is the second of the nine who has said no to me in the ordinary way and the first of them who has told me that I kept no list, and he is right and I kept no list and I am not going to start one at the seventh hour of the hundred and forty-eighth because a man with a dog has told me there is a bridge at the fourth mile. And I have written the number four and the word bridge on a line of their own and I am not going to join them to anything, and a man who joins a man's no to his yes because they were said on the same morning is arranging a thing.**

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on the wall with the barrow on the flat behind him and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and forty-eighth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was, and the girl was nine feet off his left shoulder and had not asked him one question about a road in ten days, which was about four in ten of what everybody else in this county had done.

> *The hundred and forty-eighth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A low wall the length of a stretch of road, a flat above it, a girl with a basket, two barrows, a man with a dog and a gun, and a wall.**
>
> *Second column, and the fences. **Three hundred and eleven in a market on the day before the day before yesterday, counted standing in the yard of the staging at the water end, all nine miles. Two hundred and six this morning, four miles up from the yard of the inn, and she told me the number before I asked her for it because she said out loud in a market that she would.** And then she told me the thing about the two numbers that I had not thought of: **they are about two different pieces of ground, and if I write them in the same hand one under the other I will have made a thing that is not there.** And there is a gate at about the second mile of the nine where a fence was, and the man who put it up put it up well, and she is not going to walk back down four miles to count it out of a number she has said in a market. **A man who wanted that gate is not going to hear from her that his gate came out of a number, and that is hers to decide and not mine.** **A different man wanted that gate, and she has never spoken to him, and he did not pay her and did not ask her to, and she said out loud that I am not the reason.** A number a person says out loud on a road is a fact about that road on that day and about the person who walked it, and a number in a column is a fact about whoever ruled the column, and those are two different objects and I have been writing them in the same hand for a hundred and forty-eight days.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the man with the dog. **He said no before I opened my mouth and said he had been waiting five days to find out whether I kept a list of the nine, and I did not keep one and I am not going to start one tonight.** He also told me one thing he did not have to and would not sell: **there is a bridge at the fourth mile, it is boarded, and it has been boarded a while, and a man walking past it in the wrong light goes over it, and he has picked a man up off the water there once.** That is four, and a bridge, on two lines with nothing else on them, and **I am not going to build a shape out of a man's no and a man's yes because they happened within an hour of each other on one road and I have been in this country for ten days.**
>
> *And the line at a basket. **She told me where it was in one sentence and I did not cross it and she did not have to be rude about it later. I have watched an arrangement I made in a room I paid for arrive at a gate in a different shape in a different county, from a person who has never heard of the arrangement, and the reason is the same reason in other words, and I am not going to tell her and I am not going to write it down as a pattern either. A day is a day and not a discovery.**
>
> *And what she said about my answer. **I told her that there is a road above the inn and nobody has drawn it for me and that is the only kind left, because it is true and not because it gets her to do anything, and she said she would rather have had the other reason, and the other reason does not exist.** About four in ten of what any of us says on a road is right, and being right about why you are on a road is not a thing anybody has to do for you, and she is the only person in ten days who has told me that and she is right.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a wall and a flat and a dew-dried seat are not buildings.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, seventy-one days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to, and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am barred from any public place in that county and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and I am not going to it, and it does not travel, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and forty-eight days, including this one, and including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and three hundred and nine, and a bridge at the fourth mile, and a man who would sell me neither, and not one of the four is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and a man with a dog told me he had been waiting five days to find out whether I kept a list and I do not and he is right.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a gate a man built properly where a fence used to be, a number that a person said out loud twice and did not improve, a bridge that is boarded at the fourth mile, and a man with a dog who will not sell what he knows about water.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A true sentence is the smallest thing anybody in this kingdom can carry, and she carried one about a gate this morning and would not let me near her basket about it, and I have not asked her one question about this road in ten days and I am not going to make it eleven.**
