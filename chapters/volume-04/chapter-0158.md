# Chapter 0158 — A Bench That Asks Him To Say A Thing He Cannot Certify

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and forty-fourth day and Tomas Vale left the barrow on the landing at the foot of the outside stair with the four bolts made fast and went up, and the door at the top of it was open, and there were about nine people in a room with a bench along one wall and a table on the other side of it.

It was a bench of a river canton. Not a court, not a district office, not the Crown, and not a round. **Eight or nine of those on this river and every one of them has its own bench, its own clerk and its own notion of what a repair is for, and there is no command among them and there has never been one, and I have known that for a hundred and forty-four days and I have never once had occasion to be glad of it until I was standing in a room with one.**

Nobody looked up.

“**Say what you want and then wait.**”

---

At about the fifth hour he said it, and he said it in the same words he had said to the woman on the stair the day before, and a clerk at the table with a book wrote nothing.

“**I am asking one question about a road. Whether anybody in this room will walk nine miles of it and tell me what is on it.**”

“**No.**”

“**You have not asked anybody.**”

“**I have not asked anybody and the answer is no, and it is no on my own account and not because of you.**” The clerk shut the book. “**There is no form in this kingdom on which a man with no standing may be entered as evidence of anything, and if there were one, you could not fill it in, because the form wants a thing you were there to see and you were not.**”

“**I could say what I saw.**”

“**You could say what you saw at a bench and about nine of every nine hundred people who hear a sentence repeated out of one of these rooms will have it right the first time and more wrong by the fourth telling, and one of the nine will have a name in it, and it will not be your name, it will be whichever of the nine of us was standing nearest the door.**”

“**So the bench will not carry a sentence I cannot certify.**”

“**The bench will not carry a sentence anybody cannot certify, and that includes ours. There is a book on this table and the book takes what this bench decides at a sitting, and at a sitting we decide about a bar, a rate, a landing stage and a wall, and a stranger's road is not one of the four, and if a man stood here and said a thing that was true it would still not be one of the four.**”

**Being believed by somebody in this kingdom is worth exactly nothing. There is no form on which belief is entered. A man four days' road east in a chair with a lamp cannot enter a paragraph in a column, and a bench in a room over a landing cannot either, and I have been trying to get somebody to carry a true sentence for me since the hundred and first day of this year, and there is no desk in this kingdom at which it is worth anything.**

---

At about the sixth hour he did not ask them to agree to anything, which is a thing he has not done before and it took him about nine minutes of standing still to do it.

Instead he asked two of them, separately, the same question, and he asked each of them on a different side of the room.

The first was Aman Petch, who hauled at that landing and had a hand like the flat end of a spade, and the question was: **where does this canton stop and the next one begin, on the road west of here.**

“**At the boundary stone.**”

“**Where is the boundary stone.**”

“**A mile and a bit west, at the top of the rise, on the left going west.**”

“**What is on it.**”

“**A canton's arms and a number, and the number is a number.**”

The second was Nieve Ossick, who kept the ford tally and had kept it for longer than anybody in the room, and Tomas Vale asked her the same question on the other side of the room, in the same words, in the same order.

“**There is no boundary.**”

“**He has told you about a stone.**”

“**I have heard him. He is not wrong about the stone.**” She turned round in her chair. “**He is wrong about it being a boundary. It is a stone with a number on it and a district paid a man to set it once, and the cart that goes over that rise every day from the staging to the ford does not know it is there, and it goes over the same rise whether the stone is on the left or in a fire, and that is what a boundary is when you are a person and not a clerk.**”

“**Then where does it stop.**”

“**It stops where the toll bar was.**”

“**There was a toll bar.**”

“**There was, and it was taken down, and I do not know by whom and I am not going to guess, and I know it was there because my grandmother's name is on the wrong side of it in a book four days' road away and I have never been able to make a sentence of that.**”

Both of them were right. Tomas Vale wrote both of them down on the same page in the same hand and did not join them, and he had known he was not going to join them before he asked the second one, and he wrote underneath them, in his own hand: **these do not fit and I am not going to make them fit, and two true things in one room in one hour is not a shape, and if I had got four in a day it would not be a shape either, and arranging them is how you get a sentence that is true about nothing.**

---

At about the seventh hour the clerk asked him a question, which was the first one anybody in the room had asked him.

“**What will you do with it.**”

“**What.**”

“**With the two things. With any of them. You have got two true sentences in a book and a barrow that will not go nine miles in a day and no standing and no form. What happens to the sentences.**”

“**I will walk them.**”

“**That is not a plan, that is a distance.**”

“**It is what I have.**”

“**Then it is what you have.**” The clerk opened the book, wrote the word of the day in it, shut it, and did not write a line of what he had said. “**And I am going to tell you the thing this bench is for, since you have been in a room and nobody has told you. This bench shares a river and a bridge rate and a foundry between it and eight others, and that is the whole of what it shares, and we turn up. We have never needed a command and we are not going to be told what a repair is for by a man four days' road away, and if you went up four flights in that city and asked them, they would not put it in a column either, and you would come back here and be the man who has been told, and we would all be able to see it.**”

“**I have not been told anything by anybody.**”

“**No.**” The clerk put the pen down. “**And that is the best thing about you and I am not going to be the man who improves it.**”

---

Then he worked out the whole of a hundred and forty-four days standing at a bench with his hands behind his back, and it took about four minutes.

**The only instrument I have ever had that costs nobody anything is the arrangement of three.**

**I said it out loud in a room I paid for on the hundred and twentieth and there is no paper in it and no plate and no clerk, and the four things in it are that there is no paper, and that I do not keep the terms, and that I will not take the same words to two people and come back with two the same, and that I will not ask either of them for anything, and if either of them does a thing I will not ask what it was for and I will not write it down and I will not say thank you.**

**And it costs nobody anything. Eleven people on a landing cost a day each. A bench costs a day. A man at a board cost me nothing and cost him nothing and there is nothing in it for either of us except a number of nine, and that is the cheapest thing that has happened to me in a hundred and forty-four days and I have got four of them.**

**And I am not allowed to make another one, and I would not want to, and the reason is four inches long and it has been one sentence in three months: a man with no standing who arranges a thing has made the thing about himself, and there is not one instrument in this kingdom that a man with no standing can hold without it becoming about him, and the three of us are the exception, and the exception is not mine and I do not hold it and I cannot ask for it and I am not going to ask for it.**

**A bench is not a court and a court is not a bench, and there is one more difference and I have it now and it is the smallest one: a room where a thing is said out loud and written down is a different object from a room where a thing is said out loud and is not. A man at a board under a roof writes about four hundred things a year into a book. A man in a smithy told me three words about a coupling in the air. A woman on a stair told me nine hundred yards of road and shut a book. Nine people in a room here have said two true things to me and there is no mark of either of them anywhere in this kingdom and that is why I am the one who has to carry them, and it will be forty years after I am dead if I write them down wrong.**

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step by the water at the bottom of the landing with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and forty-fourth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and forty-fourth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **An outside stair, a room with a bench along one wall, about nine people, a table, a book, a clerk, a boundary stone, a toll bar, and a step by the water.**
>
> *Second column, and the refusal. **The bench will not carry a sentence anybody cannot certify, and that includes theirs. There is a form in this room and the form takes what the bench decides at a sitting, and at a sitting they decide about a bar, a rate, a landing stage and a wall, and a stranger's road is not one of the four and would not be if it were true.** Being believed in this kingdom is worth exactly nothing: no form on which belief is entered, a chair four days' road east cannot enter a paragraph in a column, and a bench in a room over a landing cannot either. **I have been trying since the hundred and first day of this year to get somebody to carry a true sentence for me and there is no desk in this kingdom at which that is worth anything.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and two things that do not fit. **A boundary stone a mile and a bit west at the top of the rise, on the left, with a canton's arms and a number on it. And there is no boundary, because a cart going over that rise does not know the stone is there, and the real edge is a toll bar that was taken down, and the woman who keeps the ford tally does not know by whom and is not going to guess, and her grandmother's name is on the wrong side of it in a book four days' road away.** Both true. Both about the same mile. **I did not join them and I wrote underneath them that I was not going to, and I wrote it before I asked the second one, which is the whole of the rule I have had since the ninetieth.**
>
> *And the thing I have been carrying for a hundred and forty-four days without seeing it. **The only instrument I have ever had that costs nobody anything is the arrangement of three: no paper, I do not keep the terms, I will not take the same words to two people and come back with two the same, and I will not ask either of them for anything, and if either of them does a thing I will not ask what it was for and I will not write it down and I will not say thank you.** It cost nothing and it has moved a sentence the length of a kingdom and it is not mine. **Eleven people on a landing cost a day each. A bench costs a day. A man under a roof cost me nothing and cost him nothing and all I got was a number of nine.** And I am not allowed to make another one and I would not want to.
>
> *And the last difference, which is the smallest. **A room where a thing is said out loud and written down is a different object from a room where a thing is said out loud and is not.** Two true things said in this room in an hour exist nowhere at all, not in the book, not in a column, not on a board, and I am the only carrier, and if I write them down wrong they will still be wrong in about forty years.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for and nothing taken out of that room, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is on a landing and it is forty feet from me and it did not go up the stair with me.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, sixty-seven days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag, and she was on the landing all day and nobody asked her for a reason.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about them except the oldest one, and two true sentences in this room that are in no book in this kingdom, and not one of them is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked, and the clerk told me what a bench is for and I am not going to be the man who goes up four flights and comes back with it.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a bench of a river canton that shares a river and a bridge rate and a foundry with eight others and turns up, and a book that takes four kinds of thing and not a fifth, and a woman who has a book of about nine hundred names a floor below and has never been asked what it is.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A bench is not a court and a court is not a bench and a room that writes things down is not a room that does not, and being believed by anybody in this kingdom is worth exactly nothing, and I have two sentences and a road and no form on which any of the three of us owes the other anything.**
