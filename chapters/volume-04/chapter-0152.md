# Chapter 0152 — A Woman At A Pitch Who Asks Him What The Road Is For

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and thirty-eighth day and Tomas Vale had the barrow on the flat below a village of about two hundred people at a ford, and the four bolts were made fast, and the frame had not been in a building the day before either and was not going to be in one today, because the lowest thing he could see from where the barrow stood was a wall and the highest was a horse.

The market was on the ground, which is what a market at a ford is. There was no hill to put the seller on and nobody needed one.

**A ford is not a place. That is the first thing I have got wrong about this road in two days and I have been wrong about it since I came up out of the flat. A ford is four or five days a year and a man crosses it at a place, and the place is a different place on each of those days, and there are about nine stones in it that are always in the same places and everything else is opinion.**

---

At about the fifth hour he was on the flat with the barrow and a woman at a pitch against the low wall, and her name was Brida Tolliver, and the pitch was needles and a bit of string and about nine dozen of each, and she was doing what she had been doing and he stood at the end of it the way he stands at the end of things, which is at a distance where he can leave.

“**You are the one with the barrow and the thing under the oilcloth.**”

“**I am.**”

“**Where is it going.**”

“**West.**”

“**Then you are going to the ford and you are going to go round it, and everybody who tells you different is selling you a look at something.**”

He did not argue with her about that, and she was right.

“**How many days a year can a man cross it.**”

“**Four or five.**” She said it the way a woman says a thing she has said before. “**Five in a wet year and four in a dry one and there was a year not long back when it was six and a man in this village will tell you it was eight and he is not lying, he was not here for the other two.**”

“**And the stones.**”

“**The nine stones are where your father put them.**” She looked at him then, properly, for about a second. “**That was a joke and it was not a good one, and you are a great deal older than the joke, and the stones are where the last lot of men put them, and they are the only fixed thing in this ford. Everything else moves about four feet in a fortnight and so does the road.**”

**She is right about the ford and she is wrong about the year, in the ordinary way. I asked her a thing about water and she gave me a number and a name for a number and one of them is nine and one of them is not, and I have not written down which is which because it does not matter and it is the first thing in two days that does not matter.**

---

Then she asked him something, and he had not asked her for it.

“**What is the road for.**”

“**I do not know.**”

“**That is not an answer, that is a refusal, and I have had about nine of them today before the water was down.**” She put a needle down on the oilcloth and put her hand flat on it. “**You come down that bank on foot with a thing on a barrow and you stop at the end of my pitch, and you have not asked me one single question about the ford, and you have asked me two about the stones, and the two about the stones is a person who has come from somewhere with a paper.**”

“**I have a paper.**”

“**I did not say you had not. I said you have.**”

He did not say anything for about four seconds.

“**I am not going to show it to you.**”

“**No.**” She took her hand off the needle. “**And I would not have read it, and you knew that, and the reason you knew it is that you have the look of a man who has been told there is no form for showing a person a drawing, and there is not, and it is not the district's fault that there is not.**”

**A man with no standing and a drawing in his coat. And a woman at a pitch who worked that out in about four seconds from a barrow and a question about stones. I have been in five counties and a city asking people what a document is and she has just done it to me in one sentence and I did not know it was coming and there was no office in it anywhere.**

---

At about the sixth hour she told him two more things and she did not wait to be asked for either of them, and the second one is the reason the day is in my book.

“**The district had that road open in the spring.**” She nodded up the bank. “**And there is a notice on a stake at the top of it and it has said *wires* since the year before last, and nobody has ever come and asked me what the wire was for, and I have told the two people who asked me that I do not know, and it is not that I am keeping it. I do not know. It went under the road in the time of my grandmother and it was not hers and it is not mine, and there is a hole in that road that a man dug and a lid on it, and that is the whole of what I have ever been able to say about it.**”

“**You are not sure of the year.**”

“**I am sure of the season and I am not sure of the year, and it is on a stake, and you can go up and look at how old the stake is, and I have never done that and I am not going to.**”

“**I will not either.**”

“**You will.**” She said it without any weight. “**Everybody does. It is the only thing anybody ever wants off that road and I have had four men do it in nine years and not one of them came back and told me what the stake said.**”

He did not correct her about the year. He walked up the bank at the sixth hour and looked at the cut where the road was open, and the stone was stacked at the side, and the notice was on a stake, and the notice said *wires* and did not say whose wires they were.

The wire was cut. There was a burr on the underside of the cut.

**A clean cut from above and a burr underneath, and I have been in this trade since I was nine and I know what a cut from above leaves and what a cut from the side leaves, and that is what a tool leaves and not what a man leaves, and I have not asked anybody about it and I am not going to today.**

**And I am not going to tell her what the wire is for, because I do not know what the wire is for, and I am not going to use the word for the thing that took it out of the line in a lane with a woman standing in it who has told me her grandmother's time. There is no reason on any paper in this kingdom to say it to her and there is no reason not to say it and I am not going to say it either way.**

---

At about the seventh hour he asked four people what the nine miles west of the ford are called, and he asked it the way he asks, which is the same words to each of them in the same order, and he wrote down the four answers and did not correct one of them.

A man with a scythe, working the bank above the water: **the bottom road.**

A carter going east with a load: **the cart road.**

A girl at the flat with a yoke and no load: **the long pull.**

And the woman at the pitch, without being asked twice: **the ford road.**

**Four names for nine miles of the same mud. Every one of them is right and not one of them is on a sheet and I asked the same question in the same words four times and got four different true things, and about four in ten of what any of these four people say in a day is right, and there is no way at all of telling which four in ten.**

**I have been trying for a hundred and thirty-eight days to get a person in this kingdom to say the same sentence twice, and it turns out I can get it, and it costs a day and a barrow and it is worth nothing, and that is the whole trade.**

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on the flat below the ford with the four bolts made fast and the sack against the wheel and the barrow out of the wind, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and thirty-eighth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and thirty-eighth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A village of about two hundred at a ford, a market on the ground, a pitch of needles against a low wall, a bank, a cut in the road with a notice on a stake that says *wires*, and nine stones in a ford that are the only fixed thing in it.**
>
> *Second column, and the road. **A ford is not a place, it is four or five days a year, and the nine stones are the only fixed thing in it and the rest of it moves about four feet in a fortnight, and so does the road.** I have been wrong about that for two days. Four names for the same nine miles, asked in the same words in the same order: **the bottom road, the cart road, the long pull, the ford road.** All four right. **None of them on a sheet. I asked four people for the same sentence and got four true things and I am not going to correct one of them, and I asked four in ten about the year and she was wrong about it and right about the stones and neither of those touches the other.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the thing I did not expect to be given. **Brida Tolliver worked out in about four seconds from a barrow and a question about stones that I have a paper I am not going to show her, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man with no standing may put a drawing in front of a stranger and call it evidence, and she named the reason before I had it, and there was no office anywhere in it.** And then she told me two things I had not asked for. The district had the road open in the spring. The notice says *wires* and has said it since a year before last and nobody has ever asked her what it was for. **I did not ask her for a reason and I am not going to. I did not tell her what the wire is for, because I do not know, and I did not use the word for the thing that took it out of the line.**
>
> *And the cut. **A clean cut from above with a burr underneath, and I know what that leaves, and it is a thing a tool leaves and not a thing a man leaves, and I have not asked anybody about it and I did not ask today.** A road is a road and a cut is a cut and a man can see a road that is open and cannot see a claim.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a flat, a wall, a pitch and a bank are not buildings, and the lowest thing I could see from the barrow was a horse.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, sixty-one days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is where it has been since before we left Kells, and she has been about nine feet off my left shoulder since the flat and I have not asked her what this road is for, and I am not going to today.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and four names for a road, and a word on a stake, and a thing a woman said for nothing, and not one of them is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing today and I have not thanked anybody, and I am not going to, because she did it on her own account and did not know what it was worth and I have not got the right to be the man who tells her.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a cut in a road with a lid on it, a notice that names a thing and not an owner, a stone stacked at the side, a woman who has told four men the same four sentences in nine years, and a man with a barrow who walked nine feet up a bank and looked at a stake and is not going to tell anybody what was on it.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A claim in metal cannot be edited and a sheet can be, and the difference is four inches long, and a road is a fact about the people who walk it and four of them have just given me four names for it for nothing and none of them is owed to me.**
