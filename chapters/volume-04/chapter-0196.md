# Chapter 0196 — The Water Came Over the Bank at About the Sixth Hour

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and eighty-second day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on the flat of beaten earth above the water, and it is a hundred and forty-nine days since the frame has been in a building, and a flat of beaten earth above nine foot of river is not a building, and the four bolts take four minutes, which is the whole of the second clause of a receipt in five clauses, all five standing, and he has made them fast at that hour on every day of this river since the thirty-third.

The water had not moved since the fourth hour of the day before. That was the thing nobody in Hallowmere could get used to: four days of a river going up and then a whole day of a river not going up at all, and a flat, silent river is a thing that makes a man look at the sky and then look at the water again and then look at the sky, and Abel Trope was on the boards three foot under with his launch on a painter and had cut a fifth notch at about the seventh hour of the day before and had not cut a sixth.

"It turns tonight," he said, when Tomas came down the flat. "It always turns tonight when it has stood a day. It comes up two foot between now and about the second hour and it comes over the bank at about the sixth hour and it goes out again on the day after and it is the going out that takes the wall off, and I have never once seen it do the same thing twice and neither has anybody else in this town."

"Will you be here when it comes over."

"No," Trope said. "There is nowhere here to be when it comes over. I go up the reach to the launch and I sit in her and I let her go down with the reach, and I have been doing that four times in my life and it is the correct thing to do and I would tell a stranger to do it and I would not do it myself with a dog."

---

At about the fifth hour the four legs were put together for the third time, and nobody was asked, and by the third day nobody had to be, and that is a thing that looks like a route and is not one.

Mab Sarter was on the wall with the piece of chalk and she had not counted anything since the day before yesterday and she had a thing in her own head instead, which was the two hundred and ninety-four and the three stones, and she carried leg three to the top of the passage and said the words at the top of it to a man with a barrow and a bad coat, who said them back correctly, and she went no further, and there was nobody standing at that top passage at the fifth hour who was going to say anything else. A man at the low quarter's second door said the words to his wife, and she said them to a woman on the far side of a wall about a foot high with no gate in it, and he did not know that and would not have known for a week.

The passage behind the fourth house was under three foot at the top end at the fifth hour and Tomas Vale stood at the top of it and looked down nine or ten feet of dark water and there was no version of the last leg, and there had not been one since the fourth hour of the day before yesterday, and he had not built one and he was not going to.

He said the words to four people in the low quarter and to nobody in the yard, because to get to the yard he would have had to go through a passage with three foot of moving water in it and the only way to have got there first would have been to start yesterday, and yesterday he had known about the mark and not about the passage, and the day before that he had known about neither.

---

At about the sixth hour the river came over the bank at Hallowmere and it came over a grass bank of nine foot in about a quarter of an hour without anybody hearing it, because nine foot of bank going under makes less noise than one foot of it going under a door, and the first anybody in the low quarter knew was that the water was in the rooms.

It came in at the bottom of the steps, which had been under since the sixth hour of the day before, and it came along the channel that Obin Farrow had cut in the spring and had not been paid for, and a channel takes a river four times faster than ground does, which is the whole of what a channel is and the whole of what Farrow had said it would do, and by the time the water was in the third house it was a foot deep and moving.

Farrow was on his knees at the sluice board with a mallet in his hand when it came, and the sluice board was nine foot in three pieces in front of him and not one of them in place, and he got up and got his two feet out of the channel and stood in the street with the water coming past him at about a foot and looked at nine foot of oak in three pieces that he had been holding together with nine nails for two days and had not been paid for and had been told to put in, and said one word, and Tomas Vale was thirty feet off and heard it and it was not a bad word and it was not a good one.

"Afterwards," Farrow said, at about the seventh hour, standing in a street with a foot of water in it, to a man with a barrow. "I will say it afterwards. If I say it now I am a man in a wet street and there is nothing in that anybody wants."

The water went into nine houses of the low quarter's front row and stopped there for the night, which is the part that decided the shape of the next four days and that nobody could account for: the ground of the low quarter is not level. It is a foot higher at the fourth house than it is at the first, and a foot higher at the first than it is at the far end where the passage is, and a river coming over a bank finds the low ground and stops on the low ground, and two hundred and forty people in that quarter woke up in about eleven wet houses and two hundred and twenty-nine people in that quarter woke up dry.

Tamsin Rook was in it.

She was in two foot of water at the fifth step, at about the sixth hour, and she was not doing anything for anybody, and nobody was doing anything for her, and there was nothing in that street for about an hour that was not a man with his own ground. She had her hand on the second post of a handrail that came down the steps, and the post was oak and set nine inches into the wall with a stone under that, and the wall behind it was nine courses of brick and the ninth course was holding and the tenth was not there any more and had not been there since before anybody in that street was born, and she could get all three of those facts in four minutes with a hand and a boot and could not have got any of them out of a book or a map or a sheet of a district's, and she was not telling anybody any of it because there was nobody to tell.

At about the seventh hour she got herself up out of the street onto a stone sill nine inches above the water with her bag in her hand and her boots off and in her hand and nothing on her from the waist down that was dry, and there were nine people in the street and nine of them were helping somebody who was not a stranger, and she sat on the sill with her back to a wall and waited, and nobody asked her a question at any hour of the day, and she did not get up and go and find a person to ask one, and that is not the same as not being able to hear, and it is not the same as not being asked because she is difficult.

The yard behind the steps got itself out on its own, and it got itself out the way it has got itself out four times in the life of the woman who keeps it: the small things went on the plank and the plank would not lift, and nine people went over the plank and down into a boat-shed through a door a man has to get through backwards, and the other half-odd of them went up a ladder on the inside of the far wall, which is a thing that is in that yard and not in any book, and the last of them up the ladder was the woman with the rope, at about the seventh hour, with nine hundred small things of forty people's left in the bottom corner of a yard a hundred and forty foot above two hundred and forty other people.

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on the flat with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and about a third of the low quarter of Hallowmere was under a foot of water and two thirds of it was dry, and the yard was dry because a yard a hundred and forty foot up a bank does not flood, and nine hundred small things were in the bottom of it and were going to be there in the morning, and nobody had been told anything about any of that by him or by anybody else on foot, and nobody was going to be.

> *The register is thirteen. **I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and nobody in that street was thanked either.** I was on this road with her forty-six days today and did not ask her what it was for, and she was in that street in two foot of water with her hand on a post and nobody asked her anything, and there was nobody to ask, and she did not go and find one.
