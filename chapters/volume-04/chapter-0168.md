# Chapter 0168 — Forty Houses, A Well And A Market On The Ground, And Nobody Here Is Hiding From Anything

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and fifty-fourth day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on the flat at the top of a lane, and there was a street below him of about forty houses with a well in it and a lean-to with a fire going in it and about sixty people standing about on the ground, and one of them was selling eggs off a board and there was no hill to sell them from and none wanted.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and twenty-one days, and a flat, a lane, a street, a lean-to and a well are not buildings, and I have not been inside any of the forty houses and I am not going to, and I did not ask anybody what this place is called before I had been in it about nine minutes, and the reason I did not ask is that I have asked people what things are called for ten days and every time it has been a name and never once a place.**

---

At about the fifth hour he went down the lane into the street and the four of them came down behind him, and nobody stopped any of them, and nobody said good morning, and a man at a gate who had been watching the barrow come down the lane went back to his gate.

They asked four people, one at a time, the same question in the same words, and it was: **is there a road that comes to this place.**

A man at a gate with a barrow of nothing in it: “**There is a lane.**”

“**A lane is not a road.**”

“**A lane is what there is.**” He tipped the barrow. “**We have always been here and there has never been a road to us, and a map wants a road, and if you have come to put us on something then you have come to the wrong place and I would rather you did not.**”

A woman with a key on a string and a pail: “**There is a heading with a number on it in a district's book and I have never in my life seen a road that goes where it says.**”

A man at the fire in the lean-to who had not come out: “**The inn draws a cart four days a week and the cart does not come here, and when it does not come it goes past the top of your lane and turns at the ash, and that is the whole of what a road is for and I have been at this fire for about as long as you have been alive and it has not changed.**”

And then a fourth woman, at the well, with a bucket, who did not answer the question and answered a different one, and Tomas Vale wrote that down as a separate line and did not put it under the other three.

“**It is not that nobody knows we are here. Everybody knows. There is just nothing to put down.**”

---

At about the sixth hour he stood in the middle of the street with the barrow on the flat above and Tamsin Rook about nine feet off his left shoulder on the well's kerb, where the ground was hard, and worked out the whole of a hundred and fifty-four days in about nine minutes, and it is the finding of the hundred and fifty-fourth, and it is a negative one and it is the third of three.

**There is a place of about forty houses on this ground, with a well and a fire and about sixty people on it this morning, and every one of the four of them told me in their own words that there is no road to it, and none of them was evasive and none of them was afraid and one of them told me to go away, which is a thing I had been told eleven times in ten days in nine ordinary voices and I am not going to improve on any of the eleven either.**

**And I have a rectangle about two hands together in a drawing in my coat. Not torn, not cut, the paper smooth, the rules stopping short, the ink a slightly different black either side, the numbers going on without a break, and it is in the same place, and I have walked over the top of it twice and the ground under it is exactly like the ground either side of it. And I have found today a street of forty houses that four people say is not on anything. And I am not going to join them.**

**And the reason is not that joining them is clever. It is that one of the two is a fact about a piece of paper and the other is a fact about forty houses, and a rule I have had since the ninetieth is that two empties in one day in one building may be joined and three empties in three counties may not, and this is the third time I have written that sentence in ten days and it is still a rule and not a discovery, and the third time is the last time I am going to write it in this county, because a man who writes a rule down a fourth time has started to want it to be a pattern.**

**So I am going to write down the two facts on two lines, one under the other, and not rule a box round them, and here is the finding and it is small: a road is a fact about the people who walk it, and a street of forty houses is a fact about the people who live in it, and a drawing is a fact about neither, and I have been asking the third one to do the work of the other two for a hundred and fifty-four days and there is no form on which anybody is taught it and therefore no version of being better at it.**

At about the seventh hour he asked nobody anything else. There was a thing he wanted to ask, which was what the district's book said, and he had it in his mouth, and he did not put it there, and he wrote down in his own book that he had not asked it and which one it was, the way he had written it down on a low wall nine days earlier and in his own book eight days earlier.

**There is no form in this kingdom on which a man with no standing may stand in a street of forty houses and ask to see the entry, and the absence is the same hole that a stake's small print is and that a bench's four boxes are and that a reason box is, and I have now put my hand in the same hole four times in ten days and I am not going to do it a fifth, and that is not a decision I made today. That hole has been the same shape since the thirty-first.**

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on the flat at the top of the lane with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and fifty-fourth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and fifty-fourth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A flat at the top of a lane, a street of about forty houses, a well, a lean-to with a fire in it, about sixty people on the ground, an egg board, a key on a string, a bucket, a man at a gate, and a flat.**
>
> *Second column, and the same question four times, out of earshot, in the same words: **is there a road that comes to this place.** There is a lane, and a lane is not a road, and there has never been a road. A heading with a number in it in a district's book, and a road to match it that the woman who has seen the heading has never in her life laid an eye on. The inn's cart, four days a week, which goes past the top of the lane and turns at the ash. And then a fifth person who would not answer my question and answered a better one: **it is not that nobody knows we are here, everybody knows, there is just nothing to put down.** Five people, one market morning, no evasion and no fear, and one of them told me to go away, which I have been told eleven times in ten days and I am not going to improve on any of the eleven.
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the third of three, and it is negative. **Forty houses, a well, a fire, sixty people on the ground this morning, and four voices saying there is no road to this place in four different words and not one of them a liar about it, and the rectangle is on the other page of this book and it stays there.** One of the two is a fact about a piece of paper and the other is a fact about forty houses, **and what I have had since the ninetieth is a rule about how many separate things may be laid side by side without their turning into one thing, and I have now written it out four times in ten days, which is one more than I told myself on the bank at the seventh hour would be the last, and I am not going to sit here and argue with myself about that, and a man who writes a rule a fourth time has started to want it to be a pattern.** **Three things in ten days and it is still three and not a shape.**
>
> *And the two lines, unruled, and no box drawn round them. **A road is a fact about the people who walk it. A street of forty houses is a fact about the people who live in it. A drawing is a fact about neither.** The third one has been doing the work of the other two out of my coat for a hundred and fifty-four days. **Nobody is taught it anywhere, which is the only reason I have had no help with it, and there is a difference between a thing nobody in this kingdom can teach me and a thing I could learn in a fortnight if there were somebody to teach it, and I have not been able to tell which of those two I am standing in, and I have not got anybody to ask.**
>
> *And the question I did not ask. **I had it in my mouth and I did not put it there, and I have written down here that I did not ask it and which one it was.** There is nowhere in this kingdom to put a man with no standing in front of a street of forty houses and be answered, **and that is the same hole a stake's small print is, and a bench's four boxes are, and a reason box is, and I have had my hand in it four times in ten days and I am not going back into it a fifth, and the shape of it has not altered since the thirty-first and I did not make it.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, and nothing I have stood at the edge of in ten days has had four walls and a roof on it, and I have not been inside any of the forty houses.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, seventy-seven days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to, and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am barred from any public place in that county and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and I am not going to it, and it does not travel, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and fifty-four days, including this one, and including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and a place of about forty houses, and five true sentences said in one morning, and not one of the six is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and a man at a gate told me I had come to the wrong place and he would rather I did not and he is entitled to say so and I did not improve it.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a district's book with a heading and a number in it, a cart that turns at an ash, a lane that is what there is, and a market on the ground of about sixty people where nobody is on a hill.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A road is a fact about the people who walk it and a street is a fact about the people who live in it and a drawing is a fact about neither, and I have been asking the drawing for a hundred and fifty-four days, and there is a smooth rectangle about two hands together in the one in my coat and there is a street of forty houses under my feet, and I am not going to write a box round the two of them.**
