# Chapter 0156 — Four In Five Say No And He Does Not Argue With One Of Them

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and forty-second day and Tomas Vale had the barrow on the flat below a landing stage with the four bolts made fast, and the stage was a hundred feet of plank over mud with nine or ten men on it working a chain, and a lane ran up off the flat to a market of about two thousand people standing on the ground with nothing between them and nothing above them.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and forty-two days, and a landing stage is a hundred feet of plank and nine or ten men and it is not a building, and the only roof within a hundred yards is over a board at the other end of the market and I have not gone near it.**

---

At about the fifth hour he started asking.

The question was the same in the same words to every person he asked it of, and it was not a good question, and he knew it was not a good question before he had asked it twice.

“**I am looking for anybody who will walk nine miles of the road west of here and tell me at the end of it what is on it, and I cannot pay you and I have no authority and there is no form in this kingdom on which either of those can be put right.**”

A man at the stern of a boat said no and then worked the boat. A woman with a barrow of reeds said no and then asked him whether the barrow was for sale. A man mending a net said no and did not look up from the net. A boy running errands between the stage and a yard said no and ran. A woman at a pitch of nothing in particular said no, and then said that the road west had been under water at the low end in the spring and that he should not walk it in a wet week, which was a true thing and not an answer. A man in a barrow-load of chalk said no and put a hand on the side of his own barrow when Tomas Vale's came past, which was not discourtesy and was the most eloquent thing that happened to him all day. A ferryman said no and gave the reason in four seconds: he had to be on the water from the fourth hour to the eighth and if he was not there the crossing was shut, and a man who shuts a crossing is a man who has been asked to do a day's work for nothing. A man with a dog and a gun said no and then asked whether Tomas Vale was going as far as the coach inn, and when he said he was, the man said, *then you will want to be back*, and would not say why, and did not need to.

“**Why not.**”

“**No.**”

“**That is not a reason.**”

“**No it is not.**” The man did not stop walking. “**I have a house and a dog and a trade and I have never once in my life been asked to walk a road for a man who has told me on the first line that he cannot pay me. You are not lying to me and that is the part I have not got an answer to. If you were lying I would have said no and gone, and I would not be standing here.**”

Tomas Vale did not argue with him. He wrote it down that night as the ninth and he wrote down that he had not argued, and he did not write down the reason the man gave, because the reason was the man's and he had not given it away, he had given it at Tomas Vale and Tomas Vale was nine feet behind him within four seconds.

---

At about the sixth hour he stopped asking, and worked out why he had been asking, standing on the flat with his back to a pile of stone, and it took about nine minutes.

**I have been asking for a witness.**

**A thing said twice at ten feet in the open moves about four in ten and dies with the people who heard it, one at a time, over about nine years, and there is no copy of it and nobody can produce it and it is the cheapest thing in this kingdom. A thing written down is read by every clerk who comes after for forty years and can be produced against you by a stranger you have never met. Those are the two instruments I have and I have been choosing between them for a hundred and forty-two days as though they were the only two.**

**Neither of them is a route. A route is a person on foot and the instrument cannot be edited and it costs a day, and that is the whole of it, and it is the only sentence about a warning I have written in four counties and a city that I have not had to take back.**

**And what I have been asking nine people for is a day of their own life. Not a minute. A day. Nine hours walking a road in the wrong direction for a man with a barrow and a paper he will not show them and a thing he wants and no form on which he can be asked for it. About four in ten of those nine would be right to say no and I have got nine out of nine and I am not going to make a better sentence and go back to the market with it, because a better sentence is how a man turns a refusal into a yes, and that is the arranging, and the arranging is the damage, and I have written that sentence three times in three months and it has been one sentence every time.**

He could not pay them. The second clause of a receipt in five clauses said that the frame was not to be put up as consideration for anything, and a man may not use a thing he is carrying on a receipt as a reason why a stranger should give him a day.

**There is no form on which I can pay anybody. There is not one. Not in this county and not in the next one and not in the one I came out of a city in forty-one days ago. So the only thing I have to offer is that I would be truthful about it, and being truthful about a thing is not a currency in this kingdom and has never once been one, and nine people in a morning have told me so in nine ordinary voices and not one of them was sorry.**

---

Then, on the stage, the chain came up.

He was standing on the flat and he turned round because nine or ten men made a noise. The chain came up taut through a ring in a post and three of them took it and set themselves and hauled, and a barge came in against the mud and stopped, and they made it fast and then they all stepped back at once, the way men do, and went and sat down.

Tamsin Rook was sitting on the planks about nine feet from the post with her back against a bollard and her bag on her shoulder and her hands in her lap, and she had been holding that post.

He got up onto the stage. He did not ask anybody what had happened. He put his own hand flat on the post and it was still moving and he took it off again, and she got up on her feet by herself, and she picked up her bag, and she did not say anything, and he did not ask her for a reason, and nobody on that stage asked her for a reason either, and a man carrying a coil of rope about nine feet off looked at her once and then went and coiled something else.

**A chain is a thing she reads. I have known that for eleven years and I did not know until the hundred and twenty-third that I had known it, and I stood on the flat about forty feet off and did not go, and I did not know, and I am not going to write down that I did not know, because I have been a man who is given a thing four seconds after it happens since the hundred and thirty-second and I do not want the sentence.**

---

At about the seventh hour he asked two more people and got two yeses, and one of them had a barrow of his own.

Pell Okell had a barrow and a load of sacking in it and he was eating, and he said yes before Tomas Vale had finished the sentence, and then he said:

“**I will say no to you the rest of the week if you ask me twice, and I am saying yes now, and I do not know why, and I want that written down as though I have thought about it and not as though I have not.**”

“**Why.**”

“**Because I have run that road about nine times and I have never once been asked to say what was on it. And because I have a barrow and you have a barrow, and two men with barrows on one road is arithmetic, and I am the one who knows it is nine miles and you are the one who has been counting.**”

“**I cannot pay you.**”

“**You have told me that four times today and I would have found out on the third mile.**”

And a girl at the edge of the market, sitting on a stone wall with her feet off the ground, who was called Mab Sarter and who was going home.
“**How many fences.**”

“**From here to the end? Three hundred and eleven. I counted them for a man who wanted to know where to put a gate and he did not put a gate there and I have not been paid and I did not say that was a problem, I am just saying I counted them.**”

“**I would want you to walk it.**”

“**I would like to.**” She got off the wall. “**And I am not going to be the one who goes off by herself, because I have watched two people do that on this road and one of them is not here any more, and I am not saying that to be clever, I am saying it because it is the reason. If you walk it, I will count them, and I will tell you when I get to three hundred and eleven, and I have not said yes to the gate man, and I have said yes to you, and those are two different things.**”

**Eleven people asked. Two yes and nine no, and I did not argue with one of the nine, and I have written down the two yeses and not one of the nine noes, because a man who keeps a list of refusals is a man waiting to be owed, and I have been waiting to be owed for a hundred and forty-two days and I am not going to start a book about it on a landing stage.**

**Two is not a group. Two is two people who said yes to a day, in a county where nobody says yes to a day, and I cannot ask either of them for anything again and I am not going to, and if one of them walks away in a week then there is one, and I have no argument against that and I am not going to make one.**

---

At about the eighth hour she was on a step by the water at the bottom of the stage and she wrote her own entry and she filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was, and she wrote the price side the other way up, and the count is eight.

> *The hundred and forty-second, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A landing stage a hundred feet long, ten men on it, a chain, a market of about two thousand on the ground, eleven people asked, and a step by the water at the bottom of it.**
>
> *Second column, and the day. **I read a post and a chain through a ring in it and a barge coming in. I read all of it. I have been reading things on and off for eleven years and I have never once read a thing that was coming for me on purpose, and this one came up the ring and not down the line, and I have been down two streets of noise every day for eleven years and a chain is the one thing I cannot get out of a crowd.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the price, in my own words and about me. **It took the near side. Not all of it. I can still tell a chain from a rope from a gate and I can tell nine people talking from one person talking, and that is what I have left and it is a list and I wrote it out in my own hand a while ago so I would know what was on it.**
>
> *And what it costs, since I am the one who has to pay it and nobody else in this kingdom is going to be told about it by me. **A market of about two thousand is a wall of noise and I have been at the bottom of that wall for a year and I was at the bottom of it today and I could not tell when the barge arrived, and I found out from the deck under my hands and not from the water. I have had to change how I stand.** I go where the ground changes. I go where there is a post, a wall, a rail, a sill, a chain, a door frame, a stone. If there is one of those within about nine feet of me I can put a hand on it and I can put my boots on something hard and I can work out most of a room, and today the post was there and I was slow. **That is the whole of the price and it is not a wound and it is not a story about anybody else and it is not going to get better and nobody is to ask me about it.**
>
> *And nobody asked. **A man coiling rope about nine feet off looked at me once and coiled something else, and a man with a barrow came up onto the stage and put his hand on the post and took it off again and did not ask me one question, and he has not asked me one question in a hundred and forty-two days, including today, when he had a better reason than usual, and I have noticed that and I am not going to do anything about it.**
>
> *Fourth column. **Two lines and I have not improved either and I am not going to.** The slate's price side is the other way up and it is going to stay that way. The count is eight.

---

He wrote his own entry at about the ninth hour of the evening, on a step at the bottom of the stage, with the barrow on the flat above and the four bolts made fast. He filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and forty-second, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A landing stage, ten men, a chain, a market of about two thousand on the ground, eleven people asked, two yes and nine no, and a step by the water.**
>
> *Second column, and the count. **Eleven asked. Two yes. Nine no. I did not argue with one of the nine and I have not written down one of the nine noes, and the reason is four inches long and it is this: a man who keeps a record of what people have refused him is a man building a case, and I have not got a case.** The two are a carter with a barrow of sacking in it who said yes before I had finished the sentence and then asked me to write down that he had thought about it, and a girl on a wall who has counted three hundred and eleven fences for a man who did not put a gate where she counted them. **Two is not a group. Two is two people who said yes to a day, in a county where nobody says yes to a day.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the thing I found out at the sixth hour standing on a flat with my back to a pile of stone. **I have been asking for a witness.** A thing said at ten feet in the open goes into the ears of whoever is standing there and there is no copy of it anywhere afterwards, and the people who heard it go off it one at a time, and a thing written down goes into a drawer and a stranger I have never met can take it out of that drawer in about forty years and read it to a room. **Those are the two instruments I own and I have been choosing between them as though they were the only two, and I have had both of them for a hundred and forty-two days and I have got nine hundred words in a market and about four hundred sentences a year on a wall and not one of them has ever been anywhere. Neither of those is a route. A route is a person on foot. It cannot be edited and it costs a day, and there is nothing else to say about it that I have not said already and had to take back.**
>
> *And the nine. **What I asked them for was nine hours of their own life, for a man who told them on the first line that he could not pay them.** About four in ten of those nine would be right to say no, and I got nine out of nine, and I am not going back into that market with a better sentence. A better sentence is how a man turns a refusal into a yes. **And that is the arranging, and the arranging is the damage, and it is the same sentence I wrote in the hundred and sixteenth and again in the hundred and twenty-ninth, and it has been one sentence in three months.** The one who stopped and explained himself said that if I had been lying to him he would have said no and gone, and it is the truest thing anybody has said to me in six days, and he is right, and I did not argue with him and I did not follow him.
>
> *And I have not got a list of the nine and I am not going to write one. **Nine refusals in a morning are not owed to me by nine people, they are the shape of a morning, and a man who keeps the refusals is a man building a case and I have not got a case.** I have written down two names and a carter and a girl and the arithmetic, and the arithmetic is a mile a day and a day is nine hours and I am asking for nine hours from a stranger about four times a day, and it is nobody's business to give me that except mine.
>
> *And the post on the stage, and the chain, and the barge. **I was standing about forty feet off and I did not go, and she was on the planks afterwards with her back against a bollard and her bag on her shoulder, and I put my own hand on the post and it was still moving and I took it off again, and she got up by herself, and nobody on that stage asked her for a reason and I did not ask her for a reason, and I have not asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and forty-two days and I am not going to start tonight.** She wrote her own entry about it in her own hand and she did not read it to me and I did not ask to see it. **She has less than she had ten days ago and it is hers and she has priced it and nobody is going to be told about it by me.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a landing stage is a hundred feet of plank and nine or ten men and it is not a building.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, sixty-five days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is where it has been since before we left Kells.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and two yeses and no list, and not one of them is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and the two who said yes will be able to walk away in a week and I am not going to make it awkward for them if they do.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a stage, a chain, a ring in a post, ten men who make a noise and then sit down, a market of about two thousand on the ground where the cheapest instrument in this kingdom has been standing all day doing nothing, and a man with a barrow asking strangers for a day of their lives.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A route is a person on foot and it cannot be edited and it costs a day, and a true sentence is the smallest thing anybody can carry, and I have two people who will carry one for me and no form on which either of them owes me anything.**
