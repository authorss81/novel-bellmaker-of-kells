# Chapter 0175 — The District Clerk Mending A Shelf

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and sixty-first day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on cinders outside a district store, and the store was stone with a counter window and a yard beside it stacked with posts and wire.

The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and twenty-eight days, and cinders and a yard are not buildings.

---

At about the fifth hour a clerk came out with a hammer and a handful of nails and began mending the shelf by the counter window, where a board had sagged. He was a thin man with ink on his cuffs, and he worked badly, tapping too light, bending two nails.

Pell watched with a carter's patience. "You want it propped under, not nailed through," he said at last. "Nail through split wood only splits it."

The clerk stopped. "It holds paper."

"It holds nothing if the board is rotten." Pell set his barrow down. "Give me the hammer a minute. I do this on carts."

The clerk gave it over. Pell knocked the board out, turned it, propped it on a block, nailed it clean in four strokes, handed the hammer back.

"Four strokes," the clerk said. "I took twenty." He went inside and came back with a return book open in his hands, still doing two things at once, and sat on the step to rule a line while the light was good.

At about the sixth hour Tomas, standing by with his barrow, looked without asking at what the clerk was ruling. It was a return about a thing in a ward: headings, numbers, columns of sacks and loads and days. At the foot, a column of places entered not as dwellings but as work: ford gang, market crew, landing shed, stone gang, and five more in the same hand.

"That is a long list for one ward," Tomas said.

The clerk did not look up. "Nine places. All work. Ford, market, landing, stone, timber, repair, ferry, store, ditch. Each with mouths against it."

Tomas said nothing. He had learned not to take the same words to two people, and he was not going to ask this man for anything. He waited while the clerk ruled.

Mab stood by the yard fence. "No fences in the yard," she said quietly. "Posts, not fences. I am not counting posts."

Ned looked at the stack of posts. "Good timber," he said. "Straight. That shed will stand."

The clerk finished his line, blotted it, shut the book. "Shelf holds now," he said to Pell. "That is yours."

Pell shook his head. "No thanks in it. You held the board and I held the hammer. That is work."

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on cinders beyond the yard with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast.

> *The hundred and sixty-first, at about the ninth hour of the evening. Cinders, a stone store, a counter window, a mended shelf, a yard, a flat.*
>
> *Second column. A shelf propped and nailed in four strokes by a carter. A return about a thing in a ward with nine places in it, each set down as work and each with mouths against it. Ford gang, market crew, landing shed, and six more in the same hand.*
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. Ruling: nine places can stand in one book and still not be places anyone lives in, if the heading wants work.*
>
> *All five clauses stand. Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and the warrant stands. Four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third. The carrier in the sack, not rung, eighty-four days from the seventy-seventh, and I have not put my hand on it and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour, including me.*
>
> *The register is thirteen. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today.*
>
> *Which office is it. It is a counter window, a shelf, and a return with nine lines of work in it.*
