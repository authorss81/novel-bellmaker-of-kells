# Chapter 0154 — A Smith Who Says What A Cut Leaves And Refuses The Theory

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and fortieth day and Tomas Vale had the barrow in a lane off a crossroads and the four bolts were made fast, and a smithy stood at the end of the lane with its door open and a fire going in it in the middle of a morning, and the man at the fire was working a piece of iron about nine inches long and did not stop.

He was Tib Neave. He had fitted Crown work to three bells in this county and he had not fitted a fourth, and Tomas Vale had established both of those things in about four minutes at a door and the man had answered neither of them.

“**You are the maker.**”

“**I am not the maker of anything.**”

“**You are the keeper of the thing under the oilcloth.**”

“**I am not that either. I am a man at a fire in a lane and I have about nine inches of iron to finish before the fire is wrong, and you can stand in the lane and I will talk to you and I will not stop.**”

---

At about the fifth hour he asked about the cut.

“**You know what a cut from above leaves and what a cut from the side leaves.**”

“**I know what a cut from above leaves and what a cut from the side leaves, and that is the only reason you are talking to me and I have had it said to me four times in this county and twice in the last fortnight.**”

“**The cut at the top of the bank, at the ford.**”

“**Then look at the underside of it.**”

“**There is a burr.**”

“**There is a burr.**” He turned the iron over and did not look up. “**That is a thing a tool leaves. A man does not leave a burr, a man leaves a mess. So whatever made that cut was done with something and held with something, and it was done from above, and it was not done by somebody in a hurry, and that is four things and I have given you all four and I am not going to give you a fifth.**

“**If you were to ask me who.**”

“**I am not going to ask you who.**”

“**You were going to ask me what I think and I said no four seconds ago, and I am saying it again now so that you have it twice and you cannot come back and tell anybody I only said it once.**”

He put the iron in the fire.

“**There. That is a smith's whole trade. A cut has a shape and a shape has a maker and the maker is not the interesting part.**”

**I had a theory. I had it about four seconds before he took it off me and I have had it since the hundred and thirty-first, in a trench, about four streets off a gate, with a lamp in it, and I have not written it down and I was not going to and a man at a fire told me not to in four seconds and he was right to and I am not going to argue with a man about a thing I cannot put on a page anyway.**

---

At about the sixth hour he asked the one that is allowed.

“**Where does that kind of work come from.**”

Tib Neave said it in about four seconds, in the ordinary voice, to the fire.

“**Out of a store. There is a store in every district and it is a room with a counter and a book and a man and a shelf, and a man brings an instruction with a number on it and a district and a day, and the man goes along the shelf and gives him the thing and writes down what went out and the number and the district and the day, and the number is a filing reference and not a man.**”

“**And the instruction.**”

“**A district's.**”

“**And the thing itself. What is it.**”

“**A coupling, and a jumper across it where the coupling has been cut nearly through, and a bracket at the top for the wire. It is not the bell's own and it is not a part of the bell. It is a thing that is fitted to a bell and it is not the bell and it is not what makes the bell sound.**” He took the iron out and put it on the anvil. “**And I have said the three words that are true about it and I am not going to say the fourth, which is what it does, because I do not know what it does and neither does the man at the counter and he has a book about it and he does not have me to ask.**”

“**Then what is it for.**”

“**That is the fourth word and you have had three.**”

---

Then he said a thing about himself that Tomas Vale did not ask for, and it is the reason the hundred and fortieth is worth a page.

“**I have mended one.**”

“**Your own.**”

“**My own, in my own loft, on my own account, on his day off, with a piece I bought myself, and it is the best work I have ever done and I will not do it again.**”

“**Why not.**”

“**Because a fault nobody can see is a fault somebody asks you about one morning with a reason in their hand, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man may stand at a counter and say *I have mended my own and here it is*, and a man who says it is a man who has put himself in a column, and a thing in a column is not a thing anybody owns after that.**”
He turned the iron a quarter turn and hit it once.

“**So I will do a fortnight of work to a thing a stranger can point at, and it will be worse, and it will last, and nobody will ever ask me about it. That is the trade and it is not an opinion and I am not going to discuss it with a man with a barrow who has come four days down a road for something else.**”

**And that is the ninth man in four counties and a city who has told me the shape of the thing while owing me nothing at all, and it is the first time I have walked away from a smithy with more than I came into it and with no name in my book, and a name in my book would have been the whole of the damage, and he is right and he is not a door and I am not going to thank him and I am not going to be the man who tells him what he is.**

**A service cut and a bypass is a road and not a claim. He did not say it and I am not going to put it in his mouth. A road does not know who is standing on it, and I have written that sentence in my own book four times now and every time I have written it I was one day nearer to writing down the reason, and a smith at a fire has just told me what a reason costs and it is not nine days of somebody else's work, it is never doing the good work twice.**

---

At about the seventh hour he offered the smith nothing and the smith asked him for nothing, and then Tomas Vale said the thing he had come to say, which is that he would come back in a fortnight with a second pair of hands and would not be asking the smith to say a word about who cut anything.

Tib Neave put the iron down.

“**You are going to ask me to put my hands on a district's line.**”

“**I am going to ask you to put your hands on a road that has been cut open, and to tell me what your hands find, and to say it to me and to nobody else, and if you do not want to say it to me then you will not.**”

“**And if I find nothing.**”

“**Then you will have lost a morning and I will have lost a morning and neither of us will have a column.**”

“**That is a bad arrangement for me.**”

“**It is a bad arrangement for both of us. It is the only arrangement in this kingdom in which neither of us ends up in a column and there is no form for it and I have no standing to offer you one.**”

The smith picked the iron up again and did not say anything for about four seconds.

“**The store is four days' walk from here and I have been to it nine times in as many years and it is a room with a counter in it and the man behind the counter has never once told me who held the instruction, and I have never once asked him, and I am not going to now, and if you go and ask him you are a man from a town four days east with a barrow and no office behind him and he will be kind to you the way he is kind to everybody and he will tell you a number.**”

“**Then I will get a number.**”

“**Then you will get a number. There is no hand in it. Nobody will ever tell you the hand, and not because anybody is protecting anybody, and I have thought about that for a long time and I do not have an answer and I am not a man who has answers.**”

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a bank at the crossroads with the barrow below him on the flat and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and fortieth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and fortieth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A lane, a smithy, a fire in the middle of a morning, nine inches of iron, a burr, and about five of the nine miles behind me and about four in front of me, and four days spent on a road of nine miles.**
>
> *Second column, and the cut. **A burr is a thing a tool leaves and a man does not leave a burr, a man leaves a mess.** Four seconds, and I had a theory four seconds before he took it off me, and he said no twice so that I could not go away and say he only said it once. **A coupling, a jumper across a cut that has gone nearly through, and a bracket at the top for the wire. Not the bell's own and not a part of the bell, and fitted to a bell, and he gave me three words that are true about it and would not give the fourth, which is what it does, because he does not know and the man at the counter does not know and there is a book on the counter about it.** A store in every district: a room, a counter, a book, a man, a shelf, an instruction with a number, a district and a day. **The number is a filing reference and not a man, and that is the fifth time it has been said to me and the fifth time it has been said by a man who is right and owed nothing.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the two things I did not expect. **He has mended one, his own, in his own loft, on his day off, with a piece he bought himself, and he called it the best work he has ever done, and he is not going to do it again.** The reason he gave was not honour and it was not the trade. It was that a mend you cannot see is a mend somebody turns up one morning with a paper in their hand, **and there is nowhere in this kingdom to stand at a counter and put your own mend on the counter and say so, and a man who does that has put himself inside a column, and a thing in a column is not a thing anybody owns after that.** So he will do a fortnight of work to a thing a stranger can point at, and it will be worse than the mend, and it will last, and nobody is ever going to ask him about that either. **He said not one word about a service cut or a bypass. I am not going to put words in a smith's mouth and I am not going to take any out of them. A road does not know who is standing on it, and I have written that in this book four times, and every time I have written it I was a day nearer to writing down the reason, and what a reason costs is not nine days of somebody else's work, it is never doing the good work twice.**
>
> *And what I have walked away with. **More than I came in with and no name in this book, and a fortnight to go back with a second pair of hands and no question attached to it, and a store four days' walk west with a counter in it, and a man behind it who will be kind to me and give me a number and never the hand.** Nobody will ever tell me the hand and it is not because anybody is protecting anybody.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a lane, a smithy door, a fire and a bank are not buildings, and I stood in the doorway of the smithy and the frame was forty feet off me in a lane and it did not go in.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, sixty-three days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag, and she was ten feet off my left shoulder in the lane for about an hour while a man told me the shape of a thing he was not asked about, and she has not said what this road is for and I have not asked her and nobody has asked her for a reason.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and three words about a coupling and a name I have not written down, and a number that is a filing reference and not a man, and not one of them is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing today, and a name for a thing in a smithy is not a name in my book, and that is the only decision I made today and I made it in about four seconds because a man at a fire took it off me first.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a room with a counter in it, a book, a shelf, an instruction with a number on it, a man who is kind, and nine words in a column that want to know what a thing is for and are empty from end to end in every county I have been in.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A coupling, a jumper and a bracket, and not the bell's own, and nobody in this kingdom can tell me what it does, and a man at a fire who has mended his own work once and will not do it again, and a store four days' walk west where a number is a filing reference and not a man.**
