# Chapter 0159 — Is The Road To The Coach Inn Open, And The Answer Is Yes And Has Been For Years

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and forty-fifth day and Tomas Vale left the barrow on the landing with the four bolts made fast and went up the outside stair into the room with the bench along one wall, and there were about nine people in it and about two hundred of them were in a market on the ground underneath, standing on the same level with nothing between them and nothing above them, and the frame stayed on the landing.

He did not put the schedule on the table. He had thought about it for about four minutes on the stair and he had decided against it, and the reason was not a rule and was not a habit: a sheet on a bench's table is a thing the bench has to answer, and he had nothing to stand behind it with.

---

At about the fifth hour he asked the question, in the ordinary voice, to the room and not to anybody in it.

“**Is the road to the coach inn above the ford open.**”

The man who hauled at that landing did not get up off the wall and answered it in about four seconds.

“**It is open.**”

“**Since when.**”

“**Since always.**” He said it the way a man says a thing he has been asked variants of for a long time. “**It is open now and it was open the day I started at this landing and my father hauled up to it before that. There is no date to it. It is a road. It goes from this landing nine miles to a coaching inn with a yard behind it and carts go up it four days a week and go down it four days a week, and it has not been shut in my working life except for the fortnight about two years ago, and that was not the road, that was a bridge.**”

“**It is not on any sheet.**”

“**It is not on any sheet I have ever seen, and I have seen a good many, and I will tell you the reason and then I am going back to my wall. It is not on a sheet because the sheet is a district's and the road is nine miles long and about four of those nine are in the next district, and a district draws the bit that is its own and stops at its own edge and does not go and ask, and the four miles that are not on the sheet are not hidden. They are just not anybody's business to draw.**”

He put his head back against the wall and that was the end of his contribution and he was right about all of it.

“**You are not sorry.**”

“**No.**”

“**I am not going to thank you.**”

“**Good, because I did not do it for you and I would have been embarrassed.**”

---

Then he asked the second man the same question in the same words, standing in the same place, in the ordinary voice, and the second man took about four seconds as well and gave him the same answer in different words.

“**Open.**”

“**All the way.**”

“**All the way, and it is not a road anybody has to open. It is a road.**”

And Tomas Vale wrote both of them down, in the same hand, one under the other, and did not improve either, and did not ask a third time, and asked nobody in the room to do anything at all.

---

At about the sixth hour four people said four different things about the same nine miles, and none of them agreed with any of the others, and about four in ten of what each of them said was right, and Tomas Vale had worked out in the fourth minute of it that he was not going to get them any closer than that in a day and was not going to try.

Aman Petch, who hauled: **there is a place about the third mile where the road goes along a wall on your right for about a hundred yards, and it is the only hundred yards of that road with a wall on it, and if you are going in a cart you can see the top of the wall from about half a mile back and if you are on foot you cannot see it at all until you are against it.**

Nieve Ossick, who kept the ford tally: **it goes under the water twice a year and it has gone under three times in the twelve years she has kept the tally, and the first time is always the one that catches people, and the reason is that the first time is in the ordinary season and nobody is watching the river.**

Corin Nook, a lighterman, who had done the run twice in a day and would not say so again: **it is a cart road and not a foot road, and nine miles of it is a day for a loaded cart and it is not a day for a man on foot with nothing, and the third mile is the one that is slow, and there is a hedge on the left at the third mile that a man walking will be in and out of about nine times.**

Ottilie Brane, who kept a book of hires at the staging and who had a reason for every word she said: **it was shut for a fortnight about two years ago and I have it in my book, and the reason in my book is that a man was hurt on it, and the man was not hurt by the road. There was a dispute at a gate on the second day and the district shut the road while it was settled. I have the date and I have the day of the week in my book and I am not going to say either of them out loud in a room, and that is my own rule and it is not a Crown rule.**

“**Why not.**”

“**Because a date in my mouth in a room of nine people is a date in nine mouths by the end of a week and a hire book is the only thing in this building that nobody has ever been asked to produce for anybody.**”

**She is right. A date is a thing that can be checked and I have a warrant behind me that is a copy of a sound, and I know exactly what a date does when four people have it, and I have been the ninth of that four times and I have never once been asked.**

---

At about the seventh hour he asked the question he had come two days' road to ask, and it was not about the road, and the room answered it in about four seconds as well.

“**There is a line under that road. It was cut in the spring, from above, and there is a notice on a stake at the top of the bank and the notice says *wires*. Has it ever been put back.**”

Nobody in the room moved.

“**No.**”

“**Has anybody asked what it was for.**”

“**No.**” The woman with the hire book said it. “**The district had the road open in the spring and put a notice on a stake and the notice has said *wires* since the hundred and sixth, and I go up past that stake about nine days out of ten, and I have read it about nine hundred times, and it says *wires*, and it does not say whose wires they are, and nobody has ever come up and asked me what it was for, and I have told two people in four years that I do not know and I do not know.**”

**The same word. A stake in this county and a stake in a city of about nine thousand people, and the same day, and I have a page in my own book with a day on it and a date is a thing I can check, and I am not going to write this down in the same place as the other one, and the reason is that a word on a stake is the cheapest notice there is and every district in this kingdom has got one and they all say *wires* because that is the correct word for it and not for any other reason. Two, and I am not going to make a shape out of two.**

“**What should happen to that line.**”

And the whole room said nothing, and then four of them said, in about nine seconds, in four different voices, that nothing should.

“**It is a road that has been cut and the cut is at the side of a road and it is a road.**” The hauler had not moved his head off the wall. “**I have been on it when it was whole and I have been on it now it is not, and I can tell you that nothing in my trade has changed, and I am not going to be one of nine people in a room who decides that a thing other people use should be put back because a man with a barrow asked.**”

“**Then what is the point of a notice.**”

“**The point of a notice is that it is there.**” The woman with the hire book shut her book. “**Nobody wants the line back. I want it said in this room that I do not want the line back, because in about four years there will be a version of this conversation in a market and somebody will say I wanted a road cut and I will not have said it. It was cut in the spring by the district and it was cut for a reason the district has and nobody has asked about and I am not asking either, and I would like it entered in a book that I do not have a copy of, that nobody in this room wants it put back.**”

“**Who will enter it.**”

“**Nobody. That is why I said it.**”

---

He got up and went down the outside stair and stood on the landing, and it was the seventh hour, and the four bolts were made fast, and the market of about two hundred on the ground was finishing.

He had come to this room with one question and he had got a true answer to it in about four seconds and it did not help him at all, and the reason it did not help him was that a road which is open is a fact about the people who open it and a road which is on a sheet is a fact about a piece of paper, and those are two different objects and he had been holding one of them in his coat for four days and asking it about the other one.

**And the panel. The fifth panel of a district's road sheet, the one for the district above the coach inn, gone from every copy in this county in a rectangle about two hands together, and the road is open, and open and registered are two different things and I cannot tell which of the two I am looking at, and I have not found out and I am not going to find out this week, and the man who told me the road was open told me the reason it is not on a sheet in the same four seconds, and the reason is that a district draws its own edge and stops, and that is correct, and I have been carrying a rectangle for four days as though a rectangle were a hole in a road and it is not a hole in a road. It is a hole in a drawing and the road is fine.**

He did not finish that thought and he wrote it down unfinished, which is not a habit and is the only honest thing available.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step by the water with the barrow on the landing behind him and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and forty-fifth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and forty-fifth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **An outside stair, a bench, about nine people, a table, a book, a market of about two hundred on the ground, a boundary stone, a toll bar, a notice on a stake, and a step by the water.**
>
> *Second column, and the answer, and it took four seconds. **The road to the coach inn above the ford is open. It has always been open. It is open now and it is not on any sheet anybody in that room has ever seen, and the reason is that a district draws the edge of its own ground and stops and does not go and ask, and the four of nine miles that are not on the sheet are not hidden, they are just not anybody's business to draw.** I asked the second man the same question in the same words in the same place and he gave me the same answer in different words, and I wrote both down and improved neither, and I did not ask a third time and I did not ask anybody in that room to do anything. **The man who told me was right and was not sorry and I did not thank him and he said he would have been embarrassed.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the four. **A hundred yards of wall on the right at the third mile, visible from half a mile back in a cart and not at all on foot. The road goes under the water twice a year and has gone under three times in twelve years, and the first time is always the one that catches people. It is a cart road and not a foot road and the third mile is the slow one and there is a hedge on the left a man walking will be in and out of about nine times.** Four people, four things, and about four in ten of what each of them said is right, and none of them agrees with any of the others, and I am not going to improve any of the four, and I am not going back in there with a better question.
>
> *And the line. **It has not been put back and nobody has ever asked what it was for, and the notice has said *wires* since the hundred and sixth, and when I asked the room what should happen to it four people in nine seconds said that nothing should, and one of them asked for that to be entered in a book she does not have a copy of, and nobody is going to enter it, and she said so first.** So: **nobody in that room wants the line back. I have written that down in the order it happened and it is the only sentence I have got today that anybody else is standing behind.**
>
> *And the thing I did not finish. **Open and registered are two different things and I cannot tell which one I am looking at.** The panel for the district above that inn is gone from every copy in this county in a rectangle about two hands together. **The road is open. A rectangle is not a hole in a road; it is a hole in a drawing, and the road underneath it is doing perfectly well without me.** I got that far and I did not get the rest of it and I have written it down unfinished on purpose, and there is a limit to how much a man may find out in a day in a room where he is owed nothing, and I have been in nine rooms this month and been owed nothing in all of them.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is on a landing and it did not go up the stair today and I did not put the schedule on the bench's table and I thought about it for about four minutes on the stair and decided against it, because a sheet on a bench's table is a thing the bench has to answer and I have nothing to stand behind it with.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, sixty-eight days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag, and she was on the landing and in the market and nobody asked her for a reason.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and four sentences in this room in about nine hundred words, and not one of them is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked, and a man with his head against a wall told me what a road is for nothing and would have been embarrassed if I had.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a bench that has been sitting two days about nine miles of road that has never been shut, a man who hauls, a woman who keeps a tally, a lighterman, a woman with a hire book that nobody has ever asked her to produce, a boundary stone that is not a boundary, a toll bar that was taken down, and a notice on a stake that says *wires* and has said it since the hundred and sixth.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A true sentence is the smallest thing anybody in this kingdom can carry, and I have four of them, out of four mouths that do not agree with each other, and four sentences is a route and not a coalition, and I have to walk all of it.**
