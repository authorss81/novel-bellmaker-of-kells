# Chapter 0157 — A Woman Who Keeps About Nine Hundred Names And Has Never Been Asked What It Is

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and forty-third day and Tomas Vale had the barrow on the landing at the foot of a stair, with the four bolts made fast, and the stair went up the outside of a building to a door that was open, and a woman was sitting at a table by that door with a book open in front of her.

Her name was Bel Tremayne.

The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and forty-three days. **A landing is not a building and a stair on the outside of a building is not a building and the barrow is on the landing and it is going to stay on the landing, and I did not go up that stair with anything in my hands.**

---

She was about four years into the book. He could see that from the doorway, because a book that has been kept four years has a shape and a book that has been kept one year has a different shape, and he is a man who reads things he is not going to touch.

Three columns on a page and about nine hundred names in it and the writing was small and even and it was going down the page in the same hand the whole way.

He did not go in.

“**I am not going to ask you what the book is.**”

She did not look up.

“**You are standing on my landing with a barrow and I can see the corner of a paper in your coat, and you have said the words out loud, which is either very honest or very stupid, and I have not decided which and I am not going to.**”

“**It is honest.**”

“**I did not ask.**”

---

At about the fifth hour he asked his one question, and it was about the road and not about the sheet, and he had worked out about nine minutes before he asked it that this was the only question he had a right to ask in this room.

“**The road that goes west from the staging, about nine miles, to the coach inn above the ford. Is it one road or is it two.**”

“**It is one road.**”

“**All the way.**”

“**It is one road and it has been one road since before the staging was built, and there is a bend in it at the third mile that everybody calls a turn and it is not a turn, it is a bend, and there is a place where it goes along a wall for about a hundred yards and the wall is on your right going west and it is the only hundred yards of that road with a wall on it.**”

“**That is about nine hundred yards of useful information and I have got it from a woman I have known for four minutes.**”

“**You have got it from a woman who has lived over this landing four years.**” She turned a page over and did not go back. “**And I have never once been asked about the road. Everybody who comes up this stair has wanted the book and not one of them in four years has asked me about the road, and you are the first, and I am going to answer you about the road for as long as you keep asking about the road.**”

**A road is the only thing anybody has ever asked her about that she could answer. She said that without being asked and she said it in the ordinary voice, and she is right, and I did not ask her for the reason and I am not going to.**

---

Tamsin Rook was about nine feet off the landing at the time, with her bag on her shoulder, and the two women looked at each other for about four seconds.

Neither of them said anything. **That is the whole of what happened. Two women on a landing, one of them deaf and one of them with nine hundred names in a book, and four seconds, and no sentence, and I did not find out one single thing about either of them from it and I am not going to write down what I thought I saw, because a man who describes what he thought he saw in a four-second look between two other people is doing it for himself.**

---

At about the sixth hour she told him the thing that took the rest of the afternoon, and she told him because he asked about the road and not because he asked for this.

“**There is another book like this one.**”

“**Where.**”

“**Four days' road back, in the other direction, in a town you have been in.**” She said it flatly. “**I know about it because a man came through here about two years ago who had come from that way and he told me there was a woman in a town up the river with a book the same shape as mine, and he said she had about nine hundred names in it too, and I have never seen hers and she has never seen mine, and neither of us has ever sent a line to the other, and there is no form in this kingdom on which two women who both keep a book may put one book beside the other.**”

**And it is not her. It is a woman four days' road back, on a stair, in a town I have not been in for a hundred and forty-three days, who kept a sheet on the hundred and ninth that she would not give me and did not explain, and she is not in this room and she is not owed a visit and I have not gone and I am not going to. And the woman on this landing has never met her and has never heard of her, and the two of them do the same work in the same three columns in the same way, because it is a trade, and that is a fact about a trade and not about either of them.**

**And nobody has ever compared notes with anybody. That is the second time in three days I have found that out about something and I am beginning to think it is not a coincidence in the world. It is a fact about having no form.**

---

At about the seventh hour he worked out the number and it took him about four minutes and it is the finding of the hundred and forty-third day.

**About nine hundred names in that book. About nine hundred bells in the city I came out of forty-two days ago. About nine hundred entries on nine sheets in a book behind a counter. About nine hundred matters a year into a room with a lamp in it.**

**Four nines. And I have been using the figure for two different things for a hundred and forty-three days and I did not know I was doing it.**

**A bell is a thing. A name is a person. A list of nine hundred bells is a list of nine hundred objects that somebody made and somebody owns and somebody has to store, and a list of nine hundred names is a list of nine hundred people who were in a room and owed money and went out of a door. None of them is a quantity of anything, and there is not going to be a fifth, and I have written that down at the foot of a hundred and thirty-six days and I am writing it again in a different county on a landing, and it is still true and it is still four.**

**And there is no form in this kingdom that says which of them is which.** A form will tell you about a ward, a day, a thing and a ground, in four boxes, and about nine hundred of them a year come off one plate. **It will not tell you whether the thing in the third box is a thing or a man, and if it did, the book on this table and the list behind that counter would be the same document, and they are not the same document and a woman upstairs and a man behind a counter four days' road away have never heard of each other and neither of them owes the other anything.**

---

He did not ask the second question.

It was this: of the about nine hundred in that book, how many were still in this town, and how she knew, and whether anybody had ever asked her to write it down for somebody else.

He had it in his mouth at about the seventh hour and he did not put it there, and she saw him not do it, because she has been watching people for four years.

“**You had one more.**”

“**I did.**”

“**It was about the book.**”

“**It was.**”

“**I am not going to help you with it and you were not going to ask it and we both know which of us worked harder.**”

“**You did.**”

“**I have four years of a practice at not answering things and you have five days of one at not asking them, and mine is better, and I am not going to say that to be unkind. I am going to shut the book now and you are going to go down my stair, and I have not been asked what this is for in four years and I am not going to be asked it by a man with a barrow and a paper he will not show me, and that is not because of the barrow. It is because you would be asking me so that you could put it in something.**”

She shut the book.

“**There is a room at the top of this building where nine people sit when they sit, and they have been sitting two days, and they are not officers and they are not a court and they are not going to do anything for you. You can go up and ask them a thing about a road and they will answer you. They will not carry a sentence you cannot certify, and neither will I, and you should know that before you spend a day of their time and a day of your own.**”

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step at the bottom of the landing with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and forty-third and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and forty-third, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A landing, an outside stair, a door, a woman at a table with a book of about nine hundred names in three columns, a wall on the right at the third mile, and a step at the bottom of the landing.**
>
> *Second column, and the road. **One road, all the way, and it has been one road since before the staging was built. A bend at the third mile that everybody calls a turn. About a hundred yards with a wall on the right going west, and it is the only hundred yards of that road with a wall on it.** Bel Tremayne gave me nine hundred yards of it for nothing, in about a minute, and then she said the thing: **she has kept a book four years and nobody in four years has ever asked her about the road, and I am the first, and she will answer me about the road for as long as I keep asking about the road.** And then she told me about the other one: **a second book, four days' road back, in another woman's hand, about as many names in it as in hers, and the two of them have never seen each other's, and there is no form in this kingdom on which two women who keep a book can put one in front of the other, which is why nobody ever does.** **Nobody has ever compared notes with anybody. That is the second time in three days and I have begun to think it is not a coincidence in the world, it is a fact about having no form.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the number, which is the whole reason this day is in this book. **Four nines, told apart by which room they are in: about nine hundred names in a book over a landing, about nine hundred bells in a city, about nine hundred entries on nine sheets behind a counter, about nine hundred matters a year into a room with a lamp. I have been using one figure for two different kinds of thing for a hundred and forty-three days and I did not know I was doing it, and I found it out at the seventh hour in a room over a landing because somebody counted.** A bell is a thing and a name is a person, and a list of bells is nine hundred objects that somebody made and somebody owns and somebody has to store, and a list of names is nine hundred people who were in a room and owed money and went out of a door. **There is no form in this kingdom that says which is which. A form has four boxes and the third one is a thing and it does not say whether the thing is a thing or a man, and I have wanted a form that said so since the hundred and twenty-fourth and there is not one, and there is not going to be one, and that is not because anybody is protecting anybody.**
>
> *And what I did not ask. **I had a second question and it was about her book and I did not ask it and I did not put it in my mouth, and she saw me not do it and told me she was not going to help me with it, and then she said that we both know which of us worked harder and hers is four years and mine is five days.** Then she told me about the room at the top of that building where nine people sit when they sit, and that they have been sitting two days, and that they will answer me a question about a road, and that **they will not carry a sentence I cannot certify, and neither will she, and she told me that before I spend a day of their time and a day of my own.** I did not go up tonight. I am not going up tonight because I have nothing to certify and there is no form on which a man with a barrow may be believed at anybody's desk, and being believed in this kingdom is worth exactly nothing.
>
> *And four seconds. **Two women looked at each other on that landing for about four seconds and neither of them said anything, and that is the whole of it, and I am not going to write down what I thought I saw.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is on a landing at the foot of an outside stair and I did not go up the stair, and a landing is not a building.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, sixty-six days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am not going to it, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag, and she was on that landing the whole time and nobody asked her for a reason.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and a book I did not touch and did not go in for and did not ask about, and not one of them is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing from that room, and I have not thanked the woman and I am not going to, because she did nine hundred yards of road for nothing and she is not an ally and she has not joined me and she is not going to.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a woman at a table with three columns and nine hundred names in them, and a list behind a counter with four columns and nine hundred rows on nine sheets, and a man who enters a matter in a column in about four seconds, and about nine hundred bells that go at an hour, and a court list of about nine hundred a year, and not one form anywhere in this kingdom that says which of the four is a thing and which is a person.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A list of objects and a list of people are two different documents and neither of the women who keep them has ever been asked to produce one for anybody else, and a bench nine feet above me has been sitting two days and will not carry a sentence I cannot certify, and I have nothing to certify.**
