# Chapter 0163 — A Bypass Is A Road And Not A Claim And The Households Above The Cut Are The Ones That Lose A Voice

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and forty-ninth day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on a flat at the bottom of a bank, and there was a new road on the top of the bank, about half a mile of it, newly metalled, with a ditch cut along its inside edge and the stone stacked at the side in the way stone is stacked by men who intend to come back.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and sixteen days, and a flat, a bank, a ditch and half a mile of new road are not buildings, and I have not been under this bank once.**

---

At about the fifth hour Pell Okell got to the bottom of it and looked up, and said the only thing anybody says about a bypass in about four seconds, which is what it is for.

“**So a loaded cart can get through.**”

“**Can it.**”

“**That is what it is for and that is the whole of it, and I have said it about four of them.**” He put the barrow down. “**There is a bridge at the fourth mile and it will not take a loaded cart and has not for long enough that the inn sends nothing over it, and the inn sends four carts a week down the old road, and a cart that cannot get through a bridge gets round, and that is a bypass. It is not a thing anybody did to anybody. It is a way round.**”

“**Who laid it.**”

“**I do not know and I would not have said if I did. It wants a district for it and the district laid it, and a man who tells you a name in a lane like this is trying to be the ninth of something.**”

“**Is the old road shut.**”

“**No. Nothing is shut. That is the part nobody will tell you and it is the part that matters.**”

---

At about the sixth hour a woman came out of a door under the bank with a child’s coat over her arm and said the thing, and she said it to Pell Okell and not to Tomas Vale, which was correct, and he heard it from about nine feet off.

“**You have come up the new one.**”

“**I have.**”

“**Then you have not come down here.**”

“**I have not.**”

“**That is right and it is not anybody's fault and nobody has done anything wrong.**” She shifted the coat. “**There was a man used to mend the road. I do not know what you call him, he did not mend it every day, he came past about twice a year and there was always a soft place and he filled it and he did it in about a day and he did not want money and there was nowhere to put money, and I have never once had to say out loud to a man that a thing on this road was bad, because I used to be able to put my hand on his barrow as he went past and point at it.**”

“**And now.**”

“**And now the soft place at the bottom of the bank by the ash is nine feet across and I have put a board over it and I have moved the gate so that nothing goes in at night, and I have done both of those myself, and I am not complaining to you, and I would like it understood that the man is not gone.**”

“**Where has he gone.**”

“**He is where he has always been. He is paid for the new one, because the new one is the district's, and the district's is the one that has a name on it and can be put in a return, and the nine miles underneath us has not got a name on it that I have ever seen, and a man does not mend a road he cannot write down.**”

Pell Okell did not say a word. He picked his barrow up and got out of the lane, which was the correct thing to do and was the first time Tomas Vale had seen a carting man make room.

---

At about the seventh hour he went up the bank and looked at the stake, and the stake had one word nailed to it and a line under it in a smaller hand that he could not read at that distance, and he read the big word, and he did not ask anybody what the small line said, and he wrote down in his own book that he had not asked.

Then he stood on the new road with the barrow on the flat below and worked out the whole of a hundred and forty-nine days in about nine minutes, and it is the finding of the hundred and forty-ninth.

**A bypass is a road. A service cut is a road. And a road is not a claim, and a road does not know who is standing on it, and I have written that sentence down four times and I am going to write it a fifth time and then I am going to stop, because it is not mine and it was not new this morning.**

**What I have learned today is the half of it that nobody puts on a stake. A bypass takes the traffic. A bypass also takes the man who comes past twice a year, because a man is paid for the road with a name on it and not for the road underneath the bank, and the nine miles underneath the bank has never had a name on it in this county, and there are nine houses under this bank and about forty more along it and not one of them has lost anything anybody can point at except the right to say a thing out loud to a man with a barrow going past.**

**And that is not a conspiracy and it is not a district deciding anything. It is a woman who has filled in her own handwriting what a road used to do for her, and she is right about all of it, and she said it to a carter and not to me, and I am not going to improve it or put it in a better place, and I am not going to make a shape out of it.**

**And I am not going to join it to the rectangle. I have a smooth rectangle about two hands together in a drawing in my coat and a man who is paid for a new road and nine houses under a bank, and one of the three of those is a thing about paper and two of them are things about people, and joining them is a man arranging a thing, and I have had that rule since the ninetieth and I wrote it on a wall on the hundred and forty-first and in my own book on the hundred and forty-sixth, and it is the same rule three times and it is still a rule.**

Tamsin Rook was at the top of the bank, on the grass, where the ground changed, with her back against a post, and she had not said anything for about an hour, and nobody had asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and forty-nine days, and he had not asked her one, and he was not going to.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on the flat at the bottom of the bank with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and forty-ninth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and forty-ninth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A flat at the bottom of a bank, half a mile of new road on the top of it, a ditch, stone stacked at the side, a stake with a word nailed to it, a bridge at the fourth mile, a lane under the bank, a door, a child's coat over an arm, and a flat.**
>
> *Second column, and the bypass. **So a loaded cart can get through. That is what it is for and that is the whole of it, and a man with a cart has said it about four of them.** The new road is on the top of a bank and the bridge is on the bottom of it, and the inn's carts have stopped going down the bottom, and that is the whole of the reason and it took a carter about four seconds to say it standing at the foot of a bank and it cost him nothing and it is worth about a mile of new road on the top of that bank. **And nothing is shut. The old road is not shut.** I have written that twice in this entry and I am not going to write it a third time, because a thing said three times on one page stops being a fact and becomes a man enjoying himself.
>
> *And the woman under the bank, who said it to a carter and not to me, which was correct. **A man used to mend the road and he came past about twice a year and there was always a soft place and he filled it in about a day and did not want money and there was nowhere to put money, and she used to be able to put her hand on his barrow and point. He is not gone. He is where he has always been. He is paid for the new one, because the new one is the district's and has a name that can go in a return, and the nine miles under the bank has not got a name on it that she has ever seen, and a man does not mend a road he cannot write down.** She has a soft place nine feet across by the ash and she has put a board over it and moved her own gate so nothing goes in at night, and she is not complaining to me, and she said so in words.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the whole of the finding. **A bypass is a road and a service cut is a road, and a road is not a claim, and a road does not know who is standing on it. I have written that four times and this is the fifth and I am going to stop, because it is not mine and it was not new this morning.** What is new is the half nobody nails to a stake: **a bypass takes the traffic and it takes the man who comes past twice a year with a barrow, because he is paid for the road with a name on it and not for the road underneath the bank.** Nine houses under this bank and about forty more along it, and not one of them has lost anything a person can point at, **except the right to say a thing out loud to a man going past, and that is what a voice is, and I did not have that word until this evening and I am not going to use it twice.**
>
> *And the stake. **One word nailed to it and a line under it in a smaller hand. I read the word. I did not ask what the line said, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man with a barrow may stand at a stake and be told what the small print says, and that is not the district's fault, and I have now been at four stakes in nine days and read the big word at three of them.**
>
> *And the not-joining. **I have not joined the rectangle to a paid man and nine houses. One of those three is a thing about paper and two are things about people, and that is the rule I have had since the ninetieth, and this is the third time I have written it down and it is still three and not a shape.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a flat, a bank, a ditch and half a mile of new road are not buildings, and I have not been under that bank.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, seventy-two days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to, and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am barred from any public place in that county and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and I am not going to it, and it does not travel, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and forty-nine days, including this one, and including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and a bypass, a stake, a bridge and a woman's own hand in a book about her own gate, and not one of the four is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and a carter made room in a lane for a woman he had no reason to make room for, and that is his business and I am not going to be the man who tells him what it was.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a return with a road in it, a stake with a word nailed to it, a bridge that will not take a loaded cart, half a mile of new stone on the top of a bank, and a man who is paid for the top of it and not for the bottom.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A road is a road and it is not a claim and it does not know who is standing on it, and the whole of what it takes off a place is the man who used to come past, and I got that from a woman at a door and I am not going to write a word of it anywhere that has an office in it.**
