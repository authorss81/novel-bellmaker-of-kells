# Chapter 0187 — Cobb Arley and the Small Bell on the Bracket by the Door

The fourth hour of the hundred and seventy-third day belonged to Cobb Arley, who was on his knees on the lock-house step with a scraper and a bucket, taking the wet off the top step, because a step that is wet in the morning is dry by noon and a step that is wet at noon puts a barrow over.

He had a scraper that was a piece of an old hoe blade riveted to a length of shaft and he worked it with the heel of his hand and he had done this every morning for about nine years, and about six feet behind him on the wall there was a bracket with a small bell on it, and a rope hanging out of the crown of it down to a height a man can get a hand to.

"I have not gone since the water was up," he said, before Tomas was within speaking distance. "You will want to know why and I am telling you before you ask, because you have got a barrow and a paper and you are the fourth one this year and the first one who has come up the step instead of standing in the yard."

"I have not asked you anything."

"I know you have not. That is why I said it."

The step was nine feet wide and the door frame behind it was oak and set deep, and the water was about two feet down inside the lock and coming off the second pair of gates that had gone up on the fourth day. Pell Okell was at the sixth bench at the fifth hour and had gone up at the third, and he had said, coming past, one thing, which was that the man with the hops had sent for him and that he would go at the fifth and that this was not news and was not going to be made into news.

The yard had a bench under a lean-to and a broom leaning in it and a coil of rope and a hood on a chain that a man could not reach from the ground, which was the way a hood is hung when the last man who used it is dead and nobody has wanted to get wet since.

Cobb Arley came up off the step and wiped his hands and looked at the bell without looking at it, the way a man looks at a thing he is responsible for and has not had to do anything about for a week.

"That is not a Crown bell," he said. "Do not go writing that down, because I know what you are and I know what that is, and I have never in my life had anybody ask me the second question about a thing I keep. I bought that bell with lock money out of a shed ledger about nine years ago, because the one they had was cracked across the shoulder and the shoulder is where the crack matters. It is on a bracket. It has a rope. It is not on a roll, it is not in a store's book, it is not on a post above a board, and there is one line in that ledger that says BELL and nothing after it, and the man who wrote it is dead and the man who keeps it now would not know it if you asked him."

"What does it do."

"It calls the crews. When the water is going into a lock I cannot fill alone I go, and I go about a mile a second time and I am slow and I am not getting younger, and that bell goes about a quarter of a mile, and there are four doors up that bank and about four hundred yards of bank, and one of the four answers and one of the four does not, and I know which one does not because it has not answered in about two years and I have not gone up to find out why and I am not going to now."

"You could go up the bank."

"I could go up the bank at any hour of any day of my life and there is not a form in this kingdom on which I would have to, and that is the thing I have not been able to put anywhere for about four years, and you have come up my step on purpose, so I will put it here and you can carry it, and I know you cannot carry it, and I am saying it anyway."

---

At about the sixth hour they were on the bench under the lean-to and Tomas had the sheet in the coat and did not take it out, and had not come up the step with it, and had said so at the step, and Cobb Arley had said that was the fourth thing in nine years that had been said to him on that step and that the fourth was new and the other three had been lies.

"A keeper is a person who can be asked to hold a line," Tomas said. "That is the whole of it. It is not a bell and it is not a wire. It is that there is a person at the end of it, and if the person is there, the line runs, and if the person is not, the line does not, and that is true of a lock and a rope and a bell on a bracket and it is true of a thing eleven miles off that a clerk rings four times for."

"It is true."

"Then somebody has asked you to go on holding it."

"Somebody came down the bank in the spring in a coat and asked me, in words, on this step, and he was right to ask and he did not have a form and did not pretend to, and I have thought about that for about four months and here is where I have got to." Arley took his hands off his knees. "I said I would. I did not say a reason. I have been asked by two men in nine years to say a reason and I have never had one that was not the water, and the water is not a reason, it is a subject, and I know the difference because a man told me once about a barrel of lime and I did not understand it for a fortnight."

"And the asking was in words."

"The asking was in words and there is no form, and that is the whole of the price and I have not been able to put it down anywhere and I am putting it down here." He held up a finger. "Because there is no form, nobody can be produced. There is nothing to produce, not for me and not for him. He cannot be asked again what he said to me, and I cannot be asked what I said back, and there is no fourth column in this kingdom with my name at the top of it, and if there were I could not fill it in because I have no standing in a ward and this is a lock and a lock is not a ward."

"That is a protection."

"It is a protection and it is the reason I have not slept properly about it since the spring, and both of those are the same fact and you can have it either way you like." He took the finger down. "Here is the other side and I have not said it to anybody. Four doors up that bank. If I stop, four households lose an answer they have now got and have never had to think about, and one of them has not answered in two years already and I do not know why and I have not gone up to find out because if I had gone up in the spring and found out, I would have had to tell her."

"You could tell her."

"I could tell her anything I liked. There is nobody in this kingdom who can stop me." He said it without heat at all. "And that is the answer to your question and I did not know until this second that I had one. A man who holds a line is a person who can be asked, and a person who is asked has to be able to say what it would be to refuse, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom that asks a man that, and there is not one on this step, and there was not one on that man's paper either, because he had not got a paper. So the answer to what it would be to refuse is that it is up there, and it is four doors and four hundred yards, and the only way I find out is by going, and the going is the damage."

Tomas did not say anything, and it was about four inches long, and it was the only instrument he had.

"The bell is not the line," Arley said, after a while. "I want to be exact, because you are a man who makes them. That thing on the bracket goes about a quarter of a mile and stops where it stops and I do not know where that is and neither does anybody. **The line is me, on this step, at whatever hour it is, and the bell is a convenience.**"

The bucket had a skin on it. Arley looked at the bucket and then at the water in the lock and then at the four hundred yards of bank above the yard, and did not get up.

"You cannot help me," he said. "I have known that since you came up the step and I told you so in the first minute. You have no form and you cannot certify a bell, and I would not take a certificate from you and neither would she if I sent one up that bank. There is nothing you can put between me and that district man and nothing you can put between me and four doors, and the only thing you have is true sentences, and I have nine years of true sentences of my own and not one of them has ever done a single thing about this."

"That is true."

"I know it is. I would not have said it if it were not." He got up, and his knees went, and he put the scraper in the bucket. "And I am not going to refuse and I am not going to agree, and I want you to understand what that is. I am not holding the line for the district. I am holding it because I have not gone up that bank. **Holding it and choosing to hold it are two different things and only one of them is mine, and I cannot tell you which one I am doing, and I have not been able to since the spring.**"

At about the ninth hour of the evening Tomas was on the flat above the lock yard with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and the small bell on the bracket was on the wall six hundred yards below him in the dark and it was not rung, and it was not rung by anybody that anybody saw, and it belonged to a man who had not gone up a bank and could not say whether holding was the same as choosing, and he was thirty-seven days on this road with her and had not asked her once what the road was for.

> *The hundred and seventy-third, at about the ninth hour of the evening. A lock house, a step nine feet wide, a scraper, a bucket, a lean-to, a broom, a coil of rope, a hood on a chain out of reach, a bell on a bracket and a rope, four doors up four hundred yards of bank, a flat.*
>
> *Second column. A bell bought with lock money out of a shed ledger about nine years ago, and bought because of a crack across the shoulder of the one they had, and the shoulder is where a crack matters, on a bracket, with a rope, and **one line in that ledger, and after it nothing at all: a man wrote it and he is dead, and the man who has the ledger now would not know the thing was there.** Not on a roll, not in a store's book, not on a post above a board. **The bell is a convenience and the line is a man on a step. A man at the second bench has told him for nine years to put it up higher, has never been wrong about the arithmetic, and it has never once been done.**
>
> *Second column, the price. A man in a coat came down the bank in the spring in words, on that step, with no form and no pretence of one, and asked him to go on holding it, and he said he would and gave no reason. **Because there is no form, nobody can be produced.** Not him, not the man in the coat, not either of them. **No fourth column in this kingdom with his name at the top of it, and if there were he could not fill it in, because there is no form on which a lock keeper fills a fourth column, a lock is not a ward, and he has no standing in a ward.** He said that is a protection and that it is also why he has not slept properly since the spring, and that both are one fact.
>
> *And the other side, said once, and to nobody before. **Four doors up that bank, and if he stops, four households lose an answer they have now got and never had to think about, and one of them has not answered in two years and he has not gone up to find out why, because if he had gone up and found out he would have had to tell her.** Asking a man to hold a line obliges him to be able to say what refusing would be, and **there is no form anywhere in this kingdom that asks a man that, and the man in the coat had not got a paper to ask it on. So the answer to what refusing would be is that it is up there, and the only way he finds out is by going, and the going is the damage.** He said nobody in this kingdom could stop him going, and that that is the answer, and that he had not known until that second that he had one.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. **I cannot help him. He knew it in the first minute, and said he would not take a certificate from me and neither would she if I sent one up that bank.** What I have is true sentences, and he has nine years of true sentences of his own and not one of them has ever done a single thing about this. **He is not going to refuse and he is not going to agree. He said holding a line and choosing to hold it are two different things and only one of them is his, and that he cannot tell me which one he is doing.** That is the question I have carried since the hundred and thirty-second day, and a man on a step with a scraper has now got the whole of it in about four hundred words and no form anywhere, and **it is not answered, and it is not going to be answered on this river, and no version of answering it is mine to do.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and the warrant stands, and a fitting is not called anything it is not.** Four bolts made fast, and **the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and forty days, and a lock yard with a lean-to is not a building and I sat under it for two hours and did not go in.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, ninety-six days from the seventy-seventh, and I have not put my hand on it.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour today, including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and the man with the hops has sent for Pell and that is not news and was not made into news.**
>
