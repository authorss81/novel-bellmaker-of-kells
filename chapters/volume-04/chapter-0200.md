# Chapter 0200 — A District's Own Form and Nine Places That Nobody Thanked Him For

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and eighty-sixth day and Rennick Dole was sitting at a bench-room table at the lower end of the reach with a satchel open beside him and a sheet of paper in front of him, and the sheet was his own, and Tomas Vale came up the yard behind the barrow and put it down nine feet off, and Dole did not stop writing, because a man filling in a form is a man doing work and a man doing work is not to be talked to.

The sheet had four columns and no head. Column one wanted the name of a place, column two what the place was entered as, column three a number, and column four a person with a standing, and the fourth column was empty, and it was the same empty fourth column a bench clerk had never filled in eleven years and a district's clerk was not going to fill in either.

"I will be about a quarter of an hour," Dole said, when he had done the ninth line. "There is no name on it anywhere. That is not an oversight, so do not ask me about it, because I have been asked about it twice and told the truth both times and I would rather not tell it a third time before I have had anything to eat."

"What is it."

"It is a district's form and I wrote it." He put the pen down and squared the sheet. "Not a Crown's, not out of any book anybody sends out, and not a copy of anything. It has cost four days of my own carrying it between two rooms and a satchel, and I have been asked about it twice by a man who wanted to help, and there is no name on it because I have not got a box to put one in and it is not that I do not want to."

He put a finger on column two.

"That is the whole of what I have got. There are nine of these places up this water and every one has people living at it and a ford or a landing or a market or a stack at it, and in four years of this district's books there is no box anywhere that says any of the nine is a place people live, and there are four boxes for a crew, a mouth, a rate and a keeper, and the keeper box is the only one anybody has ever wanted to fill. A house that is answered for is a house with a keeper, and a district cannot get keepers up this water, and so there is nothing to answer for, and so there is nothing to refuse. That is not my sentence. It is a clerk's, it is eleven years old, it is on a page in a room I have never been in, and it is correct."

"And column two."

"Column two is where the answer is." He turned the sheet about so Tomas could read it, which he was allowed to do, because a district's form is a district's and a man with a barrow is not the district. "Column two says the place is a place people live, and that is all it says, and it says it in the district's own hand and in the district's own book, and it is going to be the truth about those nine places for as long as this district exists. No Crown office has been asked about it, told, or able to stop it, because a district may put anything it likes in its own book about its own ground, and it is right to, and there is nothing heroic in it, and I am not going to be thanked for it, because being thanked is a thing that is done at a door and this is a table."

---

At about the fifth hour there were eleven people in that bench room and nine of them were from the nine places, and every one of them had walked to get there, and the ninth bench's woman had brought bread and a pot, which is the ninth bench's own idea and nobody has ever asked her for it.

At about the sixth hour a woman from the fourth of the nine places said thank you to Rennick Dole, and Dole said *it is a form*, and she said *I know it is a form*, and he said *then you know what it is*, and nobody else in that room said anything about it at any hour.

At about the sixth hour Cobb Arley came in and stood at the back with his hand on the frame, having come four hundred yards and a bank, and he is a keeper of a line, and the four doors up that bank are still four doors.

"There is a box on that sheet for a place," he said. "There is not a box on it for a house and there is not a box on it for four doors, and I have known that for eleven years and three years of that I have known the reason why, and the reason is that a house that is answered for is a house with a keeper and there is no keeper at the top of my bank because I am not a keeper, I am a man at a lock with a rope, and the district cannot ask me to be a keeper because there is no form that says a man at a lock is a keeper."

"One of your four doors could go on that form," Tomas said, and then wished he had not, because a suggestion from a man with no standing in a county is an order wearing a hat.

"One of my four doors could go on that form," Arley agreed, "and I have known that since the second hour of this morning, and I am not going to be the one who puts it there, and I am not going to ask a man who writes forms to put it there, because if I ask him then it is a man with four doors asking and not a district, and the whole of what that sheet is worth is that nobody asked for it." He looked at Dole. "That is not a complaint about you and it is not a favour to you. It is a keeper telling a room that the room has got one box and he is not going to be the thing that puts a thing in it."

Nobody in the room said anything to that, and Tomas Vale, who had said it, said nothing, and the four bolts on a barrow in the yard were made fast at the fourth hour and again at the seventh.

---

At about the seventh hour the two long things of the ten days came together in a room with a stove in it, and neither of them was in Tomas Vale's mouth, and one of them is about as old as the benches and one of them is three days old.

The first was the three partial answers, and there are still three, and there is still no conclusion, and a man from the sixth bench said the thing nobody has said in a hundred and eighty-six days.

"You have a reason," he said, "from a clerk's mouth, that a house answered for is a house with a keeper and a district cannot get keepers. You have a price, from a man with a scraper, that a man asked to hold a line cannot refuse, because refusing is an answer and an answer wants a standing. And you have an account, from a man coiling a rope, of four places on a reach and three of them named. **And those three do not agree with one another, and if three things that do not agree are three answers to one question then they are not three answers to one question, and I have been at that bench nine years and I have not got a fourth and I am not going to be the man who says I have.**"

The second was the form, and it is on the table in a satchel, in a district's own hand, in a district's own book, and it says nine things and not one of them is a person.

At about the ninth hour of the evening Tomas Vale was on a flat of beaten earth above the water with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and the water had gone down five foot in three days and was still going, and a yard of forty people had nine hundred small things in the bottom corner of it that had not been lifted, and two hundred and forty people in a low quarter had nine houses wet and the rest dry, and forty of them had never been told anything by anybody and were not going to be, and a landing stage had a nine-foot hole in the end of it, and a barge was upright in seven foot of water with nine tons of dressed stone under her, and a second pair of gates nine miles up had a dressed sill half in with the lime out of the top joint, and would be about nine days, and the rate would be set in a fortnight by nine men who would not agree.

The nine places are in a district's book. The man he had asked to be told nothing is on the reach and holds a line and has not been up a bank. The map he cannot read is in the inside pocket of his coat folded in three with a rectangle in it that four men looked at in a bench room and did not explain, and the two questions written down about it are narrower by three lines and answered in neither half, and a bay in a frame is what a mould maker said it was yesterday in front of nine benches and not one of them asked him a question about it.

He tried to hold a straight edge at about the seventh hour, because a man cannot be on a road for fifty days without knowing whether he can still do the thing he is, and he put the edge on the flat with a stone at each end and got nine inches out of nine feet before his hand went, and there is no form on which a man can be told that he may not hold one either.

Tamsin Rook was outside the bench room door for the last hour of it with her hand flat on the oak jamb and her boots on the step, and nobody asked her a question at any hour of that day including him, and he was fifty days on this road with her and had not asked her once what it was for.

> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and the warrant stands, and a fitting is not called anything it is not.** Four bolts made fast at the fourth hour and again at the seventh, and **the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and fifty-three days, and a bench room is a building and the barrow was in the yard on a stone the whole three hours.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, a hundred and nine days from the seventy-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. **Her bell is in her bag and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour today, including me, and she was on the step for the last hour and I stood nine feet off and said nothing.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and a district's form went into a district's book and I read it and I was in the room and I did not write it and I did not sign it and I did not ask for it. It is not a document and it is not a fourteenth, because it was never in my coat, and reading a page of it standing up is reading and it is not carrying.**
