# Chapter 0169 — A Place Can Be Absent From A Schedule And Present Under A Different Name, And He Cannot Prove Which Of Two Words Is The One On The Paper

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and fifty-fifth day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on the flat at the top of the lane above the street of about forty houses, and he had not been down in it since the day before, and nobody had come up to the flat, and there was a woman at the top of the lane with a yoke who had not been there the day before either.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and twenty-two days, and a flat, a lane and a street are not buildings, and I slept about nine feet from a wall last night and I did not ask anybody for the wall and nobody offered it, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man with a barrow may ask for a place to put it, and that has been true for a hundred and fifty-five days.**

---

At about the fifth hour he asked the question he had come down with, and it was the plainest one he had asked anybody in ten days, and it was: **what is this place called.**

The man at the gate with the barrow of nothing in it: “**Kilnhead.**”

The woman with the key on a string and the pail: “**Kilnhead. There was a kiln up the lane about a hundred yards and there is a heap of bricks where it was and no kiln, and that is the whole of where the name comes from, and it is not a pretty name and it is ours.**”

A man at the fire in the lean-to: “**Wetherin.**”

“**That is a wood four miles over.**”

“**It is.**”

“**And it is a name.**”

“**It is a name a district uses, and I have heard it twice in about nine years, both times from a man on a cart, and I have never once been asked whether it is mine, and I would have said no if I had been, and I am not going to now because it is not worth a fight with a cart.**”

And then the man at the gate, who had not spoken for about four minutes:

“**The lane is Wetherin Dyke. The street is Kilnhead. Both have been right for as long as I have been standing in this gate, and neither of them came from anywhere, and I have never seen the two of them on the same thing and I would not know which of the two you meant if you asked me which is the proper one.**”

“**Would anybody here know.**”

“**No. And that is not because nobody is asked. It is that there has never been a reason to ask, because a street with a name on it at a door does not need another name to be a street, and I have got about forty people in this street and not one of them has ever asked me, and if you want to know something about this street, that is the thing you have not got.**”

---

At about the sixth hour he went down into the street and put the schedule against the wall of the lean-to with one hand and looked at it for a long time, and then he took it out of the coat again and put it back, and then he took it out, and he did not do anything with it, and that is the whole of what happened and it took about nine minutes.

**I have got a piece of paper in my coat about two hands together when it is folded in three, and it has a row of names at the top of it in a small hand, and about a third of the way along a run there is a rectangle about two hands together with nothing in it, and the paper is smooth and uncreased there and the rules stop short and do not cross it, and the ink either side is a slightly different black, and the numbers either side go on without a break, and it is in the same place. I have carried that for eleven days. And I cannot read a run. I never could. There is no trade in it and there is no form on which anybody is taught and there is no version of being better at it, and I have known that for ten days and I wrote it on a wall on the hundred and forty-first.**

**And I have got two words. A street with a heap of bricks in it and about forty people living on it is called Kilnhead by every person in it, and a district calls the same ground Wetherin, and the lane is Wetherin Dyke, and the man at the gate has never seen the two of them on the same thing and would not know which I meant if I said which is the proper one.**

**So: a place can be absent from a schedule and be standing here with a fire going in it, and a place can be on a schedule under a name nobody at a door uses, and both of those are about two different objects and I can hold both of them, and I cannot tell you which of the two words is the word on the paper.**

**And here is the part I am not going to improve. I cannot prove that this street is the rectangle. I cannot prove that it is not the rectangle. I cannot prove that it is on a sheet at all. I cannot tell you whether there is a heading in a district's book that says Kilnhead or one that says Wetherin or one that says something else that I have not heard yet from a man on a cart, and the woman with the key has seen a heading with a number on it and has never seen a road that goes where it says. Not one of those five is a discovery. Not one of them is a step. Five things I cannot do, written down on a morning when I wanted very badly to be able to do one of them.**

---

At about the seventh hour he found the thing he was actually going to write in his own book, and it was not the name.

“**You said you would have said no if you had been asked.**”

“**I did.**”

“**You would not have said no about a kiln.**”

“**No.**” The man at the fire did not look up. “**A kiln is a thing that was here and a district's word is a thing a cart said, and one of those is mine and one of those is theirs, and I know which is which without being able to say it, and you have come down a lane with a barrow to find out which of two words is proper, and there is no such word, and I have a fire.**”

“**You have a fire and a name.**”

“**I have a fire.**” He put a piece on it. “**And a name, and the name is Kilnhead, and it is mine, and I have never had to defend it, and if I ever have to I will say it in one word and I will not say the other one at all, and I would like you to go up your lane now.**”

He went up his lane. Nobody thanked anybody, and the four of them came up after him at their own pace and in their own order, and he did not ask them to.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on the flat at the top of the lane with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and she was on the flat about nine feet off his left shoulder, and she wrote her own entry first, and then he wrote his.

> *The hundred and fifty-fifth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A flat at the top of a lane, a street of about forty houses, a heap of bricks about a hundred yards up it, a well, a fire, a man at a gate, and a flat.**
>
> *Second column, and the words. **Kilnhead, at a door, from a kiln that is a heap of bricks now. Wetherin, in a district's mouth, from a wood four miles over that has no house in it. Wetherin Dyke, for the lane, from the man at the gate.** Three words and no person here has ever been asked which of them is the proper one, and the man at the gate said so first and said it is not because nobody is asked, it is that a street with a name at a door does not need a second name to be a street.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the whole of what I have got, and it is two sentences and they do not touch. **The first: a place can be off every schedule anybody has ever shown me and still be here this morning with a fire going in it and about sixty people standing about on it. The second: a place can be written down, and the word on the paper need not be the word anybody uses at a door.** Both of those are true of the same forty houses at the same time, and the two of them have nothing to do with one another except that I happen to be holding both. **I cannot tell you which of the two words I have been handed is the one on the sheet. I have been unable to for eleven days and I have never before had a reason to want to be able to.** And the five things I cannot do, written down because I wanted very badly to be able to do one of them: **I cannot prove this street is the rectangle. I cannot prove it is not the rectangle. I cannot prove it is on a sheet at all. I cannot tell you whether a heading says Kilnhead or Wetherin or something I have not heard from a cart. And I cannot tell you which of two names is proper, because the man at the gate does not know and has never been asked and does not think it is a thing anybody asks.** Not one of the five is a discovery and not one of them is a step.
>
> *And what I will not write. **I am not going to enter a name anywhere and I have no form to enter one on and no column to put it in, and a man with a barrow who puts a name in a book is doing one thing and one thing only, and I know what it is because I have been on the other side of it four times in a hundred and fifty-five days.** I have written two words in my own book and I have written that I do not know which of them is a heading and which is a door, and both of those are correct, and that is the whole of what I am going to do with either of them. **The name of the district above the inn is not going in this book either, and it has not gone in on any of the eleven days, and I am not going to get it this month and I am not going to ask anybody in this street.**
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a flat, a lane and a street are not buildings, and I slept nine feet from a wall and did not ask for it.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, seventy-eight days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to, and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am barred from any public place in that county and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and I am not going to it, and it does not travel, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and fifty-five days, including this one, and including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and two words about forty houses, and not one of the three is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and a man at a fire asked me to go up my lane and I went.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is a heap of bricks about a hundred yards up a lane, a wood four miles over with no house in it, a heading with a number on it that a woman has seen and a road she has never seen, and a man at a gate who has three correct words and no way to say which is proper.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A place can be absent from a schedule and be here, and a schedule can carry a place under a name that is not the name at a door, and the whole of the difference between those two sentences is a piece of paper I cannot read, and I have known for ten days that I cannot read it and I have never once needed to know it as badly as I did this morning.**

> *The hundred and fifty-fifth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A flat at the top of a lane, a wall I slept nine feet off, a street of about forty houses, a fire, a well, and a flat.**
>
> *Second column, and the day. **A gate, a key on a string, a pail, a lean-to, a fire, a heap of bricks about a hundred yards up a lane, and a man at a fire who told me to go up my lane and who was right to and who had a reason and did not give it.** I was on the well kerb most of it. **The kerb is stone and there is a post at the corner and I could get my hand on all three and I could read the gate and the door frame and the fire going and about nine people.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the price of it, in my own words and about me. **Four people talking on a road is a wall and I could not get an edge of it, and I did not try, and I went to the kerb, and that is the whole of what happened and there is nothing new in it.** I have been reading things on and off for eleven years and what is left is a list and I have not added to it and I am not going to write out which items because they are mine and because a woman who writes down which things she has lost is a woman who has started to describe herself as a thing that is going, and I am not that.
>
> *And four days of it now where there is nothing new, and I am putting that on this page because in a month somebody is going to ask me what this road did to me and the true answer is that most of it did nothing at all, and I would rather write down a day where nothing happened than let a day like this one stand for the whole.
>
> *Fourth column. **Two lines and I have not improved either and I am not going to. The price side is the other way up and it is going to stay that way. The count is eight.**
>
> *And nobody asked. **A man with a barrow came into a street of forty houses and asked five people the same question and not one of them asked me anything, and he did not ask me either, and it is the hundred and fifty-fifth day and he has not asked me one question about what this is for on any of them, and I have noticed that every day since the hundred and forty-seventh and I have not done anything about it and I am not going to.**
