# Chapter 0172 — The Ford Crew At The Lower Water

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and fifty-eighth day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on a shelf of gravel above the lower water, and the river ran thin over stone and the far bank showed a line of willow and smoke from two fires.

The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and twenty-five days, and gravel and willow and smoke are not buildings.

---

At about the fifth hour they went down to the ford. The water was low enough to see the causeway stones, and a gang of six men were shifting gravel from a heap to the edge with shovels, building up the shoulder where the carts crossed.

A thick man with mud to his knees straightened and looked at the two barrows.

"You will want the hard line," he said. "Keep left of the pole. Right is soft since the rain."

Pell took the left without argument. Isa followed, judging the depth by the pole marks. Mab stepped stone to stone with the basket high. Ned stopped and looked at the shoulder the men were building.

"Good stone," Ned said. "You are bedding it or tipping it."

The thick man wiped his face. "Bedding the edge. Tipping the middle. The middle goes every spring and we put it back. District pays for the heap and we put it where the carts go."

Tomas set his barrow down above the water and watched. There were huts behind the willows, washing on a line, a child carrying a pail, a woman mending a net. Smoke from the fires smelled of cooking, not of a work camp for a day.

"How many of you here," Tomas said.

The man shrugged. "Gang is six. With women and children, near thirty. We are here till the heap is gone."

"And then."

"Then another heap somewhere else." He picked his shovel up. "That is the work."

At about the sixth hour Tomas walked up the bank and looked back. Thirty people living by a ford, houses of wattle and canvas that could be taken down in a morning, children, washing, fires. No wall with a name. No board.

Mab came up beside him. "Two hundred and nineteen," she said. "Fences stopped. Willow after that. I am writing willow now and I have not finished."

He did not ask her to explain. She had said the number was not finished and that was the whole of it.

Ned was still at the edge, running his boot along the new shoulder. "They bed the edge true," he said to nobody in particular. "Middle they tip. Tip washes. Edge holds. If it was mine I would bed it all, but it is not mine and stone costs."

Nobody thanked anybody. The gang worked on. The woman with the net did not look up.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on gravel above the water with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast, and she sat with her back to a boulder where the ground changed under it, her hand flat on the stone.

> *The hundred and fifty-eighth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. Gravel, a ford, six men with shovels, huts, two fires, a flat.*
>
> *Second column. The gang is six and with women and children near thirty, living by the water till a heap is gone. The district pays for the heap. No name on a wall and no board, and the work is bedding an edge that holds and tipping a middle that washes.*
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. Ruling: people can live a year by a ford and still be a heap of stone in a book.*
>
> *All five clauses stand. Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and the warrant stands. Four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third. The carrier in the sack, not rung, eighty-one days from the seventy-seventh, and I have not put my hand on it and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour, including me.*
>
> *The register is thirteen. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today.*
>
> *Which office is it. It is a pole in a ford, a heap paid for, and thirty people beside it.*
