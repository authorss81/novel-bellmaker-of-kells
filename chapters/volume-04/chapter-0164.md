# Chapter 0164 — A District's Man Who Is Right About The Procedure And Cannot Be Reached About The Standing

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and fiftieth day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on the flat at the top of the bypass, and a man with a level on a tripod and a chain on a reel was measuring the new road about two hundred yards off, and working alone, and the chain was dragging a little in the grass and he was letting it.

**The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and seventeen days, and a flat, a tripod and half a mile of new road are not buildings, and he has not asked me one question and I have not asked him one and we are two men about two hundred yards apart doing the same arithmetic on the same ground.**

---

At about the fifth hour he came back past and Tomas Vale asked him a question about a number, and got an answer, and then got the second half of it. The man was called Sim Searle and had been at this bypass for about as long as the bypass had been down, and Tomas Vale learned nothing else about him that he used.

“**What is the length of this road.**”

“**Six hundred and something.**”

“**Something.**”

“**Six hundred and forty on the district's sheet, and I have not got that sheet either, it is in an office four days off, and I have never asked to see it, and I would not know what to do with it if I saw it. Six hundred and fifty-one by my chain, and the difference is the chain, because a chain sags in grass and I am not going to correct it, and the true figure is somewhere between the two and I could not tell you which and neither could you.**” He coiled the last of it up. “**That is the whole of my trade. Two true numbers about one length and no way to choose and no column to put either of them in that I am allowed to be wrong in.**”

“**Then you put the sheet's number in.**”

“**I put nothing in. The sheet's number was in the sheet before I came and it will be in it after I have gone, and if I put mine in beside it then there are two numbers in one place and a man in an office four days off has to choose between them, and he will choose the one that was there first because choosing the other one means somebody's chain sagged.**” He shouldered the reel. “**And I would be the one who put it there. A column a man rules for himself is a thing he has to be right about, and a notice-reader in a market town has wanted to rule one for nine years and has not, and I have wanted to for one afternoon and I am not going to.**”

“**That is twice in ten days.**”

“**Then it is a common thing and neither of us is remarkable and neither of us is going to do it.**”

---

At about the sixth hour he took the schedule out of his coat and laid it flat on the kerbstone at the end of the bypass, and the district's man looked at it, and asked him nothing about where it had come from, and did not touch it.

He looked for about nine seconds.

“**One ink.**”

“**That is what I said.**”

“**You said two blacks and I am saying one ink, and you are welcome to it and it is not an answer, because ink that has been wet and dried has a different face to ink that has not and every sheet in this county that is a year old has a different face at one end than the other, and a man who has not handled ink for a hundred and fifty days sees two blacks where there is one.**” He put his hand on the back of his own book and not on the paper. “**The rules stopping short of it, I cannot account for. I have never seen a sheet do that and I have laid more of this county's new line than I can write down.**”

“**Can you read a run.**”

“**No.**” He said it without any weight at all. “**Nobody can. I have been paid to lay lines that go on paper and not on the ground for longer than I care to say, and I have never been able to read one and neither can anybody in the county office, and there is no form in this kingdom on which anybody is taught, so there is no version of being better at it and you may put that down and stop hoping.**”

“**You have looked at it.**”

“**I have looked at it. It is a district's sheet and I am a district's man and I have said so out loud, and I have not asked where you got it and I am not going to, and if you wanted me to ask then you have the wrong man, and if you wanted a copy of it then there is a counter four days off that will not give you one and I have never asked them for one either.**”

Tomas Vale folded the schedule in three and put it back in the inside pocket of the coat, and the eleven lines on the leaf of his own book were in the other pocket of the same coat, and he had not taken them out, and he had not put them next to each other.

---

At about the seventh hour he asked the question he came with, and got the answer he expected, and did not argue with it.

“**What would I have to be to be put in your book.**”

“**You have not got a standing in this district and neither have I, in the way you mean it. I have a job and a district and a number, and I have not got a form for a man who saw something.**” He set the tripod down. “**The form wants four things. A ward, a day, a number, and a ground. You have told me a length and I have told you a length and both of ours are true, and there is nowhere on that form to put a man with a barrow who says the second one.**”

“**There is a reason box.**”

“**There is a box for a reason and the reason box has to be filled in by somebody with a standing, and I have a standing, and you have not, and if I filled it in on your account then a man in an office four days off would have to decide whose word it was, and he would decide mine, and then the district would have taken your observation and made it the district's, and your observation is not the district's and it is not mine, and the whole of what has gone wrong in this business for a hundred and fifty days is a thing being taken off one person and put in a column by somebody who was being kind.**”

“**That is not a small print on a stake.**”

“**No.**” He picked the tripod up. “**And I am not going to read it to you and neither is anybody else, and that is not me being careful. That is there being no form on which a man with a barrow may stand at a stake and be told what the small line says, and it is the same hole your four boxes have got in it, and if you want somebody to be angry with for that you will have to be angry with the shape and I have not got a shape, I have got a tripod.**”

He went back to his chain. Tomas Vale did not follow him and did not write down the number six hundred and fifty-one in the same book as the number six hundred and forty, and he had known he would not before he asked.

**And the sheet came out of my coat in the open air this morning and went back in, and it is a drawing and not a document, and I asked that man nothing and he asked me nothing, and I have been carrying a picture for ten days and I have laid it on a kerbstone for nine seconds in front of the only person I have met in four days whose whole trade is a line on paper, and he cannot read a run either, and he told me the two blacks are one ink, and I have been carrying two blacks for a hundred and forty-nine days and one of them is just wet and dry.**

**That is the first eye of four that I have put on it and it is the whole of what it cost: nine seconds and about a mile of my own finding made smaller by a man who was measuring grass.**

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on a step by a gate at the end of the lane under the bank, with the barrow on the flat and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the last entry of the hundred and fiftieth and filled the first, second and third columns and left the fourth as it was.

> *The hundred and fiftieth, at about the ninth hour of the evening. **A flat on the bypass, a tripod, a chain on a reel, a kerbstone, a gate, a lane under the bank, and a step.**
>
> *Second column, and two numbers, from a man called **Sim Searle**, who was measuring the new road about two hundred yards off when I made the bolts fast and who asked me nothing. **Six hundred and forty on the district's sheet, which I have not got and he has not got and it is in an office four days off, and six hundred and fifty-one by his chain, and the difference is the chain sagging in grass, and the true figure is somewhere between them and he cannot tell me which and neither can I.** He put nothing in. **The sheet's number was in the sheet before he came and will be in it after he has gone, and if he puts his beside it then a man four days off has to choose and he will choose the one that was there first, because choosing the other one means somebody's chain sagged.** A column a man rules for himself is a thing he has to be right about. A notice-reader in a market town has wanted to rule one for nine years and has not. **That is twice in ten days and neither of us is remarkable and neither of us is going to do it.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and the nine seconds. **I took the schedule out of my coat and laid it flat on a kerbstone and he looked at it for about nine seconds and he asked me nothing about where it came from and he did not touch it.** Three things and no more. **One ink: the two blacks I have carried for a hundred and forty-nine days are one ink wet and dried, and a man who has not handled ink in a hundred and fifty days sees two.** Not an answer; he said so first, and he was right, and it makes my finding smaller and not larger. **The rules stopping short of it, he cannot account for, and he has never seen a sheet do it.** And he cannot read a run, and neither can anybody in the county office, and there is no form on which anybody is taught, and there is therefore no version of being better at it. **That is the first of four other people's eyes and it took nine seconds and it cost me about a mile of my own finding.**
>
> *And the standing, which is the whole of it. **The form wants four things: a ward, a day, a number, and a ground. I have a length and he has a length and both are true and there is nowhere on it to put a man with a barrow who says the second one.** The reason box wants a standing and I have not got one and he has, and if he filled it in on my account then a man four days off would decide whose word it was and he would decide the district's man, and the thing would stop being mine. **And the reason he gave for not doing it anyway is the reason I have been carrying for a hundred and fifty days without a name for it: a thing that gets lifted off a person and set down in a column by somebody who means well is how all of it went, and he said it to a stranger with a barrow in about nine seconds standing on a bank, and he has never said it to the district and he is not going to.** He is right and he did not say it kindly.
>
> *And the small print. **I asked him to read me a line under a word on a stake and he would not, and he told me why, and the why is that there is no form on which a man with a barrow may be told, and that it is the same hole the four boxes have got in it, and that I can be angry at the shape or at nothing.** I have now been at four stakes in ten days and read the big word at three and asked about the small line twice and been refused twice and I am not going to ask a third time anywhere in this county.
>
> *All five clauses stand. **Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had and none reached for, and no Crown fitting called anything it is not and the word is not used, and no mechanism described, and no document produced, given, shown or asked for, and no name transferred and no child found by a document and no sheet taken off a board, and no Crown office defeated and nobody relieved of anything, and the warrant stands.** Four bolts made fast, **and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a flat, a tripod and a kerbstone are not buildings.** The carrier in the sack, **not rung, seventy-three days from the seventy-seventh, and it is not struck, and I have not put my hand on it and I am not going to, and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and I am barred from any public place in that county and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and I am not going to it, and it does not travel, and no bell I have ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom tonight. **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of a hundred and fifty days, including this one, and including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Thirteen documents in a coat, none of them about him except the oldest one, and a drawing that came out of the coat in the open air for nine seconds and went back in, and a drawing is a picture and not a document, and not one of that is a fourteenth. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and I did not write six hundred and fifty-one in the same book as six hundred and forty and I knew I would not before I asked.**
>
> *Which office is it. **It is four boxes, a ward, a day, a number and a ground, and a fifth box for a reason that a man with no standing cannot fill in, and a tripod, a chain and about a mile of new road on a bank.** Nobody in it is wicked. **A district is right about the procedure and wrong about the standing, and the standing is a thing a man with a barrow has not got, and the whole of the difference between him and me is one box that neither of us can reach.**
