# Chapter 0177 — The Sentence Nobody Will Carry

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and sixty-third day and Tomas Vale made the four bolts fast on a verge of short grass beside the quarry road, and the cut face of the quarry showed pale above them and a gang was already at work with hammers.

The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third and it is a hundred and thirty days, and grass and a quarry face are not buildings.

---

At about the fifth hour Ned Adley went to the gang and spoke to a man with a hammer about stone, lip and bedding, and came back shaking his head.

"They tip it," he said. "Good stone tipped like rubbish. I told him how to bed an edge and he said the return wants feet of ditch and not edges that hold. Feet can be counted. Edges cannot."

At about the sixth hour Tomas tried what he had not tried since the hedge above the street of forty houses. He gathered the four at the verge, out of earshot of the hammers, and said one true thing in the same words he had used before, to see if it would hold.

"The nine places up the water are dwellings and not gangs, and the return has no box for them."

Pell looked at his boots. "That is true," he said. "And I will not carry it. A barrow of sacking is one thing and a sentence about a district's book is heavier. I fetch loads. That is what I do."

Isa shook her head. "Say it in one place and say it different in another and which of us is to be found. Write it down and I will think. Till then I carry lime."

Mab said, "I have two hundred and thirty and I have not finished. A sentence that grows every day is a man talking. I am counting."

Ned was last. "I would carry it if I could be wrong in public," he said. "Put it where a stranger forty years off can read it and find me. You have no such place. So I carry stone."

At about the seventh hour Tomas walked on alone ahead of the barrow for a stretch, because a man who has been refused four times wants to be out of sight while he learns it.

A thing said twice at ten feet in the open moved about four in ten and died with the hearers over about nine years. A thing written down was read by every clerk after for forty years and could be produced by a stranger. A route was a person on foot and cost a day, and the day belonged to whoever gave it. He had four people and not one sentence, and the district had a document that would travel forty years without any of them.

He did not blame them. A sentence could not be carried by anybody. A document could be carried by a district. They were two different objects, and yesterday had proved it.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening he was on short grass with the barrow behind him and the four bolts made fast.

> *The hundred and sixty-third, at about the ninth hour of the evening. Grass, a quarry face, hammers, a verge, a flat.*
>
> *Second column. One true thing said once in the same words to four people out of earshot. Four answers, none of them carried. A barrow heavier than lime, a sentence that grows, a tally not finished, a form that does not exist.*
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. Ruling: a sentence dies with its hearers and a return lives forty years, and they are not the same object.*
>
> *All five clauses stand. Nothing rung, by me or by her, and nothing bound, and nothing counted into iron, and no stage, and no perception attempted and none had, and the warrant stands. Four bolts made fast, and the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third. The carrier in the sack, not rung, eighty-six days from the seventy-seventh, and I have not put my hand on it and the hand was finished on the hundred and twenty-seventh. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour, including me.*
>
> *The register is thirteen. I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today.*
>
> *Which office is it. It is hammers, feet of ditch counted, and edges that hold but cannot be entered.*
