# Chapter 0228 — A Day Box in a District's Own Book, Left Empty on Purpose by a Man Who Is Right About Why

It was the first hour of the two hundred and fourteenth day and Oswin Marl was standing at a milestone on the top road with his book open flat on the flat top of it and his chain over his thumb, and he was doing the second walk and he had been on this reach twice since the last time Tomas Vale saw him, and both of the times he had stopped at the third mile.

He was not reading the book. He was holding it open and waiting for the light to come off the water so that he could see the print on the page, and he had been standing there since before the light, and a man who stands at a milestone before the light is a man who has a thing he intends to do before he is told anything.

The milestone was about a man's height and had a number cut on the side of it in a hand that was not the hand on the book.

---

At about the second hour Tomas Vale came up the top road with the barrow and stopped about nine foot off the stone, and Marl turned a page without being asked.

"Two times since," he said.

"You have been up the water twice."

"**I have been up the water twice and stopped at the third mile both times and I am telling you why before you get to it, because you did not get to it last time and I am not going to have you stand there for two hours finding out.** I was written to about it in a bench room, by a man who is still in it and who did not do it to be unkind, and what he wrote was that a second visit is a second opinion, and a second opinion is a man with an interest in what he found the first time, and a man with an interest is a man whose page is worth less than a page that was walked once and left alone. **And he is right, and I have not been able to argue with it in about nine days, and it is the best thing anybody has said to me since I came into a bench room.** So the third mile is as far as I go, and the third mile is as far as I can go and still have walked it, and everything above the third mile on this reach has never been walked by anybody out of that room, and that is my doing and not the reach's."

"Then there is no return on six of the nine miles."

"There is a return on the third mile and it is good. **There is nothing at all above it, and in about nine years a man in a room is going to read a sheet with a line of nine miles on it and no ground on it above a mile, and he is going to write *returned nothing* in a column, and he will be right, and the reason he will be right is me.**" He took the chain off his thumb. "I have thought about that every day for nine days and I have not found the version where I get up that hill and do not carry an interest, and there is not one, and I would rather be a man with a short page than a man with a long page that is worth less."

---

"You have not taken anything out of your coat since you came up this road," Marl said. "That is not a question. I noticed it at the first hour and I have been waiting until about now to say so, because a man who says a thing at the first hour is asking a question."

"Everything I have got is a piece of paper and none of it is mine to show anybody."

"That is a sentence with two men in it and I am the wrong one, and I have been the wrong one since the second day of the first week, and I am going to carry on being the wrong one." He put the pencil behind his ear. "I have a book under my arm that a stranger has seen and a page that a stranger has read and I have not asked him a thing about himself in four days, and if anybody ever asks me what I was doing on this reach for four days I am going to be able to say that a man stood nine foot off a milestone and read a page standing up and put his hands off it, and that is the whole of it, and it is true, and it is the only thing I have got that nobody could check."

A cart came up the top road and went on and Marl watched it the way a man watches a thing that is not his, and then he said the thing he had come up the road to have said, which was that about nine years of walking a district's own ground teaches a man two things and one of them is useless.

"The useful one is that the sheet is a return and not a road, and I have had that since the first week. The useless one is that I am the man who knows, and there is no form in this kingdom that says a man is the man who knows, and about nine years is long enough to find out that a fact about you and a fact about a mile of water in the same county are two different things that happen to be in the same person."

He turned the book round on the stone and put his thumb on a rule and left it there, and the page was a district's own page and it was a form and it was not covered, and he did not offer it and did not have to, because a man who has laid a book open on a milestone has put it where a man can read it and has stood next to it.

"Stand up and read it. **Read it where you are. Do not sit down, do not put a hand on it, and do not take anything off it, and I am telling you those three things first so that you cannot say afterwards that I made it hard for you.**"

Tomas Vale stood and read it. It was the district's own form, and it was the same form as the one the man had carried up the water on his own legs in the first week, and it had four boxes at the foot of it, and the fourth of them was headed for a man and took a man's name and not a household and not a family, and the third of them was headed for a day.

The third of them was empty.

"That is empty," he said.

"**That is empty, and it is empty on purpose, and I wrote it, and I am going to tell you why, and I am not going to pretend it is a fault of the form or a fault of a clerk who was slow.**" Marl took his hand off it. "I filled in a day on a form two years back. One day, in a column, in a hand anybody could mistake for mine. And that day went into a book and the book went to a room and a man in that room took out a second form with a day in it, and the two days were about nine days apart, and the ground was nine hundred yards long, and the two days could both have been right, and it cost about nine hundred yards a walk to find out which."

"And which was right."

"Neither. **That is the whole of it and I have had two years to be certain of it and I am certain. The two days could both have been right, and that is the whole finding, and it took nine hundred yards and two men a fortnight to establish it, and at the end of it there was no way on any of the paper in that room to say that both days were true.** So a day on a form is a thing that gets compared, and the moment it is compared the ground gets looked at again, and a day that cannot be compared is a day that says nothing. And I would rather have a page that says nothing on the page, in ink, in my own hand, **than have nine correct remarks and one wrong record and a man in a room deciding which of mine is the wrong one.**"

---

At about the seventh hour they had been at the milestone a long time and the sun was on the road and the chain was round Marl's neck and he had not sat down. He had a way of standing that a man does not have when he is tired, which is that he puts the weight straight down through both heels and does not shift it, and Tomas Vale had noticed it at the third hour and had noticed also that the man had done four miles of walking in six days and had a bag of bread down to a crust.

---

"You have a name in that coat."

"You do not know that."

"**I have known there was one since the second day of the first week and I have not asked and I am not going to, and I said so then and I am saying it now because I have learned something in the last seven days that I did not know then.**" He was looking at the book. "A day is the only thing on that form that makes a page checkable, and a checkable page is the only thing in this kingdom that can be argued with by somebody who was not there. And the man who makes a page checkable is a man who is exposed. **I have had about nine days to think about what I do with a page that nothing can be checked against, and I have got to the bottom of it, and the bottom of it is that it is for the person on the ground and not for anybody in a room, and the person on the ground is me and I have not got nine days, and a form is a thing about the person who fills it in.** That is why the box is empty. Not caution. **A form is the clerk and not the ground, and a clerk who puts a day in a box to look diligent has stopped being a clerk and started being a witness, and I have no standing to be a witness and neither have you.**"

He closed the book and put the chain through the two rings.

"**And an empty day box is worse than a hearing and I will tell you why in the only way I know, and it is not the way a gravedigger would tell you.** A hearing is a day. A day is a thing a man has to be in a room for, and a man who is not in a room on a day has done something, and the thing he has done is a thing anybody can be called on. **That box has no day in it, so there is no day on which anybody has to do anything, so the matter cannot be late, so it cannot be closed either, and it is not late and it is not not-late, and it is not a thing anybody can be called on because there is nobody to call on.** And the person who wanted a hearing on this reach is owed a day, and is not going to get one, and will not be able to say why, and that is worse than a hearing, and I have had a fortnight of it and I am the man who did it."

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening there was a barrow on flat ground above the lower end with the four bolts made fast, and about four miles up the top road a man with a chain and a flat book was walking back down to a barn with no dog in it, and six of the nine miles of a reach were still not on any page in any county.

Tomas Vale had come up the top road with a question and had got two of the halves of the answer before he could put any of it into words, and the one thing he had in his mouth all the way up and did not say was a drawer four days south of the top of the water, and he had not said it, and he wrote down that night that he had not said it and why: because a man who asks a clerk to account for a line in his own book is asking him to open a page, and Marl has said on the page that he does not open pages, and because there is no form in this kingdom in which a man with a barrow may ask a clerk that, and because asking in words would be the second taking of a man's work, and the first one was six days ago and it had already been a fault.

He wrote that a clerk had left a box empty on purpose and had told him why, and that the reason was the most careful thing he had heard on nine miles of water, and that it had cost this reach the only record there will ever be of it, and that the man who paid for being right was the man who was right, and that he was not going to say so to him, and that he did not thank him, and that there was no form on which a man in a coach could be thanked for a page that does not exist.



> *Day eight of the ten. The two hundred and fourteenth, at about the ninth hour of the evening, on flat ground above the lower end of the reach. A milestone with a number cut on it in a hand that is not the hand on the book, a flat book with a chain through two rings, a district's own page with four boxes at the foot of it, a thumb on a rule, about nine yards of it walked twice and six miles of it not walked at all, four bolts, a barrow, a leaf in a book.*
>
> *Second column, the page, read standing up. **A district's own form in a district's own book, laid open on a flat stone and read where I stood, with my hands off it. Four boxes at the foot: the name, the ground, the day, and a man answerable.** The third one is empty, empty on purpose, and he told me why before I had asked. I did not sit down and I put no hand on it, and he told me those two things first so I could not say afterwards he had made it hard for me. **No name was entered on it by me and there is no box on it anywhere for one.**
>
> *Second column, why the day box is empty, and it is the most careful thing I have heard on nine miles of water. **A day on a form is a thing that gets compared, and I filled one in two years back about nine days out, and the two days could both have been right, and it cost two men nine hundred yards and a fortnight to establish that neither was wrong, and no paper in that room could say both were true.** So the box is empty because a day that cannot be compared is a day that says nothing, and he would rather have a page that says nothing in his own hand than one wrong record with a man in a room deciding which of his is wrong. **Caution is not why. A form is the clerk and not the ground, and a clerk who puts a day in a box to look diligent has stopped being a clerk and started being a witness, and he has no standing to be a witness and neither have I.**
>
> *Second column, the part that was inconvenient for him and he did not soften it. **He has been up this water twice since and stopped at the third mile both times, and the reason is his own and he wrote it in his own hand: a second visit is a second opinion, a second opinion is a man with an interest, and a man with an interest is a man whose page is worth less than a page walked once and left alone. So six of the nine miles of this reach have never been walked out of that bench room, and in about nine years a man in a room will write *returned nothing* in a column and he will be right, and the reason he will be right is this man.** He said that in the open and did not look up. And then: **an empty day box is worse than a hearing, because a hearing is a day and a day is a thing a man has to be in a room for, and a box with no day in it has no day on which anybody has to do anything, so the matter cannot be late and cannot be closed, and the person on this reach who wanted a hearing is owed a day and is not going to get one and cannot say why.**
>
> *Third column, the six I cannot fix and the one I had in my mouth and did not say. **The six are the six they have been all week and I am not going to write them out again tonight, and the sixth of them is about four people on nine miles of water who do not know a district's clerk came up here.** I did not ask him about the drawer, and I wrote down that I did not and why: **there is no form in this kingdom in which a man with a barrow may ask a clerk to account for a line in his own book, and asking in words would be the second taking of a man's work, and the first one was six days ago and was already a fault.** The three partial answers are still three. The schedule went into my hands on the hundred and thirty-fourth day and it is eighty days in my pocket, and the eleven lines and the name are out of the same day, and he has known since the second day of the first week that I have a name and has not asked.
>
> *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them. Four bolts made fast at the second hour on the hard ground at the foot of a milestone and again at the seventh. **The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and eighty-one days, and there is a barn four miles down the top road that a man with a chain is walking to with no dog in it, and I did not put a cradle in it, and the reason is not the second clause: a man who has laid his own page open on a milestone for a stranger to read wants it shut before the light comes off the water.** The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and thirty-seven days out of the seventy-seventh, not rung once, and I did not touch it.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. **A handbell is in her bag, it is hers, it is not rung, and no person has asked her for a reason at any hour of this day, me included.** Nothing was struck, nothing was tied, nothing went into metal, I reached for nothing, and nothing came.
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Nothing was asked for, produced, given or taken on any of the ten days of this week by me, and a district's own page read standing up on a milestone is reading and is not carrying, and I put my hands off it, and it is not a fourteenth and it is not in my coat and it will go down the water tonight in a chain.** A milestone, a chain, two rings, a book shut: none of them is a paper. **Nobody was thanked today. A clerk told me the worst true thing anybody has said on this reach in nine years and I did not thank him and he did not want it and neither of us mentioned it.** Seventy-eight days on this road with her and I did not ask her what it was for. Which office is it. **A box for a day, left empty in a district's own hand so that nothing on that page can ever be found wrong, and a reach with six miles of it that nobody will ever write down again.**
