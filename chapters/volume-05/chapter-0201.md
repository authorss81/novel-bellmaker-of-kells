# Chapter 0201 — The Morning After the Low Quarter, and a Reach Nobody Had Named for Him

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and eighty-seventh day and the woman at the fourth door in the low quarter of Hallowmere was on her own step with a bucket and a scrap of board, throwing the water off her own flags where it had been standing all night, and she had been at it since the second hour because it had not stopped coming out from under the wall of the fourth house and it was not going to stop today, and Tomas Vale came down the nine stone steps with the barrow behind him and stopped at the bottom one, because a woman with a bucket on her own step is doing a job and a man with a barrow is not part of it.

The water had gone down five foot off the flat in one night and left everything it had been standing in lying flat and shining. The nine stone steps came out of the low quarter's ground at an angle and the bottom three of them were black and there was a line of dirty water on the fourth one, and the woman's step was the highest dry flagstone on that side of the passage.

Tamsin Rook was four steps above him with her hand flat on the stone rail at the top of the stair, and she did not come down, and nobody said anything to her, and he did not look at her twice.

"It is not fair, it is a warning, and I have said it out loud so that it is a warning and not a complaint." She put the board down flat and drew the water towards her with it, in one stroke. "Nine houses on this row had water in them and it has gone out of eight of them, and the ninth is this one, and it is coming out of the wall of it and not out of the floor, and there is a drain at the far end of this passage nine foot from my door and it is doing nothing, and I have known it was doing nothing since I came out on the step, and I am not finding out today either, so do not come back and ask me a second time."

"That is fair."

"It is not fair, it is a warning." She straightened up and looked at him. "You have come down those steps with a thing on a cart twice in three days and stopped on the bottom one both times. If you come down a fourth time I am going to be pleased about it, and I shall want to know what it is for, and there is no form in this kingdom on which you could put that down."

---

At about the fifth hour the four of them were on the flat and the barrow was on beaten earth with a stone at each wheel, and Ned Adley was on the top step with a hod over his shoulder that he did not put down, and he said he was not coming before anybody had asked him, and the reason was that a soft place on the road to the east had gone again in the wet and there was nobody on that road but him who filled soft places, and he was going up there this morning and would be there until it was mended.

Nobody asked him for more than that. He went up the bank with the hod and did not look back, and he was right, and a soft place on a road that a loaded cart finds is a thing that kills a horse, and there was no version of the day in which a man with a barrow was more use on that road than a man with a hod.

Pell Okell had a barrow of his own across the top of the steps and was sitting on the axle of it. Isa Trevail had a barrow of lime and a yoke and no horse, the yoke over the barrow, the barrow on the ground, and she had come up the steps on her own legs and would go down them the same way and said so.

"Four," Pell said, looking at the four of them. "There is Bel Tremayne and there is me and there is you two, and Adley has gone up the bank, and that is the arrangement and it was the arrangement four days ago and none of us agreed to it."

"It was not agreed to. It happened four times."

"You keep saying that as though it were different."

"It is not different. Nobody has signed anything." He turned round so that he was facing the low quarter and the yard behind the steps, which nobody on that flat could see. "Here is what I am telling all of you and then I am walking. There are nine houses of this front row wet and two hundred and twenty-nine people dry, and I can account for which and I cannot account for why. There are about forty people in that yard behind those steps who were never told anything by anybody at any hour on the day the water came, and they are not going to be told anything now, and I did not build a last leg into that yard and I am not going to stand at the top of a woman's yard and take thirty people off her. There is a barge upright in seven foot of water at the upper landing with nine tons of dressed stone under her. And there is a dressed sill half in on a second pair of gates nine miles up, with the lime out of the top joint, and that is about nine days, and the rate for it is to be set in a fortnight by nine men who will not agree about it."

"And the yard," Pell said.

"And the yard. I wrote the yard down first and I am going to write it down first again, because it is the one I would be able to stop doing about." Tomas took the leaf out of the inside of his coat, on the back page where the other things were, wrote a line on it, and shut the book. "None of those five is mine to fix. Not one. I have said that out loud to you and I am not asking you to do anything about any of them, and I am not thanking anybody, because there is no form in this kingdom in which gratitude is entered."

Nobody said anything, which is the correct thing for four people to do when a man with a barrow has told them he is leaving.

---

At about the sixth hour he made the four bolts fast where the frame sat, which is the thing he does at the fourth hour of every place the frame is set down, and the frame was in its mounting frame in its reinforced padded cradle on the barrow with nothing in it that can strike, and a child-sized wax handprint was in the crown of it, and the lip of it was warped, and a thin copper jumper was soldered across a coupling that was cut nearly through.

It is a hundred and fifty-four days since the frame has been in a building. There had been a barn mouth on that flat nine foot off the barrow all morning with a roof over it and beaten earth under it, and a frame in a cradle is a building-sized thing for a bell and the mouth of that barn was four foot high, and he had looked at it and had not thought about it again, and the reason went in the book in the ordinary way, which was that a barrow that goes into a building comes out of it differently.

Mab Sarter came down off the wall and stood at the sack against the cradle and looked at the crown of the carrier in it without touching it, the way a person looks at a thing in a sack in somebody else's yard.

"That is the one that is not rung."

"That is the one that is not rung. You have not rung it since the seventy-seventh day."

"Not since the seventy-seventh." He put his hand flat on the cradle rail, which is not the crown and is not the bell and is a piece of wood that came from a wright in another county. "A hundred and ten days. Four times in its life. Two of those were one occasion with two blows on a leather before a man signed for it. One was in a market square in front of about nine hundred people, and one was in a market square in front of about nine hundred people on a later day, and I am not going to tell you which of them cost me more, because I do not know."

"Does it get dull."

"It does not get dull. It does not get anything. I put my hand on the crown of it four times in its life and the fourth time was the last time and I knew it was the last time while I was doing it." He took his hand off the rail. "There is a thing I am going to say once and then not again. For a hundred and ten days, on nine miles of water, I have been the only man anybody has ever slowed down for, and there is no reason for it that I can put in a sentence, and I am not stronger today than I was a year ago and I am not going to be. If you want to know what I am, I am a wheel."

Pell Okell laughed, once, and stopped.

---

At about the seventh hour they were on the road above the water, four of them and a barrow, and the road went along the top of a bank and then left the water and came back to it, and the country on both sides of it was a clay flat with ditches in it, and every mile Tomas Vale asked about produced the same sentence in a different mouth.

He had asked twice in nine days about the reach he was on, and both times he had been told the same thing, which was that the reach is on the sheet and nothing else about a river is. That reach was behind him. This one was not behind him.

He asked a man at a gate about the water and got, in about nine seconds, a flat refusal and a correction: the water had no name, it was called the reach, everybody called it the reach, and there was a name on a sheet four counties off that the man had seen once and would not repeat out of a memory like that.

He asked a woman at a pitch about the next place down and she gave him nine names for four miles and would not say which of them was on anything, and one of the nine was not a place at all and she said so, and one of them was a word for a ford.

And at a gate at the top of a long pull a carter he did not know put a hand on his own gatepost and said the place ahead was called Aldbarrow, and Tomas Vale wrote Aldbarrow down, and the carter then said that Aldbarrow was the name of a place two miles on and not this one, and that this one was called Ostwick, and that both were correct, and that if a man wanted the third name he could have it at the wall at the third mile, and then said the third name is not much use to anybody and that he had said it because there is nobody on this road to say it to.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening they were on beaten ground above a reach that has no name on any sheet he has ever been shown, and there was a fire going in a yard behind a building on the far side of it, which is a thing you can see at a mile and which means that about nine people are inside it and are not hidden from anything.

> *Day one of the ten. The hundred and eighty-seventh, at about the ninth hour of the evening. A flat of beaten earth, nine stone steps, a bucket and a board on a step, a frame on a barrow in a cradle with four bolts, a sack against the cradle, a gully nine foot from a door that is not moving water, a barrow-load of lime and a yoke, a wall with a chalk in it, a gate, nine names for four miles, a yard with a fire in it a mile off.*
>
> *Second column. **Eight of the nine houses on that row are out and one is not, and the one is the fourth, and what is coming out of it is coming out of a wall and not off a floor, and there is a gully at the end of that passage nine foot from where she stands that has not moved water since before the water came, and she has been watching it since the second hour and has decided against knowing why.** Nine of us were in that yard and nine of us are not in it and two hundred and twenty-nine people out of the low quarter are dry and I cannot account for why. **Ned Adley is not coming and gave his own reason before anybody asked him and nobody asked him for more.**
>
> *Second column, the four. **It is four and it is not an arrangement and there is nothing in writing anywhere.** Pell Okell says it happened four days running and that we have not signed anything, and that is true, and the reason it is true is that I will not take the same words to two people and come back with two the same, and I have not taken the same words to two people. **I told all four of them the five things I cannot fix and I asked them for nothing and I thanked nobody, and there is no form in this kingdom in which gratitude is entered.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. **A hand went on the crown of that carrier four times in its life and the fourth was the last and I knew it while I was doing it, and I did not put it on today.** This is a different reach and I have never been shown it on anything. A man at a gate called the place ahead Aldbarrow and then said Aldbarrow is two miles on and this one is Ostwick and that both are right, and that the third name is at the wall at the third mile, and then said the third name is not much use and that he had said it because there is nobody on that road to say it to. **I have a sheet in my inside pocket with three creases in it and a rectangle about two hands together in it that four men looked at in a bench room and did not explain, and it is fifty-three days in my pocket, and I still cannot read a run, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man is taught, and so there is no version of being better at one.**
>
> *All five clauses stand and I am not going through them again today. **Nothing was struck, nothing was tied, nothing went into metal, and I did not reach for anything and nothing came.** Four bolts made fast at the fourth hour, and **the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and fifty-four days, and there was a barn mouth nine foot off this barrow all morning with a roof on it and I did not put a cradle in it, and a barrow that goes into a building comes out of it differently.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. Eight houses of that front row are dry and a gully nine foot from a door is not, and I know which house has not come out and not the reason. The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and ten days out of the seventy-seventh, not rung once in that time, and I did not touch it today.** **Her bell is in her bag and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour today, including me.**
>
> *The register is thirteen. **I have produced nothing and asked for nothing and been given nothing and taken nothing, and nobody was thanked today, and a woman at a fourth door told me about her drain before I had got to the bottom step and I have not carried one word of it away from her.** I was fifty-one days on this road with her and I did not ask her what it was for. **Being the only wheel on nine miles of water is worth exactly nothing and I have said that out loud to four people and one of them laughed.**
>
> *Which office is it. **A drain nine foot from a door that is doing nothing, and a name on a gate that belongs to a place two miles on, and nobody on a road to say the third name to.**
