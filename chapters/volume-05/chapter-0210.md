# Chapter 0210 — The Reach Is a Place People Live, and the Two Lists Are the Same Length

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and ninety-sixth day and Wilna Crease was shutting the shop, and she shut the shop at the fourth hour on a day when a barrow was going to be standing against the butt end of it by dark, because a shopkeeper who knows where a barrow is going to be shuts her shop on the way to it rather than after, and Tomas Vale came up the outside stair behind her with no hands in his pockets and asked her whether he could come up and say a thing in front of people.

"You can," she said. "Say it in the room and not in the lane, and I will put the kettle on because it is that kind of thing and not because I am glad."

The room was over the shop. There were nine people in it by the sixth hour, most of them on the floor, and Bel Tremayne was at the table with her book shut in front of her, and Isa Trevail had come up from the lower end with a yoke over her shoulder and had put it in the corner where a thing gets put when its owner is going to stay a while.

"You have been on this reach ten days," Wilna Crease said, from the table. "Say what you have got."

"I have got three things I cannot make into one sentence, and I am going to say them badly and you are going to stop me wherever I start making them sound better than they are."

"That is a reasonable arrangement and it is the first one anybody has offered me in about two years."

---

At about the sixth hour he said it, and the room did not make a noise, and he did not say it well.

"I came up this reach to get to a thing that is two miles up the water, and I have got three sentences about what it is and cannot make one out of them, and a woman at a landing told me there is a name on a district's sheet for a mile that is not that mile, and the district is right and the name is not a lie, and there is no form on which a man with a barrow may ask for it to be changed. And a man at a wall has kept that thing going for nine years with a besom and a column with nothing in it, and would not make his three sentences into one either, and was right about every part of it, and I have no network, no keeper, no agreement and no bell, and I got all of that in nine days and none of it is anybody's fault."

He stopped, because he had heard himself.

"Now the part where I have to be stopped. I am about to tell this room that a man swept a piece of ground for nine years, and a woman at a door gave me the true sentence about a node in one go, and a man at a lock could not refuse me and told me why, and a waller gave me a rule about mending that is the truest thing anybody has said to me in five volumes. **None of those people is better off than they were on the day I arrived, not one is going to say a word about it to anybody, and if I stand here and let this room think they were honoured then I have turned four trades' worth of work into a story about me.**"

"Then do not let us," said Wilna Crease.

"That is what I am trying not to do and I am doing it badly."

"You are doing it about as badly as a man can who is trying."

---

At about the seventh hour the nine of them had it in front of them, all of it.

"Now say the other one," Wilna Crease said. "You have said what you have got. Say what the place is, and say it in this room, and do not say it kindly."

"Tallow Reach is a place people live."

"Say the rest of it."

"Nine places with people in them and about nine miles of water, and a thing two miles up that four people and a long habit are holding up between them, and nobody is waiting for it and nobody has been waiting for it for nine years."

"**That is the sentence, and I have never had it before, and you did not give it to me, you took it out in front of nine people, and I am not going to pretend I liked it.**" She put her hand flat on the closed book. "And now the part that makes it useless, and then this evening is over. A place people live is not a thing anybody has to do anything about. There are nine places on this water with people in them and a fire going in eight of them tonight, and that will not change because a man with a barrow stood in my room and said it, and if you go up that bank tomorrow and tell anybody I said the other half, I will say you made it up."

"Nobody is telling anybody."

"That is the first thing you have said all evening that I believe, and I have believed about four things in two years."

There were four of the ten days in it that he had asked for something and been refused in words, and not one of the four refusals was cruel and not one of them had the same shape as any of the other three.

A waller at a stone yard would not say what a thing was in a sentence, and gave three true things in the grammar of his own trade, and would not be asked twice.

A man at a landing would not change a word in a district's book, and gave the reason that there is no form on which a man with a barrow may ask for it, and did not go back to the board when the same question was put to him in front of nine people.

A man at a lock could not refuse a thing he had been asked in words with no form, and said so, and said that the only way to find out what refusing would be was to go up the bank, and that the going was the damage.

A woman at a door gave him the sentence he came for and then said the thing that made it useless, and being right about that was the hardest of the four to sit with.

"Four trades," Tomas said. "Four grammars. Not one of them the same as any of the other three, and not one of them unkind, and I have been refused four times in ten days by people who owed me nothing and were not cruel and were not frightened and are not waiting for me to come back and be different."

"And what is the sentence under all four of them," Isa Trevail said, from the corner with the yoke. "You have not said the sentence."

"I have not got one. I have got three in my own book on one line with two bars in it and the bars are my own invention and there is no form in this kingdom that says what a bar between two true things means, and I have been carrying three of them in this book for about two hundred days and they still do not fit and I have stopped expecting them to."

"Then that is what you have got," Wilna said, "and I will tell you what it is worth on this reach, and I am telling it as arithmetic and not as a kindness. **There are nine places on this water with people in them and there is a fire going in eight of them this evening, and there is no box anywhere in this kingdom that would hold all of them at once, and a man with a barrow is a thing that stops people.** In about nine years the four people on my left-hand page will have mostly stopped being able to put a hand on a gate. The eight on the right did that in about a fortnight. The reach is not waiting for you and it has not been waiting for you for nine years, and it did not know you were coming, and it will not know you have gone."

"I have not said a word about leaving."

"You did not have to. I have had a barrow against my butt end for ten days and I have counted the nights."

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening there were four people on the reach and a barrow on flat ground with the four bolts made fast at the foot of a low wall, and about four hundred yards down at the lower end of the water there was a bench room with a table in it and about eleven people in it and two of them on opposite sides of it who had been there since before the light went.

Tomas Vale went down and asked the same question of each of them, in the same words, got two true sentences, wrote both on one line in his own book with a bar between them, and carried neither to the other man. The second time he did it in his life he found out he already knew the whole of it, and wrote that down as a fact and not a discovery.

The first man said the reach had been fine for nine years and would be fine for nine more and that a thing nobody has to answer for is a thing nobody can be moved off.

The second said that in about nine years this reach had been asked nine times for something and had said yes four times, and that the four yeses were all in the same week, and that a reach which says yes four times in a week and nothing for the rest of it is a reach that is being polite.

They are both true. He has now asked the same question at two benches on purpose twice, and written four sentences on two lines with two bars in them, and carried none of the four anywhere, and he is the clerk of nothing and he keeps no terms, and no person in either room knows that he asked anybody at all.

Tamsin Rook was on the step of the bench room with her hand flat on the frame of the door, in the last hour of it, and nobody asked her a question at any hour of that day including him, and he was sixty days on this road with her and had not asked her once what it was for, and neither of those two facts is a scene.

He had not put the name in a column, was not going to, and was not going to say it out loud in a room of nine people, because there is no form on this reach on which a sentence in a book about a woman is anything but a sentence somebody can find in forty years and act on.

He asked to leave the two lists with her, on the outside stair at about the ninth hour, so there would be two copies of a thing on this water, and she said no in one sentence and gave a reason.

"**A second copy of a book of refusals is a thing that gets produced.** A list that lives in one room on one table gets read by the people who come into that room, and a copy of it in a coat in a different county gets read by a man in an office who is checking something. You have wanted to put a thing in a second place for a hundred and ninety days and every time you have wanted to it has been so that a person could not get at it by walking to you. I am not going to be a second place."

He did not take them. He had asked, and she had said no in one sentence, and he did not argue, and it is the fourth thing in ten days that has cost a person something and been worth nothing to him.

> *Day ten of the ten. The hundred and ninety-sixth, at about the ninth hour of the evening, on flat ground above the lower end of the reach. A shop shut at the fourth hour, nine people, six on the floor, two books shut on a table, a yoke in a corner, a bench room with about eleven in it, two men on opposite sides of it, a low wall, four bolts, a barrow, a leaf with a name on it that is in no column of anything.*
>
> *Second column, the ten days, said flat. **Three sentences about a thing two miles up the water and no way to make them into one. A name on a district's sheet for a mile that is not that mile, the district right, the name not a lie, no form on which a man with a barrow may ask for it to be changed. A man with a besom and a column with nine years of nothing in it.** I got to the part where I would have told that room four trades had been honoured, and heard myself, and was told to stop by the only person in it entitled to. **No network, no keeper, no agreement, no bell, in nine days, and none of it anybody's fault.** A place can be on a sheet under a name nobody at a door uses and have a fire going in it this evening, and both are true.
>
> *Third column, the four refusals, four grammars, joined to nothing. **A waller would not put it in a sentence and would not be asked twice. A man at a landing would not alter a district's book, and gave me the same answer again in front of nine people and I did not go back to the board. A man at a lock could not refuse a thing asked in words with no form, and told me the going up the bank is the damage. A woman at a door gave me what I came for and then took the use out of it.** Not one unkind, not one the same shape as another, every one from a person who owed me nothing.
>
> *Third column, the second time, at the bench room. **I asked the same question at two benches on purpose, in the same words, got two true sentences, wrote both on one line with a bar between them, and carried neither to the other man.** Second time in my life, and I already knew the whole of it, and that is a fact and not a discovery. The first man said the reach has managed for nine years and will manage for nine more, and that a thing nobody is accountable for cannot be shifted off. The second said this reach has been asked nine times in nine years and said yes four times, all four in one week, and that a reach which says yes four times in a week and nothing the rest of it is being polite. **Four sentences, two lines, two bars, carried nowhere. I am the clerk of nothing and nobody in either room knows I asked.** I did not carry the name in the other pocket to either of them, and I am not putting it in a column.
>
> *Third column, the lists. **I asked to leave the two with her so there would be two copies on this water, and she said no in one sentence: a second copy of a book of refusals is a thing that gets produced, and a list that lives in one room on one table is read by the people who come into that room, and a copy in a coat in another county is read by a man in an office who is checking something.** I wanted a second place so a person could not get at it by walking to me. **Four on the left, four on the right, the two pages the same length, and that is neither an oversight nor a scandal: about four in five of anybody she could ask would say no, and a person who has not been asked has not said no, and the four on the right are the four who were asked.** I did not take them.
>
> *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them. The four bolts went in at the fourth hour at the butt end of a shop and again at the seventh, and at about the ninth hour at the foot of a low wall at the lower end of the reach. **The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and sixty-three days, and I have not put it in one, and no clause stopped me and no clause had to.** The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and nineteen days out of the seventy-seventh, not rung once, and it will be a hundred and twenty at whatever day I put the barrow down at the top of this road, and it will not be rung then either.** There is a handbell in her bag and it is hers and it is not rung, and nobody has asked her for a reason at any hour of these ten days, including me. My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. Eight of the nine places on this water have a fire going tonight; one of the nine is this room.
>
> *The register is thirteen, and on none of the ten days was anything asked for, produced, given or taken except a district's own return, read standing up, in a district's own book, nine miles behind me, which is reading and is not carrying. No name was entered on any paper by anybody in these ten days. Nobody was thanked. **Sixty days on this road with her, and I did not ask her once what it was for, and this is the sixtieth and it is a count and not an act.** Nine houses of a front row are wet and two hundred and twenty-nine people are dry, and I can account for which and not why. Forty people in a yard were never told anything. A barge of nine tons of dressed stone is upright in seven foot of water. A dressed sill is half in on a second pair of gates and is about nine days, and the rate is set in a fortnight by nine men who will not agree.
>
> *Which office is it. **A barrow he cannot pay for, a walled square he has stood in, three sentences he cannot join, two lists he was not allowed a copy of, a name in his own hand he has not said out loud, and about four days of road to the top of that sheet, and no form on which a man asks another man for four days of his life.**
