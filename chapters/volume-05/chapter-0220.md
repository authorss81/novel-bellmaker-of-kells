# Chapter 0220 — Nine Sentences, No Sentence, and a Man Who Will Not Say Whether Four Trades Agreeing Is Anything

It was the third hour of the two hundred and sixth day and Aylwin Garrow was sweeping the flat ground inside the low wall with a besom of about nine foot of hazel, and he had been sweeping it since the second hour, and he sweeps it every morning of nine years, and the sweeping takes about an hour, and what it is for is not that anything is on it.

Tomas Vale came up the last four hundred yards on foot and left the barrow nine hundred yards down, the way he had left it eleven days before, and Garrow saw him come and did not stop sweeping and did not say anything at all until he had finished the near end and turned round at the wall.

"You have come up on foot again."

"Yes."

"That is twice in eleven days that you have come up that road without a barrow and I have never worked out what you think that means, and I have decided to go on not working it out, because the two explanations I have are both about you and neither of them is about this ground." He leaned the besom against the post. "Now. You have come up here to say something you have said to me before, which is that you cannot make three sentences into one, and you are going to say the three again, and then you are going to ask me what to do with them, and I am going to tell you before you start that the second half of that is the only part I am interested in and I am not going to help you with it."

---

At about the fourth hour they were both on the flat and he had not written anything out of the book, which Garrow noticed and did not comment on, and the nine stones were in a line across the middle of the ground with the space of about two hands between the fourth and the fifth, and neither of them looked at the space and neither of them said anything about the fact that neither of them was looking at it.

"Go on, then. Say the three."

"I am not going to say the three, because you gave them to me eleven days ago and I wrote them down in a book in your words and I have read them about nine times since and they have not got any closer." Tomas Vale stood about nine foot off with his hands behind him. "**I have nine sentences now. You had three. I have had a waller, a man at a lock, a woman at a door, you, a man at a byre, a man with a chain, a woman with a kiln, a drover, and a farrier. Nine trades, nine grammars, not one of them the same shape as any of the other eight, and none of them will sit next to another one, and I have been carrying them about six days and I have made a row of them in my own book because a book is the only place a row can go, and two men on this water have told me in two different trades not to do it.**"

"And you have done it anyway."

"I have done it anyway and I am telling you that I have done it, and I am not going to pretend that a man telling me not to do a thing is a reason I would not do it." He turned the ring of his hand over.

---

At about the sixth hour the sweeping had been finished for about an hour and the besom was on the nail that is not part of the board, and they were standing at the gap in the nine stones, and Garrow spoke for about as long as it takes to turn round twice.

"**You are going to ask me whether four trades saying true things about their own work and none of them agreeing about anything is a network of a reach. I am not going to answer that, and I want you to hear why, and it is not that I have not got an answer.**" He was not sweeping now. "I have got an answer. If I say yes, then a thing four men said in four trades in four days on a reach where about four in five of anybody would say no is a network of a reach. And I would have made it one. **I would have made it one by saying one word to a stranger, and that is the whole of how every one of them on this water that ever got made was made, and I have watched about nine of them get made and every single one of them was made by somebody saying the word.**"

"And if you say no."

"Then in about nine years somebody comes up that road with a paper, and the paper says this reach, and the reach has got a thing in it that four people do not agree about, and there is no way on the paper to write four people who do not agree, because the paper has a line and a line wants one thing in it. And that is the other way it gets made and it gets made by a man in a county four days off with a box." He turned round and looked at the board on the post of the roof. "**So I am not going to say yes and I am not going to say no, and the reason is not diplomacy and I will not have it written down as anything clever. The only way anybody finds out whether those nine are a network is by being here in nine years, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man is a witness for nine years, and I have not got nine years of anything except a besom.**"

---

At about the seventh hour he said the last of it and he said it while he was hanging the besom, and he said it once and then he said he was not going to say it again.

"The man with the satchel goes down the water in the morning. I know that because he came past at about the fourth hour and did not come up the bank, and he has been on this reach four days and he has not come up this road once, and I have been watching the road every morning for nine years and nobody comes up it, and so I noticed." He set the besom on the nail. "**In about a season he will come back with the same book. He will fill in the same column, and the last of the four boxes will be empty, and he will say the same thing he said, and I will say the same three things I have said, and about four people on this water will have noticed that a district man came and went and did not come up the bank.**"

"And about nine people will say a reach did not want a keeper."

"That is what will be said. Not by me and not by any of the four of them and not by the two women." He turned round. "It will be said in about nine years by men who were not here, in rooms, and it will be the truth as far as anybody can tell from outside, and there is nobody on nine miles of water who is going to correct it, and I am not going to be the man who goes down that reach and says *we did want a keeper, we wanted about nine years of one and nobody ever came up this road to ask us.*"

"Then what do you want me to do with that."

"**Nothing. I want you to do nothing with it and I want you to go away knowing that I know.**" He had the besom off the nail in his hand again. "I said a name to you on this ground eleven days ago, one, out loud, and you did not say it back, and I have thought about that for eleven days, and I have decided that you did not say it back because you had already got it and not because you were careful, and I would like you to know that a man who sweeps a piece of ground for nine years can tell the difference between a man being careful and a man holding something, and you are holding something, and I have not asked you what and I am not going to, and that is the last time I am going to mention it at all."

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening there were four people on the reach and a barrow on flat ground above the lower end with the four bolts made fast, and about four days of road and water away a district's clerk was going down with a flat book in which one middle column had one more line in it than it had on the day he arrived, and two miles up this water a man with a besom was hanging it on a nail that is not part of a board.

Tomas Vale had nine sentences and no sentence, and a bar he had put in his own book across two things on Day 195 and had not put in his book across eight more since, and a bar he had invented for a pair of true sentences is not a bar for nine trades, and he knew that and had known it since about the third hour and had carried the nine up four hundred yards anyway.

He wrote down three things and they were the only three he wrote down.

He wrote down that he had come up that road with a question and had not asked it, and that the man had answered the other half of it before he could get to it, and that this was the third time in about ten days that had happened and that he did not know what to call it and was not going to invent a word for it on a piece of ground where a man keeps a besom.

He wrote down that the fourth and fifth gates below Marrowby and the third gate above it and the wall at the byre and the bank under the row of doors, were five men, and that about four in five of anybody on this reach would have said no, and that the only person on nine miles of water who had a list of the ones who had been asked was a woman over at the lower end with two pages the same length, and that he was not going to go and get the page, and that the arithmetic of that was the batch's and not his.

And he wrote down the decision he had not made, which was that he did not know whether to go up the water or down it, and that going up the water was four days and a barrow he could not pay for and a sheet he could not read, and that there was nobody on nine miles of water he could ask, and that the only two people who had ever told him anything about a woman who went into that thing two miles up this water were at opposite ends of it and neither of them would say it a second time, and that he was going to have to decide that in the morning on his own with nobody to hand, which was the ordinary condition of every decision he had ever made and was not going to be made better by any of the nine.




> *Day ten of the ten. The two hundred and sixth, at about the ninth hour of the evening, on flat ground above the lower end of the reach, and I walked the last four hundred yards again, because a man who sweeps a piece of ground for nine years should arrive at his own place first. A low wall on three sides, nine knee-high stones and a space of two hands between the fourth and the fifth, a besom on a nail that is not part of the board, a board with a keeper’s column and nine years of nothing in it, a barrow nine hundred yards down, four bolts, a leaf in a coat.*
>
> *Second column, the answer I did not get. **I asked him whether four trades saying true things about their own work and agreeing about nothing is a network of a reach, and he said he has got an answer and would not give it.** If he says yes, then four men saying true things in four trades in four days on a reach where about four in five of anybody would say no is a network of a reach, and he would have made it one by saying one word to a stranger, **and he has watched about nine of them get made and every one was made by somebody saying the word.** If he says no, then in nine years somebody comes up that road with a paper that has a line in it, and a line wants one thing, and there is no way on the paper to write four people who do not agree. **The only way anybody finds out is by being here in nine years, and he has not got nine years of anything except a besom.** Then, said once each. **The clerk goes down the water in the morning, and nobody comes up this road, and in about a season he will come back with the same book and the last of the four boxes will be empty. About nine people will say a reach did not want a keeper, in about nine years, by men who were not here, and nobody on nine miles of water is going to correct it.** And he gave me a name on this ground eleven days ago and I did not say it back, **and the clerk had a box that wanted a man and he put his hand on the page when I came near it.***
>
> *Third column, the three things I wrote down. **That I came up that road with a question and did not ask it, and that he answered the other half of it before I got to it, and that this is the third time in ten days and I have no word for it. That five men on this reach turn up, and that the only list of the ones who was asked belongs to a woman and has two pages the same length, and that arithmetic is hers. And that I do not know whether to go up or down, and that up is four days and a barrow I cannot pay for and a sheet I cannot read, and that there is nobody here I could ask.** I have to settle that in the morning on my own. **And the bar: a bar is a thing I invented for two true sentences I was sure were one, and I have had eight more since and I put none across the nine tonight on purpose, because a farrier and a woman who keeps two lists have each told me what I have is four men and may not be nine. There is no bar across the nine, and that is not a resolution.** The schedule went into my hands on the hundred and thirty-fourth day and it is seventy-two days in my pocket, and the name is out of the same day and is seventy-two days, and it is a woman’s name, and I have not used it for anything.*
>
> *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them. Four bolts made fast at the fourth hour at the foot of the low wall and again at the seventh. **The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and seventy-three days, and that is not a reason and not a clause: this ground is a walled square, a roof on four posts and nothing at all over the nine stones, and there is nothing here a cradle could go inside of.** The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and twenty-nine days out of the seventy-seventh, not rung once, and I did not touch it.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. **There is a handbell in her bag and it is hers and it is not rung, and nobody has asked her for a reason at any hour of any of the ten days of this week, including me.** Nothing was struck, nothing was tied, nothing went into metal, and nothing came.*
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Nothing was asked for, produced, given or taken on any of the ten days, and no name was entered on any paper by anybody.** The one permitted thing is a district’s own form, in a district’s own book, about a district’s own ground, read standing up, in a field gate, and one line in a middle column saying that there is ground here and that people live on it, and no name on it anywhere, and that is reading and is not carrying and is not a fourteenth. A drove book, a book of nine hundred names, a rule and a margin, a rack of shoes, a sieve, a chain, a besom: none of them is a document, because no form in this kingdom can produce them. **Nobody was thanked on any of the ten days, and one woman told me a fault in my own conduct and got nothing for it.** Seventy days on this road with her and I did not ask her what it was for. Which office is it. **Nine sentences in a book with no bar across them.***
