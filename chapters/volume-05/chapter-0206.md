# Chapter 0206 — The Man Who Is Not a Keeper, and the Only Way to Find Out What Refusing Would Be Is to Go

It was the second hour of the hundred and ninety-second day and Rell Dray was on a lock ground with a barrow-load of chain in it and a stove going, working on the coping at the head of a lock. The coping is the strip of stone a man puts his boot on when he is shutting a gate against nine foot of water, and Dray was not shutting a gate. He was putting a new length in where the old one had gone soft, and he had his piece out already, and the gap was about the length of his forearm.

Tomas came down the bank with the barrow and stopped at the top of the lock ground, which is nine feet of gravel held in by a wall on the river side and a bank on the land side. There are two things on a lock ground: a lock, and a room about nine foot square with a stove in it whose door shuts.

Dray did not stop working, and did not look round, and said, "You cannot bring that down the bank. The gravel takes it at the point and the barrow will go over, and I have seen one go over into nine foot of water and I am not going to be the man who has to look at it."

"That is the second clause of a receipt in five and not your gravel."

"Then it is both."

---

At about the third hour the coping was done, the stove door open, the barrow still up the bank, Tomas had asked, and Dray had not answered.

The question was not a good one and Tomas knew it before he had got it out, which had happened four times in five volumes and had never once improved the asking.

"You are on a book," Tomas said. "A woman over at the lower end keeps a list of people on this water who have said they would hold a line, and your name is on it, and I want to hear you say it out loud."

"No."

"That was fast."

"Not fast. The same answer four times in eleven years, and I gave it before you had finished the sentence, which is the part I am proud of." Dray laid the coping down and stood. "You have asked me a thing in words, on a bank, with no paper and no clerk and nothing to write it in. Do you know what you have done?"

"Yes."

"Say it back. Everybody thinks they have."

"You have asked me nothing and told me nothing, and there is no form anywhere on which anybody could come back afterwards and ask either of us whether this happened. So I cannot be held to a yes and you cannot be held to a no, and if I said yes it would be a thing that happened in the air on a bank, and in nine years neither of us could prove it, and I would be stood up here in the dark some night with four people and no keeper, because I said a word on a bank once.""

"That is the whole of the objection."

"The first half, and it is the easy half." He sat down on the coping. ""**A refusal is an answer. An answer is a reason. A reason goes in a box so somebody can hold you to it later. I have no standing in this county or in this kingdom, so a no from me would not be a refusal. It would be a sentence in the air with nobody behind it, and any man in a coat who wanted a no out of me would only have to put my face to it, and I would have no way of standing there saying *that is not what I said*.** So I cannot refuse you. Write that down, because you have gone away thinking I am careful. I am not careful. I am a man with no box."

"And you do not want the standing either."

"And I cannot say that, because there is no way in this kingdom to say the second half. **The only way to find out what refusing would be is to go up the bank, and the going is the damage.**" He said it without weight, the way a man says a price he has set himself. "Four doors up that bank and about nine people behind them. If I stood here in the dark and said *no, I am not doing it*, then in a month somebody would come to find out why not, and they would go up the bank, and those four doors would find out there is a man on this reach who was asked and said no, and every one of them would have to decide tonight what to do about me. And I have no way of saying *do not go up that bank* that a man in a coat would hear. So the asking is the damage, the going is the damage, and the not-asking is the only one that costs nobody else, and you have come up a bank and asked me."

---

At about the fourth hour he said, "Now. You have told me what you want. I am going to ask you one thing back and it is not about the line, and I am telling you it is not about the line, because if it were it would be another thing in the air."

"Ask it."

"**What are the four on that road for?**"

He looked up the bank, where four people were sitting on a wall nine hundred yards off with a barrow-load of lime between them.

"They are four people I met on a road," he said. "We are walking the same ground. That is all it is and that is not agreeing about anything. I did not recruit them. I have not asked them for anything. One of them is not coming today and gave his own reason before anybody asked him, and one of them has a book of about nine hundred names in it that nobody has ever asked her what it is for, and one of them is a woman with a barrow of lime and a yoke and no horse who walks up a road because it goes where she is going. I have been calling them legs. I have not said that out loud to them and I am not going to, and I have not got a word in this kingdom in which what I actually want from them goes, and I have wanted something from them since about the fortieth day and I have not asked for it once."

"That is honest."

"A true one, which is not always the same thing, and you should know I know it."

"It changes nothing," Dray said. "I asked whether you had come up a bank to make four people into a thing. You had not. That is worth knowing and it is worth nothing else. I have not said yes and I have not said no."

---

At about the fifth hour they were still on the gravel, the light had gone flat, and the water at the head of the lock was doing what water does there when nothing is happening at it.

"Last part and then I stop," Dray said, "and then you go up that bank, and I am not going to be asked again by you or by anybody you send. I am saying that now so that if I am asked in nine years I can put this conversation in front of whoever asks and they will look foolish rather than me."

"Go on."

"**I have not taken the standing and I have not refused it and I am never going to be able to tell you which of the two I did, and I would rather say that out loud than have you write down one of them.** You will go back up that bank and write *the man at the lock did not take it*, or *the man at the lock would not*, and I am telling you that whichever you write will be true and will not be what happened, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom on which a man can write *he did both and he does not know which*."

He did not say that he had not decided which one he was going to write, because he had not, and he did not say it afterwards either.

"What I will write is the true one, and it will not be a resolution, and I will not improve it, and I will not write down that you did not take it. And I will not write down a non-use of mine, because a non-use is not a use and is not a refusal and I have had four of them in a book for about sixty days and they are not a fifth thing of any kind."

"You are a strange man for a bellmaker."

"I have not made a bell in a hundred and ninety days," Tomas said, "and if I made one tomorrow it would be in a room I have no right to be in, with metal I am not allowed to spend, and no form in this kingdom on which a man is told he may not hold a straight edge either, and I got nine inches out of nine feet with one on the last day of the last year and my hand went."

He looked at him for a while. "Then you are asking me to hold a line on a reach where a man with a bell cannot set his barrow down, and I have been turning that over since the third hour and have no shape for it, and I am not inventing one at this hour."

---

At about the seventh hour there was one thing left on that lock ground that neither of them had mentioned, and Tomas mentioned it, because he had been carrying it two days and there was no third person on that ground to hand it to.

"Three men have said three things to me in about fifty days about holding a line, and the three do not fit one another, and I wrote all three on one line in my own book with a bar between them and carried none of them to the other men. You are a fourth, and what you have just given me is not an answer to the same question the other three answered. It is a fourth thing that fits none of them, and I am not joining it to anything, and a fourth thing that fits none of the three is not a fourth answer."

"I have not got anything else."

"You have not got anything else," Tomas said, "and I have not got a fourth, and there is a man at a bench in another county who has been at that bench nine years and has not got a fourth and has said on the page that he is not going to be the man who says he has. I have carried that sentence round in my coat for ten days and used it this evening without putting it next to yours, and I am writing down that I used it, and I am not going to say which of you I think is right, because I think neither of you is, and I have no way of finding out and no right to.""

The coping needed nine days.

The ninth bench had been going to spend those nine days on a relief bank at the upper end, which is a thing a bench does once every few years, and which is why the gate at the bottom of that reach has been wearing the heel off it for about a year. Dray had had both in his head since the third hour of that day and said nothing about either until later in the same day, and had said nothing about the nine days since, and will not now, and there was a stranger with a barrow in the yard of a lock ground at the ninth hour of the evening.

Nobody thanked anybody for what that cost, nobody had to, and it was not going to be refunded.

> *The hundred and ninety-second, at about the ninth hour of the evening, on the bank above the lock ground. Nine feet of gravel, a stove, a barrow-load of chain, a coping with a new length in it and a gap the length of a forearm, a room nine foot square with a shut door, a barrow at the top of the bank, four bolts.*
>
> *Second column, the line-question, asked here for the first time in five volumes. **A refusal is an answer, an answer is a reason, and a reason is a thing a man puts in a box so somebody can hold him to it afterwards. He has no standing anywhere, so a no from him would be a sentence with nobody behind it, and any man in a coat who wanted a no out of him would only have to put his face to it.** He said he was not a careful man and he was right. **The only way to find out what refusing would be is to go up that bank, and that going is the damage** — four doors, about nine people, no way to be heard saying *do not go*. Asking is the damage, going is the damage, not-asking is the only one of the three that costs nobody else, and I came up a bank and asked him.
>
> *Second column, the question back. **What are the four on that road for.** I said the true thing: I met four people on a road, we are walking the same ground, that is not agreeing about anything, I did not recruit them and have not asked them for anything, and I have been calling them legs without saying so to them and have no word for what I actually want. **A true answer is not always the same thing as an honest one, and he did not need to tell me that.** He asked whether I had come up a bank to turn four people into a thing. I had not. He said it changes nothing, and it does not.
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. **He did not take the standing and he did not refuse it and he cannot tell me which he did, and he made me agree to write the true one down before he would say the rest, and I am not improving it and I am not writing a non-use of mine, because a non-use is not a use and is not a refusal and I have four of them in a book and they are not a fifth thing.** No form anywhere on which a man can write *he did both and he does not know which*, and I am not building one this evening. **The three partial answers are still three. A fourth man has given me a fourth thing and it fits none of the three, and a fourth thing that fits none of them is not a fourth answer.** I used a sentence off a bench in another county and did not put it next to his, and I am recording that I used it, and that I think neither man is right, and that I have no way of finding out and no right to.
>
> *Third column, what it costs. **The coping needs nine days, the ninth bench was going to spend them on a relief bank at the upper end, and a gate at the bottom of that reach has been wearing the heel off it for about a year.** He had both in his head since the third hour and said nothing about either until the fourth, and has said nothing about the nine days since. **I am the man who asked. I cannot tell whether I have done a kindness or a damage, I will not be told, I will not find out, and I want that in the third column and not the second.**
>
> *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them. The four bolts went in at the fourth hour on flat ground at the top of the bank and were still there at dark. **The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and fifty-nine days, and there is a room on that ground nine foot square with a stove in it and a door that shuts, and I did not put a cradle in it, and the reason is not the second clause: the reason is that a man who has been asked a question at about the fourth hour does not go into a shut room on the same ground at about the fifth.** **She was at the head of the bank from about the fifth hour with her hand flat on a coping stone and did not come down the gravel, and I did not go up to her, and that is the same silence as not asking and I have stopped pretending the two are different. Nothing of hers was rung today and nobody asked her for a reason at any hour of it, including me.** The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and fifteen days out of the seventy-seventh, and I did not put my hand on anything today.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. And there is a shut door nine foot square on that ground that I have not been in and am not going to be.
>
> *The register is thirteen, nothing was asked for, produced, given or taken today, no name was entered on any paper by me. **Fifty-six days on this road with her and I did not ask her what it was for**, in the same words I have used for six days, because a man who improves his own arithmetic every day is a man who is enjoying it.
>
> *Which office is it. **A coping with a new length in it, a shut door nine foot square, four doors up a bank, and a man who cannot refuse a thing nobody asked him in writing to refuse.**
