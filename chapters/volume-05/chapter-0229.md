# Chapter 0229 — Three Men Asked in Words With Nothing Written, and Three Answers That Do Not Fit Each Other

It was the second hour of the two hundred and fifteenth day and Curno Balle was cutting the foot of a peat bank with a long spade, and he cuts that bank twice a day, at the second hour and at about the eleventh, and has done for about nine years, and the second cutting is the one that counts because the peat is wetter in the dark and it comes off the face in slabs.

He was up to the thigh in it and the water was coming up behind him and there was about nine hundred feet of face left in the season, and he takes about nine spadefuls a stroke and sets each one on the bank behind him with the face out, and he does not stack them and he does not cover them and he has never once in nine years covered them.

Tomas Vale came out along the bank on foot and stopped at the top of the cut, and Balle went on for four more spadefuls before he turned round, and he was not unfriendly about it.

"You have come to ask me whether I will keep on."

"No."

"**Then I have got a thing out of this morning that I did not have an hour ago, so do not tell me you have not come about something.** What have you come about."

---

At about the fourth hour the face had about four hundred feet left above the water and they were sitting on the bank with the spades across their knees, and the light was coming up off the water and there was about a mile of the reach between them and any roof.

"About nine days ago a man came up this bank in the wet," Balle said. "I do not know him. **He said would you go on cutting this bank, and he said it in about nine words, and there was nothing in his hand, and he did not ask me my name, and he did not give me his.** And I said aye. And that is the whole of what has happened to me in nine days and I have been not-quite-right about it every morning since."

"What were you doing about it."

"Nothing. That is what I have been doing about it." He turned the spade over. "**I cannot say no. I have worked it out and it is not about him and it is not about me and it is not about the bank. A no is a thing you have to give an account of. If I say no, then some time there is going to be somebody in a room who wants to know why a man who has cut this bank for nine years stopped cutting it, and the answer is going to be that a man said nine words to him on a wet bank, and the answer is going to be true, and it is going to sound like an excuse, and an excuse in a room is a thing in a hole in a paper.** So I say aye, and the aye costs me about a morning a week of daylight I could have been cutting, and I pay it, and it is cheaper than a hole in a paper."

"And if he comes back tomorrow with a form."

"**Then I will fill it in, and I will put my name in it, and I will not read the third box, and that is the arrangement I have made with myself and it is the whole of my politics and I have not got another one.**"

---

They had been on the face about two hours and the spade had stopped coming up clean, which is what a face does at about that point, and Balle had gone back along it and cut a new line about nine foot along, in the ordinary way, without saying that he was doing it.

At about the sixth hour they left the bank and went up the lane to the mill, and Jesca Vane was in the mill loft letting the meal cool, and she had been in there since the second hour and there were about nine sacks down and a stone floor with the dust of it in every joint, and she knew what Tomas Vale was before he was in the door, because a mill is a place where a sound arrives before a person does.

"You have been up the peat bank talking to Curno," she said.

"I have."

"**Then you know I have been asked in words too, and I am not going to be surprised and I am not going to be a relief about it, and I am not going to tell you the same thing he told you in the same words, and if the two of you have compared notes you may as well go home, because that is the only reason a person like me would be in this mill with a stranger in it.**"

"What were you asked."

"Go on cutting. The same shape, about nine days, a man in the wet, nothing in his hand." She swept the floor with her hand. "**Here is the part that is mine and his is not. I have said aye about four times since and I am going to go on saying aye, and the reason is not a hole in a paper, it is that a mill is a mill and the price of a no is the mill. If I say no to a man with nothing in his hand, then about nine people who get their meal from this floor find out about it before the week is out, and it is not because anybody is wicked, it is because that is what a mill is. A peat bank is mine. A mill is nine mouths with me in the middle of it. So I pay, and the price of my aye is the mill, and there is no name in this kingdom for a man who pays that on purpose.**"

---

At about the seventh hour they were on the hard ground by the low side of the top road and there was a string of beasts coming up out of the low ground with a woman walking at their heads who had not been to the peat bank and had not been to the mill and had been walking beasts for about nine years, and she was called Nita Frew and she had been asked in words too, and she said so before anybody spoke, and Tomas Vale wrote the three in his own book that night as a peat bank, a mill and a drove, and not as a man with a paper.

He had wanted, for about nine days, to have a thing to ask her. He had gone up that road with a question about the three of them and it had gone out of him somewhere between the mill and the low side, and what replaced it was a question about the low side itself, which was whether a man who has not been asked anything in twenty days and a man who has been asked in words with nothing written can be in the same lane at the same hour and both be free, and he had no answer and did not ask it, and wrote down that he had not asked it and that the not-asking was the ninth of a week and not an act.

"**I am going to tell you so that you do not waste a morning of the light on it, and then I am going to take these up, and you can walk with me if you are going that way and you can hold nothing.**"

"Say it, then, and say it fast."

"About nine days. A man at a gate. Would you go on taking them up this road, and nothing in his hand, and he did not say who he was. **And here is the third one and it is not his and not hers.** A drove has to be fed. That is the first thing anybody learns and it is not a sentence, it is arithmetic. If I say no, then about nine people have to be told why a drove that has gone up this road for nine years is not going up it, and I would have to say it four times in four mornings to four different people at four different gates, and I have not got four mornings, and I am not the woman who is going to have them. **And there is a second thing and I will say it once. A fed thing is a thing that is owed. If I say no, then I am not owed to, and being not owed to is a thing I have to say out loud at four gates, and I have said it at two of them in my life and I am not going to say it at four in one season.**"

"Have you met either of those two men."

"**No. And you have, and he has, and if she has then there is one man doing this and I do not want him described to me, and if there is not one man then I do not want to be told who there is either, and those are both my own reasons and I would like them kept separate.**"

---

At about the eleventh hour Tomas Vale was back on the peat bank in the dark and Curno Balle was cutting it, and the two of them worked the face for about half an hour without saying anything, and then Balle put the spade in and stood on it.

"Two things, and I am saying them because you have not asked me a question and I have found out over nine days that a man who does not ask is the only kind worth talking to."

"Say them."

"**One. I was on this bank at the twelfth the night before and I was in it up to the thigh, and the whole of this reach could have come up that lane and heard me, and nobody did, and that is a fact about nine miles of water and not about me.**" He looked down the lane. "**Two. You have now met about three of us and I can see from your face that you are about to do the thing that I would do, which is to put us together. Do not. We have never met, and I have never told Jesca what you told me and she has never told me anything, and if you get us in a room the three of us will agree about nothing and you will have three men who agree about nothing standing in front of you, and that is not a harder thing to carry than three men who agree about something, it is a softer one, and you will not be able to tell the difference until about nine years.**"

"I had not thought of it."

"You had thought of it and I have watched you not say it, and that is a different thing and I am not going to thank you for it."

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening there was a barrow on flat ground above the lower end with the four bolts made fast, and there was a man cutting the foot of a peat bank in the dark on the sixth mile who had been asked in words to go on doing it and could not say no, and a woman in a mill loft about a mile down who had said aye four times and would go on saying it, and a woman taking beasts up a road about a mile up who could not say no without saying it at four gates, and the three of them had never met.

Tomas Vale had the inherited question in front of him for the third time in a week and the answer in the same shape in three mouths, and the three shapes did not fit one another, and that was not a finding, and he wrote it down as not a finding: **a hole in a paper, the whole of a mill, and nine people at four gates are three different prices for the same yes, and a man who says that is the same thing three times is doing to this reach what a sheet does to a mile of ground, and I am not going to do it.** A refusal is an answer and an answer is a reason and a reason goes in a box, and he wrote that on the page as it stands and did not answer it and did not ask a fourth man, and the two seeds of the other thing he did not think about tonight and wrote down that he did not think about it.

He wrote the six things down, and the ninth, and the eighty-one days of the schedule and the eleven lines and the name, and that a woman at a gate had asked him on the seventh mile for a name and he had said no and she had said aye and gone on up the road, and that the aye had cost her nothing to say and everything to mean, and that he had not thanked her and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man is thanked for a no.



> *Day nine of the ten. The two hundred and fifteenth, at about the ninth hour of the evening, on flat ground above the lower end of the reach. A peat face with about nine hundred feet of it left in the season, about nine spadefuls a stroke, four hundred feet above the water, a mill loft with about nine sacks down and dust in every joint, a string of beasts up the top road, a hard ground by the low side, four bolts, a barrow, a leaf in a book.*
>
> *Second column, the three, and none of them is a keeper and none of them is a network and they are not a party and they have never met. **A man cutting a peat bank at the second hour and at the eleventh, asked about nine days ago by a man in the wet with nothing in his hand: would you go on cutting this bank. A woman in a mill asked the same shape the same week: go on cutting. A woman taking beasts up the top road asked at a gate the same week, in the same nine words, by a man she cannot describe.** Three different mouths and no man in common in any two of the three accounts, and I asked two of them who and the third told me before I asked and asked me not to describe the man to her, and I am keeping that separate from the asking because she asked for it separately.
>
> *Second column, three prices for one yes, and the three do not fit. **A peat bank is his alone, and a no is a thing he would have to give an account of, and an excuse in a room is a thing in a hole in a paper, so he pays a morning a week of daylight and it is cheaper than the hole.** A mill is nine mouths with her in the middle of it, and the price of a no is the mill, and she will go on saying aye, and there is no name in this kingdom for a man who pays that on purpose. **A drove has to be fed, a fed thing is a thing that is owed, and to say no she would have to say *I am not owed to* out loud at four gates in four mornings, and she has said it at two gates in her life.** Three prices and one yes. A man who says that is the same thing three times is doing to this reach what a sheet does to a mile of ground, **and so I have not done it, and I have written the three prices and not the one yes, and the three do not fit each other and I am not going to make them.**
>
> *Second column, the question I have not answered and am not going to. **A refusal is an answer. An answer is a reason. A reason goes in a box so that somebody can hold you to it later.** That is on the page as it stands and I have had it three times this week in three trades and I am not answering it and I did not ask a fourth man tonight, and I did not ask the man on the flat ground, and I did not ask the woman at the ford a second time.  He told me at the eleventh hour not to get them together, because three men who agree about nothing is a softer thing to carry than three men who agree about something and you cannot tell the difference for about nine years. **I have not got them together. I did not have it in my mouth and I watched myself not have it, and that is not the same as being told not to, and I am writing the two apart so a later pass can see which one it was.**
>
> *Third column, the six I cannot fix and the ninth that is not mine. **The six are the six they have been all week, and the ninth is about nine roofs on the fourth mile with no thatcher on them and a stack of reed going in a yard a man cannot get at.** The three partial answers are still three. The schedule went into my hands on the hundred and thirty-fourth day and it is eighty-one days in my pocket, and the eleven lines and the name are out of the same day, and a woman at a gate asked me for the name tonight on the seventh mile and I said no and she said aye and went on up the road.
>
> *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them. Four bolts made fast at the second hour on the hard ground at the top of the peat cut and again at the eleventh. **The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and eighty-two days, and there was a mill loft about a mile down with a stone floor and a woman in it, and I did not put a cradle in it, and the reason is not the second clause: that floor is the one floor on nine miles of water I would have wanted to put a straight edge across, and I have not held one since the hundred and thirty-second, and there is no form in which a man can be told he may hold one either, and I did not go in and find out.** The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and thirty-eight days out of the seventy-seventh, not rung once, and I did not touch it.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. **A handbell is in her bag and it is hers, it is not rung, and nobody has asked her for a reason at any hour of any of the nine days of this week, including me.** Nothing was struck, nothing was tied, nothing went into metal, and I did not reach for anything, and nothing came.
>
> *The register is thirteen. **No paper has been asked for, produced, given or taken by me on any of the ten days of this week, and no name has been entered on any paper by me, and a peat face and a mill floor and a string of beasts are not documents, because there is no form in this kingdom on which any of them can be produced.** **Nobody was thanked today. I asked two of the three who had asked them and neither could tell me, and one told me before I asked and asked me not to carry the man, and I have carried nobody's answer to anybody.** Seventy-nine days on this road with her and I did not ask her what it was for. Which office is it. **Three prices for one yes, and a box that a refusal goes into, and nine miles of water on which about nine people have been asked in words with nothing written and about nine who have not been asked at all.**
