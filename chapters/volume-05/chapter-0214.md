# Chapter 0214 — A Drove of Beasts on Four Miles of Bank, and a Number That Is Wrong Every Ninth Day

It was the first hour of the two hundredth day and Jory Selman was moving eleven head out of the fold at the top of the fourth mile, and eleven is not the number they are, it is the number that came out, and by the third hour of the same day it will have been fourteen and by the fourth hour of the day after it will have been nine, and every one of those numbers will have been correct when it was said.

He walked behind and to the left, with a stick about four foot of hazel cut off square, and a whistle on a string round his neck that he did not use, because a whistle is for a dog and he has not had a dog since about the fourth year.

Tomas Vale came up the cart way with the barrow and Selman put the stick across the beasts' path without stopping them.

"You will not get past here for about a quarter of an hour. They go four miles of bank in about nine hours and they are strung out over a hundred and forty yards, and if a wheel comes into the gap at the wrong end it goes through the whole length of it."

"I will wait at the wall."

"You will wait at the wall and you will not put a book out while I am moving them, and that is not a rule, it is because about nine of them have never seen a stranger standing still in a field and two of them have opinions about it."

---

At about the second hour they were a quarter of a mile down the bank, the beasts were strung out, and Selman had come back up the line on foot twice, counting.

"You have not asked me one thing in an hour," Selman said.

"You said not to ask while you were moving them."

"I said not to ask while they were moving, and they have been moving for four hours, and I have been watching you not ask for about three quarters of that." He shifted the stick. "Ask it, and I will answer it, and then you can stand there again."

"Will you go on holding a line for this water."

"**No, and it is not because I will not. It is that there is nothing on this bank for me to hold, because the line is a road with beasts on it and I am on it, and I am not beside it.** In about two years a man came and asked me twice in one year whether I would have my drove road called a line, and both times I said yes, and both times nothing happened. **Nothing happened because a drove road is not a line. A line goes where a thing has been agreed to go. A drove road goes where the grass is and the water is and the nine gates are, and I move it about two hundred yards a season, and it has been moved about four hundred yards in nine years. If the line is put on paper, then in about nine days there is a line on paper and a drove on a bank, and they are two things, and the paper is the one that gets believed, and the paper is the part that does not change.** So the day a line is drawn on this bank I am the man they ask where the beasts went."

"Who asked you."

"I told you what I am not going to say and I have said it: I am not going to say which two men. They are not the same two. One asked me in a good year and one in a wet one, and they did not know of each other, and I have told neither about the other. If I gave you the names you would go and ask them, and one is a man who is asked by nine people a year and the other by none."

---

At about the third hour the beasts stopped at a gate and he got them through it in about four minutes with a vocabulary of eight words and one of the stick, and then he sat down on a stone without being asked to.

"Now. I am going to say a thing the way I say things about my own work, which is the only way I have got, and you are going to find it very small."

"Go on."

"**A number in a book has to be the same number tomorrow.** That is the whole of what a book is for. When a man writes eleven in a column today, then eleven is a thing the world has agreed to, and tomorrow the column has to say eleven or the man who wrote it is a liar. Mine are not the same number tomorrow and I am not lying to anybody, I am just early, and the book would still be wrong on the ninth day of every month. That is not a reason not to keep a book. That is a reason the book is not the thing. A drove is held by about nine men and a length of bank and a habit of about forty years, and if you put the number in a book then in about nine days there is a number in a book and a drove on a bank, and the number is the one that gets believed, and the number is the part that does not change. I have kept a drove book for about nine years and it is a good book and I have written in it every day of every season, and I have never let anybody read it, including the two men who asked me about the line, and I have never read it out loud, and I have never said a figure in it to a person who could not see the beasts. **The book is not the drove. The book is a thing I do so that I can prove to myself that I was not asleep.** That is what it is for and I have never told anybody that and you are the first and I would like you to be careful."

"Nobody will read it."

"Nobody will read it, and I have told a man I met about nine days ago, and I have not said his name, and he is a stranger with a barrow and he is about nine feet off me and he has not asked me a single question about it, which is more than the last four people did."

---

At about the fourth hour they were on the flat ground above the water, the beasts a mile off down the bank, and the barrow was on beaten earth with a stone at each wheel, and there was a cart shed about four hundred yards off with a roof on it and about nine beasts in the lee of it, and Selman put his stick down and looked at it and then at Tomas Vale, and said nothing, which was the first thing Tomas Vale had got out of a man that day without opening his mouth.

"You have looked at that shed and you have not gone near it and I have watched you not go near it for about four seconds." He said it evenly and he did not make a thing of it. "I am not going to ask what is on the barrow. I am telling you that I have noticed, and that in about nine days a stranger with a barrow who does not go into a shed with beasts in it is going to be talked about, and I would rather you heard it from me."

"Nothing is the matter with it. It is a cart shed and there are nine head in the lee of it and the door shuts."

"Yes."

"And you have a thing on that barrow that is the size of a cart and four bolts in it, and you are a man who has been on this water eleven days, and I have been in four counties with carters, and not one of them ever asked me to be anywhere." He turned the stick a half turn in his hand. "That is all I have got and I have had it since the third hour."

---

At about the sixth hour he was at the gate at the bottom of the mile and Tomas Vale was nine hundred yards back at the wall and the drove was gone, and it was Selman who came back up, which he did not have to do.

He stood about nine foot off and did not come closer than that.

"One more and then I go and count them again, and this one is not about the beasts and it is about what you are doing and I have been turning it over since the third hour."

"Say it."

"**Eleven head is not a drove and nine men is not a network and one true sentence is not a habit.** I say all three of those to nine people a year and about six of them nod as though I have said something clever. And what I have got is this, and I have never said it out loud. **The beasts are not held by me and not by the four gates and not by the bank. They are held by the fact that the man who drives them did it on the same day he is going to do it next, and every beast on this reach knows which day that is, and it is not in anything. It is not in me either.** If I stop driving this drove for one season, the habit is gone in about nine days and there is nothing anywhere that it was ever there, and no form in this kingdom on which I could put that down even if I wanted to. That is what a habit is worth. It is worth exactly as much as the last time it was done, and nothing at all the day after."

"You have just described eight or nine keepers on this water."

"I have described a man with a drove." He took the stick off his shoulder. "You go and say that to the man with the besom and he will tell you what he has already told you, which is that he is not going to be a man who stands at a wall and says he has. And I will tell you where the ground is thin, because I am a man who drives beasts. **A drove is not a keeper because a drove has to be fed. Everything I have said about my own ground is true because there are animals on it that die if nobody turns up, and that is not a reason and it is not a metaphor and it is nine tons of it a season.** You are going to ask a man to hold a line and he is going to say yes because he turns up anyway, and then in about nine years you are going to write that a network agreed, and there will be nothing in that sentence that was not already there before you arrived."

He went down the bank to count the beasts and did not look back.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening there was a barrow on flat ground with four bolts made fast and about four hundred yards off a cart shed with a door that shuts and nine head of beast in the lee of it, and about two hundred yards down the bank a man with a stick about four foot long was counting eleven things that were not eleven.

Tomas Vale had an eighth sentence and it was the only one of the eight that came out of a man who was doing a job while he said it, and the sentence was the shortest of the eight and it was about a day of the week that is not named in anything, and he had written it down on a wall at about the fourth hour of the day without being asked and he was now going to have to find out what to do with it.

He stood there a while longer with the stick across his knees. The beasts were a mile off down the bank and the drove book was in a box under a bed four miles the other way, and the number in it would be wrong again inside a week, and there was nobody in the county he could hand that to who would not make it into a column.

> *The two hundredth, at about the ninth hour of the evening, on flat ground above the water. A fold, a hazel stick of four foot, a whistle on a string he does not use, nine head out and fourteen by the third hour, a gate taken in four minutes with eight words and the stick, a drove book about nine years old that nobody has read, a cart shed with a shut door and nine head in the lee of it, four bolts, a barrow.*
>
> *Second column, the line. **A drove road is not a line. A line goes where a thing has been agreed to go; a drove road goes where the grass and the water and the nine gates are, and it has moved four hundred yards in nine years.** Twice in two years a man has asked him to have that road called a line. Both times he said yes and both times nothing happened. **If the line goes on paper then in nine days there is a line on paper and a drove on a bank, and they are two things, and the paper is the one that gets believed, and the paper is the part that does not move.** He will not say which two men, and his reason is good: one is asked by nine people a year and the other by none, and neither knows of the other and neither is going to hear it from him.*
>
> *Second column, the number. **A number in a book has to be the same number tomorrow, and mine is not the same number tomorrow and I am not lying, I am early, and I would be wrong in a column about the ninth day of every month.** He keeps a drove book and it is the fourth thing on his own ground worth anything to anybody, and it is a thing he does so that he can prove to himself he was not asleep. He has never read it out loud and never said a figure in it to a person who could not see the beasts. **The book is not the drove.** He has told one person in nine days, a stranger with a barrow, and gave him no name.*
>
> *Second column, said once, on a bank, standing nine foot off, after I had stood at a wall for an hour asking nothing. **The beasts are not held by me and not by the four gates and not by the bank. They are held by the fact that the man who drives them did it on the same day he will do it next, and every beast on this reach knows which day that is, and it is not in anything, and it is not in me either.** Stop for one season and the habit is gone in nine days and there is nowhere to put that it was ever there. **A habit is worth exactly as much as the last time it was done and nothing at all the day after.** And then, before I had said anything: **a drove is not a keeper because a drove has to be fed**, and that is nine tons a season and not a metaphor and not a reason. He also said, unasked, that in about nine days a stranger with a barrow who does not go into a shed with beasts in it is going to be talked about, and that he would rather I heard it from him.*
>
> *Third column, the eight. **Eight sentences in twelve days, eight trades, and no two in the same kind of words, and none of the eight will sit beside another.** A mending. A box a man has none of. A place to find each other at with the use taken out of it. Three sentences. A figure that dies with the man. A thing true only on the day it is true. A heap that has to be spent. A day of the week. **The three partial answers are still three. These are eight things and not eight answers, and a bar is what I put between a pair I was sure was one sentence and I am not sure of any of these.** He told me I would go up the water and ask the besom-man to hold a line, that he would say yes because he turns up anyway, and that in nine years I would write that a network agreed, **and that there would be nothing in that sentence that was not already there before I arrived.** The schedule went into my hands on the hundred and thirty-fourth day and it is sixty-six days in my pocket, and the name is out of the same day and is sixty-six days, and it is a woman’s name, and I have not used it for anything.*
>
> *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them. Four bolts made fast at the first hour and again at the fourth. **The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and sixty-seven days, and there is a cart shed with a roof, a shut door and nine head of beast in the lee of it, and I did not put a cradle in it and did not open the door, and the reason is not the second clause: the reason is the beasts and the door, and the man who drives them said it out loud to me rather than let me find it out.** The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and twenty-three days out of the seventy-seventh, not rung once, and I did not touch it.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. **There is a handbell in her bag and it is not rung, and nobody has asked her for a reason at any hour of today, including me.** Nothing was struck, nothing was tied, nothing went into metal, I did not reach for anything, and nothing came.*
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Nothing was asked for, produced, given or taken today, and no name was entered on any paper by me.** A drove book with a wrong number in it on the ninth day of every month is a book, and no form in this kingdom can produce one. Sixty-four days on this road with her and I did not ask her what it was for. Which office is it. **A drove on four miles of bank, held by a man who will not be there in nine years, and a shed with beasts in it that is going to be talked about because a man with a barrow did not open it.***
