# Chapter 0205 — A Place That Is on a Sheet Under Somebody Else's Name, and a Man Who Throws Out Nine Inches

It was the fourth hour of the hundred and ninety-first day and Rell Dray was standing in the middle of a landing stage with a spade, throwing out mud, and he had been at it for about an hour before anybody came, in the way of a man who does a thing at a particular hour because a thing has to be done at a particular hour and not at any other, and there was a place on that stage about four foot square where the boards were not boards any more but a kind of soup with stone in it.

The stage was the second of the lower landings on Tallow Reach. A tally board was nailed to a post at the head of it with a number cut into the top and no name, a stack of about nine tons of dressed stone stood behind a wall not high enough to be called a wall, and there was a spade, and a man.

Tomas Vale came along the stage with the barrow behind him and stopped at the dry end.

"You cannot come past me with that," Dray said, without turning round. "The boards are nine foot long and the mud is in the middle of them and you would put a wheel in it and I would have to throw it out again, and I have done this for eleven years and I am not going to start doing it twice a day because a man came along with a barrow."

"I will wait."

"You will wait, and that is the correct decision, and I am going to say something else and I am saying it before you ask so that I have not been asked." Dray threw about nine inches of mud over the side and it went into the water and the water took it. "You want to know whether that board has got a name on it. It has not got a name on it and it is not going to get one, and the reason is that a name on that board is a thing a man can be held to, and I have watched a man be held to a thing on a board and I did not care for it."

"Who put the number on it."

"Nobody put it on it. It is cut in, it is the stage's own number, and it has been cut in since before the boards were boards. There are nine of these boards between here and the top of the water and every one has a number cut in it and not one has a name, and ask nine men on that reach and you get nine numbers and no names. That is not a mystery. That is a stage."

---

At about the fifth hour the mud was out, the boards were boards again, the spade was against the wall, and Tomas had the schedule out of his coat and unfolded along the three creases it already had and was holding it flat against the post of the tally board, which took the wind. Dray looked at it for about as long as it takes to wipe a blade.

"You have got a sheet on you and you cannot read it," Dray said.

"I cannot read a run. I can see wards and numbers and a scale."

""Turn it round."

He turned it round, and Dray did not touch it.

""That is a return," Dray said. "A road has a scale on it and no number in the corner. A thing with a number in the corner is a thing somebody filled in and filed and wants found again. That is a bench clerk's argument and I am a man with a spade, and I am using it because it is true and because you would have taken it in four seconds off a road and not at all off this.

"And this panel is this mile."

"That panel is this mile in the way a rate is a mile. No name in it, never has been, and that is what a return is: a thing, a figure, and a place to send it, and the place to send it is a place somebody answers for." He put his thumb on the post. "**Nobody is coming up this reach to answer for this, and that is not an insult, it is arithmetic. A panel with nobody answering for it does not get a name, and if you want a name on a board somewhere you are going to have to be the sort of thing somebody answers for, and that is not a thing a man can do in a day.**"

---

At about the sixth hour the man at the landing said the thing that had been sitting in Tomas since the fourth hour, as a man moving nine inches of mud, unasked.

"There is a name on that sheet for this mile."

"There is not."

"There is. Not in this panel — in the head of the column, two words, and I have seen it twice in four years, both times a man from the district's office reading a slip out loud at the far end of this stage." Dray stood the spade against the post. "It is a name I have never heard at a single door on this mile, and I have been on this water eleven years and been at about nine hundred doors. I will not say what it is, because I do not know whether the man who wrote it meant this mile or a mile he was looking at, and I am not handing a stranger a word out of a column I cannot read."

"You told me what the column is for. A place somebody answers for."

"I know what a return is. I do not know what that line is, and a man who says he does not know is a man who is going to sleep."

Tomas folded the sheet in three along the creases it already had. It is fifty-five days in his pocket.

"Who put it there."

"**Nobody asked me to put it there, and if I did change it nobody asked me to change it, and it is a district's own book about a district's own ground.**" Dray came and stood about nine feet off, which is further than he had been standing all morning. "You are the first man in a long while to ask me about a word on a piece of paper instead of about my rate, and the straight answer is that there is no form on which a man with a barrow may ask for a name to be changed in a district's own return. If you were a district I could not change it either without writing to the office, and if you were the office it would take a day and a half and a form with four boxes on it and one of the boxes would be for a reason, and there is no box anywhere in this kingdom for a man to say *because two people at a door were right*.

"So I would like you to understand what you have just done. You have found out that there is a name on that sheet that does not belong to this mile, and you are the only man on nine miles of water who can see that, and there is nothing at all you can do about it, and you are not going to be thanked, and if you go back up that road tomorrow and ask me again I will tell you the same thing and I will not be angry about it and I will also not have learned anything new."

"That is the fifth time in nine days a man has told me a true thing that cost him to tell it," Tomas said. "I am putting it down and I am not putting your name next to it, because I have a page in this book that is a page of men being right and it is the only page I have never wanted anybody to read."

"Leave it," Dray said, and went back to his boards.

---

At about the seventh hour the four people were on the stage and the sheet was out on the boards and nobody had been asked to look at it, and it lay there about four minutes while about nine people came and went round it, and two of the nine stopped, and one of those two said it was a schedule and the other said nothing at all.

Then Tomas asked the question out loud, in front of all of them, about why a name was on a district's sheet that did not belong to a mile it was on, and the answer came back the way it had come back from the man with the spade, and he did not argue with it, did not put it a second time, and did not go back to the board, and stood nine feet off his own barrow for the rest of the hour.

He wrote in his own book, in the third column, ruled apart, that this was the first time in five volumes he had been in a position to fix a thing and had no form to fix it with, that the two facts had arrived in the same year, and that he did not know which was the cause and did not intend to find out.

He also wrote that a settlement at this landing is entered as work, with a figure of mouths against it and coin and meal paid, and the paying is done and the mouths are fed, and that he had looked at the figure and not touched it, because it is a district's own.

And he wrote down the nine-line form in another county nine miles behind them, in the words of a man with a spade who had heard of it and had not been shown it: nine lines, one hand, no name anywhere on it because there was no box for one. He wrote it once, did not write it twice, did not improve it, and joined it to nothing.

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening the four people came down off the bank to the boards and stood about the sheet where it had lain and nobody put a hand on it, and then they went and sat on the wall. At about the eleventh hour there was a light on the reach, the boards were washed, the spade was on the post, and Tomas was at the head of the tally board with the barrow on flat ground and the four bolts made fast.

"Dray," he said. "If you are going to tell me the last thing then tell me now and once."

"That is two miles up the water, and there is a thing up there on a flat piece of ground with a low wall round three sides of it, and nine stones standing in a line, and a roof on four posts with a board nailed to one of the posts, and a thing on a frame the size of a cartwheel with no bell in it." Dray did not look up the water. "**Nobody has asked me to go and look at it. I have passed it about four hundred times in eleven years and I have never once been asked, and there is nobody on this reach I could ask, and I have not asked.** You are the first man I have met in about eleven years who has asked me anything at all about it, and I am not going to tell you what it is, and if you go up there do not tell anybody I sent you."

He put the spade on the post and went along the stage.

Tomas stood at the head of a tally board with nine cuts in it and no name and did not move for about as long as it takes a man to decide a thing, and the thing he decided was not to go up there that night with a barrow, because a barrow at the eleventh hour on nine miles of water is how a man drowns somebody.

> *The hundred and ninety-first, at about the ninth hour of the evening, on the flat above the stage. Nine-foot boards, four foot square gone to soup with stone in it, a spade, a tally board with nine cuts and no name, nine tons of stone under a tarpaulin, a sheet with three creases laid on the boards for four minutes, about nine people, four bolts.*
>
> *Second column, the sheet. **A road has a scale on it and no number in the corner; this has a number in the corner, so it is a return and not a road, and a bench clerk told me that in a bench room in front of nine benches' people, and this man used the same argument off a landing stage in four seconds, and they were both right.** The panel for this mile has no name in it and never has, and a panel nobody answers for does not get one. **Nobody is coming up this reach to answer for this, and that is not an insult, it is arithmetic.**
>
> *Second column, the name. **There is a name for this mile in the head of that column and no door on this mile has ever used it, and the man with the spade has been on this water eleven years and been at about nine hundred doors and would not repeat it out of a column he cannot read.** I asked who put it there. Nobody asked him to put it there and nobody asked him to change it, and it is a district's own book about a district's own ground. **And it is not a form anybody can use. If you were a district it would need a form with four boxes and one of the boxes would be for a reason, and there is no box anywhere in this kingdom in which a man can write *because two people at a door were right*.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. **I asked the same question out loud in front of nine people, got the same answer, and did not argue, did not put it a second time, and did not go back to the board.** A settlement at this landing stands in the district's return as work, with a number of mouths against it and coin and meal beside the number, and the coin is paid and the mouths are fed, and I looked at the number and did not touch it, because it is a district's own figure about a district's own ground. **A man heard of a form in another county nine miles behind us — nine lines, one hand, no name on it anywhere because there is no box for one. I have written that once and am not going to write it twice, not going to improve it, and not joining it to anything.**
>
> *Third column, what it is to be right. **In five volumes this is the first time I have been able to fix a thing and had no form to fix it with, and the two facts arrived in the same year, and I do not know which caused the other and am not going to find out.** A man with a spade told me to leave his name off a page of men being right, and I am leaving it off, and that is the fifth man in nine days to have cost himself something to tell me a true sentence. **Being the only man on nine miles of water who can see that two papers are about one mile and are each right about a different thing is worth exactly nothing, and I would rather have had the four minutes on the boards back.**
>
> *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them. The four bolts went in at the fourth hour on flat ground above the stage and were still where I put them at dark. **The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and fifty-eight days, and there is a boat-shed at the head of that stage with a roof on it and a shut door, and I did not put a cradle in it, and the reason is not the second clause: the reason is that a frame in a cradle is a building-sized thing for a bell, and I am not walking into a shut door on a stage to find out what is in it.** The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and fourteen days out of the seventy-seventh.** **Her bell is in her bag and it is not rung and nobody has asked her for a reason at any hour today, including me; she was at the head of the tally board at the fourth hour with her hand on the post and did not look at the sheet when it was out, and I did not ask her why, and that is the whole of what I know about it.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. There are nine cuts in that board and no name on it, which is a thing I can count, and counting is not using.
>
> *The register is thirteen, the district's book is not mine, nothing came out of that board into this coat, nothing was asked for, produced, given or taken today, and no name was entered on any paper by me. **Fifty-five days on this road with her and I did not ask her what it was for. I have stopped writing the not-asking down as an entry. It is a count, not an entry.**
>
> *Which office is it. **A board with nine cuts in it and no name, a column head two words long that belongs to a mile it is not on, and about nine miles of water with nobody on it who can be asked to change either.**
