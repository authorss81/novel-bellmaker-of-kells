# Chapter 0209 — A Frame, a Bay, and a Man Who Will Not Make Three Sentences Into One

It was the second hour of the hundred and ninety-fifth day and Aylwin Garrow was sweeping the flat ground inside the low wall with a besom made out of about nine foot of hazel, and he had been sweeping it since the second hour, and he swept it every morning of nine years, and the sweeping takes about an hour, and what it is for is not that anything is on it.

Nine stones stand in a line across the middle of that ground about the height of a man's knee, and between the fourth and the fifth there is a space about two hands across with the footing continuous under it, and about four hundred yards down the reach a man called Bos Ottery laid that line and did not put one in the space and would not say why.

There is a thing on a frame the size of a cartwheel with a bracket at the top of the post and nothing inside it that has ever been struck, and there was a carrier of fourteen pounds two ounces in a sack against a cradle on a barrow nine hundred yards down the road, and it was not brought up, and the not-wanting is one of the reasons he wrote down afterwards and did not call the clause.

There is a roof on four posts over a four-foot square of the near corner with a board nailed to one of the posts, and on the board there is a keeper's column, and the column has had nothing in it for nine years.

Garrow had seen a barrow come up the road at about the fourth hour the day before and done nothing about it. Tomas came up the last four hundred yards on foot at about the fifth hour and left the barrow nine hundred yards down, because the man with the hoe had said on the road that a man who has kept a thing going for nine years by coming when people arrive ought to be allowed to arrive at his own place first, and that was right.

Garrow met him at the low wall.

"You came up on foot and left your barrow at the bottom."

"Yes."

"Nine years." Garrow leaned the besom against the post. "About nine people have come up that road in nine years, and every one of them came up it with something they wanted me to do, and not one came up it to stand in it. I am not complaining. I am telling you what you are standing in."

---

At about the sixth hour they were both on the flat ground, Tomas had asked the question he had carried nine days for, and the man swept about nine foot of ground and did not answer it, and then answered it three times.

"I asked you once," Tomas said. "I am going to tell you now that I will not ask you a second time, and if you have decided not to say it you can go on sweeping."

"That is the fourth time in nine years somebody has said that to me at that wall." Garrow did not stop. "I am going to give you three things and I am not going to make them into one, and when you have had a minute you are going to want to make them into one and I am telling you now that you may not."

"Go on."

"**One. It is a place a man comes back to.**" He said it in the voice of a man reading a line off something. "That has been true of this ground since before I came and it will be true tomorrow, and it needs nobody's permission, and it is the only one of the three I would stake anything on."

"And the second."

"**Two. It is a set of stones that tell you where you are when nobody is asking.**" He put the besom down at the fourth stone. "Nine stones, and nine places in this kingdom where a man can stand and say with certainty that he is at a particular spot on nine miles of river, and eight of the nine are churches and graveyards and the ninth is this. I cannot tell you what it is for, and if you ask me what it is for I will tell you the same thing again."

"And the third."

"**Three. It is a place a keeper's name goes.**" He turned and looked at the board on the post of the roof. "And there has not been a name in that column for nine years, and the district does not fill it in, and the reason is a sentence a clerk in a county nine miles behind me said to me about a different matter and did not know he was saying it about this: a district cannot get keepers up this water. He was right. **A thing nobody owns gets mended. A thing a man owns does not.** I have mended the ridge of that roof twice in nine years out of my own pocket and nobody has ever known and nobody ever will, and if the district put a name in that column tomorrow I would not touch that roof again as long as I live, and neither would the next man, and there is nobody coming."

"You have just said the whole of what I came nine days for, as three things that do not fit."

"I have said three things. Whether they fit is a thing you do, and I have watched about nine men try and never once watched one of them manage it." He picked the besom up. "I have not got a fourth and I am not going to get one, and I am not going to be a man who stands at a wall and says he has."

---

At about the seventh hour the light was off the water and came up the bank behind them, and the two of them were standing at the gap in the nine stones, and Tomas Vale looked down at it for a long time and did not ask about it.

A bay in a mould frame, in the same place in every copy, because it is the same frame. About two hands together. The paper smooth and uncreased there, the rules stopping short and not crossing it, the ink either side a slightly different black, the numbers either side going on without a break. Sixty-one days in his coat, and a mould maker in a bench room saying it was a bay nothing was run into and that he did not know what and was not going to guess, and a bench clerk saying the sheet is a return and not a road, and a weighmaster saying every panel that has gone has gone off an end and what is in the place of one that has gone is a rate.

"There is a space in your nine stones," he said.

"There is."

"I have seen four other men look at a space in about fifty days and every one of them told me something about it."

"I am not one of the four." Garrow went on sweeping. "I laid them. I could tell you in about four seconds. I have been asked twice in nine years, once by a man from a district and once by a woman, and I said the same thing both times and the same thing is *that is not the question you have come with*, and I have got no reason to say it a third time to a man with a barrow."

"There is no form on which I could have asked you and written down afterwards that I had."

"Then you will not ask me, and there will be nothing, and in forty years a stranger will come along and ask me and there will still be nothing, and that is how a space stays a space." He stopped sweeping. "I am not being difficult. I am being the only person within nine miles who is not."

---

They stood on that flat for a quarter of an hour and said nothing for about two minutes of it, and then Tomas took the book out and wrote on the back page, on one line, with two bars in it.

*It is a place a man comes back to* | *it is a set of stones that tell you where you are when nobody is asking* | *it is a place a keeper's name goes, and there has not been one in it for nine years, and a thing nobody owns gets mended.*

The two bars are his own invention. They are not a form and they are not a document and they are not a fourteenth, and there is nothing anywhere in this kingdom that says what a bar between two true things means, and he has been writing them since about the two-hundredth day of a year in which he asked the same question at two benches on purpose, got two true sentences, carried neither to the other man, and decided that was the method and not a failure of it.

"I asked a man at a bench in another county a question in words in the spring and he could not refuse me, and I have carried what he said for about two hundred days. You have given me three things and none of them fits him. I am not putting yours next to his, and I am not going to tell you which of us I think is right, because I think neither."

"That is the first sensible thing said at that wall in nine years," Garrow said. "The other eight were *thank you*, and there is nowhere to put it."

"You are not thanked."

"I am not thanked and you are not thanked and that woman down at the row is not thanked, and there is no form in this kingdom in which any of us can be, and we all go on." He hung the besom on the post of the roof, on a nail that was not part of the board. "You did not get a network out of this. You did not get an agreement, a keeper or a bell, and I would like it written that you did not get them because nobody was cruel to you. They are just not here. They have never been here. I have been here and there has been a broom."

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening the four people were nine hundred yards down at the barrow and Tomas was on the flat above the water with the four bolts made fast at the foot of the low wall, and that is not a building: it is a walled square of ground with a low wall on three sides, a roof on four posts, and nothing at all over the nine stones, and a cradle is a building-sized thing for a bell and there is nothing on this ground it could go inside of.

He did not put his hand on the crown of anything, and the carrier in the sack is a hundred and eighteen days out of the seventy-seventh, and a hand has been laid flat on that crown four times in its life and the fourth was the last one and he knew it was the last one while he was doing it, and he did not do it here.

"One more thing," Garrow said, from the corner of the roof, "and it is not about the node, and I am telling you that, because I have watched nine men go up that road and come back different depending on what they were asked first."

"Go on."

"Somebody on this reach has been asking after a woman who went into that." He nodded at the frame and did not go near it. "About twelve years. I do not know her. I have never seen her and nobody on this reach will discuss it, and I have been asked twice in nine years to say where she went, and both times I said I did not know, and both times I was not lying, and both times the person asking me thought I was."

He said a name.

It was a woman's name, and it is one he has been carrying since the hundred and thirty-fourth day, and it is in two places: on a leaf inside his coat, and in a cell of a district's own book nine miles behind him that has no box and no name in it. The woman who keeps two lists over a shop said the same name out loud once, three days ago, and would not say it a second time.

He did not say it back. Two men on a piece of flat ground, and one had told him the thing he had walked nine days to be told, and he stood in the open air with four bolts made fast at his feet and made no noise about it, and would never be able to say whether he had been right not to.

> *The hundred and ninety-fifth, at about the ninth hour of the evening, on the flat with the low wall round three sides of it. Nine knee-high stones and a space of two hands between the fourth and the fifth, a besom of nine foot of hazel, a board with a keeper's column and nine years of nothing in it, a roof on four posts, a thing on a frame the size of a cartwheel with a bracket on the post of it and nothing in it that has ever been struck, a barrow nine hundred yards down, four bolts.*
>
> *Second column, the three, on one line with two bars in it, and the bars are mine. **A place a man comes back to | a set of stones that tell you where you are when nobody is asking | a place a keeper's name goes, and there has not been a name in it for nine years, and a thing nobody owns gets mended.** He gave them in that order, would not make them one, and told me before he began that I might not. **They do not fit. Whether they fit is a thing I do, and nine men have tried and I have not watched one manage it, and neither have you.**
>
> *Second column, the third thing at length. **The district does not fill in that column, and the reason is a sentence a clerk said to me nine miles behind me about a different matter entirely: no district can get keepers up this water.** He has mended the ridge of that roof twice out of his own pocket and nobody has ever known, and if the district put a name in that column tomorrow he would not touch it again as long as he lived. **About nine people have come up that road in nine years, wanting him to do something, and not one of them came up it to stand in it.**
>
> *Third column, ruled apart. **I looked at the space between the fourth stone and the fifth for a quarter of an hour and did not ask, and I am not writing down that I did not ask, because writing that down is a way of having asked.** Four other men have looked at a space in about fifty days and every one told me something. He is not one of the four; he laid them; he could tell me in four seconds and has been asked twice in nine years and said the same both times, and the same is *that is not the question you have come with*. There is no form on which I could have asked and written down afterwards that I had. **The bay in my schedule is a bay nothing was run into; a gap in nine stones with the footing continuous under it is a space where nothing was laid. I am not joining the two, and nobody asked either man.**
>
> *Third column, what I did not get, said plainly. **No network. No agreement. No keeper. No bell** — and I did not get them because nobody was cruel to me. They are not here and never have been, and there has been a broom. I walked nine days to stand in a place and it gave me nothing I can use, and the man has been right about every part of it, and I am not going to be thanked and am never going to be able to say whether I deserved it, and I have written that down so that whoever reads this in forty years knows the doubt was mine and not his. **The bars between the three sentences are my own invention: not a form, not a document, not a fourteenth, and there is nothing in this kingdom that says what a bar between two true things means.**
>
> *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them. The four bolts went in at the fifth hour at the foot of the low wall, and again at the seventh, and at dark. **The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and sixty-two days, and this ground is not a building: a walled square, a roof on four posts, and nothing at all over the nine stones. There is nothing here a cradle could go inside of.** I walked the last four hundred yards on foot, which is one of the reasons and not the clause. The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and eighteen days out of the seventy-seventh. That crown has had the flat of my hand on it four times, the last of them is behind me, and I understood that while it was happening. It is not going to be touched here.**
>
> *The register is thirteen, nothing was asked for, produced, given or taken today, no name was entered on any paper by me, and **the two bars are not a fourteenth.** The bay is not explained and nobody was asked about it. **Fifty-nine days on this road with her and I did not ask her what it was for.** I did not ask her this morning at the barrow before I came up either, and the counter is a day and not an act, and I have known that since about the fortieth day.
>
> *Which office is it. **A space of two hands in nine stones with the footing continuous under it, a board with a ruled column and nine years of nothing in it, a bracket and a frame and nothing in it that has ever been struck, and a man with a besom who has not got a fourth thing and is not going to be the man who claims one.**
