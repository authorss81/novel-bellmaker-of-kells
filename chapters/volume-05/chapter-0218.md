# Chapter 0218 — A District's Own Form, Four Days of a Clerk's Own Carrying, and a Box With a Word on It That Wants a Man

It was the fourth hour of the two hundred and fourth day and Oswin Marl was on the road at the top of the third mile with a flat book open on the low wall of a field gate, and he had been on this reach for two days and had slept in two barns, one of which had a dog in it and one of which had a man in it who had got up and let him in and had not asked him anything, and he had a bag of bread down to about a third and a stone bottle down to about a half, and he had come up the water on foot from a bench room four days south of the top of this reach with the book under his arm and a form in it, and the form was his district’s own and the ground was his district’s own and the four days were his own legs.

He had a rule on the left-hand page and a chain and he wrote with a pencil and not a pen, because a pencil can be rubbed out and a pen cannot, and a district wants the mistakes left in.

Tomas Vale came up the road with the barrow and stopped about nine foot off, because a man with a book open on a wall at the fourth hour is a man doing a thing and a man with a barrow is not part of it.

"You will not want to look at that," Marl said, without moving the pencil.

"I did not say I wanted to."

"Nine men in four days and none of them said it, and one of them said *I do not want to see that* before I had opened it, and that man I have not trusted since. It is a form. It is the district’s, not a schedule and not a map and with no scale on it, and it has four columns, and the middle one is what is on the ground and the last one is for a man."

---

At about the fifth hour he had finished the rule and put the pencil behind his ear and turned round and sat down on the wall, which is a thing a tired man does and not a thing a careful man does.

"Right. I am going to say four things and then I am going to work, and I am saying them because you have a barrow and I have seen what a barrow does to a man's afternoon."

"Say them."

"I have not built anything."

"**One. I am here about water.** The district’s own sheet carries a channel at the top of this mile and I have to walk what is on the ground and put it in the middle column, and if the ground and the sheet do not agree then one of them is wrong and I have to write down which one, and that is the whole of my trade and I have been doing it for about nine years and I like it." He said it first so that nothing would be built on top of it, and he watched me not take my book out of my coat four times in a quarter of an hour and said that a man deciding not to be tempted is not the same as a man not being tempted, and that the two are not the same for him either.

"Whose name do you want in it."

"That is the third thing." He turned the book round and put it face down on the wall. "**I want a name for the ground and there is no box for one.** That is not an oversight. That is what a form for water is. A place on this form is a panel, and a panel gets its name out of a list that is printed in a county four days south of here, and a district cannot add to that list, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a district adds a name to that list, and I have wanted to about twice in nine years and both times I was told no by a clerk who was not unkind about it and was right. So I have got a channel and four boxes and no name for the ground, and I am going to fill in the middle column and leave the last one empty, and it will be a form about about nine hundred yards of soft ground with one box empty, and that is the best I can do and I have been walking for two days to do it."

"Whose name is it that I have in my coat."

Marl looked at him for about as long as it takes to rule a line.

"I do not know and I did not ask and I am not going to ask, and I would like that written down as me not asking and not as me not wanting to know, because I do not want to know and not wanting to know is a different thing and it is a worse one." He put his hand flat on the face-down book. "**And I am telling you now that you have about a name of a woman in that coat, and I have known there was one since the second day, and I have not asked and I have not looked and I am not going to, and if you put it in that box I will put my hand on the page.**"

"You do not know that."

"I know that a district’s own form is a thing that outlives every one of us, and I know what it is for, and I do not need to know what is in your coat to know what a name in a box does." He put his hand flat on the face-down book. "And it would not be a woman’s name. Look at the box. It says *man answerable*. A woman in that box is not a person on a form, it is a hole with a name in it, and the first clerk who opens that book in about nine years will read it as a mistake and correct it, and then somebody in an office will say that this reach was never answered for, and that will be true, and it will be true because of me."

---

At about the sixth hour he asked the man with the barrow for one thing and got it, and it was not what the man offered.

"You know this water."

"I have been on it fourteen days."

"Then walk the middle column with me. Not the sheet. The ground." He was already standing. "**And I want to say why I am asking a stranger, because it is the wrong thing to do and I am going to do it anyway.** A form is not a place. The man who fills one in is part of it, and if I have walked it alone then in about nine years, when the row is wrong, I can say *I was there and it was that*, and nobody can say *a man with a barrow told you*. And if I have walked it with you, then in about nine years the first thing anybody says about this row is that a stranger with a barrow put it in, and the row will be worth nothing, and the ground will still be exactly as it is and I will still have to come back."

"Go on, then."

"You will tell me what you see and I will write what I see. If we disagree we write it in the margin in pencil, and it is not erased, and there is no column for it, and I have never in nine years had anything to put in a margin." He had the book under his arm. "**And I am not going up the bank to the flat ground. You have been up there four times and I have not asked and I am not going to, and I am telling you why before you offer.** A line on a district’s form that came from a stranger takes away the one thing a form is for, which is that a man in an office can go and look, and what is left is a district’s own book with a stranger’s mouth in it, and I have seen one of those and it is worse than nothing."

---

At about the seventh hour they had walked about nine hundred yards and Marl had written one line in the middle column and one sentence in a margin in pencil, and the sentence in the margin was that the water goes where the ground says and not where the sheet says, and neither of them had argued about it for about a minute and a half, and the barrow had not gone on the walk because a barrow does not go through about nine hundred yards of soft ground with a channel in it.

"There is a thing I am going to do tomorrow and I am telling you tonight so that you cannot say afterwards that I sprang it," Marl said. "**I am going to write a line about the reach. Not this mile. The reach. Nine or ten miles of it, with the ground in it, and the column is the same column and the form is the same form and it will take me one more day, and I am going to put down that there is ground here and that people live on it, and that is all it is going to say and I have already written it in my head about nine times and it does not get longer.**

"Whose book goes in it."

"My district’s, about his own ground, in a district’s own book, and it is not going into your coat or anybody’s, and if you ever want to know what is in that row you will have to come to a bench room four days south of the top of this water and stand in front of a clerk and ask to see a book. There is no box for a name in it, and that is not a courtesy. **It will not answer anything. Nobody in this county has asked this reach anything in about nine years and I am the first and I am not answering anything.** Every man on nine miles of water will go on doing exactly what he was doing the day before I got here, and about four of them will never know I came."

"I will know."

"You will know and that is a sentence with two people in it, which is the fewest in this kingdom that will carry one."

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening there was a barrow on flat ground with the four bolts made fast, and about nine hundred yards off a man with a flat book was going back up the reach to a barn with no dog in it, and he had two days of walking in him and about a third of a bag of bread and he had four days of his own carrying on a form that would go in a drawer and be found in about forty years by a man who wanted to check something.

Tomas Vale had a thing to write down and it was not a sentence. It was that a district’s own clerk had refused a stranger’s name, and had refused a stranger’s ground, and had accepted a stranger’s company for nine hundred yards, and had given the reason for all three out loud without being asked, and that the reason in every case was that the form is the clerk and not the ground.
> *Day eight of the ten. The two hundred and fourth, at about the ninth hour of the evening, on flat ground above the lower end of the reach. A flat book with a chain, a pencil and not a pen, a form with four columns and the last one headed for a man, a soft place at the top of a mile, a rule and a margin, about nine hundred yards of soft ground with a channel in it, a bag of bread down to about a third, four bolts, a barrow.*
>
> *Second column, the four boxes. **The last one takes a man, not a household, not a family, not a holding, because what he is answerable for is a channel and a channel is a thing a man is answerable for.** So filling it puts a man on a district’s own form about his own ground, and that man can be sent for, and there is not one man on nine miles of water who would let him do it, and the day one of them did that man would belong to him and not know it. **And there is no box on the form for the name of the ground, and that is not an oversight.** A place on a water form is a panel, and a panel gets its name out of a printed list in a county four days south of here, and a district cannot add to that list, and he has wanted to twice in nine years and been told no by a clerk who was right.*
>
> *Third column, the thing in my coat. **He has known since his second day that I have a name of a woman in this coat, and he has not asked and has not looked and is not going to, and he said he did not want to know, which is worse than not asking.** A woman in a box headed *man answerable* is not a person on a form but a hole with a name in it, and the first clerk who opens that book in nine years will read it as a mistake and correct it, and then the reach will be a reach nobody answered for, and that will be true, and it will be true because of him. **I did not say the name. It is a woman’s name, it is in a leaf in the inside of this coat, and I have not said it aloud in a room in sixteen days and I am not going to start with a clerk.** The schedule went into the same pocket on the same day and is seventy days in there, and I still cannot read a run, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man is taught.*
>
> *Third column, what he asked me for and what he refused me. **He asked me to walk the middle column with him, knowing that a form is not a place and that the man who fills one in is part of it, and knowing that in nine years the first thing anybody would say about that row is that a stranger with a barrow put it in, and that the row would then be worth nothing while the ground stayed exactly as it is.** We walked about nine hundred yards. There is one line in the middle column and one sentence in a margin in pencil, that the water goes where the ground says and not where the sheet says. **He would not go up the bank to the flat ground and told me why before I could offer: a line on a district’s form that came from a stranger would take away the one thing a form is for, which is that a man in an office can go and look, and what would be left is a district’s own book with a stranger’s mouth in it.** And he told me tonight what he was going to do tomorrow so that I could not say afterwards that he sprang it: a line about the reach, saying that there is ground here and that people live on it, and that it will not answer anything, and that about four people on nine miles of water will never know he came, and I will know.*
>
> *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them. Four bolts made fast at the fourth hour and again at the sixth. **The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and seventy-one days, and there is a barn about nine hundred yards off that a man with a dog in it is sleeping in, and I did not put a cradle in it, and the reason is not the second clause: a man who has let a stranger into his barn and asked him nothing is not a man who wants a barrow brought in beside him at the fourth hour.** The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and twenty-seven days out of the seventy-seventh, not rung once, and I did not touch it.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. **There is a handbell in her bag and it is not rung, and nobody has asked her for a reason at any hour of today, including me.** Nothing was struck, nothing was tied, nothing went into metal, I did not reach for anything, and nothing came.*
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Nothing was asked for, produced, given or taken today by me, and no name was entered on any paper by me, and a district’s own form is a district’s own and is in a district’s own book and was read standing up and is not in this coat, and reading a page standing up is reading and is not carrying and is not a fourteenth.** A pencil line, a rule and a margin, a bag of bread, a book shut under an arm: none of them is a document and none of them is mine. Sixty-eight days on this road with her and I did not ask her what it was for. Which office is it. **A form with four columns and a box headed for a man and no box anywhere for a name, and about four people on nine miles of water who will never know he came up here.***
