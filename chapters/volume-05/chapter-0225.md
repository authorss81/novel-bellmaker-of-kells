# Chapter 0225 — A Woman Who Keeps the Stones and Not the Water, and Cannot Say Out Loud That She Does Not Want the Standing

It was the first hour of the two hundred and eleventh day and Dela Munce was walking the ford in her own boots, and she walks it three times a day and has walked it about nine years, and the three times are at the first hour, at about the sixth hour and at the dark, and they are the three times the water changes its mind about where it is going.

The ford was at the fifth mile and it was about nine feet of shallow across on a gravel bar with a post on the near bank that had four iron marks in it at about a man's knee, and nine stones came up out of the bed in a crooked line, and four of them were in the right place and five of them had walked about a foot to the left since the last spate.

There was no roof within reach of it. There was nothing at the ford but the water, the bar, the stones and the post, and a woman going along the line of the stones with a boot and a stick, and the four marks on the post were for four spates she had stood and watched go over, and she had cut them herself with a cold chisel, because a mark made with a chisel is a thing nobody else can say they did.

---

Tomas Vale came up the bank from the sixth mile at about the third hour and the barrow stood a hundred yards up, and he came down on foot, and he did not ask, and she finished the crooked line before she said anything.

"**You are standing on the fourth one.**"

He moved.

"**That one is sound and it has been sound since the spate before last. Two and three walk. Five and six and eight and nine are all in about a foot and I have been putting them back since the dark and they have not stayed, and I am not going to move them again today, and if you want to know why that is, it is because a stone you have just put back is a stone you are responsible for at the sixth hour, and I would rather be wrong at the sixth hour than right at the first and wrong at the sixth.**" She came out of the water and wrung her boot. "You did not come down here to look at a ford."

"No."

"**Then you have come down here to ask me a thing, and I have been asked about four things on this water in nine years and about three of them by people who had been here a fortnight, and I am going to tell you which one of the four you are before you open your mouth, and then I am going to carry on walking.**"

---

At about the sixth hour the water had come up about a hand and she had walked the line again, and the two of them were on the near bank with the cart of a peat-cutter going by up the top road, and the post with the four marks in it was about nine foot off them, and neither of them had looked at it.

"**The one is this. I do the work of a keeper and I have never wanted the standing, and I am telling you that first and I am telling you that I cannot tell you the second half, and the first half is free and the second half is not.**"

"You want to tell me the rest."

"I am going to try and I am going to fail, and I would rather fail in front of you than in front of somebody who came up here with a reason to write it down." She sat down on the post end. "I have been asked four times. The first time was a woman who keeps a list over at the lower end, and she asked me in words with nothing written, and she was in my kitchen and she wrote nothing down, and I said yes, and that is a real yes and I am not going to unsay it. **The other three were not women and were not from this reach and each of them wanted it for about nine minutes and then wanted something else.**"

"Go on."

"**A no is a thing that has to be defended.**" She said it the way she had said the stone, without weight. "If I say no to a man with a paper in his hand, then that no is a sentence in the air with nothing behind it, and any man in a coat who wanted a no out of me would only have to put his face to it and wait, and I have watched that done to one man on this water and it took him about two years. **The only way anybody in this kingdom ever gets to say no safely is if there is a person standing behind the no. And the only thing on nine miles of water that puts a person behind a no at a ford is a keeper's standing. So the standing is the one thing that would make a refusal safe, and I do not want the standing, and that is the whole of the knot and it has been the same knot for about nine years.**"

---

"And you cannot say that you do not want it."

"**I will not.** And I want it written down that I will not, and not that I cannot, because there is a difference and the difference is mine and I am the only one who is allowed to be precious about it." She got up off the post. "If I tell a man with a barrow that I do not want a keeper's standing, then that is a sentence of mine in a room, and in about nine years there will be a man in an office with a piece of paper and he will read it, and **there is no form in this kingdom that says Dela Munce is not a keeper. I have looked. I have looked in about four places and one of them was a bench room and I was not asked for anything and I was not sorry to be there.** So the second half of the sentence is the one sentence that would make me one, and I am not going to say it to you, and you did not ask me for it and I am not being careful with you, I am being careful with a thing that does not exist yet."

"You are the third person on this water who has said that to me."

"Then you have three of the same thing and not three of a thing, and if you write them in a row you will have a reach and you will not have a person, and I know a woman who has said that better than I can and I am not going to say her words back to you because they are hers and she gave them to you on her own ground." She looked down the water. "**A ford is not a bridge. I keep the stones and I do not keep the water. That is the whole of the trade and it is the whole of the answer, and I have given it to four people and I am giving it to you, and I am not a door and you are not going to be able to come back through here with a sentence of mine in your mouth and put it in front of somebody.**"

---

At about the seventh hour the water had come up about a finger and the four good stones had gone under and two of the five bad ones had come up, and she went into it and shifted them and came out and did not explain any of it, and Tomas Vale stood on the bank and worked out that she had said the word stones eleven times since the first hour and had not once said the word water except in the sentence about the water being nine foot deep, and that a woman who will not say a word for nine hours is not hiding a thing, she is doing a trade.

He said so out loud, because he had learned in about ten days that a man who thinks something and says nothing on nine miles of water has it written on his own face and somebody will ask him about it, and being asked is how he had got the first two of the nine.

She looked at him for about as long as it takes to count a room, and then she said the one sentence that made the whole morning, which was that the water on nine miles of water is a thing that gets up and walks about and comes back, and that a person who is responsible for a thing that gets up and walks about has to be out in it three times a day for about nine years before anybody will say a word to her about it, and that if she stopped coming at the sixth hour then the first time it came up over the bar there would be nobody there at all, and that this is the whole of the standing and the standing has nothing to do with anybody's column.

Then she told him to get out of the wet, which he did.

At about the seventh hour a man came down the bank on the other side with a cow and could not cross, and stood there, and she went back into the water and moved two stones and he crossed, and said nothing to her about it and did not say thank you, and neither of them noticed that Tomas Vale was standing about nine foot off watching a woman do a keeper's work for a man who would not be told she had.

"There," she said, coming out. "**That is the standing and there is no box on it, and if the water had gone up while he was on it I would be the one they came to, and I have never once been able to say to anybody that I would rather it was somebody else's name on it, and it is not a form and it is not a habit, it is a thing I have wanted to say for nine years and cannot.** You can go up. And I am not going to ask you for anything and there is nothing you can do for me, and that is not a door closing, that is a ford."

"At the ford. Which office is it."

"I have not the faintest idea," she said, "**and I have never been asked, and neither have you.**"

---

At about the ninth hour of the evening there was a barrow on flat ground above the lower end with the four bolts made fast, and about five miles up the water a woman was walking a crooked line of nine stones in her own boots with a boot and a stick and no roof anywhere within reach, and the post with four chisel cuts in it was standing in about nine inches of water that would be a foot by the dark.

Tomas Vale had a term in a fourth trade that was the same term in a different mouth, and he had a woman who had given him a sentence and then refused him the half that made it a sentence, and he had been refused twice on nine miles of water in two days and neither refusal was the same shape as any of the others, and he was not thanked for any of them.

He wrote down that he had not got a new answer tonight and had not gone after one, and that a knot that has been the same knot for about nine years is not a thing a man with a barrow unties in a fortnight, and that the sentence she would not say would have made her a keeper if it had ever reached a room, and that there is no form in this kingdom that says she is not one, and that he is the fourth man on this reach to find that out and the first one to be told it by the person it is about, and that he is not going to carry it to anybody because carrying it is the only way it gets said in a room.

He wrote the six things down, and the third column, and the seventy-seven days of the schedule and the eleven lines and the name, and the fact that he had not used any of the three for anything and had not been asked for any of the three today by anybody including the woman at the ford who had asked him for a name on a step two days ago and taken a refusal for an answer.



> *Day five of the ten. The two hundred and eleventh, at about the ninth hour of the evening, on flat ground above the lower end of the reach. A ford at the fifth mile, about nine feet of shallow on a gravel bar, nine stones in a crooked line with four in the right place, a post with four chisel cuts in it at about a man's knee, a boot and a stick, a peat cart going by, a cow on the far bank, and no roof within reach of any of it. Four bolts, a barrow, a leaf in a book.*
>
> *Second column, the trade, and it is the same term in a fourth mouth. **She does the work of a keeper and has never wanted the standing, and she told me the first half free and the second half she will not say.** A woman over at the lower end asked her in words with nothing written, in her kitchen, and she said yes, and that is a real yes and she is not going to unsay it. Three others came in nine years and each wanted it for about nine minutes and then wanted something else. **A no is a thing that has to be defended; a no with nothing behind it is a sentence in the air; the only way anybody in this kingdom gets to say no safely is if there is a person standing behind the no, and the only thing at a ford that puts a person behind a no is a keeper's standing. So the standing is the one thing that would make a refusal safe, and she does not want it, and the knot is the same knot it has been for about nine years.** I have not got a new answer tonight and I did not go after one. Three of the same thing is not three of a thing.
>
> *Second column, the half she would not say, and it is the whole of the batch. **If she says out loud that she does not want a keeper's standing, that is a sentence of hers in a room, and there is no form in this kingdom that says she is not a keeper — she has looked, in about four places, and one of them was a bench room and she was not asked for anything and was not sorry to be there — and so the one sentence that would stop it is the sentence that would start it.** She said that standing up in her own boots, and she said she would not say it and not that she could not, and that the difference is hers and hers alone. **And she would not give me another woman's words back to me, and she did not name her, and that is the second time in three days somebody has refused to be the mouth for a person on this reach.** A ford is not a bridge: she keeps the stones and she does not keep the water, and a cow came down the far bank at the seventh hour and she moved two stones and he crossed and did not say thank you and she did not wait for it and I stood nine foot off and wrote nothing down about it.
>
> *Third column, the six I cannot fix and what is not mine. **Nine houses of a front row wet and two hundred and twenty-nine people dry; about forty people in a yard who were never told anything; a barge of nine tons of dressed stone upright in seven foot of water; a dressed sill half in and about nine days; a rate to be set in a fortnight by nine men who will not agree; and about four people on nine miles of water who do not know a district's clerk came up here and put one line in a drawer.** The three partial answers are still three and there is no fourth one tonight and none is going to come. The schedule went into my hands on the hundred and thirty-fourth day and it is seventy-seven days in my pocket, and the eleven lines are out of the same day, and the name is a woman's name in the same pocket and is seventy-seven days, and I have not used it for anything and was not asked for it by anybody today.
>
> *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them. Four bolts made fast at the third hour on the hard ground a hundred yards up from the ford and again at the seventh. **The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and seventy-eight days, and there was no roof within reach of any part of this day and I did not go and look for one, and a day with no roof in it is not a day on which I chose not to go under one.** The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and thirty-four days out of the seventy-seventh, not rung once, and I did not touch it.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. **A handbell is in her bag and it is not rung, and nobody has asked her for a reason at any hour of any of the five days of this week, including me.** Nothing was struck, nothing was tied, nothing went into metal, I reached for nothing at any hour, and nothing came.
>
> *The register is thirteen. **No paper has been asked for, produced, given or taken by me on any of the ten days of this week, and a column ruled in a hand is not in my coat, and four chisel cuts in a post at a ford are not a document, because there is no form in this kingdom on which a man can produce a cut in a post.** A bent iron rod for a bad joint on the third mile, a post with four marks in it, a book of his own that says how many came over: none of them is a paper. **Nobody was thanked today and a woman gave me a knot that has been the same knot for nine years and would not give me the half of it that unties it, and got nothing, and I am not going to go back there and ask again.** Seventy-five days on this road with her and I did not ask her what it was for. Which office is it. **A standing that is the only thing that would make a no safe, and a woman who cannot say she does not want it, and nine miles of water on which nobody has been able to help.**
