# Chapter 0221 — Four Boxes and No Day in Any of Them, and a Man Who Dug for a Woman He Never Saw

It was the third hour of the two hundred and seventh day and Ansel Wraye was standing in a hole at the end of a row of eleven, and he had been in it since the light came off the water, and he talks down into a hole more than anybody else on nine miles of water, and his daughter says it is because there is nobody up there to answer him back in the same words.

The hole was for a woman whose husband was still living and had not chosen the ground, which is the case in about four graves in nine, and Wraye had set out the line at the dark the night before and had cut and dressed the sides by the second hour, and the bottom was a foot deeper than it needed to be because a man who is in a hole all morning digs deeper than he means to.

Tomas Vale came down the lane from the water with the barrow behind him and stopped at the gate, and he came on foot from there, and a man who has come on foot from a gate is a man who has decided the barrow was not coming in.

"Stay off the head," Wraye said, without turning round. "Nine feet back and hold the board flat. That board is flat and it stays flat or I want to know why it is not."

---

At about the fourth hour the woman came down with the child and stood at the gate for as long as it takes to look at a hole, and then she went back up the lane, and Wraye dug for a while after she had gone, and did not say anything about her going, and Tomas Vale noticed that about four men on this reach had said something about it and this one had not.

"Which one is that."

"Which one is what."

"You said a case in four. That is three rows back and one of them is new, and one of them is a child, so it is one of the two in the middle."

Wraye came up the ladder on his hands and knees and stood on the spoil and looked at the barrow and did not look at the man.

"**A man does not choose the ground. That is the whole of it. A man in that condition will say yes to four places and mean none of them, so we go on about nine hundred yards past the water and pick one.**" He wiped his face with the back of his wrist. "And you are not going to tell me your business at the gate, because a man who is at a gate is a man on a road, and there is only one road on this water."

"I have come about a thing that is not on a road."

"Then it is a thing I have never heard of, and I am at the bottom of a hole, and if you want to say it you can say it while I work and I will not stop, and if you want me to hear it you will have to come down, and if you want it written down you have come to the wrong nine miles."

---

At about the fifth hour he had got the hole to the depth and had stopped, and he stood in it with his hands on his back and looked up at Tomas Vale on the head, and the two of them had been in that arrangement for about an hour without either of them having named it.

"Right," Wraye said. "Say it."

"I have come about a woman who went into the ground about twelve years ago."

The spade did not go down. Wraye stood in his own hole and looked up, and the first thing that came into his face was not surprise, and Tomas Vale wrote that down later in the ordinary hand with nothing round it: *the first thing was not surprise.*

"**You have come about that, and you have brought a barrow to a gate to do it.**" He said it without any weight at all. "I am telling you the shape of it, because I know the shape and I would rather give it to you straight. Somebody has been at me about that before. Twice that I know of, and one of those was about nine years back and one of them was not as long ago, and both of them were a man with a satchel and both of them wanted to know a day, and I have told both of them the same thing, and I am telling you it, and I would like you to understand that I am not telling you it because you are the third."

"Then what are you telling me it for."

"Because you have a barrow at a gate and a look on you of a man who has come about nine miles to be refused, and I have been in this hole since before the light, and I would rather you were not disappointed and I would rather I had not watched you be." He got a step and sat on it, which put his head at the level of the ground. "**I do not know the day. I have never known the day. There is a woman in this row of eleven who I dug a month ago and I can give you her day to the hour, and there is a man in the third row I buried in the wet of a winter and I can give you that one too, and I can give you the day of a dog that went into a ditch off this lane, and there is one thing on nine miles of water I cannot give a day for, and I have been asked for it twice, and it is not because I was not there. I was not there, and nobody who was there is left, and that is the answer and it has not got better in twelve years.**"

"You were not there."

"I was thirty-one and I was on a different water and I did not know her name, and I have had twelve years of being asked about it by people who assume there is a hole. **There is no hole. That is the finding. Everybody on this reach wants a hole because a hole is a thing a man can be blamed for, and there is no hole, and there is nothing for anybody to be blamed for, and that is the reason a man asks twice and then stops.**"

---

At about the sixth hour they were sitting on the spoil at the head of the lane with the barrow on the hard ground and a bit of board and a stone, and Wraye had taken a folded paper out of the inside of his coat, and he did not unfold it, and he held it the way a man holds a thing he has carried a long way and has never once had to put down.

"**I have had this about twelve years.** A man came up this lane from the water with a satchel and about four days of walking in him and he asked at every door and about eight of them were shut to a paper, which is what a paper is at a door. He came to me because a man who digs is the man you can ask where a person went. It is the only question I have ever been asked in this trade that I could have got wrong, and that is why he asked it of me, and I have thought about that a great deal since and I still do not know whether that was sense." He turned the fold over once and put it back in his coat. "**It has four boxes on it. Four. Not three and not five, and there is a reason for four, and the reason is that a district can only ask four things about a person and there is a fifth thing it wants and there is no box on this kingdom's paper for the fifth thing.**"

"What are the four."

Wraye took it out again and unfolded it to the size of his own two hands and did not turn it round, and he held it where Tomas Vale could see it and where he could not get at it.

"**One. The name. Two. The ground. Three. The day. Four. The man answerable.**" He said the four the way a man says the parts of a thing he has carried in a coat. "And the ground one I filled in, and it is the ground of this row of eleven and I filled it in myself and I would fill it in again. And the fourth one has my own name in it in ink, and it has been my own name in it for about twelve years, and the fourth one is the only one I was ever asked to put a name in and it is the only one I can answer for, because I dug and I know where she went into the ground, which is nowhere, because there is no hole, and a man who can be blamed is a man somebody can be sent for, and I would rather be that man than a name in a column."

"And the third one."

"**The third one is empty and it has been empty for about twelve years and it is going to be empty in about nine more, and that is the whole of what I have to say to you, and you can put it down and go on up the water.**"

Tomas Vale looked at the four boxes on the paper in a gravedigger's hands at the head of a lane, and the third of them was empty, and the paper was in a man's coat nine foot off him and not in his.

"Then that is what I came about," he said, "and I want you to hear what it is before you answer it. **A thing with a day in it is late or it is not late. A hearing is a day and a day is a thing you can be too late for, and there is a man in it and I have found out in about a hundred and thirty days that being too late for a hearing is the only standing a person has in this kingdom.** This has no day in it. So it is not late and it is not not-late. There is nothing about it that can come. **That is worse than a hearing, and I have been three weeks on this water working out why, and this is the first time anybody has put it in a hand in front of me.**"

---

At about the tenth hour the light had gone off the water and the row of eleven was a line of ground and Wraye had got out of the hole and filled it in above the boards, and he did the filling the wrong way on purpose, because a grave filled in wrongly can be opened again in nine years and a grave filled in rightly cannot, and that is not sentiment, that is a trade.

"Three things," he said, "and then I am going to wash, and you can have them for nothing because there is nothing to have."

"Say them."

"**One. You are not taking it.** And you are not copying it, and you are not putting a mark on it, and if you had asked I would have said no before you finished the sentence, and I want you to know that I would have said no and I would not have felt one thing about it, so do not make it a favour.** **A copy of a form with a name on it is a second copy of a person, and the second copy is the one somebody gets produced, and I have been to about nine funerals of men who were ruined by a piece of paper nobody could explain, and I am not going to be a man who made the copy."

"Then what did you do."

"**Two. I have taken it out about four times in twelve years. That is not a habit, that is four things I was looking for. Once for a man with a satchel, once when a woman came from off the water and wanted to know a day, and twice for no reason at all except that a thing in a house gets looked at.**" He put his hand flat on his own coat where the fold was. "**And three, and this is the one I would keep if I was allowed to keep one: the name is on the first box and I have never read it and I will not, and I did not read it in about twelve years ago when it was still an open sheet on my own table, and I have never opened it folded, and if you were to look at it now you would be a second man who has read it, and there is no form in this kingdom on which either of us could say what we were allowed to do about it afterwards.**"

Somewhere below the water a door went and nobody on nine miles of water could have said whose it was.

"You have not asked me a name," Wraye said. "Nine men have not asked me a name and I would have said no to all nine, and I want it noted that a man who does not ask is not a man I have refused, and the difference is not nothing, and you can put that in whatever you are going to put it in."

---

Tomas Vale walked back up the lane to the gate and the barrow came back on the grass, and he had not taken a paper and he had not copied one and he had not put a mark on anything, and a gravedigger had given him a fourth thing with a man at the bottom of it and a reason nobody had put on any form in this kingdom, and none of it was a document he could carry and all of it was true.

He wrote it down at about the ninth hour of the evening on flat ground above the lower end, with the barrow against a stone and the four bolts made fast, and he wrote the form in his own book in four words and no name, because that is the whole of a gravedigger's rule and he works by nothing else, and it was given to me by a man who owed me nothing and was not thanked for it, and the giving had cost him the whole morning of a grave.

He wrote four things in the book that night and one of them was the sentence he had said out loud at the head of a lane, and he wrote that he had said it out loud to a man who was right and who had not asked for it and who was not thanked, and that the sentence was about the shape of a box and not about a woman, and that the woman was still a woman he had never seen and a hole that was not there.



> *Day one of the ten. The two hundred and seventh, at about the ninth hour of the evening, on flat ground above the lower end of the reach. A row of eleven graves and one of them opened and filled in the wrong way on purpose, a board and a stone, a folded paper in a coat and not in mine, a barrow, four bolts, a leaf in a book.*
>
> *Second column, the form, and the four. **It is a district's own form and it has four boxes and one of them is the day and the day has been empty for about twelve years. One, the name. Two, the ground, and he filled that one in himself and it is this row. Three, the day. Four, the man answerable, and his own name is in it in ink and he has been the man anybody can send for ever since without knowing it.** The name is folded under and he has never read it and will not, and I did not read it and did not ask and am not going to. **He said out loud that a man who does not ask is not a man he has refused, and the difference is not nothing.** Four times in twelve years he has had it out, twice for nothing, which he said is not a habit.*
>
> *Second column, the part that is the batch. **An empty day box is worse than a hearing and he said a thing I have not been able to get: a thing with a day in it is late or it is not late, and this is not late and it is not not-late, and there is nothing about it that can come.** A district can ask four things about a person and wants a fifth and there is no box for the fifth, and he said that standing up out of a hole and he has no reason I can see to say it twice. **He dug for a woman he never saw and the finding is that there is no hole, and every man on this reach wants a hole because a hole is a thing a man can be blamed for, and there is no hole and there is nothing for anybody to be blamed for, and that is why a man asks twice and then stops.** Three weeks of a reach and this is the first time anybody put that in a hand in front of me. And he asked me nothing and gave me the shape of the whole week for nothing, and a man on nine miles of water who gives a stranger the finding and asks for nothing at all is not doing you a kindness, and I have written that down in two places today and I am not going to make it a third.*
>
> *Third column, ruled apart, and I am putting nothing on one line tonight. **Four boxes and a day that is not going to come is not an answer to anything I can carry and it is not a fourteenth thing. It is a third person's shape, and I have had two, and it stands by itself and I am not putting it in a row with the others, because a row is a claim and I do not know that I have one.** A day is the only thing on a form that makes a page checkable and checkable is the only thing that lets a man who was not there argue with it, and there is no day in this one and there is not going to be, and so the one page in this kingdom that could have been argued with by about nine people in about nine years is a page nobody can argue with. **That is a thing about a form and not about a woman, and I have to keep it a thing about a form, because the alternative is that I have been on a reach for three weeks thinking about a woman instead of about the place she is written in.** The three partial answers are still three and these do not go near them. The schedule went into my hands on the hundred and thirty-fourth day and it is seventy-three days in my pocket, and the eleven lines are out of the same day, and the name is a woman's name in the same pocket and is seventy-three days, and I did not say it and was not asked for it.*
>
> *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them. Four bolts made fast at the sixth hour on the hard ground at a gate and again at the tenth. **The frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third, a hundred and seventy-four days, and there was a grave-shed roof nine foot from that barrow with a body in it and a man in it and I did not put a cradle under it, and a man who is at the bottom of a hole all morning does not want a barrow brought in beside him at the fourth hour.** The carrier in the sack, **a hundred and thirty days out of the seventy-seventh, not rung once, and I did not touch it.** My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel. **There is a handbell in her bag and it is hers and it is not rung, and nobody has asked her for a reason at any hour of today, including me.** Nothing was struck, nothing was tied, nothing went into metal, I did not reach for anything, and nothing came.*
>
> *The register is thirteen. **Nothing was asked for, produced, given or taken on any of the ten days of this week by me, and a form with four boxes in it is in a gravedigger's coat and is not a fourteenth and is not in mine and I am not going to be the reason it changes hands.** A board and a stone and a hole filled in the wrong way on purpose are not a document, because there is no form on which a man can produce a hole. **Nobody was thanked today and a man gave me the shape of the whole thing for nothing and I did not thank him and I am not going to.** Seventy-one days on this road with her and I did not ask her what it was for. Which office is it. **Four boxes, one of them for a day, and the day is the only one that could have made it a record.**
