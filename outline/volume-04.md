**Three disagreements between `outline/series.md` and the prose of Volume 03, resolved here in favour of the prose, on the Volume 01 audit precedent.**

1. **The series outline says *the trio*.** There is no trio and there is no third person on the road. **The three-person arrangement exists and was made out loud in a room he paid for on Day 120 and there is no paper in it** (Chapter 0134's four clauses, and Chapter 0148, where the only writing that exists is four lines in Tamsin Rook's hand that he does not hold, that he saw from about thirty feet, and that he wanted and did not ask for). **He has one of the three answers and not two, and the one is his own, and a no said in advance is not an answer to a claim because there was never a claim — so the three of them are three refusals and not one agreement.** *If there were three of me* is the sentence Volume 04 opens on and it is not resolved. **A later volume may not write a trio, may not write a third person who has agreed, and may not resolve the count.**
2. **The series outline says *Tomas, Tamsin, and a small traveling group*.** **Nobody came up the road. The only person from Kells who has come up the road in three volumes is Oren Pike, in a chair, in a real building, four days' road away, in a letter of four sides that came on Day 135** (Chapter 0149) — and he is not thanked, is not owed a visit, none is coming, and Tomas is not going to write to him this month or next. **Being believed by somebody in this kingdom is worth exactly nothing: there is no form on which belief is entered.** **The permitted-letter allowance is spent twice, on Day 105 and on Day 135, and no second letter from Kells may be staged in this volume without the volume that earns it.** *A small travelling group* means a group assembled on the road out of people the road puts in front of him, and it is assembled in this volume and not inherited.
3. **The series outline says *the Unrung sabotage lines that they believe are carrying forced answers*.** **That belief is not on the page and this volume may not open with it.** What is on the page is a **cut from above with a burr underneath it** in a district's trench, under a notice that says *wires* and not *wires belonging to anybody* (Chapter 0145), and a district's bypass, and a man at a rail who does not know whether he is to strike a bell and has nobody above him to ask. **Whether a line is cut, and by whom, and what a cut line does to the households above it, is this volume's question and not its premise.** A man who believes a thing on Day 137 and is wrong about it is a better opening than a man who is right.

**Central pressure:** **The route map is incomplete because the Crown removes names and sections of road from every public copy.** Tomas has it and cannot read it. A rectangle about two hands together is gone out of the middle of a run — not torn, not cut, the paper smooth and uncreased, the column rules stopping short and not crossing it, the ink in the panels either side a slightly different black, the numbers either side going on without a break — **and it is in the same place**, and the fifth panel of a district's road sheet, the panel for the district above the coach inn, has been gone from every copy in the same rectangle since Day 82. **He does not know whether that is a thing done to every copy or a thing done to the plate, and he is not going to find out in a hurry.** Tomas, Tamsin, and a small travelling group try to restore the physical routes. **The map is a map and not a key, there is no form in this kingdom for reading one, a map shows where a line runs and cannot show whose hand altered it, and four people in four counties have said that to him one after another and none of them owed him anything. Volume 04 may not hand him the ability to read it for free.**

## Starting state

**Day 137. Tomas Vale is on a road above the water about a mile out of Sennet, at about the ninth hour of the evening on Day 136's night, with a barrow, going on foot, and he is not going back to that city.** He came up the road from Miremarket on the eighty-seventh and arrived in Sennet on the ninety-fifth and was forty-one days in the city and has left it. **Nothing in Chapters 101 to 150 puts anybody back in Miremarket and nothing in this volume may.**

**What he has, and the register is THIRTEEN and this volume may not make it fourteen:**

- **The fixed stripped Crown survey carrier, ON A BARROW**, in its mounting frame, in its **reinforced padded cradle**, four retaining bolts made fast at the fourth hour of every place the frame is set down, and **nothing in it that can strike.** Child-sized wax handprint in the crown, warped lip, a thin copper jumper across a coupling cut nearly through. **It has not been in a building since the thirty-third and a cradle is a building-sized thing for a bell: no clause and no card may put it inside anything.** The receipt is not voided, broken, renegotiated or extended and **all five clauses stand, including on the mornings they are inconvenient.** The second clause is the reason he cannot pay for anything with the barrow and he pays cash.
- **The plain carrier that is his own bell — IN THE SEALED YARD AT KELLS.** Barred on the twenty-sixth from any public place in that county; **the bar is on the bell and not on the licence. It does not travel and no chapter of this volume may have it travel.** He says in his own hand that he is not going to it.
- **The certified plain carrier — IN A SACK AGAINST THE CRADLE ON THE BARROW**, fourteen pounds two ounces, no wax, no dressed groove, only the caster's mark and a day, bought at Bramden on the fifty-second for one pound fourteen shillings. **NOT RUNG SINCE THE SEVENTY-SEVENTH — SIXTY DAYS AT DAY 137.** It was rung on four occasions in its life and **no state file may say *twice* and no chapter may say the fifty-third was the only one.** He does not know where either of his two public ringings went, is not going to be told, and **no volume may give him that as a consolation.**
- **The two counters on that carrier are separate and no writer may merge them.** The **strike** counter is the figure every chapter states, in days from the seventy-seventh. The **hand** counter is a hand laid flat on the crown **four** times, on the hundred and sixth, the hundred and twenty-second, the hundred and twenty-fifth and the hundred and twenty-seventh, **the fourth being the last one**, and he said he knew it was the last one while he was doing it. The inherited phrase *not touched since the seventy-seventh* covers the first and not the second.
- **Eleven lines in his own hand on the back of a leaf of his own book**, copied out on a stair landing on Day 134, for the third time, and kept. **The only person in the world who can copy one out of a head is the man it is in, and the moment he did it they stopped being the only instrument that cannot be produced and became the most expensive one there is. A copy of eleven lines out of a head is not a copy of a court record and is not a fourteenth document.** The reason he did it is about him and not about her and it is on the page. **Nothing in this volume may say or infer what became of his mother.**
- **A physical route map he cannot read**, a schedule folded in three, a district's, part of a paper, put into his hands by a man in a coat **who was not asked to do it**, because there is no form that lets a district keep a man and no form on any paper that wants a man to give one back.
- **A warrant that stands.** A sheet with his name at the top of it, a day, a number, and a fourth box that is the sound somebody made in a yard. It went up the river on a district's cart in a day or two with the person named in the paper, **and the person is walking on foot.** **There is nothing in this kingdom that lifts a warrant and no form on which a man asks for one to be lifted.** No Crown office has been defeated.

**What he has not got, and the volume's arithmetic:** **he has no room, no place, no standing in any county, no forge, no licence in a workshop, no metal he may spend, and no bell he may ring that he made.** He can walk, ask, read a document he is allowed to read, write in his own book, and be refused. **He cannot fill in a form, cannot enter an answer for a household, cannot certify a bell, and cannot speak for any ward in this kingdom, and all four of those are clauses of his own receipt and he asked for them.**

**The three questions, and the state of each on Day 137.** *How does a Crown office turn one clerk's answer into a whole settlement's consent* — **answered on Day 86**, in a form and not in a room, and untouched. *Where a name goes when it is registered* — **still not answered**, and the nearest thing the last volume produced is that a number is a place a thing is filed at and a reference going somewhere is not a person. *Which office is it* — **answered in the only way it can be, and the answer is worse:** there are about nine hundred rooms and one format, and a format does not have a door, a name is a thing you can be refused and a format is not a name, and a signal is a real signal and a warrant is what an office does with a real signal. **Answering it closed nothing.**

**And the sentence the volume opens on, in his own hand, unresolved:** ***if there were three of me.*** He has got one of the three answers and not two, and the one is his own.

## Starting power level

He does not identify a copied answer, does not perceive, and **is not going to learn where a sound went.** In Chapter 0146 a forced relay came through him and he decided nothing and cut nothing and **gained nothing he can carry**: he could not keep his feet for about four minutes and could not hold a straight edge afterwards. **He is not told where a sound went and no volume may tell him.**

**The one question this volume inherits from Chapter 0146 is not a power question and must not become one:** ***whether a Crown clapper can be refused by a route, and what a person who holds a line is obliged to do about it.*** A line is worked by a district. ## Major factions

- **The displaced settlements along the missing route.** **Not a faction, because that is the volume's finding and it is not available at the start.** At the start they are a rumour of a place: a district nobody can name, a road that is on a schedule and not on the ground, and a coach inn whose panel is gone from every copy.

## Midpoint reversal

**Reached at about Chapter 0176 and not anticipated before it, in the shape Volume 02 established with the roll that does not need anybody's answer and Volume 03 established with the format that does not have a door.**

He goes up the river looking for **a removed section of road** and finds **a line of settlements.** Not a town that was forgotten and not a village that was burned: **nine places, each with people in it, each with a market or a ford or a landing stage, and each of them entered in a district's papers as something other than a place people live.** The removed sections of the route map are not a concealment of geography. **They are the removal of those places from the panel that says who is owed what, and the reason is on the page in a clerk's own hand and the clerk is right: a household that is answered for is a household that has a keeper, and a keeper is a person who may be asked to hold a line.** **The people living along the removed sections have been made administratively invisible so that the Crown can use their labour without registering their answers** — they work, they are paid in coin and in food, they are counted in a return about a thing in a ward, **and no form in this kingdom anywhere has a box that says they are a household.** The reversal is not that they are hidden and not that they are guilty: **it is that the map is a list of obligations, and a person who is off the map is a person nobody owes anything to, and that is cheaper than a conspiracy by a very long way.**

**The one permitted seed about it already exists and is Chapter 0145: a man stacking sacks in a yard, talking about his father's line and the day it stopped answering and the ones above him — and the chapter says on the page that this is a man talking about his father and not a finding. That is Volume 04's inheritance. IT HAS NOT BEEN NAMED AND IT MUST NOT BE NAMED BEFORE THE VOLUME EARNS IT, and no chapter of Batch 0001 or 0002 may state the reversal, propose it, hint at it in a day-book, or have any character say a sentence that is a version of it.**

## Volume climax

**Chapters 191 to 200, on a river town and the roads above it.**

He opens a route to warn a flood-stressed town. **A route, not a bell: a line of people on foot from the flat to the low quarter, each of them saying one thing that cannot be edited, and the whole of it true.** The signal is true. **The town cannot agree on what to do** — three answers and not one agreement, in the volume's own arithmetic — and the disagreement is not villainy; it is that a household that is owed something and a household that is afraid of something are two different people and a signal that flattens them is a false thing. **Panic kills a shipment and leaves a poorer quarter exposed.** And he must choose: **repeat the warning without adding an order, even when the result is incomplete.** He repeats it. He does not add an order. **The result is that some of the low quarter is wet and some of it is not, and he can account for which and cannot account for why, and nobody is told what to decide and about forty people in a yard are told nothing at all about what to decide and are not going to be.** No Crown office is defeated. Dain is not named. No bell he made is unbarred. **Nobody is rescued and nothing is restored.**

## Concrete resolution

By Chapter 200:

- **The local promise is answered: a route is a person on foot and the instrument cannot be edited, so the only thing that can be added to a warning is a lie.** It costs somebody: a shipment, a quarter, a week of a river canton carrying a repair it did not agree to carry, and a man who cannot hold a straight edge for a day and a half after the hundred and thirty-second and still cannot at the end of this volume.
- **The missing route is restored as a physical route, not as a copy.** **Sections of road are walked and put back in the ground, and the map is a map and not a key, and a route that has been walked by four people is a fact about four people and not about a plate.** The rectangle is still not explained and **may not be**.
- **The nine settlements are entered as places people live**, and how that is done is a form, and the form is a district's, and the district is right, and **nobody is thanked and the entry does not restore anybody's answer.**
- **The old network's question is advanced and not answered:** whether a Crown clapper can be refused by a route, and what a person who holds a line is obliged to do about it. **Three partial answers and no conclusion, and the seed from Chapter 0145 is still a man talking about his father.**
- **The route map, read at last, shows that the First Answer is draining obligations from the edges to sustain the capital.** Every removed section is a district that stops being answered for and starts being worked. **The First Answer chamber is not opened, described or approached, and this is what a map says about it and not what anybody sees in it.**
- **The river cantons agree to share physical repairs and refuse a central command**, and the refusal is stated as a fact about habit and not about principle, and **Tomas is not the clerk of it and does not keep the terms and will not take the same words to two cantons and come back with two the same.**
- **A warrant stands. A notice comes off a board because it is done. A store issues a fitting against somebody else's instruction. A man in a chair four days' road away is not thanked and is not owed a visit. A woman inside a building is not met. A bell in a sealed yard in Kells is not unbarred and does not travel.**
- **The Crown is not defeated.**

## New question for the next volume

**Can consent survive a distance that makes every answer inconvenient?** Volume 04's resolution is a map that says the capital is fed by the edges, and a federation of eight or nine benches that will share a repair and will not take a command. **What neither of those can do is make an answer convenient.** Every claim in this volume has to be carried by somebody on foot to somebody who does not want it, at the price of a day and a half of work, and there is no instrument in this kingdom that makes that cheap — and the next volume's question is whether anything can.

- **No new stage and no new sense at any point in this volume.** No perception is attempted, none is had, and **no chapter may describe him reaching for one.** He does not learn to identify a copied answer, does not learn where a sound went, **and is not told, and is not going to be.**
- **The one permitted power change in Volume 04 is not a power change.** It is a physical and civic capacity he acquires by practice and cost: **he can walk a route and get other people to walk it with him, and he knows what a road is for.** That is a fact about feet and habit and is not a stage and may not be written as one.
- **Tamsin's hearing is not restored and is not rescued, and is worse at the end of Volume 04 than at the end of Volume 03.** **No chapter may restore any of it, ask her for a reason, hold it over her, treat her as a casualty of his decision, or treat her as a person who has been exposed** — and **she is not to be improved, softened or made sentimental about it in order to make a scene work.**
- **The leash is at eight uses. No ninth use of the slate is staged in this volume, and a non-use is not a use.**
- **The growth is civic, relational and physical:** a man with a map he cannot read, a warrant standing behind him, and a road. **He becomes a man who can get a true sentence to a place four days' walk away, and who knows exactly what that costs and cannot make it cost less.**
- **The cost is not a wound, a fever or a fade.** It is a shipment, a wet quarter, a week of somebody else's labour, a hearing that is worse, and a sentence he is not allowed to finish.
- **The register is thirteen and may not become fourteen**, and the frame is never in a building, and no bell of his is ever unbarred.

## What Volume 04 may not do, listed so that a card cannot quietly authorise it

- **It may not answer how an office gets into a bell, where a Crown clapper came from, the official behind OFF. 411, the name-transfer mechanism, what became of Lira Vale, or Iven Sore's service entry.** All six are open and belong to Volume 05, Volume 08 and later.
- **It may not name Dain and may not introduce a new final enemy.**
- **It may not open, describe or approach the First Answer chamber.** **Volume 05 finds Lira alive in it and not before.**
- **It may not restore Tamsin's hearing, rescue her, ask her for a reason, hold her deafness over her, or treat her as a person who has been exposed.**
- **It may not give Tomas a new stage, a new sense, or any way of knowing where a warning landed; may not let him learn to read a map for free; and may not tell him where either of his two public ringings went.**
- **It may not restore the Reckoning 619 bench-book or the Vale Bellhouse archive, or invent a surviving copy of either.**
- **It may not lift the warrant, defeat a Crown office, relieve an officer, or unbar any bell he made.**
- **It may not find Salla Keene by a document. She is not found.**
- **It may not bring a Master Key, a charter, a commission or a Crown licence into existence as a solution.**
- **It may not coin a noun for the Crown's third kind of bell, a bell that answers somebody else's stroke.**
- **It may not name a weekday or a month, and no state file may attach a half-day to an hour that a chapter does not attach one to.**
- **It may not write a trio, may not resolve *if there were three of me*, and may not stage a second letter from Kells.**
