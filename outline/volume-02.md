# Volume 02 Outline: The Unpaid Names

**Volume number:** 02
**Chapter range:** 51–100
**Created:** by the Volume 01 volume-close audit, **after** the audit passed (`reviews/volume-01/volume-audit.md`, and `PHASE_SYSTEM.md:179`). It is written against the prose of Volume 01 and not against any summary of it.

**Provenance of the inherited facts, added on review.** A review flagged two names in this outline as possible new canon. **Both are already on the page in Volume 01 and neither is a proposed canon change**, and the outline is now cited to the chapter for each so that no later writer treats them as inventions:

- **Jory Fenn** is eight (`ch5:15`), was taken six days before Day 1 and so on **Day −5** (`ch5:21`, `ch5:145`, restated at `ch20:149` and `state/continuity.md:48`), which is **before the L-4 notice**, and his only records are a schedule line and a household fragment with his line cut away (`ch5:47`). All inherited.
- **Marta Vell** is named by Tamsin Rook at the towpath on the thirty-third (`ch50:229–231`): about sixty **on Tamsin's word**, thirty years of the water bell, and **entered on a schedule in a building in Fourmile as a bell of this ward with no keeper against it, which nobody has told her and which Tomas did not tell her.** All inherited, including the fact that she does not know.
- **One thing this outline did get wrong, and it is corrected here:** the sister's count **is spent.** Nell Fenn made it on the twelfth, kept it nineteen days, and spent it on the evening of the thirtieth on a single written question to the district, and says she has about four hours of use out of it and cannot account for the rest (`ch50:11`). It is not an available record in this volume.

**Central pressure:** Erasures from the Quiet Office are not merely missing records. **Names are being used as unpaid relay anchors, binding families to duties their members never accepted.** A household answers once, in one hand, and the answer is carried forward on a roll that goes on answering for that household for years — and somewhere past Miremarket, what a person is *registered as* decides what the Crown may do to the people who live with them. Tomas is on the road with an object he cannot ring, a receipt that forbids him speaking for anybody, and the two questions from his own day-book. He cannot ask a ward to be warned, cannot certify a bell, cannot cast one, and cannot enter an answer on anybody's behalf. He can write, ask, carry, and be refused.

## Starting state

**Tomas is a suspect with a traveling bell case.** He left Kells before dawn on the thirty-third on the river road north along the towpath with a barrow. What he has: the **fixed stripped Crown survey carrier in its mounting frame**, the retaining bolts made fast for transport, the frame in its reinforced padded cradle, **no clapper, and a receipt in four clauses and a fifth that forbids furnishing one, forbids certifying it, forbids saying what it means, and forbids any answer given to any signal on or by it being entered as the answer of a household of this ward.** He also has a route fragment in the custody chain, a set of day-book pages, a copy of the Measure's road-map correction, and two questions he cannot answer. He has no metal, no shop, no licence in a workshop, no forge, and **no bell he may ring** — his own bell is barred by the Bellwrights' Measure and is sitting on a bench in a sealed yard in Kells.

He is a suspect. The Crown instrument in his cradle was in a ward on the morning a renewal was conducted, and the district filed a finding that the ward's bells were unaccounted for; that finding was withdrawn on the thirty-third by a Crown surveyor, and **the withdrawal is a document three hundred people can ask for and nobody has yet asked for.** The Measure's bar and the receipt's fifth clause are the two instruments that have stopped being his to appeal.

**Tamsin joins him because the route is the only way to find a stolen child.** **Jory Fenn** is eight years old, was taken in the round before the L-4 notice, and has not come back; the only records of him are a schedule line and a household fragment with a line cut away, and a sister's count that **was spent on the thirtieth and cannot be spent again**, which is why nobody in Kells can hand this volume a starting point. **Tamsin's reason is the road and not him.** She has walked it for eleven years and knows which names a carrier's book carries and which ones a clerk's roll carries, and she has said out loud in a lane in the rain that a warning does not require her to obey him. Her hearing loss is permanent, it is public in Kells by her own act, and **she is not to be rescued out of it, and nobody in this volume is to hold it over her or ask her for a reason.** She travels on the slate Mercy Slee wrote, which neither of them wrote first, and it puts a leash on both of them.

**Mara's trail is visible but unreliable.** Notes in a hand that is hers or is not, copying wax, a page she has evidently read. She is inside the Quiet Office and she is not answering him, and every piece of the trail is a thing somebody wants him to have.

## Starting relationships

- **Tomas and Tamsin:** travelling together on a written arrangement that neither wrote, with the leash on both, four terms of hers intact, and one unanswered question about what the ward is owed.
- **Tomas and Oren Pike:** a practical alliance that has become a distance. Oren signed the last official act of eleven years on a receipt and put the watch's mark under a district wax. He is a Crown-appointed captain in a real town and he is the only person in Kells who has ever answered Tomas in his own hand.
- **Tomas and Iven Sore:** still unresolved and still not a friend. Sore is right about a bell and is right about a different thing, and his question at a gate — *did the Crown withdraw a commission the Measure granted, or did it withdraw a name* — is a guild question now and will become a Crown one.
- **Tomas and Nell Fenn:** she spent the thing she carried for nineteen days and cannot account for the spending of it. She is the person the ward acts through and she is sixteen and she is in Kells.
- **Tamsin and the road:** eleven years of it, and it is the only profession she has left, and it is now the only route to a child.
- **Mara and Tomas:** mutual abandonment, unresolved, and she will not answer a letter.

## Starting power level

**Tomas is at the first edge of Stage 1 (Near Toll) and this volume may not move him.** He has bound one bounded warning in his life and spent it. His only perception is whether a nearby answer is strained. **No new stage, no new sense, and no way of knowing where a warning landed** — and this volume makes the second and third of those into the volume's argument rather than into a power.

What he gains is **civic and relational**, and it is not a gift: he learns what a person can do with a slate, a road and a witness. He can no longer ask a ward to be warned, cannot certify, cannot cast, and cannot speak for a household — and by the end of the volume he can put a question to an office in a form the office must answer in its own name, hold a road together for eleven days with people who did not agree with each other, and stand in a market while a bell he did not make carries his own fault to a room full of strangers.

**The one thing the volume gives him that he has never had is a bell that somebody else made and somebody else certified.** He does not cast it. He asks for it, in the open, and a smith on the road casts it from a stock with a record and a master certifies it, and Tomas's whole argument for twenty days is that a bell is a mouth and a mouth belongs to the person whose claim is in it. **His own bell remains barred and remains in a sealed yard in Kells and is not in this volume.**

## Major locations

- **The river road and the towpath** north out of Kells, four miles in this county and the fifth not, and every stopping place on it: **Ocklebridge**, a river village of about two hundred at about the second mile, with a landing stage and a smithy, where **Marta Vell** keeps the water bell, has rung it every morning for thirty years, is about sixty on Tamsin Rook's word, **has been entered on a schedule in a building in Fourmile as a bell of this ward with no keeper against it, and has not been told by anybody, and Tomas did not tell her.**
- **The four counties' line**, and a **smithy** on the far side of it where metal can be bought with a record and a bell can be certified.
- **The towpath carriers' hut**, a room above a net-shop that is not an office and holds no record anybody is obliged to keep, and is the nearest thing this volume has to a court.
- **The Fourmile renewal office** — a real building, in a real town, with an address, and **District Officer Marten Cope** in it. He is a named man and he is **permitted and useful**: he wrote the letter of the thirty-second that caused the custody review, he answers in an officer's own name, and the ward's pinned reply of the thirty-second is his office's most careful piece of writing. He is not a villain and he is not a door. **The district must become a place a reader can walk into.**
- **Miremarket** — a river market town downstream, already on the page as the place that took the chandler's work and the miller's feast bell. **Its clerk's hall holds the Answer Rolls.**
- **The route map**, incomplete: sections have been **removed from every public copy**, and the removals are in the same places in every copy, which is a fact about a hand and not about a fire.

## Major factions

- **The Unpaid Names.** Not an organisation. A category, and the volume's title: names entered on a roll that carry a duty their owner never accepted, and families that go on answering for them. Their only organisation is that they have never been in a room together.
- **The towpath.** Carriers, ferry crews, a smithy, a landing stage, and a set of informal rules about who carries a name and how far. It has no charter and it is the only thing in this volume that has never once filed a return.
- **The Fourmile district renewal office.** A desk with a name in it, a building with an address, and a man who answers questions. It can be the volume's first ally and it cannot be its solution.
- **Miremarket's clerk's hall.** Clerks who record one household member's signature as a response for an entire household, and who are not all monsters: the distinction they keep is a *practice*, and practice is what this volume has learned to ask about.
- **The Bellwrights' Measure.** Its bar stands on the plain carrier in Kells and it will not certify, inventory, or attend with a spade. **In this volume the guild begins to split between licensed central control and independent craft**, and the split is not a matter a bellmaker settles.
- **The Crown's renewal district.** Not a villain in a room. Schedules, forms, returns, a finding, a withdrawal, and a set of people who file them.

## Mystery progression

1. The road answers a question the Kells pages could not: **a household that answered once, in one hand, is still answering six, nine, twelve years later.**
2. The same signature appears on lines belonging to houses in four counties, written by people who have never met, in a hand that is careful.
3. **Miremarket keeps the Answer Rolls**, and a clerk's roll and a ward's memory disagree about the same household, and the roll is the one with a stamp.
4. **Where a name goes when it is registered** stops being a question and becomes a place with a floor, a corridor and a form.
5. **Pell Rill** is the auditor who believes the distinction between one person's signature and a household's answer is a useful fiction — and he can show, with a number, that he is more useful than the truth.
6. The volume resolves **what an answer is made of and who has been signing**, and leaves the mechanism, the Crown clapper and the higher office exactly where Volume 01 left them.
7. **Mara's trail** is confirmed to be hers and is still not an answer.

## Escalation sequence

- **Chapters 51–60 (Batch 0001) — The road asks a question.** Day 34 onward. Ocklebridge, the towpath, and the first household that has answered a Crown form for six years through a man who has not been in the house since. The **Answer Rolls are not reached and their existence is not known**; the volume opens on a road, a slate, a receipt and a woman who is entitled to be slow about a bell. A **name on a roll and a person in a house are two different things** is established and tested. **Pell Rill does not appear in this batch in any form.** Ends with a decision to go to Miremarket, taken on a signature, and with one phrase at a landing that neither of them can explain.
- **Chapters 61–70 (Batch 0002) — Four counties, one hand.** The route map with its sections cut out of every public copy; the smithy on the far side of the county line and the first bell Tomas has ever rung that he did not make; a household that has been moved out of a house because of what one of its members is registered as; and **Marten Cope's office used properly for the first time**, in person, with an address. **Pell Rill's name arrives on a document in this batch and the man arrives at its end.**
- **Chapters 71–80 (Batch 0003) — Miremarket and the Answer Rolls. The midpoint reversal.** They reach Miremarket and find the rolls: clerks record one household member's signature as a response for an entire household, and the district can certify anything inside a column and cannot certify the column. Rill is encountered as a courteous, competent auditor who can produce a better answer than the truth in about nine minutes. The reversal: **the ward's whole argument — that an answer is a person and may be withdrawn — is true and is useless here, because the roll does not need anybody's answer, it needs one signature and a stamp.** What was a theft of consent in Kells is a bookkeeping practice here, and it is defended by people who are not lying. Ends with the first household in Miremarket that will say no on the record, and it costs them.
- **Chapters 81–90 (Batch 0004) — The Unpaid Names are a list.** The names on the rolls begin to be read as duties, and families are bound to them; a child is moved; Tamsin's reason for being on the road becomes a name on a roll in a town four counties away; the truth begins to cost somebody other than Tomas. **Tomas learns what a person can do with a slate, a road and a witness**, and it is not enough, and it is not a stage. Mara's trail is confirmed and refuses to answer. Ends with a merchant's house on a roll and an inheritance that cannot be claimed while the roll stands.
- **Chapters 91–100 (Batch 0005) — The apology bell and the confiscation. Climax and resolution.** **Tomas rings a bell he did not make, whose entire claim is his own fault, in a market in Miremarket, in front of the people who are owed an answer — and a local official's part in a disappearance comes out of the room, in the open, because he cannot produce a document and then says something into the record.** The truth opens inheritance claims and a merchant revolt. The Crown confiscates the room they were keeping the copies in and the bench they were lent, **and the evidence that would have protected the victims goes with them.** Tomas and Tamsin recover a fragment of the name ledger **and prove the Silent Muster is one clause of a larger Renewal operation — NOT DELIVERED, carried to Volume 03; see the close note at the end of this file.** The Bellwrights' Measure splits. **No coronation, no cure, no trial won, no Crown office defeated.**

## Midpoint reversal

**Reached at about Chapter 76 and not anticipated before it.** Tomas and Tamsin get to Miremarket and find the **Answer Rolls**: clerks who record one household member's signature as a response for an entire household. **Pell Rill is the auditor who believes the distinction between one person's signature and a household's answer is a useful fiction** — not a man who says so in a room, but a man who can show a district clerk, with a number, that a fiction that saves a clerk four hours a day is cheaper than a truth that opens four hours of argument. He is earned across the first half: his **name arrives on a document in Chapter 63 or thereabouts, before the man does**; he is quoted approvingly in an auditor's return in the sixties; he is met once, courteously, in the seventies; and the midpoint is not his arrival but the moment the ward's method is found to be *correct and insufficient*. **He must not be summoned in the first batch and he does not appear in Batch 0001 in any form.**

## Volume climax

**Chapters 91–100, in Miremarket.** Tomas rings a bell he did not make, in a public place outside this county — the Measure's bar is on *his* bell and in *this* county — and its whole claim is an apology: what he did, what he could not do, and the names of the people who did the rest. It names the known facts, the unknown facts and his own fault, and **it tells nobody what to decide.** A local official in that room cannot answer a question on paper, and answers it in words, and the words are his part in a disappearance. **The truth opens inheritance claims and a merchant revolt**, because a signature on a roll is the only thing standing between six families and an inheritance they are owed. **The Crown confiscates the workshop they were lent and the evidence that could have protected the victims**, and the confiscation is lawful, and Tomas's receipt makes no move against it, because the receipt does not let him claim anything.

## Concrete resolution

By Chapter 100:

- **The local promise is answered.** What an answer is made of, and who has been signing, is known and is on the public record in Miremarket: a signature is one person's, a household is many, and a roll that carries the first as the second is a document about the clerk and not about the house.
- **A fragment of the name ledger is recovered** — not the ledger, a fragment, by a person who had no legal right to it and a witness who wrote the hour down.
- **The Silent Muster is proved to be one clause of a larger Renewal operation**, and proved by a document and a date, not by a bell. **NOT PAID. See *Volume 02 close — two required payoffs that were not delivered* at the end of this file. Carried to Volume 03.**
- **Tomas is not newly powerful.** He has rung one bell he did not make and has no stage, no sense, and no way of knowing where a warning landed. He is a man with a bar on his own bell, a receipt with a fifth clause, and a road.
- **Tamsin's hearing is permanent and she is not rescued out of anything.** She carries, she refuses, she is refused, and she is not treated as exposed.
- **The Bellwrights' Measure splits** between licensed central control and independent craft, and Tomas does not get to settle it.
- **The Crown is not defeated.** A finding is withdrawn, an officer is relieved, an office answers a question, a workshop is seized, and a set of rolls in a real building is still on a shelf.

## Relationship and emotional progression

- **Tomas and Tamsin:** a leash on both of them, held. He asks, she refuses, he does not press, and the refusal is on the page with a date. They learn to work a road together and to disagree in front of other people, which is the test a private arrangement cannot pass. **No binding bell, no confession scene, and no rescue.**
- **Tomas and Nell Fenn:** she is in Kells and he is on the road, and the volume's best civic act is something she can do without him. She is sixteen and she gets better at it.
- **Tomas and Oren Pike:** a correspondence with a man in a chair in a real building, and the volume's proof that an alliance is not a leash.
- **Tomas and Iven Sore:** the guild splits and Tomas is on the wrong side of the split in a way that costs him something he wants.
- **Tomas and Mara:** her trail is confirmed, and she does not answer, and he does not go into an office to make her. **NOT PAID. She does not appear in any chapter of Volume 02. See the close note at the end of this file. Carried to Volume 03.**

## New question for the next volume

**Where did the first Crown clapper come from, and why is the old network accepting it?** Volume 02 establishes that a roll can be made to answer without a person, and stops. It does not name the Crown clapper, does not identify the higher official, and does not answer how an office gets into a bell — and Volume 03 is where the answer is earned, in Sennet, inside the Quiet Office.

## Planned power and relationship progression

- **No new stage and no new sense.** Tomas is at the first edge of Near Toll at the end of Volume 02 exactly as at the end of Volume 01. He does not learn to read an answer, does not learn where a sound went, and does not gain a second binding.
- **Tamsin's hearing is not restored and is not rescued.** Permanent, public by her own act, and hers to price. **Nobody in this volume asks her for a reason, holds it over her, or treats her as a person who has been exposed.**
- **The growth is civic and relational:** a man who has lost the right to speak for a ward learning what a person can do with a slate, a road and a witness — and learning that this is a real method with real limits, not a consolation prize.
- **The cost is a confiscation, not a wound.** He loses the room, the bench and the copies. He does not lose a stage, a memory, or anybody's ability to hear.

## Final chapter image

A road after the confiscation, in the rain, with a barrow and a frame in a cradle that still cannot be rung, and a handbell in a bag belonging to somebody else. **The two questions from Kells are answered and a third one is on the page in its place, and it is not a better one.** A woman who has been deaf since the fourth of the month reads Tomas's shoulder from behind him, because she has been doing it for eleven years, and says the one sentence that closes the volume, and it is not a promise and it is not a refusal: it is a question, asked by the person who has the right to ask it, and the man answers it in four words and does not improve it in the morning.

**Delivered in Chapter 100, beat for beat, and not improved.**

## Batch 0005 card as written, and what it cost

### Volume 02 close — two required payoffs that were not delivered

**Volume 02 is complete at Chapter 100 and the ending above is the ending that was written. Two items this outline required were not delivered, and both are recorded here as UNPAID rather than quietly struck, because the alternative is a state layer that claims a payoff the chapters do not contain — the specific failure this project has produced most often.**

**1. Mara Vale does not appear in any chapter of Volume 02.** She appears in Volume 01 in Chapters 1, 4 and 8 only. This outline required her at `:23` (premise — her trail visible and unreliable), `:32` and `:104` (relationship — mutual abandonment unresolved, she will not answer a letter, her trail confirmed and refusing to answer), `:68` (mystery item 7), and `:75` (the Batch 0004 card). `bible/characters.md:120` also places *Tomas gives Mara a copy of a ledger and accepts that she may publish what he would prefer hidden* in Volume 02. **Batch 0003 dropped her, Batch 0004 dropped her, and Batch 0005 inherited the gap; each recorded its own shortfall in its own summary and the thread was allowed to fall.**

**2. The Silent Muster is not proved anywhere in Volume 02.** The Concrete Resolution above and the Batch 0005 card at `:76` both require it proved by Chapter 100 to be one clause of a larger Renewal operation, **by a document and a date and not by a bell.** The words *muster* and *Renewal* appear in no chapter from 91 to 100, and the substance is absent from the whole volume. The Batch 0005 prompt forbade only the *phrase* being spoken aloud; nothing carried the substance, and that distinction went unexamined until the volume closed.

**Neither is a new plot element and neither introduces anything.** Both are established in `bible/`, `outline/series.md` and `outline/ending.md`, and **Mara is load-bearing in `outline/ending.md` for Volumes 9 and 11** — she reconciles enough to work with Tomas, refuses to enter the Bellwrights' Measure, keeps the record that proves the old warning, and makes sure the reforms include the right to remove her own name from a public role.

**Nothing in the written prose has to be undone to place them.** The relationship is *unstarted*, not damaged: Volume 02 never said anything about Mara that a Volume 03 appearance has to retract. The Silent Muster is *unproved*, not contradicted.

**These are therefore Volume 03's first two obligations, in this order:**

1. **Mara's trail becomes visible and is confirmed to be hers and refuses to answer, and he does not go into an office to make her.** Her Volume 03 appearance must not resolve the family mystery and must not become a rescue.
2. **The Silent Muster is proved, by a document and a date and not by a bell, to be one clause of a larger Renewal operation** — a clause, not a plot, and not a new final enemy.

**A Volume 03 plan that does not place both of these has repeated the failure, and the next volume-close audit should check for them by name rather than by scanning for the word.**
