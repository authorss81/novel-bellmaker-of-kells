# Volume 03 Batch 0001 Outline

**Batch number:** 0001
**Chapter range:** 101–110
**Days:** 87–96, one chapter to a day, no day renumbered and no day repeated
**Batch goal:** Put a man with no standing, no room, no place and thirteen documents on a road to the capital, and show him **the third kind of bell** — a bell on a post above a notice-board that is rung from a district store with nobody near it, so that **a sheet of paper is made out of a sound.** Then pay the two obligations Volume 02 left unpaid, in this order: **Mara's trail, confirmed and refusing (Chapter 0105), and the Silent Muster proved by a document and a date (Chapter 0107)** — and then make the Office put a notice in his own name, and end on the top terrace of Sennet with the discovery that the Office is a **format** and not a room, so that the third question cannot be answered with a room because it is not about one.

**This batch does not enter an archive, does not read a trial record, does not see a Crown clapper, does not name a higher official, does not describe the First Answer chamber, and does not touch the name-transfer mechanism.** It sees a **fitting** and learns that fittings come from a **store with a counter and a book and a number.** That is the whole of the mechanism this batch is allowed, and the finding is the store, not the fitting.

## Starting condition

**Day 87, the morning after Chapter 0100.** Tomas Vale is on a road up off the water from Miremarket with a barrow in the rain. He has: the fixed stripped Crown survey carrier in its frame in its reinforced padded cradle, four retaining bolts made fast at every place the frame has been set down since the thirty-third, **no clapper**, and a receipt in four clauses and a fifth, all five standing; the certified plain carrier in a sack against the cradle with nothing in it; **thirteen non-bell documents** and **five receipts**; a fragment of nine lines out of a book of numbers; four columns and a slate with the price side the other way up; and Tamsin Rook.

**He has no standing anywhere and is not looking for a place.** His three questions are on the page and the third one is the one a woman put to him in the rain: *Which office is it.* He answered it in four words and has not improved it.

**Sennet is about six days' road up the river and then inland.** The last two days of it are the Crown's maintained road, with notice-boards and bells on posts.

**What this batch may not do to the objects:** the Crown carrier is not rung and cannot be; the frame never leaves the cradle; the certified carrier is not rung again (it has four occasions in its life and two of them are his); **no bell Tomas has made is ever unbarred, and his own plain carrier stays in a sealed yard in Kells**; Tamsin's courier handbell may be rung **once**, in Chapter 0106, on her own terms, and never for him.

**And the register is thirteen before this batch and thirteen after it.** No chapter of 101–110 gives him a document, takes one from him, or leaves one behind. **The leaf in Chapter 0105 stays on the board. The notice in Chapter 0108 is posted and not served, because a man with no standing cannot be served a document.** Both refusals are refusals of a thing he wanted, and both are on the page.

## The volume's third kind of bell

**A bell that is rung by somebody else's stroke is neither a keeper's bell nor a plain bell, and this kingdom has no name for it on a form.** The batch establishes the description and does not coin a term in prose. Its three facts: a person does not have to be near it; the person who decides when it rings is not the person who reads the sheet; **and the sound arrives before the man with the book, so that by the time a household knows what it is, it has already been. **The first sheet he reads under a bell is dated four days forward of the ringing, and no man in the book has ever recorded how far ahead the bell goes.****

## The two inherited obligations, and the order they are paid in

1. **Chapter 0105 — Mara's trail becomes visible, is confirmed to be hers, and refuses to answer, and he does not go into an office to make her.** Confirmed by **comparison of the hand against her own hand in Volume 01**, and not by copying wax, because the one loose leaf in Amos Trewin's stock book is a leaf of unknown hand and Volume 01's note never bore her signature. **She must not resolve the family mystery, must not be rescued, must not become an ally he can summon, and must not be given an age by the narrator.**
2. **Chapter 0107 — the Silent Muster is proved, by a document and a date and not by a bell, to be one clause of a larger Renewal operation.** A printed standing instruction with a number and a date at the head, **eleven clauses**, headed with a word that contains **Renewal**; **clause seven concerns children not entered on a roll**; and a second dated sheet in another county refers to clause seven by number. **The sheet carries no signature, only a store number. Six of the eleven clauses are about things he has never heard of and he writes down that he does not know what they are and does not guess. It is a clause, not a plot, and it names no enemy.**

## Chapter cards

### Chapter 0101 — A Bell On A Post

- **POV:** Tomas Vale. **Location/time:** the road up off the water, four miles on, Day 87, from the fourth hour into the evening.
- **Goal:** get a hundred yards further up a road he has no reason to be on.
- **Resistance:** a barrow goes at the speed of a barrow; the second clause of the receipt is the reason he cannot pay with it; there is no yard behind the draper's any more; and he has no destination and has stopped looking for one.
- **Turn:** at about the sixth hour he passes a **notice-board with a bell hung on a post above it**, and the bell rings, and **there is nobody near it and nobody near it afterward.** Nobody strikes it. It rings because somebody far off struck something.
- **Information:** the Office's ordinary bells are rung for paper — a court date, an inspection, a notice. **The third kind of bell.** A bell with no keeper near it and a keeper's decision about eleven miles off.
- **Emotional change:** he has learned for six weeks that a document is what a person is read by. He finds out that **a sound is cheaper and goes further, and the sheet is only there to say what it was.**
- **Power:** none; no perception attempted; nothing rung by him.
- **Continuity:** the sheet on the board is dated and the date is not the day the bell rang; no office is named beyond a district; no weekday; no month.
- **Ending:** he writes the number of the board and does not improve it.
- **Pull:** the bell rang in a village of about two hundred at the fourth mile, and nobody in it chose the hour.

### Chapter 0103 — The Same Hand

- **POV:** Tomas Vale. **Location/time:** a smithy at a crossroads about eleven miles on, Day 89, from the fourth hour into the evening.
- **Goal:** have the fitting in a Crown bell looked at by somebody who works with metal every day.
- **Resistance:** **Doss Harrow has fitted Crown work to three bells in this county and can say what it is and is not going to say what it does.** He is right about the work and he is not a door and he is not going to be thanked.
- **Turn:** **the work is a coupling, a jumper, and a fitting that is not a clapper and is not the bell's own** — and Tomas recognises it as the same hand that mended a coupling cut nearly through in a reinforced padded cradle, with a thin copper jumper across it, in a yard behind a draper's shop, about ten days and four counties ago.
- **Information:** Crown fittings are **issued from a store, against a district instruction, with a number, like any other equipment.** Not a man in a room with a plan in it. A counter, a book, a store number.
- **Emotional change:** he has been carrying a question as *who did that to my bell*, and the answer is *a store*, and **a store is a smaller and more answerable thing than a man and it is also nine days' road away.**
- **Power:** none; nothing rung; he does not bind, does not read, does not perceive.
- **Continuity:** **this is a fitting and not a Crown clapper, and no chapter may call it one**; the two bells' comparison — a technique and a bench and a Crown pattern and about a year between the two, and not a person — is carried forward and not overturned.
- **Ending:** he has a store number and a district and a place, and he writes them flat and does not improve them.
- **Pull:** Sennet is six days up the road and a store is a room with a counter in it.

### Chapter 0104 — A Bell On A Roll

- **POV:** Tomas Vale. **Location/time:** the same village and a market town four miles beyond it, Day 90, from the fourth hour into the evening.
- **Goal:** find out what the district does with a thing it cannot send a man to.
- **Resistance:** the return book is a book of **things**, and the office's own form has four fields and none of them is the words, and the man who keeps the book in the village has been keeping it about four years and has never been asked what a line means.
- **Turn:** **a bell can be entered on a roll.** A market bell of this county, with a keeper's field, and the keeper's field empty on one line. **A water bell, entered on a district schedule two counties back with the keeper's field empty, is the same form, and Tomas writes that down and does not join the two into a pattern.**
- **Information:** **the Office keeps a list of objects.** He has spent two volumes looking for a list of people. **A thing is easier to take than a person and cheaper to enter than a household and there is no hand against a line for a bell.**
- **Emotional change:** the discovery is not a victory and it is not even a shock. It is that the thing he has been unable to find **is not the thing that is frightening**, and that fright was the wrong tool.
- **Power:** none.
- **Continuity:** **no name is transferred and no mechanism is described; a district number is a filing reference and not a man**; Marta Vell is not visited and not written to again.
- **Ending:** a bell is on a roll with nothing in the keeper's box, and nobody in the village has ever asked.
- **Pull:** the roll in this book is a roll of things, and the roll he has been looking for is a roll of people, and both are in the same county.

### Chapter 0105 — A Leaf On A Board

- **POV:** Tomas Vale. **Location/time:** a market town on the Crown's maintained road, Day 91, from the fourth hour into the evening.
- **Goal:** get to Sennet, and see what is on the boards on the way.
- **Resistance:** **Mara Vale.** A leaf pinned at an angle under a drawing-pin among the Office notices on a public board, in a hand he knows, and the hand is confirmed by comparison and not by wax, because the one loose leaf in Amos Trewin's stock book is a leaf of unknown hand and the page does not support the other attribution.
- **Turn:** **the leaf is five words, it is about him, and it refuses.** It does not say where she is, whether she is alive, or what she wants. It says one thing he did not know about himself, in her hand, on a public board, and it has been there nine days.
- **Information:** **she is inside the Office and she is answering a public board and not a brother, and the board is read every morning.**
- **Decision:** **he does not take the leaf off the board** — the first thing in three volumes he has refused that he wanted, refused on the ground that he was in a town where a man was taken at about the sixth hour for five sheets off a table, and that a paper off a public board in a Crown town is the same act with a different name. **And he does not go in anywhere to ask after her.** A woman at the board tells him the board is read every morning and that a leaf which has been there nine days has not been taken down, and that this is not because they do not see it.
- **Emotional change:** he learns that being answered in public by a person who owes him nothing is not the same as being answered, **and that the sentence he wanted was about him and not about her, and that is the shape of the whole of this and he is not going to improve it.**
- **Power:** none.
- **Continuity:** **her name is not said out loud in this chapter**; no age for her; nothing is resolved; she is not rescued, not met, not made an ally; the register stays thirteen.
- **Ending:** he leaves the leaf where it is, and a second leaf is not on this board, and Sennet is four days up the road.
- **Pull:** she is four days ahead of him and she is writing on boards, and there is more than one board.

### Chapter 0106 — The Box For The Person Who Repeated It

- **POV:** Tamsin Rook. **Location/time:** the same market town, Day 92, from the fourth hour into the evening, closing on a stone by the water with her boots off.
- **Goal:** find out what her eleven years of road work looks like on the other side of a form.
- **Resistance:** **a man in that market fills in a form with a box for the name of the person who repeated a thing, and about four hundred people a year fill that box in, and none of them are wicked, and the form is cheaper than a witness and a witness can refuse.** And the leash is **not** used a ninth time, and the non-use costs him anyway, and she refuses and gives a reason, and the reason is about her **method** and is the first such reason in eleven years.
- **Turn:** **she rings her own handbell once, in a market, on her own terms, and it is not for him and it does not help him.** It is for a man who is writing down who repeated what, and it is four inches long and it is the loudest thing in that square for about nine seconds, and it reveals to about nine hundred people that the woman with the slate is the woman they saw say things back, permanently, in a town with a district store in it.
- **Information:** **a box is cheaper than a witness and a witness can refuse, and that is the whole of why a form wins, and the same sentence is the one a clerk in Miremarket said in nine seconds about a document and he was right and she has been carrying the other half of it for eleven years without knowing it.**
- **Emotional change:** she finds out that the thing she has been proudest of — that a thing said twice in a market cannot be got back — **is a thing a form can count, and being countable is worse than being lost**, and she says so, and nobody disagrees with her.
- **Power:** none. Her hearing is not discussed in a sentence about her hearing.
- **Continuity:** **nobody asks her for a reason at any hour in this chapter, including him**; her bell is rung once and it is hers; the four columns stand and the fourth has two lines in it now and she has not improved them.
- **Ending:** her entry on the stone: the price of a box, and that she rang a bell once, and that nobody asked her why.
- **Pull:** Sennet is four days up the road and a form with a box in it is going there too.

### Chapter 0109 — Two Kinds Of Paper

- **POV:** Tomas Vale. **Location/time:** Sennet, the middle terrace and the foot of the top, the public counter of the Quiet Office, Day 95, from the fourth hour into the evening.
- **Goal:** get somebody in a building in the capital to say what a document is, one more time, and this time say more.
- **Resistance:** **Perrin Lowe is correct about every part of it.** The counter takes papers and not people. A man may look at a list of fittings if he has a reason, **and a reason is a form, and the form is the Office's own.**
- **Turn:** the answer is longer than the sentence he has carried for fifty-four days, and the extra part is the volume: **there are two kinds of paper — the record of what you said, and the thing that says it for you.** Four clerks in four counties gave him the first half and every one of them was right. **He is not angry and he does not blame the clerk and he does not get a better sentence; he gets a longer true one.**
- **Information:** the second kind is not kept in this building and is not Perrin Lowe's business. He is given a **store number, a district, a counter and a form**, and a form is the one thing he cannot fill in for himself. **A counter takes papers and not people, and that is the volume's last finding and it is not a defeat.**
- **Emotional change:** the sentence that stopped being a door in a clerk's hall in Miremarket turns out to have been a **door with one leaf**, and the other leaf is in a building he cannot walk into, and **a sentence that is true and incomplete is worse to carry than a wrong one, because a wrong one can be thrown out.**
- **Power:** none.
- **Continuity:** no copy of anything is given and none is asked for; **a document nobody has asked for is a thing that has not happened** and the form that would let him ask is the Office's own; no official above a district is named.
- **Ending:** a store number, a district, a counter, and about nine days.
- **Pull:** a store is a room with a counter in it and a man in it, and it is four streets and four flights up.

- **Turn:** the third question is answered in the only way it can be and the answer is worse: **the third question is not about a room, so it cannot be answered with a room, and a name — the Quiet Office — is the name of about nine hundred rooms and a set of forms.** He writes it flat and does not improve it. **And a second leaf is pinned to the Office's own board in a public place with a date and a store number on the back of it, in her hand, and he does not go in.**

- **Emotional change:** ninety chapters of looking for a man in a room with a plan in it, and **the finding is a format, and a format cannot be refused, and the one thing he has learned to do for six weeks — ask a person, and be refused — does not work on a format, and he has nothing else.**

- **Continuity:** **the First Answer chamber is not opened, described, or approached**; no official above a district is named; no Crown clapper is seen; the register stays thirteen; the hearing is nine days off and a warrant does not exist yet.

- **Pull:** Sennet is a city of about nine thousand and a store is four streets and four flights up and a hearing is in nine days, and in a market four counties behind him a queue is still saying names back to each other and about nine of them know their names are on a piece of slate.
