## Batch goal

**Get Tomas Vale off the step by the river with a barrow he cannot pay for and a map he cannot read, and put him on a road that is on a schedule and not on the ground — and end the ten days with a travelling group of four or five people he did not recruit, who have agreed about exactly one thing, and a river canton that has said no to him in the correct way.**

**The batch's own promise, which is small and must be kept: a road can be walked.** Not a road found, not a road restored, not a road explained. **He walks nine miles of copper and does not find the section that is missing, and a second man does find it, and the difference between them is that the second man was born at the end of it.** Nothing more.

## Starting condition

Day 137, the morning after Chapter 0150. **A step by the river on the flat about a mile out of Sennet at about the ninth hour of the evening on Day 136's night, a barrow, on foot, and he is not going back to that city.** The certified plain carrier is in a sack against the cradle and has not been rung in sixty days; the fixed stripped survey carrier is in its frame in its reinforced padded cradle on the barrow with four retaining bolts made fast and nothing in it that can strike, and **it has not been in a building since the thirty-third and no chapter of this batch may put it in one.** The register is thirteen. A warrant stands and there is no form on which a man asks for one to be lifted. A physical route map is in a coat, folded in three, with a rectangle about two hands together gone out of the middle of a run in the same place, and he cannot read it. Eleven lines in his own hand are in the inside pocket with a letter from Kells he has read four times. **No weekday and no month may be named. Every day-time hour is bare; the ninth hour of the evening is the only half-day and it is the opening hour of the day-book.**

## Starting relationships

- **Tomas and Tamsin:** eight uses of the slate, four standing non-uses, **and no ninth use in this batch.** She is ten feet off his left shoulder and neither of them has said what the road is for.
- **Tomas and the arrangement of three:** one answer and not two, no paper, and he is not the clerk of it.
- **Tomas and Mara Vale:** a choice she made over him, a record she kept, nothing given, nothing resolved, and **no letter may be written to her in this batch.**
- **Tomas and the people who are right:** every one of them correct, not a door, not thanked.

## Batch midpoint

**Chapter 0155, Day 141.** He has been walking a line for four days and has proved that a line is a line — **he has been on the ground the whole time and there is a schedule in his coat that puts a rectangle in the middle of it, and the rectangle is not a gap in the road, it is a gap in a line he cannot read.** The mid-batch turn is negative and it is the point: **the thing he is carrying cannot tell him where to walk, and he has to stop treating it as an instrument and start treating it as a thing four people have to look at.** The batch's first half ends with him admitting that in his own book.

## Batch climax

**Chapter 0159, Day 145.** A river canton's bench, in a room over a landing stage, with about nine people in it, and a question he has asked in the ordinary way: **is the road to the coach inn open.** It is not a question about a bell and nobody in the room treats it as one. **The bench answers him correctly, in about four seconds, and the answer is that the road is open and has been for years and is not on any sheet anybody in this room has, and the man who says it is right and is not sorry and is not a door and will not be thanked — and then four people in the room say four different things about the same road, and about four in ten of what they say is right, and none of them agrees with any of the others.** He gets no decision, no coalition and no bell. **He gets four sentences he can walk, and he writes them down in his own book, and the day-book's third column says the finding and refuses to improve it.**

## Required character changes

- **Tomas learns that being right about a column is not the same skill as getting a road walked, and that the second one costs days.** He is not thanked for either.
- **He stops asking the map a question.** He lays it flat on four tables in four rooms and asks four people what they can see, and the answer is that four people can see more of it than he can, and that is the first time in three volumes that another person has been the instrument.
- **Tamsin is further reduced in hearing in this batch, in her own chapter, and it is hers to price.** She says what it costs her in her own words on her own day. **Nobody asks her for a reason, nobody holds it over her, nobody treats her as a casualty of his decision, and nobody treats her as a person who has been exposed.**
- **The travelling group is met, not recruited.** About four in five people he asks say no, in the ordinary voice, and he does not argue with one of them and does not write down two of the noes.

## Required power changes

**None, and the batch may not manufacture one.** No stage, no sense, no perception attempted or had, nothing bound, nothing counted into iron, **no learning where a sound went, and no way of learning it.** He cannot fill in a form, cannot enter an answer for a household, cannot certify a bell, and cannot speak for any ward. **The one thing he acquires is a fact about feet: he can walk a line and get other people to walk it with him, and that is a habit and not a power and may not be written as one.**

## Required world reveals

- **A notice-reader keeps a book, has no office, is paid about nine shillings a quarter, and is the person through whom a schedule becomes a sound — and he can be right, and he is the first man in the batch who tells Tomas something he did not ask for.**
- **A district's trench, cut and left open, under a notice that says *wires* and not *wires belonging to anybody*, and a cut from above with a burr underneath it.** A service cut and a bypass are a road and not a claim, and a road does not know who is standing on it.
- **A road that is on a schedule and not on the ground, and a second man who finds it because he was born at the end of it.**
- **A sheet with a panel gone from it — the district above the coach inn — and four people in four counties who have each said, in their own words and unasked, that a map shows where a line runs and cannot show whose hand altered it.**
- **A form with four boxes in which the fourth is the ground, about nine hundred a year, and one of them filled with three words that are not a lie.**

## Open questions after the batch

### Chapter 0151 — A Road With Nine Miles Of Copper Under It And Nothing To Read

- **Chapter:** 0151
- **POV:** Tomas Vale, with Tamsin Rook on the edge of it.
- **Location/time:** A road above the water about four miles west of Sennet, the flat, a barrow on a bank, a step by the river, a lane. Day 137, from the fourth hour into the evening.
- **Immediate goal:** get the barrow nine miles along a road without putting the frame in a building, and find out whether the schedule in his coat agrees with the ground under his boots.
- **Resistance:** **he cannot read the schedule.** A rectangle about two hands together is gone out of the middle of a run, not torn and not cut, the paper smooth and uncreased, the rules stopping short, the ink either side a slightly different black, the numbers either side going on without a break, **and it is in the same place and he does not know whether that is a thing done to every copy or a thing done to the plate.** And: **the frame is on a barrow and a barrow is a building-sized thing for a bell and it has not been in a building since the thirty-third**, and the second clause is the reason he cannot pay for anything with it, and the post he built on the flat in Sennet with an empty bracket on it is still in the ground behind him and is not his to leave and he left it anyway. And: **he has a warrant standing behind him and no form on which a man asks for one to be lifted, and a person named in a paper goes with the paper.**
- **Information revealed:** **a claim in metal cannot be edited and a sheet can be, and that is the whole difference between the two instruments he owns and it is four inches long.** A road under his boots is nine miles long and a schedule is a drawing of nine miles, and he can walk the first and not read the second.
- **Action or decision:** **he walks the first four miles and does not pretend to the second, and he writes down in his own book that he has been treating a drawing as an instrument and it is not one.**
- **Emotional change:** the relief of a man who has been carrying a thing he thought was a key and finds out it is a picture. **And underneath it the thing he does not say: the four lines in his mother's hand are in the same coat and he has not taken them out.**
- **Power or resource change:** none. No stage, no sense, no perception, nothing rung, nothing bound. **The carrier in the sack is not rung, and the figure is counted in days from the seventy-seventh and is sixty.**
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** the frame has not been in a building since the thirty-third; all five clauses stand; the register is thirteen; his own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and is not going to it and does not travel; **the two counters on the carrier are separate** and the hand counter is at four and is finished; nobody from Kells is on the road; Mara Vale is not written to.
- **Ending type:** decision — he decides to walk the road instead of reading the map, and the road is nine miles long and he has four days of it in front of him.
- **Next-chapter pull:** **a ford, a smithy, and a woman selling needles two days' walk from a capital he has left, who asks him a question he cannot answer with a map.**

### Chapter 0152 — A Woman At A Pitch Who Asks Him What The Road Is For

### Chapter 0155 — The Thing He Is Carrying Is A Picture And He Cannot Read It

He can see wards and numbers and a scale and he has never been able to see a line and there is no trade in it and reading metal is the whole of what he can do.** And: **the honest arithmetic — four miles walked in four days is a mile a day with a barrow, and a man with a barrow stops people and a man on foot does not, and he has been on foot for four days and has learned nothing about the rectangle at all.**

### Chapter 0159 — Is The Road To The Coach Inn Open, And The Answer Is Yes And Has Been For Years

- **Chapter:** 0159 — **the batch climax.**
- **POV:** Tomas Vale, with Tamsin Rook on the edge of it.
- **Location/time:** A room over a landing stage, about nine people, a bench, a table, a landing stage, a lane, a market of about two hundred, a step by the water. Day 145, from the fourth hour into the evening.
- **Immediate goal:** ask one question in the ordinary way in a room of nine people, and get a true answer to it.
- **Resistance:** **he gets the answer and the answer does not help him.** The road to the coach inn is open and has been for years and is not on any sheet anybody in that room has, and the man who says it is right and is not sorry and is not a door and will not be thanked. And: **the panel for the district above the coach inn is gone from every copy in the same rectangle, and a road that is open is not a road that is registered, and those are two different things and he cannot tell which one he is looking at.** And: **four people in the room say four different things about the same road and about four in ten of what they say is right and none of them agrees with any of the others, and he cannot make them agree and there is no form on which they could.**
- **Information revealed:** **a road that is walked is a fact about the people who walked it and not about the paper, and a road that is on a sheet and not on the ground is a fact about the paper.** And, in the fourth voice: **the district cut the wire in the spring and the notice has said *wires* since the hundred and sixth, and nobody has ever asked what it was for, and the answer is that nobody in the room wants the line back.**
