# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline

**Batch number:** 0001
**Chapter range:** 1–10
**Batch goal:** Establish Tomas's ordinary bellmaking life, the stripped Crown carrier's impossible warning, and the first practical consent rules he can test without pretending to understand them. Tomas meets Tamsin and Nell, reaches the edge of Stage 1, and secures provisional watch custody of the evidence. He does not solve the copied-answer mechanism, identify the Silent Muster, close the Vale Bellhouse, or leave Kells.

## Starting condition

Tomas is trying to finish a Crown survey-bell repair without drawing attention from the reeve or the Bellwrights' Measure. He believes a skilled maker can keep a dangerous sound useful by handling it himself. Lira is missing, Mara has sent no direct message, Oren distrusts him, and Tamsin is an untrusted courier passing through town.

## Starting relationships

Tomas and Oren have a history of blame; Tomas and Sabin have a history of unfinished training. Tamsin does not yet trust Tomas or Crown bells. Nell Fenn knows the Low Ward families but does not know Tomas's political importance. The first batch must make each relationship offer a different kind of resistance.

## Bell-object identity

These are three different physical objects and must remain distinct in prose and state:

1. **The stripped Crown survey carrier:** the waist-high, fixed Crown carrier delivered to the Vale Bellhouse in Chapter 1. It has no clapper, a child-sized wax handprint on its lip, and buried bellwire damage that explains how it sounded. It is not hand-sized and not portable.
2. **Tamsin's stolen courier handbell:** Rook's small handbell, missing from the north-gate relay lockbox. It carries a Low Ward signal through wear on its handle and crown, but it does not bear the same physical wax handprint. Tamsin recovers and retains it.
3. **Tomas's trial handbell:** a new small bell cast in Chapter 7 from ordinary workshop stock. It is the portable private carrier used for Tomas's first binding. It is neither the stripped carrier nor Tamsin's handbell.

The fixed watch bell at the north gate and any later Crown carrier are locations or other bells; neither is a synonym for these three objects.

## Batch midpoint

Around Chapters 5–6, Tomas proves that the stripped carrier did not prophesy or direct itself: a Crown inspection order was filed before it rang, and a physical bellwire carried the pulse into the locked workshop. He begins to understand that a warning can precede a danger because someone has already accepted responsibility for the action or for the duty to warn. He still cannot identify the source, prove that any later response is free, or explain how a Crown answer appears to represent Kells.

## Batch climax

In Chapter 10, Halden arrives at the Vale Bellhouse with a temporary Crown holding writ naming the stripped carrier, the trial handbell, and every loose relay bell in the workshop. Tomas refuses to ring the private trial handbell as a town-wide command or let its single answer stand for Low Ward families. Oren formally takes only the fixed stripped carrier into neutral watch custody, while the trial handbell and ordinary working bells remain at the Bellhouse under a temporary no-public-ringing order. Tomas gains protected evidence and time, not control of the town. The Vale Bellhouse stays open, Tamsin keeps her stolen handbell, and the copied-answer mechanism remains unexposed. The broader seizure Halden threatens remains unexecuted for Chapters 11–20.

## Batch aftermath

Chapter 10 must complete the custody decision, then show a quieter consequence before its final hook: Tomas cleans the casting-room residue and packs away the split tuning file while accepting the loss of paid work caused by Halden's order; Tamsin agrees to help only for claims she has chosen to carry; Nell decides what she will do without treating Tomas's warning as parental permission. The next immediate obligation is a three-answer public test, not a rush to the dawn operation.

## Batch beat map

- **Beginning — Chapters 1–3:** Tomas's paid repair and ordinary competence collide with a stripped Crown carrier that rings a warning. The Low Ward becomes a human problem, and Tamsin's separate stolen handbell introduces a rival way of carrying a signal.
- **Escalation — Chapters 4–7:** Sabin's consent rules, Nell's missing family, and the pre-filed inspection turn the sound into a moral and civic problem. Tomas's first deliberate binding costs material and sensory resources, and his private answer is immediately vulnerable to being mistaken for a public command.
- **Midpoint — Chapters 5–6:** The investigation turns from supernatural omen to planned human action: a dated Crown order precedes the carrier's sound, while a physical route offers an ordinary explanation for how the warning reached the workshop. The operation's full purpose remains hidden.
- **Second escalation — Chapters 7–9:** The new trial handbell's two-note answer is repeated through the north-gate route, forcing Oren to separate evidence from blame and forcing Tomas to distinguish a physical relay from a free local answer. The missing surveyor and the threatened holding writ make delay costly.
- **Climax — Chapter 10:** Halden attempts to convert the warning into a seizure. Tomas refuses to use one private answer as a town-wide command, while Oren, Tamsin, and Nell each act only within the responsibility they personally accept.
- **Aftermath — Chapter 10:** The fixed carrier enters neutral watch custody, the trial handbell and working bells remain under a no-public-ringing order, and Tomas absorbs the lost contract, damaged tools, ringing ears, and public scrutiny. The completed decision is to seek three independent local answers, not to declare Kells safe.

## Required character changes

- Tomas moves from treating a warning as a craft problem to recognizing that usefulness does not grant him the right to answer for others.
- Tamsin agrees to supervised testing without giving Tomas authority over her route or handbell.
- Oren begins treating Tomas as a person who can publish evidence and accept custody, not only as a suspect.
- Nell learns that Tomas will not promise a lost father merely to make her feel comforted.
- Sabin reveals that consent rules are an old craft discipline, not a sentimental belief.

## Required power changes

Tomas starts at Stage 0 (Hand), a competent ordinary bellmaker with no supernatural reading. He ends at the first edge of Stage 1 (Near Toll) after binding one short, private warning in Chapter 7. Only after that binding can he hear whether a nearby answer is strained; he cannot identify the person behind it, recover context from a route, predict another tone, or read a Crown clapper.

The generic Stage 1 cost is time, a risk of a failed small casting or damaged tool, and one episode of ringing ears. Batch 0001 makes Tomas pay both material costs explicitly: one failed small casting and one damaged tuning tool. There is no permanent memory loss in this batch. A permanent or lasting sensory-memory cost belongs to later Stage 2 practice unless a later reviewed outline explicitly assigns it.

## Required world reveals

- A useful bound bell requires a physically identifiable witness matter, a living keeper's specific acceptance, and an answer from someone who understands the claim.
- A local public signal normally requires three independent answers. A private handbell may be valid with one answer, but it cannot be represented as a town-wide answer.
- A future warning can be valid because a keeper accepts the duty to warn before the danger; the bell does not verify the underlying claim or compel a response.
- The stripped carrier can sound without a clapper only because a damaged physical route drives it. It is not self-directing or a living oracle.
- A filed dawn inspection makes the warning plausible and ties it to the Low Ward, but the batch does not yet reveal the Silent Muster's full purpose, name-transfer mechanism, or copied-answer source.

## Required plot boundaries

The following remain reserved for later Volume 01 beats:

- Halden's complete seizure of bells and Oren's public confrontation over Tomas's Hush rope cut in Chapters 11–20.
- The copied answer, the higher official behind it, and the mundane reversal that replaces prophecy in Chapters 21–30.
- The broader public warning, panic, costly false alarm, and damaged Tamsin route in Chapters 31–40.
- The dawn operation, archive fire, arrests, Tamsin's decision to travel, and Tomas's departure in Chapters 41–50. Before Tomas leaves, the later custody review must explicitly release the same fixed stripped carrier to him; no second stripped bell replaces it.

## Chapter cards

### Chapter 0001 — The Bell Without a Clapper

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** Vale Bellhouse, Kells; first light before the Crown surveyor's arrival.
- **Immediate goal:** Finish a routine repair to the fixed Crown survey carrier and keep the workshop open for the day's contract.
- **Resistance:** The carrier's lip is warped, its clapper is missing, and Tomas's fear of the Hush makes him distrust the evidence of his own hands.
- **Information revealed:** A stripped fixed carrier rings once without being struck. A child-sized handprint is pressed into wax on its lip, and a note from Mara says not to let anyone call it an answer. Ordinary inspection of the buried route shows a cut or loose coupling beneath the workshop.
- **Action or decision:** Tomas locks the fixed carrier in the casting room and chooses to preserve the route damage and repair marks before testing it again.
- **Emotional change:** His ordinary sense of control gives way to the knowledge that someone has used Crown infrastructure to reach his family craft.
- **Power or resource change:** No new power. Tomas gains temporary possession of the damaged Crown survey carrier, Mara's note, and a physical route fragment.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** This object is the fixed, waist-high Crown survey carrier, not Tamsin's later stolen handbell. It is not a prophet; its missing clapper permits sound only when a connected physical route drives it.
- **Ending type:** Discovery.
- **Next-chapter pull:** Tomas must find the handprint's owner and determine whether the route damage explains the carrier's first warning.

### Chapter 0002 — The West Road Warning

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** Low Ward river landing and west road, late morning.
- **Immediate goal:** Trace the damaged route and learn what the people near it heard without turning his own conclusion into their answer.
- **Resistance:** The Low Ward has already learned not to trust official notices; Halden Crake orders Tomas to keep the fixed carrier away from the public; route dust and conflicting accounts make the tone difficult to interpret.
- **Information revealed:** The carrier's sound warns that children connected to the west road may be taken during a dawn inspection, but Tomas learns this through ordinary testimony, paper evidence, and bellcraft inspection.
- **Action or decision:** Tomas asks three witnesses separately what they heard, does not prompt them with a conclusion, and records both agreement and disagreement. He speaks the warning himself rather than using resonance.
- **Emotional change:** He realizes that being right is not enough; the people affected must retain the right to say they do not understand or refuse.
- **Power or resource change:** No supernatural gain or recognition. Tomas gains a written list of local witnesses and ordinary physical evidence about the route.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Tomas does not hear a strained answer in this chapter. His method is observation, witness separation, and craft diagnosis, all available at Stage 0.
- **Ending type:** Decision.
- **Next-chapter pull:** A courier arrives asking about a small handbell stolen from the north-gate relay lockbox. It is a separate object from the fixed carrier.

### Chapter 0003 — The Stolen Handbell

- **POV:** Tamsin Rook.
- **Location/time:** Kells market lane and old north gate, midday.
- **Immediate goal:** Recover her stolen courier handbell before the watch searches every house and blames her for the broken route.
- **Resistance:** The watch wants an immediate search, Tomas wants to use the fixed carrier to force the thief out, and Tamsin refuses both because neither method gives local people a choice.
- **Information revealed:** Tamsin is a former Crown road-ringer with a permanent hearing injury from forced resonance. Her portable handbell carries an authorized Low Ward signal but has been physically altered to repeat it farther than its route permits.
- **Action or decision:** Tamsin follows handle wear, wax transfer, and market-children's observations. She asks the children for help rather than ordering adults to cooperate, then agrees to a supervised comparison at the Bellhouse while retaining the handbell.
- **Emotional change:** She reveals that her skepticism comes from having obeyed a route that lied to her, not from indifference to danger.
- **Power or resource change:** Tamsin keeps her stolen handbell. Tomas gains access to a courier route map and a physical comparison of altered wear; neither man gains resonance ability.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The stolen courier handbell is hand-sized, portable, and distinct from the fixed survey carrier. It does not bear the same physical wax handprint.
- **Ending type:** Relationship turn.
- **Next-chapter pull:** Tamsin offers to help test what the fixed carrier carried but makes Tomas promise not to decide what her answer means.

### Chapter 0004 — What a Bell Can Carry

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** Sabin Fell's repair shop, late afternoon.
- **Immediate goal:** Learn enough about the old craft rules to explain the warning without pretending to understand it.
- **Resistance:** Sabin refuses to answer questions about the Hearthline and challenges Tomas to state what he knows, what he assumes, and what he cannot hear.
- **Information revealed:** A bell carries one bounded accepted responsibility, not objective truth. A valid binding names physically identifiable witness matter, a living keeper's specific duty, and an answer from someone who understands the claim. A public local signal normally requires three independent answers; a private handbell may have one.
- **Action or decision:** Tomas admits that treating the warning as a command makes his fear simpler. He asks Sabin to teach him the refusal test and states that the future first test will be private, not a town signal.
- **Emotional change:** The mentor relationship changes from guarded instruction to a conditional partnership.
- **Power or resource change:** Tomas learns the formal mechanics of keeper, witness matter, witness, and answer. He gains no supernatural ability and cannot yet perform a safe binding.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Sabin knows the old consent rules and is withholding Hearthline history out of fear of the Crown, not because he wholly distrusts Tomas.
- **Ending type:** Training choice.
- **Next-chapter pull:** Nell Fenn arrives with a child's handprint copied from the Low Ward register and asks Tomas to make a bell that will bring her father home.

### Chapter 0005 — The Child Who Was Not Named

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** Low Ward, Nell's rented room, evening.
- **Immediate goal:** Learn whether Nell's missing father is connected to the warning and keep her from treating a bell as a request for a miracle.
- **Resistance:** Nell is grieving and has already been threatened over her father's name; Tomas's instinct is to make a promise that will calm her.

- **Ending type:** Emotional cost.

### Chapter 0006 — The Order Before the Bell

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** Kells market court and survey store, the next morning.
- **Immediate goal:** Verify the filing date and route number without stealing a secret record that cannot later be defended.
- **Resistance:** Halden invokes emergency custody, the store clerk is frightened, and Tomas's request would itself signal that the warning matters.

It does not yet reveal the operation's name, the use of names as relay anchors, or how an official answer was made to sound communal.
- **Action or decision:** Tomas asks the clerk to write the date, seal, and route number on the public notice board rather than handing him a secret copy, accepting that the act will expose him.
- **Emotional change:** Tomas moves from investigating privately to accepting that evidence must be public if it is to remain useful and contestable.
- **Power or resource change:** He secures a date, route number, and a witness-matter candidate, but the Crown notices his presence. He still has no supernatural read.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The order predates the carrier's sound and supports a future-warning mechanism, but Tomas cannot yet identify who accepted responsibility, who rang it, or whether a local answer was copied.
- **Ending type:** Discovery.
- **Next-chapter pull:** Oren tells Tomas that the inspection arrived through a watch route his men were ordered not to question and that the relevant local surveyor is already missing from the roll.

### Chapter 0007 — A Warning Without an Order

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** Vale Bellhouse casting room and the closed inner yard, afternoon.
- **Immediate goal:** Test whether Tomas can make one bounded private warning without pretending it speaks for Low Ward families.
- **Resistance:** Halden forbids public ringing, Tomas is unpracticed at binding, the first small test blank cracks during tuning and must be remelted, his correction splits an old tuning file, and Nell is the only person available to understand the proposed claim.
- **Information revealed:** A future warning is valid when a living keeper accepts a specific duty before the danger. Tomas can make such a claim without promising that the danger will be handled or telling a listener what to do.
- **Action or decision:** After remelting the cracked blank, Tomas creates the new **trial handbell** from ordinary workshop stock and binds it with a clerk-authorized sliver of the posted Crown notice as witness matter, bearing the inspection date, route number, and seal. Tomas is the keeper and accepts only this duty: “I will warn that a dawn inspection may take unregistered children, name the evidence and its limits, and leave each family's decision to them.” Nell, understanding the claim, gives the one independent answer required for a private handbell by accepting a limited duty to carry those words to one neighboring household. She is not installed as keeper and the signal is not presented as public. To test the private carrier, Tomas then strikes two clear notes: the first carries his bounded claim; the second acknowledges Nell's answer.
- **Emotional change:** Tomas feels the first clean satisfaction of making a useful sound, immediately followed by fear that usefulness has become another authority.
- **Power or resource change:** Tomas reaches the first edge of Stage 1 (Near Toll). After the binding he can hear that Nell's answer is strained, but cannot identify the emotion, the person behind it, or any hidden context. He pays with one ruined small casting, a damaged tuning tool, and one episode of ringing ears. He has no deep read and loses no permanent memory.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The trial handbell is a third, newly cast object. The consent chain is explicit: posted-notice sliver as witness matter, Tomas as living keeper, Nell as the one informed private answer. It cannot stand for the Low Ward.
- **Ending type:** Earned hook.
- **Next-chapter pull:** Later that evening, before anyone rings the trial handbell publicly, the fixed north-gate watch bell repeats the same two-note pattern despite having no visible live connection to the Bellhouse.

- **Emotional change:** Oren moves from treating Tomas as a reckless survivor to treating him as a man who must answer publicly and accept formal evidence conditions.
- **Power or resource change:** Oren supplies a watch escort and a sealed copy of the route docket. Tomas gains evidence protection, not a new resonance ability.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Oren's grief remains active. The Bellhouse remains open, and Tomas does not yet learn how the north-gate bell repeated the tone.
- **Ending type:** Reversal.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The route docket names a local surveyor who has already disappeared from the official roll, while the north-gate bell has rung the pattern again.

- **Next-chapter pull:** Crown officers enter Kells with a temporary holding writ naming the fixed carrier, the trial handbell, and the Bellhouse's loose relay bells. Halden demands their surrender before morning, but the broader workshop seizure cannot be executed until the later public hearing.

### Chapter 0010 — The Answer Is Not Consent

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** Vale Bellhouse casting yard, the night before dawn; aftermath at the same workshop.
- **Immediate goal:** Keep the stripped carrier and trial handbell out of unquestioned Crown custody without ringing either as a command or surrendering the evidence entirely.
- **Resistance:** Halden has a temporary holding writ and threatens a broader seizure if the hearing goes against him; Crown officers are at the north gate, paid customers fear the workshop, Nell's family does not agree on what to do, and Tomas's single private answer cannot support a public signal.
- **Information revealed:** The posted inspection and witness accounts make a real danger plausible, but the records do not identify who accepted responsibility on the town's behalf or prove that the repeated tone represents local consent. Tomas identifies the trial handbell honestly as a private, one-answer warning.
- **Action or decision:** Tomas refuses to ring the trial handbell in the market or order families to move. Oren uses the public notice, separated witness statements, and the dated watch seal to challenge the holding writ's scope; he detaches the sealed fixed stripped carrier from the Crown list and formally removes only that carrier to neutral watch custody for the hearing. Halden accepts the substitution for tonight, while the trial handbell and ordinary working bells remain at the Bellhouse under a no-public-ringing order; the broader seizure is stayed pending the public hearing, not defeated. Nell, Tamsin, and Oren each state only what they personally will do, but those statements remain ordinary choices outside the trial handbell's one-answer chain and are not converted into a common answer.
- **Emotional change:** Tomas loses the fantasy that being right allows him to decide. He also discovers that other people can act carefully without obeying him. Tamsin agrees to help test claims she chooses to carry, not because the trial handbell claims authority over her.
- **Power or resource change:** Tomas completes one private Stage 1 warning and confirms only its narrow strain-sensing limit. The immediate costs are a ruined casting, damaged tooling, lingering ringing ears, a suspended Crown repair contract, watch scrutiny, and a public requirement for three independent answers before any local public signal. No copied answer is identified, no full public warning is completed, and no forced-echo reading is gained.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The Vale Bellhouse is open but closely watched. Tamsin retains her courier handbell. The stripped carrier is in watch custody, not Crown possession. Tomas remains in Kells. The Silent Muster's full operation and the copied-answer mechanism stay unresolved.
- **Aftermath beat:** After Halden leaves, Tomas cleans the casting-room residue, packs away the split tuning file, and accepts that some repair work has been withdrawn. Nell chooses to bring the household record rather than ask him for a promise about her father. Tamsin returns the borrowed tools and agrees to help only with claims she has chosen. The relationship and practical costs complete before the hook.
- **Ending type:** Batch climax and aftermath.
- **Next-chapter pull:** Oren gives Tomas three blank witness slips and orders a public refusal test before the dawn inspection. Tomas must learn whether three local answers can preserve disagreement without becoming a command.
