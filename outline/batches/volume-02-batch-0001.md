# Volume 02 Batch 0001 Outline

**Batch number:** 0001
**Chapter range:** 51–60
**Batch goal:** Put a man who has lost the right to speak for a ward onto a road with a woman who cannot hear a bell, and show that the thing following them is **not a conspiracy in a room but a copyist with a column and a stamp.** The batch establishes the volume's working distinction — **a name on a roll and a person in a house are two different things** — puts a real district office at a real address with a named man in it, ends with the one piece of evidence that says the two can be compared, and sends them downstream. It does not reach Miremarket, does not find the Answer Rolls, and does not put Pell Rill anywhere near the page.

## Starting condition

**Day 34, the morning after they left Kells.** Tomas Vale is on the river road north along the towpath with a barrow, the **fixed stripped Crown survey carrier in its mounting frame**, the retaining bolts made fast for transport, the frame in its reinforced padded cradle, and a receipt in four clauses and a fifth. He cannot ring it, cannot certify it, cannot say what it means, and cannot have any answer to a signal on it entered as the answer of a household. He has no metal, no forge, no shop, no licence in a workshop, and **no bell he may ring**: his own plain carrier is barred by the Bellwrights' Measure and is sitting on a bench in a sealed yard in Kells.

Tamsin Rook is with him on a slate **Mercy Slee wrote, which neither of them wrote first**. Her four terms of the thirteenth day stand, the leash is on both of them, and she travels on her own account. Her hearing loss is permanent, public in Kells by her own act, and **nobody in this batch asks her about it.** The answer she gave him on the towpath on the thirty-third was a refusal with no reason, and **he does not re-open it and does not treat it as a wound.**

**Tamsin's reason for the road is a child.** Jory Fenn is eight, was taken in the round before the L-4 notice, and has not come back. The only records of him are a schedule line, a household fragment with a fourth line cut away, and a sister's count. She says it in her own mouth in Chapter 51 and it is not about Tomas.

**Marta Vell, at Ocklebridge, has been entered on a schedule in a building in Fourmile as a bell of this ward with no keeper against it, and nobody has told her, and on the thirty-third Tomas did not.** That is the batch's first debt and it is paid in Chapter 52, badly, because a man with a bell in a cradle is not a person a woman at a water bell is going to take a page from.

## Starting relationships

- **Tomas and Tamsin:** travelling together on a leash neither of them wrote, in a working agreement that has never yet been tested in front of another person. Every chapter must give them something to disagree about that is not about him.
- **Tomas and the towpath:** a stranger with a Crown instrument in a cradle on a road that has never once filed a return, and a road that prices a barrow.
- **Tamsin and the road:** eleven years of it. She is competent here in a way he is not, and this batch is the first in which that is the engine rather than the accompaniment.
- **Tomas and Kells:** a correspondence, not a presence. Nell Fenn is sixteen and in the town and is the person who acts there without him.
- **Tamsin and a deafness nobody is to mention:** a working condition, not a subject.

## Bell-object identity

**Seven named bell objects and one archive** are in play, and they must stay distinct in prose and in state. Only the first and the second are on the road:

1. **The fixed stripped Crown survey carrier** — the waist-high Crown object in its frame and reinforced padded cradle, no clapper, child-sized wax handprint in the crown, warped lip, a thin copper jumper across a coupling cut nearly through. **It is never rung in this batch and cannot be:** the receipt forbids any person from furnishing a clapper, and breaking that receipt is a legal act and not a dramatic one. Its bolts are made fast at every place the frame is set down and the frame never leaves the cradle for the carriage.
2. **Tamsin's stolen courier handbell** — hand-sized, crown re-cut inside by a chisel to about a sixteenth of an inch with a **riffle file** mark at four points, the wax laid over an older signal still in it. Hers. Her route and her bell are hers. **She rings it in this batch at most once, and only on her own terms, and it is not for him.**
3. **Tomas's own plain carrier** — barred, in a sealed yard in Kells, **not in this volume and not in this batch.** It must not appear, be described as travelling, or be rung.
4. **Tomas's trial handbell** — Crown property, not in the Bellhouse, never returned, and **Volume 01 does not state where it is after the twenty-eighth.** It is not on the road.
5. **The millrace bridge bell** — in the sealed yard of the Vale Bellhouse. Lawfully moved to the north gate on the evening of the twenty-seventh on a six-line watch grant with Jerm as named keeper; sounded **twice** at the sixth hour of the twenty-eighth; brought back to the yard on the afternoon of the twenty-eighth and sounded **once** on the way by Jerm, unasked, with no claim and no duty, and that is **the last bell rung in Kells in this month**, of which there is no record anywhere. **It is not on the road and is not in this batch.**
6. **The market court call bell** and **the wheelwright's counter bell** — both in Kells, both untouched, and **neither is mentioned in Chapters 51–60** unless a chapter stages the event.
7. **The Vale Bellhouse archive** — taken and destroyed. The only record of Reckoning 619 is gone. **No surviving copy may be invented in any later batch.**

**One thing that is not a bell enters this batch and must stay distinct from all seven:** the metal on the stock book at the smithy past the county line, which is **a record of what a smith sold and to whom.** It is not a bell and nobody casts one in this batch.

## Provisional batch midpoint

**Around Chapters 55–56.** Tomas is shown a **stock book** — a record of what a smith sold and to whom — and puts it beside a Crown renewal roll and finds that the office's roll is built out of the ordinary paper a trade already keeps. The enemy stops being a room with a plan in it and becomes **a column, a stamp and a habit**. The line that fixes the batch's shape is the smith's, and it is Sabin Fell's rule arriving from a stranger: *a bell is traceable when the metal of it can be shown to have come from a source that keeps a record of what it sold and to whom.* Tomas gains a method — compare a trade's paper with an office's paper — and no power, no bell, and no standing.

## Batch climax

**Chapter 60.** Tomas has a signature, a stock book, a map with the same sections cut out of every copy, eight refusals in eight hands, a night in which his own receipt's third clause is used against him, and one unanswered question sitting in writing in an officer's own name. He has no authority, no metal, no money, no bell he may ring, and two people who may stop at any hour. **He does not decide.** He writes the decision down in four columns, asks two people who do not agree, and the road is chosen **because of what one of them refuses to do.** The batch closes with the phrase **Answer Rolls** said in passing by a ferry-man on the last page and explained to nobody.

## Required character changes

- Tomas goes from a man who is asked questions to a man who **asks them in a form an office must answer in its own name**, and is refused one of four, and takes the refusal.
- Tomas pays for the un-told woman at Ocklebridge and is refused the first time he tries, and does not improve the refusal in the morning.
- Tamsin's professional competence, not Tomas's, is what finds the answer in Chapter 54 — and the chapter says so.
- Tamsin states her own reason for the road in her own mouth, and nobody in the batch treats her deafness as a subject or as an explanation.
- Both of them discover that the slate's leash is a working instrument and not a formality: the answer may be no, and the person who asked may not ask again that day.

## Required power changes

**None. This is a hard boundary.** Tomas is at the first edge of **Stage 1 (Near Toll)** at the end of this batch exactly as at the end of Volume 01. He does not bind anything, does not learn a second perception, does not identify a copied answer, does not learn where a sound went, and is not described as reaching for any of them. **He rings no bell in this batch.** The plain carrier is barred and in a sealed yard; the Crown carrier has no clapper and may not be furnished with one; the smith casts nothing.

**What he gains is civic and relational:** a method, a letter in an officer's own name, a page in a woman's own hand, eight refusals in eight hands, and the beginning of a practice of being refused. **What he pays:** a night without sleep, a debt at Ocklebridge, and the discovery that a clause he asked for can be used against him.

**Tamsin gains nothing and loses nothing.** Her hearing loss is permanent. She is not rescued out of it, no chapter has her discover she has been exposed, and **no person asks her for a reason at any hour in this batch.**

## Required world reveals

- **A carrier's book and a Crown's roll are different objects and carry different things.** A carrier's book says who carried a thing and how far. A Crown roll says what a household answered.
- **A signature can be carried forward indefinitely, and carrying it forward is lawful.** A man who answered once, on behalf of a household he belonged to, is still answering six years later, and the office is not falsifying anything.
- **The route map has the same sections cut out of every public copy, and the removals are in the same places in all of them**, which is a fact about a hand and not about a fire. Tamsin reads it as a professional who once traced a theft by wear in metal; Tomas does not see it.
- **Traceable stock exists on this road**, and a bell could be bought from a smith who keeps a record — and none is bought in this batch.
- **Fourmile renewal office is a real building at a real address with a named man in it.** District Officer Marten Cope answers three of four questions in writing, in his own name, and refuses the fourth with a reason that is worth more than the three answers.
- **Asking a household about its own line puts a file on the house, and being on a roll and not knowing it is a different and worse position.** Eight of nine households refuse the question. One of them has been signing.

- **Miremarket and the Answer Rolls.** Not reached, not found, not explained. The phrase may be heard once, in passing, unexplained, on the last page.
- **Pell Rill.** **He must not appear in this batch in any form** — not present, not quoted, not named on a document, not in a report, not in a margin. His name first arrives on a page in Batch 0002, in Chapter 63 or thereabouts, **before the man does**, and the man arrives at the end of that batch.

- **The Crown clapper, the higher official, OFF. 411, the copied-answer mechanism, how an office gets into a bell, and the Silent Muster's name-transfer mechanism** — all untouched. **The phrase *Silent Muster* is not spoken by anybody.**
- **Iven Sore's withdrawn service entry** — untouched and still genuine-or-borrowed.
- **Mara.** Her trail may appear once, as a thing somebody left where it could be found, and it must not confirm itself.
- **The confiscation.** No room is seized, no bench taken, no evidence destroyed. That is Chapters 91–100.

## Two house rules that bind this batch

1. **No weekday and no month is named in any chapter.** Days are numbered. A recurring day of the week is referred to as a market day, a round, a bell day, or by its number. **Volume 01 is clean; do not reintroduce what the volume-close audit removed.**
2. **The hour numerals run 1 to 12 twice a day, and no half-day may be attached to an hour that a chapter does not attach one to.** A state file that says *the fourth hour of the afternoon* when the chapter says *the fourth hour* is a false fact, and four such errors were found and fixed in the Volume 01 close. Write the half-day only when the chapter gives it.

## Chapter cards

### Chapter 0051 — The Book and the Roll

- **POV:** Tamsin Rook.
- **Location/time:** The towpath north out of Kells, from the low turn to the second mile, Day 34, morning into afternoon.
- **Immediate goal:** Walk far enough up the road to find out whether a name on a Crown roll can be found on a road at all.
- **Resistance:** The towpath is a working road and a woman with a bag is not a question. Every carrier she asks wants a name and a house, and she is not going to give either. **And she cannot ask about Jory Fenn without saying whose child he is**, and the first three carriers she asks hear the question correctly and answer it correctly, and the answer is that no carrier on this stretch ever carried a Crown child's name, because carriers carry parcels and people are not parcels and always were not.
- **Information revealed:** **A carrier's book carries who carried a thing and how far; a Crown roll carries what a household answered.** The two are not the same object and never were. Tamsin's own reason for the road, in her own mouth: Jory Fenn, eight, taken in the round before the L-4 notice, and the only records of him are a schedule line and a household fragment with a fourth line cut away — **and a name that is on a roll in a building four counties away is on a road somewhere, and the road is the only place anybody looks.**
- **Action or decision:** She does not ask about the child again. She asks about **paper** instead — what a carrier writes down, what a carrier is not allowed to write down, and what a carrier does when a clerk comes with a form and a box for a child's name. The third carrier tells her that a clerk once came down this road with a list and a copyist, and that the carriers were not asked anything, and that the list was filled in anyway.
- **Emotional change:** She expected the road to be simpler than the ward. It is not; it is the same document with more weather on it. And she is the one who can see it, and she is the one who will never be thanked for it, and she says so to nobody.
- **Power or resource change:** No power. Tamsin gains a working distinction that will carry the volume, and three carriers who will answer her again.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Her four terms of the thirteenth day stand. **The Day 24 request is not re-opened.** Her deafness is permanent; she reads a shoulder and a hand rather than a mouth, and **nobody asks her about it.** Her bell stays in her bag and is not rung.
- **Ending type:** Discovery.
- **Next-chapter pull:** At the second mile there is a village of about two hundred with a landing stage, a smithy, and a water bell that a woman called Marta Vell has rung every morning for thirty years — **and Tamsin said, on the towpath on the thirty-third, that telling her was not hers to do.**

### Chapter 0052 — What Marta Vell Is Not Told

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** Ocklebridge — the landing stage, the smithy and the water bell — Day 35, from about the seventh hour.
- **Immediate goal:** Put right the one thing he did not do on the morning of the thirty-third, which was to tell a woman that her bell has been entered on a Crown schedule as a bell of this ward with no keeper against it.
- **Resistance:** **He has read his own fifth clause as a bar on telling her a fact, and it is not; it is a bar on entering an answer on her behalf, and he knows the difference and cannot get his hands to do it.** Then the real resistance: a stranger from Kells with a Crown bell in a cradle and a schedule in his coat is precisely the sort of thing that goes on a roll, and she has been rung by strangers who wanted a bell for thirty years, and she asks him to leave before he has finished the first sentence.
- **Information revealed:** The water bell at Ocklebridge **is** on a schedule in a building in Fourmile, entered as a bell of this ward with no keeper against it, and nobody told her. She has rung it every morning for thirty years and has been paid nothing and asked for nothing and has no surname on any page. **A village can be entered in a schedule it never answered to.**
- **Action or decision:** He does not ask her to look at anything. He asks her to write down, in her own hand, with the hour, **what she does every morning and who has ever asked her to repeat it.** She does that, and she does not let him read it, and the first name written on the page is not his.
- **Emotional change:** He is refused the first thing he does on the road as a free man, and he does not improve the refusal in the morning, and he writes down in his own book that a receipt is not a permission.
- **Power or resource change:** No power. He gains a page in a woman's own hand that he has not read, and loses the idea that the fifth clause governs him.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** **Marta Vell is about sixty on Tamsin Rook's word and has no other age; she has no surname on the page; she has rung the water bell every morning for thirty years.** She asks for a week before she does anything with what he has told her, and the week is hers to ask for. **The Crown carrier is not rung and is not carried into the village square.**
- **Ending type:** Cost, with a hook.
- **Next-chapter pull:** A letter comes up the road from Kells in a hand that is not Nell Fenn's, and it is about a household of six that has been answering a Crown form since before the Fenn boy was taken.

### Chapter 0053 — Six Years of One Hand

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** A hamlet two miles past Ocklebridge, Day 36 into the evening.
- **Immediate goal:** Find out who has been answering a Crown form on behalf of a household of six for six years.
- **Resistance:** The household will not open the door to a man with a Crown instrument in a cradle, and the person who signs will not be named, and the form in their hands is a **supplementary return** — the Crown's own kind form, with the reason in it, no signature required, and a statement returned before the deadline will be certified. It is a good form. It is the best thing the Crown put in front of this ward, and it is the document that has been answering for them since before Jory Fenn was taken. And when the household does open the door, **one of the five is furious and does not thank him.**
- **Information revealed:** **The signature is not a forgery and not a deception.** A man who belonged to the household answered, lawfully, on a form that asked for the household's answer, and the office has carried his hand forward for six years, and the form says in its own small hand that a certified answer is a return. **Nobody has done anything wrong and the household has been answered for since before it moved.**
- **Action or decision:** Tomas does what he did in Kells and it does not work here: he asks questions separately, writes down what people say, and finds that the household will not be a witness to itself. He asks instead for the **form** — the paper, the date, the hand — and gets a copy of it from a person who is allowed to give it away, and **does not ask the household to stand in anything.**
- **Emotional change:** The household's answer to the only question they care about — *who is answering for us* — is that they do not know and have never known, and that they would like to stop. Tomas cannot stop it for them and says so, and is not forgiven.
- **Power or resource change:** No power. He gains a dated copy of a Crown form and a signature, and he does **not** gain the name of the person who signed, which he is not entitled to and does not ask for.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** This household is not a Kells household and the L-4 route does not reach it. **No age is supplied for anybody in this household.** The form is a supplementary return and is the same species of form as the one the ward published a question about in Kells.
- **Ending type:** Reversal.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The same hand is on three more doors in two counties, and one of the three is a smith's bill.

### Chapter 0054 — The Map With the Corners Cut

- **POV:** Tamsin Rook.
- **Location/time:** An inn and a coach office four miles on, Day 38.
- **Immediate goal:** Buy or borrow a route map and learn where the road's answers are supposed to go.
- **Resistance:** The map is the same in four places and **the same sections are missing from every copy**, and the man who sells them says the sections are not printed, which is a lie with a printer's name on it. And nobody has ever put a question about it: **a hole in a map is not a line, and a line is the thing this volume knows how to ask about.** Tomas looks at the map and sees a map with gaps in it.
- **Information revealed:** The removals are **in the same places in every copy**, which means a hand and not a fire, and means the removal was done to the thing and not to the copies. Tamsin sees it because in Chapter 3 she traced a theft by reading wear in metal, and the same professional reading applies to a sheet of paper: *somebody took a section out of the part of the map that says where an answer goes.*
- **Action or decision:** She does not accuse the seller. She buys the map, and she asks the one question that is hers to ask — **who orders a map printed, and what are they paid by the sheet or by the year** — and gets a better answer than Tomas would have got, because the seller will talk to a woman who is plainly asking about paper and will not talk to a man with a bell in a cradle.
- **Emotional change:** She is the one who sees it and nobody credits her, because she cannot follow a conversation across a room and reads a shoulder instead, and because the man who sees the gap is a man with a bell.
- **Power or resource change:** No power. Tamsin gains a map, a printer's name, and a smithy's name on the far side of the county line. Tomas gains nothing in this chapter.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** **This map is not the Bellwrights' Measure's road map**, which is in a district file in Fourmile read as a schedule of non-response, and whose fifty-three withdrawals are not on this map and must never be conflated with any count on it. The sections cut out of the public copies are a **different** fact from the Measure's fifty-three.
- **Ending type:** Discovery.
- **Next-chapter pull:** On the far side of the county line there is a smithy that keeps a record of what it sold and to whom, and metal in it that a bell can be made from.

### Chapter 0055 — A Bell Somebody Else Made

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** The smithy past the county line, Day 39, all day.
- **Immediate goal:** Find out whether there is metal on this road that anybody can show a record for.
- **Resistance:** The smith will not sell to a man with a barred bell in a cradle, and the bar is a real instrument: the Measure's finding is that the bell may not be rung in any public place **in this county**, and the smith is in the next one, and the smith wants to know what a man with a professional bar intends to do with a bell. Then the harder resistance: **the smith will not cast anything and will not certify anything, and says so before he is asked, and Tomas finds that a man who is competent and refuses in advance is not an obstacle, he is a colleague.**
- **Information revealed:** A **stock book** — every sale, with what it was, what it was made of, and who bought it — and the line that fixes the batch's shape, which is Sabin Fell's craft law arriving from a stranger who has never heard of the Bellwrights' Measure: *a bell is traceable when the metal of it can be shown to have come from a source that keeps a record of what it sold and to whom.* **Traceable stock exists. A certified bell can be bought on this road. None is bought in this batch.**
- **Action or decision:** Tomas does not order a bell. He asks to **read the book**, and is allowed to, and reads it standing up all afternoon while the smith works, and writes nothing down, and at the end asks the smith whether he may buy a *page* of the book — a list of the households that have bought metal from him in nine years — and is refused, and pays for the refusal with a promise to come back with a reason in writing.
- **Emotional change:** He is told, by a stranger who owes him nothing, the exact thing Sabin told him in a Measure room in Kells, and it lands harder for coming from a man who is not his master and is not disappointed in him.
- **Power or resource change:** **No power and no bell.** He gains a supplier, a price, a county line and a method. **He casts nothing and has no metal of his own, and the half-barrel in Kells has been empty since the nineteenth.**
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** **The smith is not a master of the Bellwrights' Measure and cannot certify anything.** No certificate issues from this shop in this batch. **Tomas's own plain carrier is barred and in a sealed yard in Kells and is not on this road.** The Measure's bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it binds this county and not the next one.
- **Ending type:** Decision.
- **Next-chapter pull:** On the last page of the book, in a column of households that bought metal, one name is the name on the household of six — and the shop is four counties from any Crown roll Tomas has seen.

### Chapter 0056 — The Name on the Stock Book

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** The smithy and then two days on the road, Day 40 into the evening.
- **Immediate goal:** Find out why a smith's customer list and a Crown renewal list have the same name on them.
- **Resistance:** The two documents are not the same kind of document. The smith's is a bill, and a bill is private paper, and the Crown's is a roll, and **there is no law anywhere that says a man may not be on both.** Tomas has no standing to ask about a Crown roll in a county where he has never been, and the only person he can ask is a woman with a handbell and a permanent hearing loss who is not going to be used as an errand.
- **Information revealed:** **The names are not the point. The point is that a household which bought a bell once is a household an office can find** — because a trade already keeps, for its own reasons, the thing an office needs: who lives where, who buys, and who signed. **The Crown's roll is built out of the ordinary paper a trade keeps, and the office is not stealing anything.** That is the batch's midpoint and it is worse and smaller than Tomas wanted.
- **Action or decision:** He does not build a case. He writes a method on a page and gives it to Tamsin to read over his shoulder without saying a word, which she does, and he asks her one question: *if a trade's paper and an office's paper are the same paper, what is the one question a person can put to a clerk that the clerk has to answer in his own name?* She gives him an answer that is better than the one he had.
- **Emotional change:** He came looking for a room with a plan in it and found a column and a stamp, and the horror of that is that it needs nobody to be wicked. He says out loud, in a doorway, that this is a worse thing than a villain, and Tamsin tells him not to be dramatic about it in a lane, and he is right and she is right.
- **Power or resource change:** No power. He gains a method that will work four counties away and a page in his own book that he will not be able to read at without feeling sick.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** **Nothing in this chapter touches OFF. 411, the copied-answer mechanism, or how an office gets into a bell, and the mark is not on the page.** The Crown carrier is not rung. **The word *Silent Muster* is not spoken by anybody.**
- **Ending type:** Discovery.
- **Next-chapter pull:** Fourmile is a real building with an address and a man in it, and the man has a name, and Tomas has a question that only an office can answer.

### Chapter 0057 — Somebody Took the Cradle

- **POV:** Tamsin Rook.
- **Location/time:** An inn yard four counties on, the second hour into morning, Day 40 into Day 41.
- **Immediate goal:** Keep the frame, the cradle, the papers and the barrow through a night with three other vehicles in the yard.
- **Resistance:** **The receipt's third clause requires the object to be produced at the request of the district, the watch or the market court — and Tomas wrote that clause, and any person at all may make that request.** At the second hour a man with a written request in a hand Tamsin cannot place asks for the object, and **the request is lawful, and it is not a crime, and there is nothing to refuse it with.**
- **Information revealed:** **A clause written to stop an office taking a bell can be used by a person with a pen and an hour, and it will work, and it will work on the man who asked for the clause.** The request is produced, and it is dated, and it names a requester who gives no name, and a clause that says *a request may be made by any person* does not say what happens when a person who cannot give a name makes one.
- **Action or decision:** **Tamsin does not refuse the request.** She asks for the hour, the witness and the name of the requester to be entered in the yard book in front of eleven people, and reads the request back, and the requester cannot produce a name and cannot produce a witness and does not wait to be asked twice. Tomas is not in the room and is woken at the end of it and does not say that he told them it would happen.
- **Emotional change:** Tamsin discovers that she is good at this and that being good at it is not the same as liking it. And Tomas discovers that a thing he was proud of can be used against him, and takes it as a bill that came in rather than as a defeat.
- **Power or resource change:** **No change of stage and no new sense.** She gains a witness, a page in a yard book, and a night without sleep. The frame is never out of the cradle, the bolts are made fast at every place it was set down, **nothing is rung, nothing is broken, and the object is intact with no clapper and no clapper to come.**
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The receipt is not voided, broken, renegotiated or extended. **Its four clauses and its fifth all stand.** The fifth clause — no answer to a signal on or by the object may be entered as the answer of a household of this ward, and the holder has no standing to speak for any household in Kells or in any ward — **is what makes the request checkable, and Tomas does not get to forget it on the morning he finds it inconvenient.**
- **Ending type:** Cost.
- **Next-chapter pull:** They go to Fourmile in daylight with a letter to a man with a name, and one of the two of them has not slept.

### Chapter 0058 — An Office With an Address

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** The Fourmile renewal office, a real building on a real street, Day 42, morning into the afternoon.
- **Immediate goal:** Use District Officer Marten Cope properly for the first time — in person, in his own building, with four questions in a form he must answer in his own name.
- **Resistance:** The district is not obliged to answer about another district's roll, and **Fourmile's roll does not include the two counties up the road**, and the fourth question is about a line on Fourmile's own file, which means the fourth question is the only one he can answer at all, and answering it will require him to write down that a roll is not a return. And Tomas is a person whose name is in a Crown file, and he arrives with a bell in a cradle and does not get past the first desk without it being looked at.
- **Information revealed:** **Cope answers three of the four in writing, in his own name, within the hour, and refuses the fourth in writing, in his own name, with the reason.** The reason is that his office has no instrument for a roll, that a roll is not a return, and that answering the fourth question properly would require him to say so in a document that goes to a district office, and **he is not yet willing to do that and says so and does not pretend to a reason that is braver than the one he has.** He is a man whose desk became a person. He is useful. He is not a door, and he does not open one.
- **Action or decision:** Tomas does not argue with the refusal and does not thank him for the three answers. He asks for the refusal to be **filed where a person can ask for it**, and gets that too, and writes the four questions down in four columns with the hour against each and the answer or the refusal in the answer's own column, and **nobody merges them, including him.**
- **Emotional change:** He came for a door and got a colleague, and the thing that changes in him is that he stops believing that being right is the beginning of an exchange. **He is on a road with three written answers and one written refusal and it is the best afternoon he has had since Kells.**
- **Power or resource change:** No power. He gains a letter in an officer's own name, three answers, one refusal, and a man who will answer again.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** **Marten Cope is not a villain and is not converted.** The letter of the thirty-second, the custody review of the thirty-third, the withdrawal of the district's finding, and the release of the carrier to Tomas are his acts and are not repudiated by him here. **Nothing he says in this chapter identifies a person in OFF. 411, a higher official, the Crown clapper, or the mechanism.** The district's finding was withdrawn on the thirty-third and **the withdrawal is not a victory for the ward; nobody has asked for it.**
- **Ending type:** Earned hook.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The fourth question is now in writing in an officer's own name and unanswered, and it can only be answered household by household, because the people who are on the roll are the only people who can ask about it.

### Chapter 0059 — Nine Households, One Signature

- **POV:** Tamsin Rook.
- **Location/time:** Nine doors along the towpath and two lanes off it, Day 43, from the fourth hour.
- **Immediate goal:** Put the fourth question to nine households and learn how many of them are on the same roll.
- **Resistance:** **Nobody wants to know.** A household that asks about its own line gets a file; a household that is on a roll and does not know it cannot be told without being entered by the telling; and the question cannot be carried by a bell, and Tamsin is not going to be asked to carry it, and will not offer. And Tomas cannot help, because **Tomas is four streets away at one door with a slate and a barrow, and that is now a rule and not an accident.**
- **Information revealed:** **Eight of the nine refuse the question, and one of the eight is the person who has been signing for the others.** The signing is lawful, the signer is one of them, and he has not been asked to be it and cannot remember agreeing to it. **A household is not a person, and a person inside a household is not the household, and the roll does not care which of them it is.**
- **Action or decision:** Tamsin does not persuade anybody and does not use Tomas's name. She asks each of the nine the same three questions in the same words and writes down what each of them says and the hour, and **enters eight refusals as eight refusals and not as eight silences**, which is Nell Fenn's practice and which she learned in a lane in Kells in a different month.
- **Emotional change:** She is the person at the doors and the thing she has is a slate and a habit from a town that is four miles behind them, and it works, and **it works at a cost that is paid by eight households who now know they are on something.**
- **Power or resource change:** No power. She gains eight refusals in eight hands, one signature, and a signature that is the same hand as the hand on the smith's bill and on the form at the door of the household of six. **Her bell is not rung. Her hearing is not discussed. Nobody asks her for a reason.**
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The slate's leash is a working instrument and she uses it: neither of them uses the other's road without asking, an answer may be no, and the person who asked may not ask again that day. **No house number, name or age is recorded in a way that would put a household on a list, and no age is supplied for anybody.**
- **Ending type:** Reversal.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The signature is the same hand in three documents in three counties and in two kinds of paper, and Miremarket is downstream, and there is a boat.

### Chapter 0060 — The Road To Miremarket

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** The towpath above the ferry, Day 44, morning into the evening; the decision is made in the open at a landing stage.
- **Immediate goal:** Decide whether to go to Miremarket, downstream, on the strength of a signature and nothing else.
- **Resistance:** **They have no authority, no metal, no bell they may ring, no money worth the name, one unanswered question in an officer's own name, and two people who may stop at any hour without giving a reason.** Going to Miremarket is going to the place where the answers are kept, which is going to the office's own ground, on a road where nobody has asked them anything yet. And Tamsin's four terms say she may stop at any hour, and says so out loud, and means it, and he may not ask her again that day.
- **Information revealed:** **The same signature is on a household of six two counties back, on a smith's bill, and on a roll in a building in Fourmile, and it is the same hand in all three** — and the four counties do not share a bellwright, a market day or an office, and the only thing they do share is a person who once signed something on behalf of a house and has been signing ever since.
- **Action or decision:** **He does not decide.** He writes the decision down in four columns and asks two people who do not agree with each other, and asks them in the words he has learned in a ward — the same words, read the same way, and what they actually said, written down and not improved. **The road is chosen because of what one of them refuses to do**, and he records the refusal in its own column, and it is not a silence and it is not an answer, and the boat is booked and the frame is strapped and nothing is rung.
- **Emotional change:** He is a man asking a road for permission and being refused politely by one person and sharply by the other, and he accepts both, and **it is the first time in the volume that accepting a refusal costs him nothing in public** — because there is no crowd, and no board, and no Crown officer, and the only people watching are a ferry-man and a woman who cannot hear him.
- **Power or resource change:** No power and no bell. He gains a decision, a date, two refusals in two columns, and a boat. **He is further from the right to speak for anybody and closer to being useful, and he writes down that those are the same movement and not a good one.**
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The fixed carrier stays in its cradle with its bolts made fast and is not rung and may not be. **The Day 24 request is not re-opened and the refusal is not treated as a wound.** **No weekday and no month is named.** **No half-day is attached to an hour the chapters do not attach one to.** The answer to the Day 32 district question about the fifteen L-4 lines is still owed by the district and still unasked by any household, and it is not answered in this batch.
- **Ending type:** Decision, with a hook.
- **Next-chapter pull:** On the last page, in passing, at a landing, a ferry-man uses a phrase neither of them has heard before — **there are rolls at Miremarket that answer for people who never went** — and does not explain it, and is gone up the path before either of them can ask him a second question, and the tide is what it is.
