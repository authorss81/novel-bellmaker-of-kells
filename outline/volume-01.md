# Volume 01 Outline: The Bell Before the Crime

**Volume number:** 01
**Chapter range:** 1–50

> **Corrected at the volume-close audit** (`reviews/volume-01/volume-audit.md`, Part 2 finding 12). **Nine** places below did not match the prose and were corrected **here, in the outline, and not in the chapters**: (1) the starting state's *Lira Vale* and *six years ago*; (2) the placing of Crake's writ in Chapters 1–10; (3) the seizure, which belongs to Chapters 21–30 and not to 11–20; (4) *Tamsin's handbell route is damaged* and *a costly false alarm*; (5) *Tomas saves one child and the surviving register fragment*; (6) *one episode of ringing ears*; (7) *a route fragment* at the departure; (8) the next-volume question as *the volume's last page*; and (9) the bolts, the district's wax and the watch captain's mark, and the village in the final image. **The prose is the authority in every case.**

**Central pressure:** The Crown's **Silent Muster** is planned for dawn. A temporary holding writ will buy only a seven-day public hearing before the rescheduled dawn; the operation will still remove unregistered children from Kells's Low Ward, use their names as relay anchors, and burn the Vale Bellhouse archive. Tomas has one warning bell, a local watch that may not protect him, and a bell rule that cannot tell him whether the warning is fact or merely accepted belief.

## Starting state

Tomas Vale is a 32-year-old third-generation bellmaker running the Vale Bellhouse above Kells's north gate. He is a competent metalworker and a careful reader of routes, but he has no reliable supernatural practice. He is trying to finish a Crown maintenance contract, keep the workshop solvent, and prove that his family did not cause the Hush of Kells.

**Their mother has been missing since the Hush twelve years ago; she is named nowhere in the prose of this volume**, and the twelve-year span is a series fact rather than a number any chapter supplies. Mara Vale, Tomas's younger sister, left Kells after a public hearing over their mother's disappearance; **no chapter of Volume 01 gives a year for her departure, and the mother's name is never spoken in the prose** — she is on the page only as *our mother's name spoken into a record that would not release it*, and Mara as an unsigned note behind the frame and a memory of her at twenty in a market court. Tomas carries guilt for cutting a bell rope during the Hush; Oren Pike's brother Ewan died after the cut. Tomas believes he can protect people by carrying decisions alone. He is wrong about the cost of that belief.

## Starting relationships

- Tomas and Mara are estranged; Tomas wants to find Lira, while Mara believes he still treats secrecy as protection.
- Oren watches Tomas with grief and suspicion, not open hatred.
- Sabin Fell is Tomas's former master and reluctant mentor; he knows more about the Hearthline than he admits.
- Tamsin Rook has not yet joined Tomas. She is a Crown-adjacent signal courier who distrusts both royal bells and men who claim to hear hidden meanings.
- Nell Fenn is a Low Ward apprentice whose missing father gives the coming name levy a human face.

## Starting power level

Tomas is at **Stage 0: Hand**. He can cast, file, tune, and diagnose ordinary bells, but he cannot safely read a bound bell or identify a copied answer. The volume earns **Stage 1: Near Toll**: he binds one bounded local warning and learns only whether a nearby answer is strained. In this first binding he pays with both a failed small casting and a damaged tuning tool, plus the first episode of ringing ears. **The ears come back twice more — at the flood crest on the seventeenth and in the market lane on the nineteenth — so the volume's cost is one episode with the binding and two returns after it. He does not gain deep contextual reading or a permanent memory loss; that cost belongs to later Stage 2 practice.**

## Major locations

- The Vale Bellhouse and its locked casting room
- Kells's Low Ward, river landing, and west road
- The Kells watch house and market court
- The Crown survey store, where the maintenance order and bell records are held
- The old Hearthline route beneath the north gate
- The quiet side of the river, where Tamsin uses a stolen handbell to move a warning

## Major factions

- **Kells watch:** Oren wants public order, not a martyr or a riot.
- **Crown survey office:** A local official wants the bell secured so the Renewal schedule is not disrupted.
- **Low Ward families:** They have legal reason to fear any new registry and no collective authority to answer for one another.
- **The Bellwrights' Measure:** It can condemn Tomas's unsafe craft or offer a technical defense.
- **The Unrung:** A few local people want every Crown-linked bell removed, even if that removes the only public warning system.

## Mystery progression

1. The stripped Crown bell rings without a clapper and carries a child-sized handprint.
2. Tomas learns that a warning can concern a future event when someone has accepted the duty to warn before it arrives.
3. The first signal is paired with a Royal survey order that predates the bell's ringing.
4. A copied answer in the survey office makes the Crown's future order sound like a Kells-wide acceptance.
5. The **Silent Muster** is revealed as a real operation, not a prophecy.
6. Tomas finds evidence that Mara is alive and involved from inside the Quiet Office.
7. The volume resolves who the first bell is warning about and why it sounds, while leaving the source of the copied answer and Lira's fate open.

## Escalation sequence

- **Chapters 1–10 — The stripped bell:** Tomas traces the immediate warning to a pre-filed inspection order and a physical route, meets Tamsin and Nell, learns the first consent mechanics, and completes only one private Stage 1 test. Halden Crake's temporary writ names the fixed carrier, the trial handbell and the loose relay bells, and its pending-hearing clause delays the inspection by seven days; the copied answer and the operation's full purpose remain unresolved.
- **Chapters 11–20 — The town's answer:** Tomas tests the warning against ordinary promises and flood danger. Oren forces Tomas to explain the old rope cut in public. Kells discovers that a three-answer public signal can preserve disagreement without becoming an order. **The seizure itself belongs to the next block, not to this one.**
- **Chapters 21–30 — The order before the crime:** Crake's writ is executed — the emergency custody hearing, a lawful open seizure of the trial handbell and the eighteen objects, and the round that is conducted on the strength of the Crown's own response column. Tomas traces the Crown survey office's handling of the order and finds a copied answer. The formal midpoint reversal is that the warning is a real planned operation, not a supernatural omen.
- **Chapters 31–40 — The road opens:** The town splits over whether to move children, close the west road, or obey Halden. Tamsin loses the note and can no longer carry; the courier bell's own alteration, a chisel and a riffle file to the inside of its crown, is not read until the last chapter. Extending Tomas's bounded warning across a broader route saves a few people and costs a carrier thirty-six pounds, a girl of nineteen her wrist, and about a hundred and eighty persons a market lane — **not a false alarm: a true sentence sent without its difficult part, which the volume says is a version and not a claim.**
- **Chapters 41–50 — Dawn answer:** The Crown arrives before the full warning can be understood. Tomas refuses to convert the bell into a command. Kells answers in several ways: some families move, some stay, some hide, and some refuse. The archive burns and the Vale Bellhouse is closed. **One child of nine is not taken from house 114, and Chapter 48 says in print that the bell had nothing to do with it: the reason is a cart that did not come up a hill. The surviving register fragment is moved off a bench in the Crown's yard by Bevin Sarr, who is on no list, and neither of them is Tomas.**

## Midpoint reversal

Around Chapter 25, Tomas expects the main mystery to be a hidden prophetic maker or a magical curse. He finds instead a mundane and more damaging truth: the Crown survey office has already registered a future civic order as an accepted answer. The bell is not seeing the crime. It is carrying a claim that a responsible official accepted, while a clapper makes that claim sound like consent from the whole town.

## Volume climax

At the rescheduled dawn in Chapters 44–48, Crown officers enter Kells through the west road. Tomas rings a bounded warning that names the known danger, identifies his own duty, and refuses to tell residents what decision to make. Low Ward families answer independently. Tamsin carries her stolen courier handbell along the river route; Oren holds the watch gate; Nell leads children away from the river landing. The operation is disrupted but not erased. The archive burns, several people are arrested, and the Crown uses Tomas's own warning as evidence that independent bells are dangerous.

## Concrete resolution

By Chapter 50:

- The Silent Muster's first seizure is prevented from becoming a complete roundup, though some records and people are still lost.
- Kells publicly knows that the bell warning was true, that the Crown order preceded it, and that the Crown tried to claim a false answer.
- Tomas has bound only one small warning and can hear only whether a nearby answer is strained; he is not newly powerful. His Volume 01 cost is both a failed small casting and a damaged tuning tool plus the ringing ears that came with the binding and returned twice, with no permanent memory loss. **Verified on the page at the audit: the casting at Chapter 7, the file at Chapters 7 and 9, the ears at Chapter 7 and again at the crest and in the market lane, and nothing anywhere in the volume resembling a memory loss.**
- Tamsin chooses to travel with him after recovering her stolen handbell, placing its route under mutual supervision, and accepting that a warning does not require her to obey him.
- The Vale Bellhouse is sealed by the Crown. After a final custody review, Oren releases the same fixed stripped survey carrier to Tomas in its mounting frame, with the retaining bolts released only for transport and the frame secured in a reinforced padded cradle; Tomas leaves with it and with a question about who taught an office to turn one clerk's answer into a settlement's consent. **The route fragment is in the custody chain from Chapters 1, 8 and 10 but is not among the things Chapter 50 lists as leaving Kells, and the audit records it as not on the page at the departure rather than assuming it.**

## Next-volume question

Who taught the Crown to turn one clerk's answer into a whole settlement's consent, and where are the erased names being sent when they are registered as relay anchors? **Answered in Volume 01 by nobody. It is the last page of Tomas's day-book — the two questions are set down in his own hand at the top of the towpath on the thirty-third and are carried out of Kells with him — and the volume's final page after that is the road, not the question.**

## Final chapter image

Tomas leaves Kells before dawn on the thirty-third with the fixed stripped Crown survey carrier still seated in its mounting frame, the retaining bolts **made fast for transport**, and the frame secured in a reinforced padded cradle. Behind him the Vale Bellhouse sign hangs crooked over a locked door, the wax on it is the district's wax with the district's number on it, and the watch captain's mark is under the wax. On the river road, Tamsin rings her stolen courier handbell once, waits, does not ring again, and receives an answer about twenty minutes later from Ocklebridge, a village of about two hundred that has not yet heard of the district's answer of the twenty-fifth day. Her last sentence to him is **a refusal**: *Do not tell me where to go.*
