# Volume 03 Outline: The Quiet Office

**Volume number:** 03
**Chapter range:** 101–150
**Created:** after the Volume 02 close, because `state/current.md`, `state/volume-index.md`, `state/continuity.md` §11 and `outline/volume-02.md` all record that **no volume outline for Volume 03 existed** and that Volume 03 planning needed it closed. It is written against the prose of Volume 02 and against `outline/series.md` and `outline/ending.md`, and it inherits two obligations as its first two items because the Volume 02 close named them as unpaid.

**Central pressure:** **Sennet.** Tomas Vale walks up the road to the capital to find the source of the Crown clapper and his mother's trial record, and finds that the Quiet Office does not need a villain, a secret, or a bell. **It rings bells for paper.** A court date, a work inspection, a public notice — each one is a sound with a date on a sheet, and the sound reaches a village a day before a man with a book does. **Ordinary bureaucracy is the coercive infrastructure, and the volume's discovery is that the Office is not a room. It is a format**, the same way Volume 02's discovery was that a roll does not need anybody's answer.

**The two obligations this volume inherits and must place FIRST**, recorded as unmet at `outline/volume-02.md:129–146`, `state/continuity.md` §11, `state/current.md` and `state/open-threads.md`:

1. **Mara Vale's trail becomes visible, is confirmed to be hers, and refuses to answer — and Tomas does not go into an office to make her.** `outline/series.md` places her inside the Office and refusing to meet him directly; `outline/ending.md` makes her load-bearing in Volumes 9 and 11. **The relationship is unstarted, not damaged: Volume 02 said nothing about her that this volume has to unsay, and no appearance of hers may resolve the family mystery, and none may be a rescue, and she may not be made into a thing he summons.**
2. **The Silent Muster is proved, by a document and a date and not by a bell, to be one clause of a larger Renewal operation.** It is a **clause, not a plot**, and there are other clauses he cannot read and does not guess at. **It is not a new final enemy and it does not name one.**

## Starting state

**Day 87. Tomas Vale is on a road up off the water from Miremarket with a barrow, in the rain, on the morning after he walked out of a town of about two thousand people.** He has: the **fixed stripped Crown survey carrier** in its mounting frame in its reinforced padded cradle, four retaining bolts made fast at every place the frame has been set down since the thirty-third, **no clapper, and a receipt in four clauses and a fifth, all five standing**; the **certified plain carrier** in a sack against the cradle, no claim in it, rung four times in its life and twice by him; **thirteen non-bell documents** in a coat and **five receipts**; a fragment of nine lines out of a book of numbers; four columns and a slate with the price side the other way up; and a woman ten feet off his left shoulder.

**He has no room, no place, no standing in any county, and no reason to be in any of them. He is not looking for a place and has stopped looking without deciding to.**

**His three questions are on the page.** One is answered: *a roll does not need anybody's answer.* Two is not answered: *where a name goes when it is registered.* And the third is the one a woman put to him on a road in the rain on Day 86 and he answered in four words: ***Which office is it.*** — *Nobody has told me.*

**Tamsin Rook** is partly deaf for the rest of her life, permanently, public in Kells by her own act, and **nobody asks her for a reason at any hour in this volume.** Her hearing is discussed in two sentences across fifty chapters and in both of them she is talking about something else. She rang nothing in Volume 02. Her courier handbell is in her bag and is hers, and **she may ring it once in this volume, on her own terms, and never for him.** Her answer is in a clerk's hall in Miremarket, in an auditor's own book, with the hour against it, and **Tomas has known since Chapter 0096 that it is there and has deliberately not asked for it, on the ground that she did not ask him, and that stays true through this volume.**

## Starting relationships

- **Tomas and Tamsin:** a leash at eight uses, five worked and three of which cost the man who asked, held. Her four terms stand. They work apart and neither improves the other's half. **He is on a road with no destination and she is on a road looking for a child; neither of them has said what the road is for this time.**
- **Tomas and Mara:** unstarted. Six years of not writing, one note in Volume 01 that never bore her signature, and ninety chapters in which she did not appear. **She is inside the Office and she does not answer.**
- **Tomas and the Office:** he has never been in it and has never been refused by it. **It has never noticed him, and this volume is about the day it does.**
- **Tamsin and large institutions:** she has lost trust in every one of them and has said so in the ordinary way, and the volume must not argue her out of it and must not make her sentimental about it.
- **Oren Pike, Iven Sore, Sabin Fell, Nell Fenn, Mercy Slee and every Low Ward person** are in Kells, and **nothing in the first half of this volume puts any of them on the road.** Letters are permitted; appearances are not, before the volume's own midpoint.

## Starting power level

**Tomas is at the first edge of Stage 1 (Near Toll) and this volume may not move him until the climax.** He has bound one bounded warning in his life and spent it. His only perception is whether a nearby answer is strained. **He does not learn to identify a copied answer, does not learn where a sound went, and is never described as reaching for either.** He is not told where either of his two public ringings went, and **no batch of this volume may let him find out as a consolation.**

**What the volume gives him instead of a stage, in this order:** a trade fact about what a Crown fitting is and where fittings come from; a piece of paper about his mother that is **edited, and about a trial, and does not say what happened to her**; a **physical route map** he cannot read; an arrest; and a working arrangement with two other people that he does not run.

**One power change is permitted in this volume and it happens in the climax, at a cost, and it is the volume's most important line:** in Chapter 0146 a forced relay comes through him and takes more of Tamsin's hearing than the volume began with, and Tomas **does not become stronger and does not become the man who cut it.** He is given the choice and he is not the person who gets to make it, which is the whole of what he has failed to learn for three volumes.

## Major locations

- **The post road up from the lower river** — six days' road from Miremarket to Sennet, of which the last two are the Crown's maintained road with notice-boards and Office bells on posts. **A notice-board is a sheet on a board; an Office bell is a bell hung on a post above a board, rung from a district store with nobody near it.**
- **Wendle**, a village of about two hundred at the fourth mile up the road, with a ford, a smithy, a notice-board, and a man with a book and no office who reads notices and keeps the return book. **This is the volume's engine at the scale of one household.**
- **The middle terrace of Sennet** — a market of about nine hundred on a river town of about nine thousand, the river courts, and the Office's **public counter at the foot of the top terrace, where anybody may bring a paper and be told what it is.** A counter takes papers and not people, and that is the volume's last finding and not a defeat.
- **The top terrace** — the Quiet Office, the Crown archives, and a street in which an Office bell rings on a schedule in front of about two hundred people. **The sealed First Answer chamber is in this volume's geography and is not opened, described, or approached.**
- **A district store** — a room with a counter and a book and a man, like a chandler's shop, from which Crown fittings are issued against a district instruction. **The answer to where a Crown clapper comes from is: a store, like any other, with a list.**

## Major factions

- **The Quiet Office.** Not a secret order with one mind. Clerks who separate a person's stated duty from a household's presumed duty because it is useful in a famine. **It is the volume's first real ally and it is also the thing the volume is about, and those are the same fact.**
- **The district round.** A returning officer, a form, a printed slip with a number. Already established and already proved not to be a villain.
- **The notice-reader.** Every village has one. He has a book, no office, about nine shillings a quarter, and he is the person through whom a schedule becomes a sound. **He is not a puppet. He is a man doing his work, and he can be right.**
- **Sennet's ordinary people.** About nine thousand, crowded, dependent, and **not summoned by this volume's doings and not saved by them.**
- **The Bellwrights' Measure.** Split since Volume 02 between licensed central control and independent craft. **Tomas is on the wrong side of the split in a county whose bar he does not argue, and the capital is the last place in the kingdom where a licensed master bell is the only kind anybody has heard of.**

## Mystery progression

1. **The Office's bells are rung for paper.** A court date, an inspection, a notice — a sound and a sheet, and the sound arrives first and the sheet is what the sound is for.
2. **A Crown fitting is issued from a store like any other store, against an instruction, with a number.** Tomas has had a Crown repair done to his own carrier in a cradle and has not known who did it.
3. **A bell can be entered on a roll.** The Office has a list of *objects*. He has spent two volumes looking for a list of people and there is a list of things, and a thing is easier to take than a person.
4. **Mara's trail is confirmed to be hers and refuses to answer.**
5. **The Silent Muster is proved by a document and a date as one clause of a larger Renewal operation**, and the other clauses stay unread.
6. **The Office names him.** A notice in his own name, and he cannot answer it, and the reason he cannot answer it is a clause of a receipt he asked for.
7. **The volume's midpoint reversal:** the edited transcript of his mother's trial shows that **Lira Vale discovered counterfeit answers and accepted responsibility for hiding the first clappers** — and it does not say whether she was imprisoned, killed, or used as an anchor. **He wanted a sentence about his mother and he has got a duty of hers, and a duty is a thing she accepted and a death is a thing done to a person, and the Office's edit is the difference.**
8. **Why the old network accepts a Crown clapper** is approached and **not answered**: a route that answers is a route that works, and a keeper who withdraws is a keeper whose district goes quiet, and eleven thousand people a year are not clerks.

## Escalation sequence

- **Chapters 101–110 (Batch 0001) — The road that rings on a schedule.** Day 87 onward. A notice-board with a bell on a post, a village of about two hundred, a smith who has fitted Crown work to three bells, a market with a form that has a box for the person who repeated a thing, **a leaf in Mara's hand on a public board that refuses to answer, a notice in his own name, and the top terrace of Sennet at the end of it.** **The batch proves the Silent Muster by a document and a date.** It reaches no archive, names no higher official, and opens no chamber.
- **Chapters 111–120 (Batch 0002) — Three terraces.** Sennet on the page: the Flat, the Middle, the Top. The public counter and what a counter can and cannot do. The district store, its counter, its book and its number. **The batch's job is to make a reader able to walk into the capital.**
- **Chapters 121–130 (Batch 0003) — The store of fittings, and his mother's trial.** Where a Crown clapper comes from, and by whose hand the first ones were hidden. **Midpoint reversal at about Chapter 0125: the edited transcript.** A duty his mother accepted, and no sentence about her fate, and the edit itself becomes the evidence.
- **Chapters 131–140 (Batch 0004) — The Office copies him.** He is copied, quoted, and entered; a person who is right is arrested on his account; **Mara chooses once and is not thanked for it;** and the three-person arrangement begins as a negotiation and not a reunion.
- **Chapters 141–150 (Batch 0005) — The public truth bell and the warrant. Climax and resolution.** **He rings a public truth bell in Sennet. The Quiet Office copies his signal and turns it into a warrant. A forced relay comes through the public route and takes more of Tamsin's hearing, and the man who could have cut it is not the man who does. He is arrested. Mara must choose between exposing him and preserving the archive, and she chooses the archive, gives him nothing at all, and does not meet him — the means he walks out with arrive from the officer in the coat, unasked, because there is no form that lets a district keep a man.** He escapes with a **physical route map** and a **surviving copy of Lira's account**. **No Crown office is defeated. Dain is not named. No bell he made is ever unbarred.**

## Midpoint reversal

**Reached at about Chapter 0125 and not anticipated before it, in the shape Volume 02 established the pattern with and not the shape a reader expects.** He goes into the archives for a **trial record** and finds an **edited transcript of a duty**: his mother discovered that answers were being copied and accepted responsibility for hiding the first Crown clappers, and the transcript is in her own voice, and it is about eleven lines long, and it is a **duty** and not a **sentence**. **He came for a woman and he has got a woman who accepted something.** The transcript does not say whether she was imprisoned, killed, or used as an anchor and **may not be made to say so by any chapter of this volume.** The reversal is not that she was guilty. It is that **a duty is a thing a person accepts and a conviction is a thing a person suffers, and the Office's edit is the difference between those two, and the edit is visible only because the Office kept the duty and cut the rest.**

## Volume climax

**Chapters 141–150, in Sennet.** Tomas attempts a public truth bell — a plain claim naming what is known, what he does not know, and his own fault, telling nobody what to decide, which is the shape of every bell he has rung and the shape the volume's own rules require. **The Quiet Office copies the signal and turns it into a warrant.** Not a lie: the signal he rang is a real signal, and a warrant is what an office does with a real signal, and the Office is right about the procedure and wrong about the standing and **he has no standing to be wrong about.** A forced relay runs the line to exhaustion and Tamsin takes the worst of it, because she was holding a sill and a floor and a wall and she is the one who reads vibration. **She loses more hearing. Nothing is restored. Nobody asks her for a reason, including him.** He is arrested in a room with a form in it, lawfully, on a warrant that is a copy of his own bell. **Mara is in the building and does not come to him, and he does not ask her to, and he has learned enough in this volume to know that asking would be the fifth-clause thing to do.**

## Concrete resolution

By Chapter 150:

- **The local promise is answered: the Office's ordinary bells are for paper, and a sound with a date on it is the cheapest coercive instrument in this kingdom because nobody can tell a notice from an order when both are made of the same metal.** That is on the public record in Sennet and in four counties behind it, and it costs somebody.
- **The Silent Muster is proved, by a document and a date, to be one clause of a larger Renewal operation, and it is a clause.** Paid, and paid in the first batch.
- **Mara's trail is confirmed to be hers and refuses to answer, and he does not go into an office to make her.** Paid, and paid in the first batch. **She appears in person in this volume and gives him nothing and is not turned into an ally he can summon.**
- **Where the first Crown clapper came from is answered: a Crown store in Sennet, issued against a district instruction, with a number, like any other equipment.** **Why the old network accepts it is not answered** and becomes Volume 04's opening pressure.
- **Lira Vale's edited transcript is on the page, and it is a duty she accepted, and it does not say what happened to her.** Whether she lived is **not** answered in this volume. That is Volume 05's finding and Volume 08's.
- **A physical route map leaves the capital with him**, and it is a map and not a key: he cannot read it, there is no form for reading it, and four people in four counties have said a map shows where a line runs and cannot show whose hand altered it.
- **Tomas is arrested and is not rescued.** He escapes — **or is released — with help he did not ask for from a person he cannot command, and he goes on foot.**
- **Tamsin's hearing is worse at the end of this volume than at the start of it and is not rescued, not restored, not treated as a casualty of his decision, and nobody asks her for a reason.**
- **The three-person working arrangement is made, out loud, with three sets of terms, and he is not the clerk of it. THERE IS NO PAPER IN IT.** *(Corrected after the volume was written. The outline used to say *in writing*; Chapter 0134's four clauses make a written set of terms impossible, and a last chapter that suddenly produces one unmakes all four. The terms were said in a room he paid for and the only writing that exists at the end of the volume is four lines in Tamsin Rook's hand that he does not hold. The prose was right and was not touched — see `state/continuity.md` §10h.)*
- **The Crown is not defeated.** A notice is withdrawn, an officer is relieved of nothing, a store issues a fitting, a warrant stands, an archive is preserved by the person who could have given it away.

## Relationship and emotional progression

- **Tomas and Tamsin:** a leash at eight uses entering the volume. **She rings her own bell once, on her own terms, in a market, and it costs her the only cover she has left** — and it is not for him, and it does not help him, and he is not thanked for it. She loses more hearing in the climax while holding something for the road, and **he is not the man who decides that, and he is not the man who fixes it.** The volume's relationship movement is a question asked and a no accepted: *what help do you want* — and she answers, and it is not what he would have chosen, and he does not improve it.

## New question for the next volume

**If Lira survived, what is holding the First Answer together after her disappearance — and can a route map that has had its sections cut out of every public copy be used to answer a question about a building nobody may describe?** Volume 04 opens on the physical route map and on the removed sections, and the two are the same object seen from two ends.

## Planned power and relationship progression

- **No new stage and no new sense before the climax.** Tomas is at the first edge of Near Toll at the end of Volume 03 exactly as at the end of Volume 02, until Chapter 0146, when a forced relay goes through him and he pays with Tamsin's hearing and his own balance and gains nothing he can carry.
- **He attempts no perception in any chapter of this volume before 0146 and is not described as reaching for one.** He does not learn to identify a copied answer. He does not learn where a sound went. He says so in his own hand and it has not changed.
- **Tamsin's hearing is not restored and is not rescued.** Worse at the end than at the start, public by her own act, and hers to price. **Nobody in this volume asks her for a reason, holds it over her, or treats her as a person who has been exposed** — and the one person who could most easily ask is the man she is travelling with.
- **The growth is civic and relational:** a man with no standing learning that an institution is a format and not a villain, and learning it from clerks who are right.
- **The cost is a notice in his own name, an arrest, a hearing he cannot answer, and somebody else's hearing.** It is not a wound, a fever, or a fade. It is paperwork and a bell and the price of both.

## Final chapter image

A road out of Sennet on foot, with a physical route map he cannot read in a coat and a surviving copy of his mother's account against his chest, and a woman beside him whose hearing is worse than it was ten days ago and who is carrying a bell she rang once, on her own terms, in a market, for a reason that was not him. **Behind them a capital of about nine thousand people in which a bell rings on a schedule every day and nobody rings it, and about forty people in a yard on the top terrace have been told nothing at all about what to decide, and are not going to be, and a warrant stands, and an archive is intact, and a third person who is not on the road is holding a record that could end all of it and will not hand it over because she has decided what it is for.**

## Batch 0001 card as written, and what it cost

*(recorded at the volume's first batch, for the next writer. The plot above is unchanged. What follows is what Batch 0001 actually delivered, so that a later writer inherits the shape of the prose and not an idea of it.)*

### Volume 03 Batch 0001 — what the ten chapters paid, in order

1. **Chapter 0101** established the volume's engine: **an Office bell is a bell on a post above a notice-board, rung from a district store with nobody near it**, and a notice is a sheet, and the sound arrives before the man. **The sheet under the first bell he stands under is dated four days forward of the ringing, and a man with a book who has kept the same board for nine years has never once written down how far ahead the bell goes, and there is no column for it.** It also established the one thing the volume needed and did not have: **a third kind of bell.** Not bound and not unbound. A bell that answers somebody else's stroke. **This kingdom has no name for it on a form and the batch does not invent a name for it in a chapter; it exists as a description.**
2. **Chapter 0102** put the engine at the scale of one household in **Wendle**, and gave the volume a person the Office can do to: **Salla Keene**, who did not come to a court date, and whose name went into a column, and whose second notice went by the same bell. **She is not found, not rescued, not named on a roll in this chapter, and the chapter's last line is her husband's sentence about a bell and it is unkind and it is right.**
3. **Chapter 0103** is the craft chapter and it is the first time in three volumes that Tomas touches Crown work on purpose. **Doss Harrow has fitted Crown work to three bells in this county and can say what it is: a coupling, a jumper, and a fitting that is not a clapper and is not the bell's own.** Tomas recognises the same hand that mended his own carrier in a cradle. **He does not learn what it does. He learns where it comes from: a store, with a counter, a book and a number.** A number, not a name. That is the whole of the chapter and it is the volume's second finding.
4. **Chapter 0104** gave the volume its best civic discovery: **a bell can be entered on a roll.** The Office keeps a list of *objects*. The keeper's field is empty on one line and the form has four fields and none of them is the words — ****a second water bell, in a second county, on a second return, entered in the same way and with the same keeper's box empty and the same rule running past the end of the line as Marta Vell's water bell in Fourmile — not her bell and not her village and not her number, and Tomas writes down that it is the same form and does not join the two into a pattern.**** Nobody is transferred and no mechanism is described.
5. **Chapter 0105** pays the **first inherited obligation: Mara's trail, visible, confirmed to be hers, and refusing.** A leaf pinned at an angle under a drawing-pin among the Office notices on a public board, in a hand confirmed by comparison against her own hand in Volume 01 — **and not by wax, and not by inference, because the one leaf in Amos Trewin's stock book is a leaf of unknown hand and the page does not support the other attribution.** The leaf is five words. It is about him. It does not answer where she is or whether she is alive. **He does not take it off the board, and that is the first thing in three volumes he has refused that he wanted, and he refuses it on the ground that he was in a town where a man was taken at about the sixth hour for five sheets off a table.** And **a woman at the board tells him the Office reads that board every morning and that a leaf that has been there nine days has not been taken down, and that is not because they do not see it.**
6. **Chapter 0106** is Tamsin's chapter and it is the batch's cost. **She rings her own handbell once, in a market, on her own terms, and it is not for him and it does not help him.** A man in that market fills in a form with a box for **the name of the person who repeated a thing**, and about four hundred people a year fill that box in, and none of them are wicked. **Her eleven years of road work has a form on it and she has never seen the form**, and the finding is not that they are wicked, it is that **a box is cheaper than a witness and a witness can refuse.** **The leash is NOT used a ninth time in this volume: he puts no question on the slate, she tells him nothing and he asks her nothing, and each of them notes the other's not-asking in his own or her own book. It is a non-use and it is not a ninth use, and the count stays at eight.** She refuses and does not explain, and there is a reason on the page and it is the first reason in eleven years she has given for a **method** rather than a want.
7. **Chapter 0107** pays the **second inherited obligation: the Silent Muster, proved by a document and a date.** A printed standing instruction on a board in a market town, headed with a number and a date, listing eleven clauses under a heading that contains the word **Renewal**; and clause seven concerns **children not entered on a roll**; and a second dated sheet in another county refers to **clause seven** by number. Tomas puts the two on a table in a market and gets the direction of one of them wrong and a man in the room corrects him and is right. **The sheet has no signature on it and only a store number, and eleven clauses is the whole of what is on it, and six of them are about things he has never heard of, and he writes down that he does not know what they are and does not guess.** It is a clause, not a plot, and it names no enemy.
8. **Chapter 0108** is the price of Chapter 0107. **Two sheets on a table in a market is a thing about four hundred people saw, and one of them wrote down, and a district wrote up, and Sennet wrote back.** The Office puts a notice up. It is a summons to a hearing. It names him. **And it is not served on him, because a man with no standing cannot be served a document, only posted, and the fifth clause of his own receipt — the one that stopped five sheets leaving a room in Miremarket and stopped a form entering a household — is the reason he has to read it off a board in a street.** A notice in a man's own name, and the emptiness of his own paperwork is what puts it in the street.
9. **Chapter 0109** is the volume's third finding and it is the answer to the question he has carried for fifty-four days. **There are two kinds of paper.** *The record of what you said* — which is what four clerks in four counties have told him, and which is true, and which is not the whole of it. *And the thing that says it for you.* **A counter takes papers and not people, and a man may look at a list of fittings if he has a reason, and a reason is a form, and a form is the Office's own and is not a thing a man in a coat can fill out for himself.** He gets a store number and a district and a counter and nothing else. **Perrin Lowe is correct about every part of it and gives him nothing and is not a door and is not sorry.**
10. **Chapter 0110** closes the batch on the top terrace and does not resolve anything, and its finding is the volume's central pressure stated flat: **there is no such thing as the Office. There are about nine hundred rooms and one format, and a format does not have a door, and the third question cannot be answered with a room because the third question is not about a room.** And: **a second leaf, on the Office's own board, in her hand, with a date and a store number, in a public place, in front of about two hundred people — and he does not go in.** The batch ends with a hearing in nine days, a warrant that does not exist yet, a woman ten feet off his left shoulder, and a slate with two lines in the fourth column that she has not improved and is not going to.

**And what the batch did not pay, stated flat so that Batch 0002 inherits it as work and not as an idea:** no archive was entered; no trial record was read; no Crown clapper was seen, only a fitting; **nothing was said about why a route that answers is a route the old network accepts**; nobody in the building he came to has been identified above a district; **the name-transfer mechanism is untouched and no child was found by a document**; the First Answer chamber is not opened, described, or approached; **OFF. 411 is not on a page in this batch and no person is attached to it**; the five dates in a drawer were not touched; and **a name went into a column in a village of two hundred people and nobody was rescued and nobody was told the refusal was worth it.**
