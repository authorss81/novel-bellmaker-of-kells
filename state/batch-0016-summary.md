## What the batch was for, and what it actually is

**A man who cannot read a map walks nine miles of road with a barrow he cannot pay for and finds out that the map was never going to tell him where to walk.** Volume 04 opened on Day 137 at about a mile out of Sennet, on foot, with a warrant standing behind him, a rectangle gone out of a drawing in his coat in the same place, a receipt in five clauses, eleven lines in his mother's words in the same coat, and a woman ten feet off his left shoulder whose hearing is worse than it was ten days ago.

It paid exactly one promise and refused to inflate it. **He walked nine miles in ten days and did not find a section of road that is missing, because there is not one, and a second man does find the road, because he was born at the end of it and has never been asked what it is called.** No road is found, restored, registered or explained. The rectangle is not explained. The panel above the coach inn is not restored. Nobody is entered anywhere as a household. Nothing is rung, nothing is bound, no stage comes, no sense comes, and nobody is thanked.

**Nobody in this batch is wicked and that is the engine, not a softening.** It held for Brida Tolliver at a pitch, who named the reason there is no form on which a man may show a stranger a drawing before he had said it himself; for Row Kellow under a roof with a book and no office; for Tib Neave at a fire, who said no twice so that a stranger could not go away and say he only said it once; for Bel Tremayne at a table with about nine hundred names in it who shut the book; for the clerk of a bench, who would not carry a sentence anybody could not certify; for Aman Petch with his head against a wall; and for nine people in a market who said no in nine ordinary voices and were right.
