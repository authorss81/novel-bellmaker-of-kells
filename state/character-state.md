# Character State — Volume 03 Batch 0001, after Chapter 110

**This is the live cast. The per-batch addenda that used to be stacked above this file are gone; where a batch said something about a person, the most complete statement is here and the earlier one is not repeated. The do-not-age list is in `state/continuity.md` §4 and it binds. A new person enters with no age, and eight people entered in this batch with none.**

## Tomas Vale

**Thirty-two — his own number is in Sabin Fell's mouth in Volume 01 and is nowhere else. In Sennet on Day 96, at the foot of a stair on the top terrace of a city of about nine thousand people, with a barrow.** He came up the road from Miremarket on the eighty-seventh and arrived on the ninety-fifth: about eight days, about seventy miles, and six days' road by the reckoning of people who know the distance. He was in Miremarket twenty-nine days and left it, and nothing in Chapters 101 to 110 puts anybody back in that town.

**What he has, and it is the same list he wrote out flat under a hedge in the rain on Day 86, and it has not grown by one item in ten days:** about nine names that cannot be certified; four columns and a slate with the price side the other way up; a witness in a corridor; a fragment of nine lines out of a book of numbers; a receipt in five clauses, all five standing; a frame in a reinforced padded cradle with four bolts made fast and no clapper, which **has not been in a building since the thirty-third**; five receipts; a bag with a bell in it nobody can certify; and a woman ten feet off his left shoulder. **He has no standing in any county, no room, and no place, and a hearing in seven days he cannot answer.**

**And three things that are not documents and two that are the Office's: a store; a clerk's description of the second kind of paper; a printed slip and an unfilled form; a store number and a nine-day count; and a woman inside a building who has answered him twice on public boards.**

**What he lost, and none of it refunded:** a room, a bench and five sheets; a carbon that was the only copy of its form outside the building that holds the original; the use for being right; the belief that carrying a room was a right; the last person in four counties he could have been angry at. **And he has added one to that list and chose it himself: there is now nobody in this kingdom he can be angry at, and a schedule does not have a face, and that is not a comfort.**

**What he gained in ten days, none of it a stage and none of it a power and none of it a sense:** that **a sound can be what a person is read by, and a sound is cheaper than a sheet and goes further, and the sheet is only there to say afterwards what the sound was**; that the Office's bells are not a conspiracy and not a villain and **a format, and a format has no door**; that a Crown repair comes out of a **store like any other store** and he has been asking *who* instead of *where from*; that **a bell can be on a roll and the list of objects is the one that matters, and he has been frightened about the wrong object for two volumes**; that **there are two kinds of paper and the one he carried for fifty-six days had a second leaf**; that **there is no box on the form for a question**; that **a man with no standing cannot be served a document and is therefore posted, and the same emptiness that protected him is what put his name in a street**; and that **a public board is the only surface in this kingdom with no standing attached to it**, which is why two people he is related to used one and he cannot.

**THE BELL HE DID NOT RING. He rang nothing in ninety-six days and the chapters say so in eight separate day-books.** The Crown carrier cannot be rung and he did not furnish a clapper and did not go near it. The certified plain carrier is untouched since the seventy-seventh, twenty days, and he does not know where either of his two public ringings went and is not going to be told. **He rings no bell he has made anywhere in this volume, and his own plain carrier is in a sealed yard in Kells and he is not going to it.**

**HIS THIRD QUESTION, and the sentence he has to live inside: a format cannot be refused.** Ninety-six days of learning to ask a person and be refused, and he has got to the end of the road, and there is nothing at the end of it to ask. **And he is not going to write a paragraph about it, because a paragraph is a thing he would be able to carry, and he has said that four times in six weeks and once more on Day 96.**

**HIS BEST ACT IN TEN DAYS, and he refuses to have it: he did not take either leaf off either board.** A paper off a public board in a Crown town is the same act with a different name as five sheets off a table in Miremarket, and that one was lawful, and the law that let it happen was a clause he asked a clerk to write into a receipt in a watch house on the thirty-third at about the fifth hour of the morning. **He also did not go into the building to ask after the woman inside it, and the third and worst of his three reasons is written down: a man who goes and asks so that he can say he did not go is doing the thing for himself.**

**POWER: none, and no stage, and no sense, and no reaching.** He attempts no perception in any of the ten chapters and is not described as reaching for one. He does not bind anything, does not learn a second perception, does not identify a copied answer, and **does not learn where a sound went and is not described as reaching for it.** The nine words on the flyleaf at the back of the day-book, Day 62, are a comparison and not a power and are not written as one. **No bell he has ever made may be unbarred.**

## Tamsin Rook

**Partly deaf for the rest of her life, permanently. She can see a bell move and feel a bell in a sill, a floor and a wall, and cannot carry a note. She is public in Kells by her own act and by nobody else's. NO AGE IS ON THE PAGE AND NONE MAY BE SUPPLIED.** She is not rescued, nobody holds anything over her, no chapter has her discovered she has been exposed, **and NOBODY ASKS HER FOR A REASON AT ANY HOUR IN CHAPTERS 101 TO 110** — including a woman at a pitch, a man with a slip, a man in a market with a box, and the man she is travelling with. Her hearing is not the subject of one sentence in the ten chapters.

**SHE RANG HER OWN BELL, ONCE, in Chapter 0106, in a market of about nine hundred people, at about the sixth hour, on her own account, and it is not for him and it did not help him.** It is the loudest thing in that square for about nine seconds. **It cost her the only cover she had left: about nine hundred people now know there is one of her and can describe her, and about forty of them will say it in a different town inside a year, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a person may be charged with being a woman with a slate.** She had it before the second stroke, and the reason is four inches long and it is not about him: *there was a man with a box and I did not want to be in it.* **She may ring it at most once more in a later batch, on her own terms, and never for him.**

**Her finding, in her own hand, and she did not improve it: *a box is cheaper than a witness and a witness can refuse.*** Six fields on a Crown slip, four of which anybody in the room can fill in, and the fourth one is *the person who repeated them*, and it is the only one that wants a name, and about four hundred people a year fill it in and **none of them are wicked.** She has been carrying the first half of that sentence since the sixty-second without knowing it. **She is proudest of the fact that a thing said twice in a market cannot be got back, and she has found out this month that being countable is worse than being lost, and she says so, and nobody disagrees with her.**

**The leash: eight uses, and it is STILL EIGHT.** In Chapter 0106 he does not put a question on the slate; she tells him nothing and he asks her nothing, and each notes the other's not-asking in his own or her own book. **That is a non-use and no later batch may count it as a ninth.** Three non-uses now stand in Volume 02 and this is the fourth.

**Her four columns stand and nothing is merged. The fourth has two lines in it, one of them from Day 83 and one from Day 84, and she has not improved either and is not going to.** She has given away two slates in a month and has not asked for either back. The price side of the slate she carries is on the other face and it is the other way up. **She has not written down the name of the office her companion is carrying and she has written down that she did not ask, and that is the first thing in about two months that either of them has had and has not asked for.**

## The new people of Volume 03 Batch 0001 — all on the page with NO age

- **Abner Tole**, who has kept a Crown notice-board in a village of about two hundred for nine years, is paid about nine shillings a quarter, has no office, and has read out about four hundred notices. He answered four questions in about nine minutes, said in advance which one he would not answer now and why, and was neither cowardly nor clever. He counted **about four in ten** lines unattended over nine years without being asked twice. He has written in a margin on about nine notices that he said it out loud at the board, **and there is no column for that either.** He decided on purpose not to know whether a woman is in her house, and gave the reason, and it is the best decision anybody made in four counties in ten days.
- **Osten Keene**, of that village, who has had a bell on a post over his mother's door for nine years and has hung a washing line off the post, **and who said one true unkind thing about a bell at a ford on his own account and then went back up the hill.** Not angry, not drunk, and he was written down as both of those things not being true on purpose, because the scene wanted him to be one of them.
- **Salla Keene**, whose name is in a column and who is not in her house. **NOT FOUND, NOT PLACED, NOT RESCUED, NOT NAMED ON A ROLL IN ANY CHAPTER, AND NO CHAPTER OF THIS VOLUME MAY FIND HER BY A DOCUMENT.** A third notice is dated a week off and a man with a book will read it out at a board.
- **Doss Harrow**, a smith of about thirty-one years at a crossroads three miles short of Ashmoor, who fitted the same hand of Crown work — a coupling cut nearly through, a thin copper jumper soldered across it from the inside — to three bells in this county over nine years, **and who told a stranger what a store is in about four seconds and refused a theory to his face four seconds before it was offered and would not hand one over at any price.** He has said the bell in the cradle is the fourth he has seen. He is right about the work and is not a door and is not going to be thanked.
- **Ottmar Dace**, who has kept a district board under a roof in a town of about eleven hundred for about four years, has about two hundred and forty things in a column and nineteen bells in the first of three, **and a keeper's box he has filled in twice without knowing what he was writing and has never been told the meaning of.** He said *a bell on that sheet is not a bell that is taken, it is a bell that is claimed, and a thing that is claimed can be given away to somebody else* and asked for it not to be read out. **He refused to guess what is past Ashmoor and then said, in his own mouth, with the word *think* used twice and asked for: a name does not go anywhere, a name stops.** No relation to Foreman Dace of the Low Ward, and none is needed.
- **Ione Ferrar**, a man in a market who fills in a Crown slip with six fields and one of them is a name, **and who told her that he would have written *a woman with a slate* and that it would have been right, and that this is the trouble with a box.**
- **Garvin Lyle**, an officer of the district of Rill and Fen, with a satchel and a printed slip, who corrected a stranger's dates in public in a market of about two thousand people, **gave his name off his own mouth so that the stranger would not have to go and get it out of a building, said the fair copy was Crown property from the moment it was made, and then stood nine feet off the trestle and took nothing and said he was not going to be the reason.** He has his name on a form in that town. **He is right, he is not a door, he is not sorry, and Tomas did not write his county's name on anything.**
- **Perrin Lowe**, who has stood behind the public counter at the foot of the top terrace in Sennet for about nine years, has three clerks' worth of counter and about four thousand people a year bringing him a paper, has never had one of the second kind in his hand, and has been asked about them four times in nine years. **He gave the volume's third finding in about nine seconds and the rest of it unasked because four people behind him had brought papers and it was the sixth hour.** He invented no capacity, invented no enemy, refused to walk a stranger up four flights and gave the reason in four seconds, **and said that the four thousand a year are all frightened of the second kind and that being frightened of it is the correct thing to be and that nobody has ever said so out loud and that is why nobody has ever been able to write a form about it.** He shut the shutter, which is a thing a clerk does and not a thing a clerk does to anybody.
- **A woman who keeps a Crown board in a market of about nine hundred**, about nine years, who has had about nine leaves on it in nine years and put four of them back up in about the same place, **who told a stranger for nothing that the board is read every morning by about five people and not one of them will say a word about a leaf, and that this is not because they do not see it.**
- **A woman in the same market with a bell on a wall**, who put a cup of something down beside a stranger on a wall at about the seventh hour and did not say a word and went away. **Not named, not thanked, not asked anything.**

## Relationship and civic movement at the end of the batch

- **Tomas and Tamsin:** a leash at eight uses, held. She rang her own bell once, on her own terms, in a market, in front of about nine hundred people, **and he was nine feet off and did not ask her why and wrote down that he was not going to thank her, and that is the nearest anybody has come in four counties.** He did not put a question on the slate and she gave him nothing and both of them noted the not-asking. **He carried the number of the office for ten days and she never asked him for it and wrote down that she did not ask.** Neither of them says what the road is for this time. **No binding bell, no confession scene, no rescue, and nothing said in this batch that either of them would call that.**
- **Tomas and his sister:** a leaf he did not take and a building he did not enter, and **the third and worst of his reasons is written down, and it is the reason he is not going to.** He is not a man who can summon her and he has stopped trying to be one.
- **Tomas and the Quiet Office:** it has never refused him and it has now named him, and the naming is lawful, and **the naming is also the reason he cannot be told anything quietly, and both halves are in the same sentence in his own hand and he is not going to separate them.**
- **Tomas and a smith, a notice-reader, a board-keeper, an officer and a clerk at a counter in a capital:** five people who are right, none of whom is a door, none of whom wanted anything, and **one of whom told him the answer to the question he came with and then said he was going home at the sixth hour.**
- **The market of about nine hundred at the twenty-eighth mile, and the market of about two thousand four days on:** about two hundred people said their own names and their own business out loud in the open and **he wrote down the time and not one name.** That is the only thing in ten days that anybody did without being asked and without being persuaded, and it is not his.
