## Tomas Vale

**Thirty-two — his own number is in Sabin Fell's mouth in Volume 01 and is nowhere else. In Sennet on Day 116, on a step at the foot of the top stair of a city of about nine thousand people, at about the ninth hour of the evening, with a barrow. Twenty-one days in the city. A hearing thirteen days behind him that he could not answer and did not attend.** He came up the road from Miremarket on the eighty-seventh and arrived on the ninety-fifth. He was in Miremarket twenty-nine days and left it, and **nothing in Chapters 101 to 130 puts anybody back in that town.**

**What he has, and it is the same list he wrote out flat under a hedge in the rain on Day 86, and it has not grown by one document in thirty days:** about nine names that cannot be certified; four columns and a slate with the price side the other way up; a fragment of nine lines, one hand, a column of district numbers, a column of days, and a third column empty from end to end; a receipt in five clauses, all five standing; a frame in a reinforced padded cradle with four bolts made fast and no clapper, **which has not been in a building since the thirty-third and which all ten chapters of this batch say so in their own day-books**; five receipts; a bag with a bell in it nobody can certify; and a woman ten feet off his left shoulder. **He has no standing in any county, no room, and no place.**

**And, in thirty days, none of it a document and none of it a stage:** a berth on a net-loft and a man's name; a court's public board and the reason it exists; a store with a counter, a man, a shelf, a number, and a column with nothing in it, **and the sentence that the wall behind the man is shut and the book in front of him is open and both of those are correct**; nine sheets read and left; a man who did a chore for himself in his own name on a public wall; three people in three yards who said one true thing each; **a number that goes to a district he has never been in**; **the difference between a record and a file, which is the whole of the coercion and is in a form and not in a room**; **about nine hundred matters a year on four boards in the same five columns with the same empty fifth**; **a form with an empty fourth box and one plate cutting about nine hundred a year**; **eleven lines in a dead woman's own words and no sentence at all**; **three instruments and no fourth**; **a true sentence of his own on a public wall for fourpence and a copy of it in a market**; and **a standing instruction with his sister's name at the foot of it.**

**What he lost, and none of it refunded:** a room, a bench and five sheets; a carbon that was the only copy of its form outside the building that holds the original; the use for being right; the belief that carrying a room was a right; **the belief that being right about a thing is worth something, which he had since he was nine and unlearned in a store on the hundred and seventh**; **and the only sentence anybody had ever said about him, because he went and made a second one and it is in a place he did not choose.**

**THE BELL HE DID NOT RING, and this is the third batch running.** He rang nothing in a hundred and sixteen days and the plain carrier is in its sack against the barrow, **thirty-nine days untouched, the interval counted in days from the seventy-seventh and not in chapter numbers**. The Crown carrier cannot be rung and he did not furnish a clapper and did not go near it. His own plain carrier is in a sealed yard in Kells and he is not going to it. **He rings no bell he has made anywhere in this volume, and no bell he has ever made is unbarred anywhere in this volume before the climax.** He does not know where either of his two public ringings went and is not going to be told, and **this batch did not let him find out as a consolation and did not come near it.**

**THE SENTENCE THE NEXT VOLUME OPENS ON, in his own hand on Day 106 and in his own hand again on the last page of Chapter 0130, unprompted, having done nothing about it, and it is a rule about counting and not a power:** ***A man can be one of the three answers. A man cannot be two of them, and the second one he is, the first one stops being an answer and becomes a man saying the same thing twice. And there is no form in this kingdom on which a man may take the same words to three men and call the result a count. If there were three of me.*** **He has not counted anything into iron since the fortieth day of this month, and the interval is a hundred and sixteen days at the last chapter of this batch.**

**HIS BEST ACT IN THIRTY DAYS, and he refuses to have it, and it is five refusals now and they are one decision with five names:** he did not take either leaf off either board, he did not go into the upper room, he did not take the store list, he did not ask what the thing is for, **he did not ask a clerk of the district court whether a form with a third box filled wanted a reason, and he did not ask a single person in that building what became of his mother, and he did not ask the woman travelling with him for anything at all.** **And the cost of the fifth refusal is real: the only proof he will ever have is eleven lines long and he cannot copy it, cannot carry it, cannot give it to anybody and cannot make it into metal.**

**HIS WORST HOUR, and there are now two, and the second is worse.** The first was Day 106, when he asked a man for a true sentence on purpose, got one, and wrote down that the asking was the whole of the damage. **The second is the hundred and fifteenth, and it is not a bad act at all, and that is what makes it the worst hour: he went through a door he had stood in for seventeen days, and he put four true sentences about himself into a document in his own name for fourpence, and nobody asked him for a reason, and he noticed that nobody asked him, and he wrote down that he noticed, and he did it on purpose, for a thing about himself, and he has not found out what it is for and is not going to be told.** **He paid a price on purpose. A court clerk wrote down a true thing and it is on a public wall for forty years and about nine people will say his name in four towns and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom by which a man who has been named on a board can be unnamed, and he stood four feet off a man in a market on the hundred and sixteenth and did not tell him, and then he went and stood nine feet further off, which is a thing he learned from a woman and had never done on purpose until that minute.**

**POWER: none, and no stage, and no sense, and no reaching.** He attempts no perception in any of the ten chapters and is not described as reaching for one. He does not bind anything, does not learn a second perception, does not identify a copied answer, and **does not learn where a sound went and is not described as reaching for it.** The nine words on the flyleaf at the back of the day-book, Day 62, are a comparison and not a power and are not written as one. **What looks like a perception in this batch is reading: he read about nine hundred lines standing up and found a column filled on every line except nine and counted them twice, and he read four words upside down at nine feet off a clerk's tray, and comparing forms is the whole of his trade and it is the first time in four counties and a city that the trade has been the instrument. IT IS CRAFT LITERACY AND NOT A SENSE AND NO CHAPTER MAY WRITE IT AS ONE.** No bell he has ever made may be unbarred, and the only power change this volume permits happens in the climax, at a cost, and it is not stronger.

**HIS THIRD QUESTION, and the answer in the shape it now has: it is not a room and it is not a name, it is a set of tables.** A plate in a printer's room two streets off cutting about nine hundred forms a year; a four-inch box at the end of every one of them, full on nine hundred and empty on one; a rule that a sworn answer is a thing about a person and must be sent round; an open book in a store and a shut wall; a court list of nine hundred matters a year in the same five columns with the same empty fifth; and about eleven thousand lines a year that nobody is unrolling. **A hundred and sixteen days of learning to ask a person and being refused, and at the end of it a city of about nine hundred doors and every one of them is a man, and the only thing that has actually happened to him in sixteen days is that a rule put his name on a board.**

## Tamsin Rook

**Partly deaf for the rest of her life, permanently. She can see a bell move and feel a bell in a sill, a floor and a wall, and cannot carry a note. She is public in Kells by her own act and by nobody else's. NO AGE IS ON THE PAGE AND NONE MAY BE SUPPLIED.** She is not rescued, nobody holds anything over her, no chapter has her discovered she has been exposed, **and NOBODY ASKS HER FOR A REASON AT ANY HOUR IN CHAPTERS 121 TO 130 — including the man she is travelling with, in the chapter where he has the strongest reason in a hundred and sixteen days to ask her for something, and does not, and writes down once that he did not, in his own book and not in hers, and then writes that he is not going to write it down again.** **Her hearing is the subject of no sentence in this batch except one line on the hundred and fifteenth, in which she is talking about the price of a wall.**

**SHE DID NOT RING IT IN THIS BATCH.** She rang it once, in Chapter 0106, in a market of about nine hundred people, on her own account, and it is not for him and it did not help him and nobody asked her why. **It is still in her bag. Chapter 0127 is the chapter in which it is nearest her hand and is not taken out: he is holding a thing he cannot put anywhere, she is standing nine feet off his left shoulder with a rope in her hands, and she tells him the price of two of the three instruments and then goes nine feet further off without being asked for the third.** **She may ring it at most once more in the whole volume, on her own terms, and never for him.**

**THE LEASH IS STILL AT EIGHT USES AND THIS BATCH IS THE BATCH THAT NEARLY BROKE IT.** On Day 113 he had the slate under his arm all day with a price side on it and a thing in his head he wanted to say, and he did not put a question on it, and the count is eight. **A question offered on a step and not asked is not a question that was asked, and he wrote the non-use down once and wrote under it that he is not going to write it down again, because she said in her own book on the hundred and second that the third time she writes the not-asking she will be counting, and a woman who counts is a woman waiting to be owed.** A second non-use is on Day 116: he does not tell a man in a market that he is the man that man was talking about, and he does not thank him. **A non-use is not a ninth use and no later batch may count either of these as one.**

**WHAT SHE GAVE HIM IN THIS BATCH, AND SHE WAS NOT ASKED FOR IT AND DID NOT VOLUNTEER IT, AND IT WAS ABOUT A ROPE AND NOT ABOUT HIM.** Everything she has carried for eleven years has been carried in the open and repeated at ten feet. **A thing said twice in a market is heard by the people standing there and cannot be produced afterwards by anybody, and it dies with them, one at a time, over about nine years.** A thing written down is read by every clerk who comes after for forty years and can be produced against you by a stranger you have never met. **He had been refusing the cheap one for nine weeks for a reason she told him on the hundred and second, and on the hundred and fifteenth a court handed him the expensive one in a fair hand in a fold on a rail, and she told him the number — fourpence — at ten feet, and said she was not going to say one word about whether the price was worth it, because it is not worth it, and he knew that, and he has known it for thirteen days, and it moves nothing, and he had put his own name at the head of it anyway.** Then she went and stood about nine feet further off, which is what she does.

**HER FINDING, unimproved, and it is the batch's: a thing in a head is the third instrument and there is no instrument for it in this kingdom.** It is the only one of the three that cannot be got from him by anybody who wants it, it cannot be given to anybody, and it dies with him. **It is not a trade, there is no craft in it, there is no clause about it, and it is worth exactly nothing to anybody else including the man holding it.** She is not thanked and is not going to be thanked, and the best thing he could do for her on the hundred and thirteenth was exactly what he did on the eighty-third: nothing, on purpose, and to write down once that he did it.

**HER FOUR COLUMNS STAND AND NOTHING IS MERGED. The fourth has two lines in it and she has not improved either and is not going to.** The price side of the slate she carries is on the other face and it is the other way up. **Nobody is asking her for a place in anything. Her answer in a clerk's hall in Miremarket is untouched, and no chapter of this batch has anybody learn that it is there, and nobody may take it out of it, rescue her from it, hold it over her, ask her for a reason about it, or make her a spectacle.**

## The new people of Volume 03 Batch 0003 — all on the page with NO age

**A MAN'S YEARS IN A TRADE ARE A DURATION AND ARE NOT AN AGE. Every figure in this section that is a number of years is years at a job, and none of it may be converted into an age or restated as one.**

- **Corvin Hale**, a clerk of the district court of Netherby as it sits in Sennet, **about four years** in that room, a desk with a lamp on it, a tray of about nine folded forms, four or five volumes on a rail, and four boards screwed to a wall with about nine hundred matters on them. **Right, not sorry, not a door, not owed anything, and he says so.** He told a stranger two things unasked across three days: **that anybody may read the record of any matter in that room at any hour it is open, and that a record is the court's and not a copy of anything, and that the court does not give copies of a record of a matter to a person who is not a party to it — and then he said, in the right order, that he was not being careful, and that a thing said into a column in a room belongs to the room while the same thing said at a rail belongs to the man who said it.** He also said that a form is one shape cut by one plate in a printer's room two streets off, about nine hundred a year, and that the man who cuts it has never been asked whether it is the right shape; that **a form that has been argued with has a line through it, and a box that is simply empty was not argued with and was not forgotten, because a man who forgets a box fills it in later and does it with a different pen**; and that **a sworn answer is an answer and a district is required to send an answer round, and the rule is older than his room and has no face on it and no day on it.** He will not say who opens a file, and said so in that order, and then said it anyway and asked to go back to his lamp. **He is right and he is not a door.**
- **A woman with a tray of pins and a hammer**, who works along a public board in a market of about nine hundred. **She read the top of a sheet about four inches to find the place and put two nails in about four inches apart and straightened the second one with her thumb and went on down the board, and she did not read it and she is not a villain and she is not part of anything.** She is the reason about two hundred people read a true sentence in about nine minutes.
- **A man sweeping a corridor** who says good evening to about nine people including him. Not named, not asked, not thanked.
- **A woman with a tray of her own** in the corridor of the courts; **two men who came in at about the fifth hour and stood at the far end of a rail for about four minutes and left**; **a man who asked Hale for something in a low voice and got it**; and **the two men who come into that room over the course of a day in Chapter 0125.** None named, none asked, none thanked, no age.

## Relationship and civic movement at the end of the batch

- **Tomas and Tamsin:** a leash at **eight uses, held**, with a non-use on Day 113 in the chapter where he had the strongest reason in a hundred and sixteen days to spend it, and a second non-use on Day 116. **She said fourpence at ten feet and told him she was not going to say one word about whether it was worth it, and then she went and stood nine feet further off, which is what she does.** He did not ask her for a reason, or for a place in the three, or for anything. **Nobody in a city of nine thousand people has asked her for anything in twenty-one days, and no chapter of this batch has her discovered she has been exposed, and nothing in this batch is a confession scene or a rescue or anything either of them would call that.** Neither of them says what the road is for this time.
- **Tomas and his sister:** a leaf he did not take, a building he did not enter, a store list he read and left, and now **a standing instruction in her own name on a public board that tells him where she is and asks him for nothing, and four lines of it that are about him and that she did not ask him about.** He has never once been able to think of her as a person who puts a true thing in a public place and is not thanked for it, because he has been thinking of her as a thing he lost. **A thing he lost is not a person who publishes an instruction.**
- **Tomas and the Quiet Office:** it named him, held a hearing he did not attend, and wrote four hundred words about the shape of it. **In this batch it was proved, by a form, that the Office kept a duty and cut a sentence, and it was proved by a question that took four seconds and a plate in a printer's room two streets off.** It can make an answer for a person in about four seconds out of a column. It cannot stop three people answering for themselves. **Neither of those is a victory, and this batch added a third: it can put a name on a board in a market without doing anything wrong, and there is nobody in this kingdom to be angry with about that, and he went and paid for it himself.**
- **Tomas and the people who are right:** seventeen now, in six batches and four counties and a city, **none of whom is a door, none of whom wanted anything, and one of whom cost himself about four days and a hundred yards of corridor on a public wall and will never be told it was worth it, on Tomas's account.** In this batch: a storekeeper who refused a question before it was asked, a river-court clerk who said *yes* in four seconds, a district-court clerk who refused a copy correctly in four seconds and then said the rule about a sworn answer out loud, an arch clerk who would not give a ward, and a man with nine rows who taught a stranger the price of a name and was not told.
- **The volume's best civic act in this batch is a woman with a tray of pins and a hammer** who read the top of a sheet to find the place, and **the volume's worst civic act is still his**, and it is now on a board in a market with two nails about four inches apart.
