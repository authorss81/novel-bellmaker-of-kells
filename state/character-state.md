## Tomas Vale

**Thirty-two — his own number is in Sabin Fell's mouth in Volume 01 and it is NOWHERE ELSE AND THE NARRATOR DID NOT SUPPLY IT AT THE CLOSE OF THE VOLUME. On a step by the river on the flat, about a mile out of Sennet, at about the ninth hour of the evening on Day 136, with a barrow, and going on foot. Forty-one days in the city, which he has left — he arrived on Day 95, and Batch 0005 counts it as thirty-two on the hundred and twenty-seventh, thirty-five on the hundred and thirtieth, forty on the hundred and thirty-fifth and forty-one on the hundred and thirty-sixth, **and those are the only four values any chapter or state file may use for it.** A hearing thirty-three days behind him that he could not answer and did not attend — **it is Day 103 at the second hour and nothing may move it** — and A WARRANT THAT STANDS. He came up the road from Miremarket on the eighty-seventh and arrived on the ninety-fifth. He was in Miremarket twenty-nine days and left it, and **nothing in Chapters 101 to 150 puts anybody back in that town.**

**What he has, and it is the same list he wrote out flat under a hedge in the rain on Day 86, and it has not grown by one document in thirty days:** about nine names that cannot be certified; four columns and a slate with the price side the other way up; a fragment of nine lines, one hand, a column of district numbers, a column of days, and a third column empty from end to end; a receipt in five clauses, all five standing; a frame in a reinforced padded cradle with four bolts made fast and no clapper, **which has not been in a building since the thirty-third and which all ten chapters of this batch say so in their own day-books**; five receipts; a bag with a bell in it nobody can certify; and a woman ten feet off his left shoulder. **He has no standing in any county, no room, and no place.**

**And, in thirty days, none of it a document and none of it a stage:** a berth on a net-loft and a man's name; a court's public board and the reason it exists; a store with a counter, a man, a shelf, a number, and a column with nothing in it, **and the sentence that the wall behind the man is shut and the book in front of him is open and both of those are correct**; nine sheets read and left; a man who did a chore for himself in his own name on a public wall; three people in three yards who said one true thing each; **a number that goes to a district he has never been in**; **the difference between a record and a file, which is the whole of the coercion and is in a form and not in a room**; **about nine hundred matters a year on four boards in the same five columns with the same empty fifth**; **a form with an empty fourth box and one plate cutting about nine hundred a year**; **eleven lines in a dead woman's own words and no sentence at all**; **three instruments and no fourth**; **a true sentence of his own on a public wall for fourpence and a copy of it in a market**; and **a standing instruction with his sister's name at the foot of it.**

**What he lost, and none of it refunded:** a room, a bench and five sheets; a carbon that was the only copy of its form outside the building that holds the original; the use for being right; the belief that carrying a room was a right; **the belief that being right about a thing is worth something, which he had since he was nine and unlearned in a store on the hundred and seventh**; **and the only sentence anybody had ever said about him, because he went and made a second one and it is in a place he did not choose.**

**THE BELL HE DID NOT RING, and this is the fourth batch running.** He rang nothing in a hundred and twenty-six days and the plain carrier is in its sack against the barrow, **forty-nine days untouched, the interval counted in days from the seventy-seventh and not in chapter numbers**. The Crown carrier cannot be rung and he did not furnish a clapper and did not go near it. His own plain carrier is in a sealed yard in Kells and he is not going to it. **He rings no bell he has made anywhere in this volume, and no bell he has ever made is unbarred anywhere in this volume before the climax.** **HE HAS PUT HIS HAND FLAT ON THE CROWN OF THAT CARRIER THREE TIMES IN A HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIVE DAYS — the hundred and sixth, the hundred and twenty-second and the hundred and twenty-fifth — AND HE WRITES DOWN IN HIS OWN BOOK THAT IT IS THE SAME ACT THREE TIMES AND A DIFFERENT REASON EACH TIME AND THAT A MAN WHO HAS DONE IT THREE TIMES IS A MAN THINKING ABOUT IT, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO WRITE DOWN WHAT ABOUT.** He does not know where either of his two public ringings went and is not going to be told, and **this batch did not let him find out as a consolation and did not come near it.**

**THE SENTENCE THE NEXT VOLUME OPENS ON, in his own hand on Day 106 and in his own hand again on the last page of Chapter 0130, unprompted, having done nothing about it, and it is a rule about counting and not a power:** ***A man can be one of the three answers. A man cannot be two of them, and the second one he is, the first one stops being an answer and becomes a man saying the same thing twice. And there is no form in this kingdom on which a man may take the same words to three men and call the result a count. If there were three of me.*** **He has not counted anything into iron since the fortieth day of this month, and the interval is a hundred and sixteen days at the last chapter of this batch.**

**HIS BEST ACT IN THIRTY-ONE DAYS, and he refuses to have it, and it is now five refusals and one hundred and nineteen days and they are one decision with more names than he has found:** he did not take either leaf off either board, he did not go into the upper room, he did not take the store list, he did not ask what the thing is for, **he did not ask a clerk of the district court whether a form with a third box filled wanted a reason, and he did not ask a single person in that building what became of his mother, and he did not ask the woman travelling with him for anything at all.** **And the cost of the fifth refusal is real: the only proof he will ever have is eleven lines long and he cannot copy it, cannot carry it, cannot give it to anybody and cannot make it into metal.**

**HIS WORST HOUR, and there are now two, and the second is worse.** The first was Day 106, when he asked a man for a true sentence on purpose, got one, and wrote down that the asking was the whole of the damage. **The second is the hundred and fifteenth, and it is not a bad act at all, and that is what makes it the worst hour: he went through a door he had stood in for seventeen days, and he put four true sentences about himself into a document in his own name for fourpence, and nobody asked him for a reason, and he noticed that nobody asked him, and he wrote down that he noticed, and he did it on purpose, for a thing about himself, and he has not found out what it is for and is not going to be told.** **He paid a price on purpose. A court clerk wrote down a true thing and it is on a public wall for forty years and about nine people will say his name in four towns and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom by which a man who has been named on a board can be unnamed, and he stood four feet off a man in a market on the hundred and sixteenth and did not tell him, and then he went and stood nine feet further off, which is a thing he learned from a woman and had never done on purpose until that minute.**

**POWER: none, and no stage, and no sense, and no reaching.** He attempts no perception in any of the ten chapters and is not described as reaching for one. He does not bind anything, does not learn a second perception, does not identify a copied answer, and **does not learn where a sound went and is not described as reaching for it.** The nine words on the flyleaf at the back of the day-book, Day 62, are a comparison and not a power and are not written as one. **What looks like a perception in this batch is reading: he read about nine hundred lines standing up and found a column filled on every line except nine and counted them twice, and he read four words upside down at nine feet off a clerk's tray, and comparing forms is the whole of his trade and it is the first time in four counties and a city that the trade has been the instrument. IT IS CRAFT LITERACY AND NOT A SENSE AND NO CHAPTER MAY WRITE IT AS ONE.** No bell he has ever made may be unbarred, and the only power change this volume permits happens in the climax, at a cost, and it is not stronger.

**HIS THIRD QUESTION, and the answer in the shape it now has: it is not a room and it is not a name, it is a set of tables.** A plate in a printer's room two streets off cutting about nine hundred forms a year; a four-inch box at the end of every one of them, full on nine hundred and empty on one; a rule that a sworn answer is a thing about a person and must be sent round; an open book in a store and a shut wall; a court list of nine hundred matters a year in the same five columns with the same empty fifth; and about eleven thousand lines a year that nobody is unrolling. **A hundred and sixteen days of learning to ask a person and being refused, and at the end of it a city of about nine hundred doors and every one of them is a man, and the only thing that has actually happened to him in sixteen days is that a rule put his name on a board.**

SHE ALSO PUTS HIS OWN FOUR INCHES OF HIS OWN WORDS BACK TO HIM AT TEN FEET IN THE ORDER HE SAID THEM, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE HUNDRED PEOPLE, AND DOES NOT EXPLAIN IT AND IS NOT THANKED, AND THEN STANDS ABOUT NINE FEET FURTHER OFF. AND HER OWN FINDING, WHICH SHE REFUSES TO CALL ANYTHING: THERE ARE TWO PEOPLE IN THIS KINGDOM HOLDING SOMETHING THAT CANNOT BE PRODUCED, AND IT IS NOT A PARTNERSHIP AND IT IS NOT A FRIENDSHIP AND SHE IS NOT GOING TO PUT A NAME ON IT, BECAUSE A NAME IS A THING THAT CAN BE ASKED ABOUT.** It is the only one of the three that cannot be got from him by anybody who wants it, it cannot be given to anybody, and it dies with him. **It is not a trade, there is no craft in it, there is no clause about it, and it is worth exactly nothing to anybody else including the man holding it.** She is not thanked and is not going to be thanked, and the best thing he could do for her on the hundred and thirteenth was exactly what he did on the eighty-third: nothing, on purpose, and to write down once that he did it.

**HER FOUR COLUMNS STAND AND NOTHING IS MERGED. Her answer in a clerk's hall in Miremarket is untouched, and no chapter of this batch has anybody learn that it is there, and nobody may take it out of it, rescue her from it, hold it over her, ask her for a reason about it, or make her a spectacle.**

## Relationship and civic movement at the close of Volume 03

- **Tomas and the Office: the last movement, and it is the volume's.** It copied a sound and made a warrant out of it, lawfully, in about nine minutes, from a form with four boxes and one of them a day. **It is right about the procedure and wrong about the standing, and he has no standing to be wrong about, and that asymmetry is the whole of a hundred and thirty days.** It can make an answer for a person in about four seconds out of a column. **It cannot keep him: there is no form in this kingdom that lets a district keep a man, and he walked out of the middle of the city with a barrow, and nobody opened that hole for him and he is not going to thank anybody for it.** Neither of those is a victory and the chapter says so on the page.
- **Tomas and Tamsin: a leash at eight uses, held, with a non-use in Chapter 0143 that he does not write down; and a sentence in Chapter 0146 that is the volume's last relationship fact.** *You take it or I take it, and I have already decided, and I am telling you after.* **He was given the choice and he was not asked, and he is not the person who gets to make it, and she took the worst of it in her hearing and nothing is restored, and nobody asked her for a reason, including him, and that is four inches long and he is not going to describe how hard it was because a man who describes how hard it was for him is asking to be thanked for not asking.** Her bell is in her bag. Her four columns stand, the fourth has two lines, the price side is the other way up, and **on Day 134 four lines in her own hand went into the apron of a woman who lets a room by the hour and he did not ask what they said and does not have them and is not going to.** Neither of them says what the road is for this time.
- **Tomas and Mara: three payments and no resolution.** A leaf he did not take, a building he did not enter, a standing instruction he did not go under, a row he did not read, a board he did not come back to, **and a room he was held in on Day 133 in a building she was inside and did not come to.** She chose a record over a man and he is not thanked and does not thank her and does not know what she did and is not going to find out, **and it is not a reconciliation and nothing between them is resolved and she is not a thing he can summon.** A district's paper on the top terrace has a woman's name at the head of it and he still has not decided and is not going to.
- **Tomas and the bell he did not ring, the volume's standing refusal, which is now also a thing he did.** **A claim in metal cannot be edited, and the only instrument in this kingdom that could carry a true sentence out of him in one evening and put it in front of every door in this city is a sound, and a sound in a city of about nine thousand people is the hour.** He struck a bell he did not make, once, in a yard, in the ordinary way, on the evening of the hundred and thirtieth, and about nine minutes later a form four floors up had a day and a number and a person in it, **and he wanted to put four sentences in that bell and could not, and the word in that bell was the hour, and afterwards there is no form in this kingdom that can tell which of the two a man had done.** He rang nothing else. No bell he has ever made is unbarred.
- **Tomas and the people who are right: twenty-three now, in five batches, four counties and a city, none of whom is a door, none of whom wanted anything, and one of whom cost himself about four days and a hundred yards of corridor on a public wall and will never be told it was worth it, on Tomas's account.** From this batch: a district round who said there is nothing on this to detain anybody with; a storekeeper who told him he would like him to leave; a clerk who said a person who has been entered in a column once does not get entered twice for nothing; an arch clerk who said a notice comes off a board when it is done; a man stacking sacks who said he has not asked and is not going to; a clerk who strikes four times on somebody else's paper and does not read it; a man in a chair four days' road away who read a charge and would not be told what it means and told him so; and a woman who shuts a window with four lines in her apron and shut it.
- **The volume's best civic act is a woman inside a building who chose a record over a man, on her own account, unasked, and was not met and was not thanked, and the man it was about is not going to find out what she did.** **The volume's worst civic act is still his, and it is a sound in a yard, and it is the best thing he has ever done, and both of those are true at once and he writes them down in that order.**


- **Tomas and the Office: it copied him, and it copied a man, and neither of them can say so.** A true sentence on a wall went round in a cart, a man in a market used it as a precedent, and he is in a room with a form in it on a capacity the office gave him. **The instrument was four inches at ten feet and it belonged to somebody Tomas will never identify, and there is no field on any form for who said a thing to a man in a market.** The Office is right at every step and nobody is angry.
- **Tomas and the three of them: a negotiation, not a reunion, and he is not the clerk of it.** Three sets of terms, two people in a room he paid for, no paper, no plate, no copy, nobody thanked, and one of the three not in the room. **He has got one of the three answers and not two, and the one is his own, and a no said in advance is not an answer to a claim because there was no claim — three noes and not one agreement.** And the sentence he keeps coming back to and does not act on: *if there were three of me.*
- **Tomas and the bell he did not ring, which is the volume's standing refusal:** a city of about nine thousand bells and not one of them is a keeper's bell, and every one of them is the hour, **and a man who adds a tenth and expects a different ear is a man who has not listened to a yard.** The instrument that would carry a true thing out of him in one evening is the one he has been refusing since before he left Kells **and it is the wrong shape for it, and both of those are true at once.**

- **Tomas and Tamsin:** a leash at **eight uses, held**, with a non-use on Day 113 in the chapter where he had the strongest reason in a hundred and sixteen days to spend it, and a second non-use on Day 116. **She said fourpence at ten feet and told him she was not going to say one word about whether it was worth it, and then she went and stood nine feet further off, which is what she does.** He did not ask her for a reason, or for a place in the three, or for anything. **Nobody in a city of nine thousand people has asked her for anything in twenty-one days, and no chapter of this batch has her discovered she has been exposed, and nothing in this batch is a confession scene or a rescue or anything either of them would call that.** Neither of them says what the road is for this time.
- **Tomas and his sister:** a leaf he did not take, a building he did not enter, a store list he read and left, and now **a standing instruction in her own name on a public board that tells him where she is and asks him for nothing, and four lines of it that are about him and that she did not ask him about.** He has never once been able to think of her as a person who puts a true thing in a public place and is not thanked for it, because he has been thinking of her as a thing he lost. **A thing he lost is not a person who publishes an instruction.**
- **Tomas and the Quiet Office:** it named him, held a hearing he did not attend, and wrote four hundred words about the shape of it. **In this batch it was proved, by a form, that the Office kept a duty and cut a sentence, and it was proved by a question that took four seconds and a plate in a printer's room two streets off.** It can make an answer for a person in about four seconds out of a column. It cannot stop three people answering for themselves. **Neither of those is a victory, and this batch added a third: it can put a name on a board in a market without doing anything wrong, and there is nobody in this kingdom to be angry with about that, and he went and paid for it himself.**
- **Tomas and the people who are right:** seventeen now, in six batches and four counties and a city, **none of whom is a door, none of whom wanted anything, and one of whom cost himself about four days and a hundred yards of corridor on a public wall and will never be told it was worth it, on Tomas's account.** In this batch: a storekeeper who refused a question before it was asked, a river-court clerk who said *yes* in four seconds, a district-court clerk who refused a copy correctly in four seconds and then said the rule about a sworn answer out loud, an arch clerk who would not give a ward, and a man with nine rows who taught a stranger the price of a name and was not told.
- **The volume's best civic act in this batch is a woman with a tray of pins and a hammer** who read the top of a sheet to find the place, and **the volume's worst civic act is still his**, and it is now on a board in a market with two nails about four inches apart.
