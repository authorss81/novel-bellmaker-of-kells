# Character State After Batch 0005 (Volume 01 complete)

## Tomas Vale

Tomas is 32, the third-generation bellmaker of the Vale Bellhouse, and **at the end of Volume 01 he is a traveling man with a bell in a cradle he cannot ring.**

**Still at the first edge of Stage 1 (Near Toll). He gained nothing in this batch. He does not reach for a perception in any chapter of Chapters 41–50 and is not described as reaching for one. The one sense event in the batch is the single sound he hears off the river in the dark at the end of Chapter 44, and the chapter makes it ordinary hearing on an ordinary night: not loud, not a bell, it stops, nobody is near enough to have heard it, he cannot say what it was and he tells nobody. He reads no claim and no strained answer in it.** In Chapter 46, in an open Measure room, he says the sentence of the volume: **he does not know what a bell is for**, and that he has learned a great deal about paper in twenty-two days and nothing at all about bells. **No permanent memory loss in this volume, no deep read, no archive impression, no forced-echo detection.**

**What he did in this batch.** He handed his own page to somebody else and kept nothing, and found he was not lighter. He was asked four times about the market lane and refused four times, and on the fifth ask said the same thing in a lane and would not improve it. He asked a woman for something in writing, in front of a witness, refusable in advance, and did not press, and was refused. He opened the floor of his own shop in front of the ward, in daylight, on his own initiative, and entered the cost before the act. He withdrew his water claim and burned it, and rang a **barred** bell in a public place with a count of **one keeper**, and asked for the bar to be written beside the hour. He stood in his own yard while the only local record of the Hush burned and **did not ring and did not ask**, and wrote the reason down: *a bell cannot stop a fire, and a bellmaker who thinks it can has stopped being a maker.* He certified his own words as the Crown's evidence against himself. He was arrested and asked for one thing, which was to see his custody receipt, and read his own margin aloud in a lane. He put the Measure's road map on the record in a room he had been asked not to be in, and was right to and was told by a sixty-one-year-old man that being in a hurry felt like duty and was not. He added a fifth clause to a custody receipt. He left before dawn with an object he did not make.

**What he owns at the end of the volume:** the fixed stripped Crown survey carrier in its frame and cradle; a custody receipt in five clauses; a route fragment; a set of day-book pages; a copy of the Measure's road-map correction; **and two questions he cannot answer.**

**What he has lost:** his shop (sealed, with a district wax and the watch mark under it), his archive (destroyed in his own yard in daylight), his stock (the half-barrel has been empty since the nineteenth), his licence in a workshop, the only bell he has ever made (barred by the Measure and left in the sealed yard), the right to speak for a household in Kells, and **the right to ring anything in this county** except, on the morning of the 28th, once.

## Tamsin Rook

**Her age is not on the page and must not be supplied.** **She rang nothing from the 13th to the 28th, and rang twice in this batch, once each, both times under her own terms and neither for him.**

**Day 24, at the low drain gate.** She told Tomas the market lane out loud: that she had felt it come up through four feet of stone and had heard the strike and **could not tell whether he rang it once or four times**, because the note is the part that hangs on and the strike is the part that does not, and she has been carrying a bell for eleven years on the part that hangs on. She worked out the shared shape of the two facts — a signal that goes further than its keeper can govern, and a keeper who cannot hear where it went — and said the sentence neither of them can act on: *two people who are extremely good at this cannot do the work for each other and have to go on.* She took his written request, did not read the other side of the fold, told him she had decided inside forty minutes, and did not tell him for nine days.

**Day 28, before light.** **Foreman Crane came to her at a quarter past five and asked her, in his own words, and named what he wanted repeated, which was *come to this door*.** She walked the authorized route from the north gate to the low drain gate with the bell in her hand and not in her bag, and rang it **once**, at the landing door, and stood at the door while he said his own words in his own voice to about fourteen people, and **did not repeat it and did not ring again and did not ring past the low drain gate**, because the route ends there and the flood bell takes up the local signal. **She could not hear it, did not ask four people afterwards what they thought had happened, and said in nine words that this was the first honest piece of courier work she had done in a month and that she had nobody to thank for it.** Her rule about Crane is now the ward's most useful sentence: *I am shutting the stage gate and I have the key in my pocket, and I am not telling you what to do and I have no more idea than you do, and I am not saying run.*

**Day 33.** She gave back the request with one line on the outside of the fold — **no, no reason, and you did not press me once** — and told him she had found out how it feels to be on that side of it. She unwrapped her own bell at the custody review, set it on the cloth, and had it read from a distance. She travels with him on a slate **written by Mercy Slee and by neither of them**, which puts a leash on both of them and keeps her four terms intact. She says out loud in a lane: **a warning does not require her to obey him.** On the river road she rang the bell **once** and waited and got an answer from a village of about two hundred that has not heard the Crown's order, and **did not ring again and did not repeat it**.

**Her last ringing on a public road before the twenty-eighth was the fourth day of the month.** She rang once on the twenty-eighth, at the landing door, on Foreman Crane's own request, and once on the river road on the thirty-third. **No chapter may say she has not rung in a public road since the fourth day; Chapter 50 once did and has been repaired, because the twenty-eighth is in three books and a market court.**

**Her deafness is permanent and public by her own act.** The lost note went out of her left ear between the eighth hour of the sixth day and the second hour of the ninth of this month. **She can hear a strike, feel a bell in stone, and see a bell move, and she cannot carry, and she is partly deaf for the rest of her life.** Nobody in this volume asks her for a reason at any hour after the twenty-eighth, nobody holds it over her, and nothing has been rescued for her. **Her four terms of the thirteenth day stand and have not been varied by anyone.**

## Oren Pike

**Oren is a Crown-appointed watch captain whose age is not on the page. He signed the last official act of eleven years in the chair and he is still in the chair.**

**He refused twice and paid for it.** The second refusal (Day 24) carries a second reason in his own hand: that he knows who sounded the four bells in the market lane on the nineteenth, has not said it in any book, and enters that fact so that he will not hold a private file. **He names nobody.** He refused the district's bound-claim question on the 27th in the lane in front of a Crown surveyor, and the surveyor refused to certify the refusal. He entered the whole of his own arithmetic — the eleven names, his own conduct of the tenth, his refusal to lie in a return — so that the district would find it on a page and not in a story he assembled himself.

**He did the arithmetic that matters.** He worked out that the four documents of the fortnight all end in the word *names* and that he supplied every one of them, and said out loud that he had spent nine days teaching a town to be legible and had built a town out of names without entering a ward. He said *I am not relieved* when a name came into a district return after eleven years of a desk.

**He lost six of his own list.** The six people arrested on the twenty-eighth were all six on the list he sent on the seventeenth under a heading the district wrote for itself, and he wrote the two-paragraph statement of his life that he has never taken out and will not explain away. **And he found out that the Crown's schedule with a spade in it came out of his own gate book**, which he enclosed as a routine return on the seventeenth because he was proud of the paragraph.

**He did two things that were right and cost him.** He granted Crake's application for the millrace bridge bell in the lane before about thirty people, with Jerm named and a limit, and entered his reason: that a watch which tells the Crown it has no instrument is handing the Crown a reason to invent one. And he wrote the record of each of the six arrests in his own hand and gave each person a copy, **because Rudd asked him for it and because a Crown office would otherwise own the only paper any of them has.**

**He put the watch's mark under a district wax on a door he had no power to seal**, in nineteen words, with no opinion in them, and that was the last entry of the twenty-eighth in the gate book. And he released the fixed carrier to Tomas on a receipt whose fourth clause says the man who holds it is not the man who made it and may not say what it means.

## Nell Fenn

**Nell is sixteen and she is the person the ward acts through, and she spent the thing she carried for nineteen days.**

**On the twenty-third** she published a second question — *is there a name in the Witnessed by column on my line; is that person living; whose hand is it in* — knocked it at eleven doors with no slate, and found that the never-cleared column is a **practice** and not one household's bad luck, including on a line the Crown has never visited. She got Mrs Wilde to the board and had to give the terms back to her. She had the word *none* entered against a Crown officer's question about a list of households. **On the twenty-fifth** she read the ward's answer in three columns, argued that an empty column can be counted and a sentence cannot, and got **Mrs Wilde named before she stood up**. She put her own name under a Crown's question and a Crown's reply.

**On the thirtieth she spent the alignment** — the nine *not returned* lines and the six *accompanying* lines, counted on the twelfth day in a lane with a charcoal and told to two people — as a checkable question to the district, and told Mercy Slee she had written the date on the twenty-third for a day after the twenty-eighth. **She put the question on the thirty-first and the district replied on the thirty-second**, in two paragraphs, **naming her in a district file as a person of sixteen of the household of line twenty-two and saying the office does not propose to ask why.** She says she cannot account for the spending of it: *I have been carrying a thing for nineteen days and I have got about four hours of use out of it and I do not know what the other fifteen days were for, and I am not going to tell anybody they were discipline.*

**She still has the Fenn fragment and still has not used it, and nothing in this batch reversed that.** She tried for two days to make the Teage documents into one sentence, could not, and left them as two facts with a date against each.

## Sabin Fell

**Sabin is 61, and at the end of this batch he is angry with Tomas and entitled to be.** He was not in the Measure's room, as he asked, and Tomas decided in that room what to do with his objection and did not ask him, and Sabin found out within three hours from a clerk's boy. His sentence is the diagnosis of the protagonist's whole flaw: **I was in a hurry, and being in a hurry felt like duty, and it was not duty, it was the other thing, and the other thing is what I have spent nineteen years trying to get out of you and I have failed.** He asked for the copy of Tomas's correction **not to read** — to put it in his own drawer and to know it exists. He also told him, across forty feet of cobbles, that when they put a spade in his floor he must stand in his own yard, watch every inch, write the hours, hand the writing to somebody else, and **not let it be the fourth thing in a week he has kept in a drawer nobody reads.** **He has still never been told about the *Witnessed by* column and has still not named what he carried past the gate in the Hush.**

## Iven Sore

**Iven Sore is the living master of the Bellwrights' Measure and the name on the withdrawn docket, and he is right, and it is about a bell and not about the licence.**

**On the twenty-sixth he read four clauses of craft law into a room, stated his own limitation before opening, and entered a professional bar on the plain carrier**: origin not certifiable, no certificate can issue, not to be rung in a public place in this county, the deficiency attaching to the bell and not the maker. The four houses wanted the bar on the licence and **he refused it**, on the ground that a stock that could not be traced on the nineteenth could not be traced on the fifteenth. He was one of the four houses who wanted a man who loses a bell and loses a child off the roll, and he says he still is one of them and is right about a different thing. He told Tomas his bell being barred may in two years be worth more protection than a licensed bell and a stock book, and that the two things are not in the same place in his head.

**He has still not said he did not sign the withdrawn service entry** and repeated his Chapter 14 answer word for word at the door. The one thing he can support is a **question**: *did the Crown withdraw a commission the Bellwrights' Measure granted, or did it withdraw a name?* He put it to Tomas in a yard, out loud, and did not write it down, **and the genuine-or-borrowed question is exactly where Chapter 14 left it.**

## Halden Crake

**Crake was right three times out loud and paid for all of them, and on the thirtieth he was relieved of the Kells commission for it.** He filed the two-line disorder form with the district's finding and his own opinion on the same sheet and wrote *I have not been pretending I did not.* He told a full lane on the twenty-first that careful is not the same as effective; he read out the four things he was instructed to produce on the twenty-eighth and **disclosed in public that the third of them had been discharged the day before by a Crown surveyor who was not asked to**; he produced the fifth schedule that destroyed the archive and stood at the yard gate rather than come further in; he refused to take Tomas's custody receipt off him in a full court; and he asked the watch captain for the record of each arrest, in the watch captain's own hand, because a Crown office would otherwise own the only paper any of those people will have. **The reason on the form that removed him is nine words long: that the officer recorded disorder and co-operation on one paper.** He was moved to a works office on the western road, nothing was published, and **no person in Kells knows.**

## Rhea Collis

**Collis is a surveyor of the northern district, she has no perception, and she is not a source of the mechanism. She is its conscience and she has now cost herself four times.** She certified two rounds as rounds and neither set of answers. She put a sentence about what a form is for into her own return unasked. She came into the ward on the twenty-seventh for the ground, with a spade that was not a Crown issue spade, and her two objections on the page are **the authority and the spade, and a signal sounded from a gate bracket that has no duty, no limit and no place where it stops**, which she asks to have recorded before it happens and not after. She refused to certify the watch's refusal of the bound-claim question, in the lane, before the yard door was touched, and said why: *the last thing this county needs is a surveyor putting her seal on the Kells watch's conscience.* **No chapter says she wanted the register or that she would have written *not established*; an earlier version of this file did, and it was not on the page.** She asked the district clerk to let the household countersign the objection at 108 and not the answer, and put that in her return with one sentence: *an objection is a fact about a door and an answer is a claim about a person.* The objection is in the margin of the line, so a surveyor's name is on that line and not in the witness column. She refused to certify the Wilde line again in a district building. **On the thirty-third she withdrew the district's finding that the ward's bells are unaccounted for, made no finding against the watch or any person, and compared the courier handbell with the fixed carrier and stopped at a technique.** She is still not the missing bellwright and is not going to be made into the missing anything.

## Relationship and civic movement

Tomas and Tamsin ended the volume on a refusal and a slate: **he asked, she said no, and neither of them pretended the refusal was a tragedy.** The leash is on both of them and Mercy Slee wrote it. The bell she rang on the river road was not for him and nobody asked her for a reason. Tomas ended the volume holding two questions he cannot answer, an object he cannot ring, and a receipt that says he may not speak for anybody. The ward ended it with a pinned schedule, a pinned district reply, a Crown notice about a sealed shop, four columns, a witness column nobody can clear, and fifteen lines that a real office in a real town has promised to answer and that no household has yet asked about.
