## Tomas Vale

**Thirty-two — his own number is in Sabin Fell's mouth in Volume 01 and is nowhere else. In Sennet on Day 106, on a step at the foot of a stair on the top terrace of a city of about nine thousand people, at about the ninth hour of the evening, with a barrow. Eleven days in the city. A hearing three days behind him that he could not answer and did not attend.** He came up the road from Miremarket on the eighty-seventh and arrived on the ninety-fifth. He was in Miremarket twenty-nine days and left it, and **nothing in Chapters 101 to 120 puts anybody back in that town.**

**What he has, and it is the same list he wrote out flat under a hedge in the rain on Day 86, and it has not grown by one document in twenty days:** about nine names that cannot be certified; four columns and a slate with the price side the other way up; a fragment of nine lines out of a book of numbers; a receipt in five clauses, all five standing; a frame in a reinforced padded cradle with four bolts made fast and no clapper, **which has not been in a building since the thirty-third and which all ten chapters of this batch say so in their own day-books**; five receipts; a bag with a bell in it nobody can certify; and a woman ten feet off his left shoulder. **He has no standing in any county, no room, and no place.**

**And, in twenty days, six things that are not documents and are not a stage:** **a berth on a net-loft and a man's name; a court's public board and the reason it exists; a store with a counter, a man, a shelf, a number, and a column with nothing in it; nine sheets read and left; a man who did a chore for himself in his own name on a public wall; and three people in three yards who said one true thing each.** Plus **a printed slip and an unfilled form that are the Office's**, a **store number he can recite**, a **nine-day count he made once and checked twice**, and **the only document about Tomas Vale in the world, which is four hundred words in the office's hand that say the person named produced nothing.**

**What he lost, and none of it refunded:** a room, a bench and five sheets; a carbon that was the only copy of its form outside the building that holds the original; the use for being right; the belief that carrying a room was a right. **And he has added two things to that list and chose both himself: there is nobody in this kingdom he can be angry at, and a schedule does not have a face, and that is not a comfort; and a sheet of his own hand with four lines on it and a fifth struck out, and he has never in his life handed a thing to another maker to finish.**

**What he gained in ten days, none of it a stage and none of it a power and none of it a sense:** that **a city of about nine thousand bells is about the hour and not about the bells, and a man in a city of nine thousand comes out into his own door at the hour where a man in a town of two thousand walks into a market**; that **a bell is a man standing next to it, and he disbelieved that for two days and was wrong to, and the reason is a wire**; that **a bell you can blame and a wire you cannot, and a man who mends his own wire makes a fault he is asked about later, and he would rather lose nine days than own a mend**; that **everything he has been afraid of is a wire and a column and a habit and none of those three is a person, which is the relief and is not a comfort, because a thing with no person in it cannot be answered and he has come four counties and seventy miles to answer things**; that **a counter takes papers and not people, and there is no box on the form for a question, and the box for a reason wants a person with a standing in a district**; that **a court publishes and an office files and the difference is four inches long and nobody has ever been told it is a choice, and there is no box anywhere that says who may read a thing**; that **a store is a room, a counter, a book, a man, a shelf and an instruction, and none of the six does anything in front of a person, and about nine hundred things a year go out of that room and four come back**; that **a column headed what a thing is for has been empty for nine years because a man will not write down a word he cannot read back, and the same virtue in a village four counties back is a column he wanted to rule and did not, and a column is a thing two men say the same thing about and never find out**; that **a hearing is a room where one person's answer comes out of a column as the answer of the whole matter, entered in about four seconds by a man doing his job, and the machinery is the same in a town of two thousand and in a capital, and the room is not the point**; that **a claimant is summoned to produce and a witness is not, and that is not a difference of truth**; that **the best sentence anybody has written for him is a wall and a door in one sheet and it stopped his own mouth in the room he walked four hundred miles to reach**; and that **a chore is a thing a person does for himself, in his own yard, on his own day, and a district is not a person, and it is not scalable, it is not a network, it cannot be done twice by one man and it cannot be done at all by nine hundred people, and it is the only thing in this kingdom that has ever moved a line.**

**THE BELL HE DID NOT RING, and this is now the second batch running.** He rang nothing in a hundred and six days, **and the plain carrier is taken out of the sack and unrolled on a step at about the eighth hour of Day 106 with his hand flat on the crown and then put back unstruck.** The Crown carrier cannot be rung and he did not furnish a clapper and did not go near it. The certified plain carrier is untouched since the seventy-seventh, **twenty-nine days**, and he does not know where either of his two public ringings went and is not going to be told. **He rings no bell he has made anywhere in this volume, and his own plain carrier is in a sealed yard in Kells and he is not going to it.**

**THE SENTENCE THE NEXT VOLUME OPENS ON, in his own hand on Day 106, and it is a rule about counting and not a power:** ***A man can be one of the three answers. A man cannot be two of them, and the second one he is, the first one stops being an answer and becomes a man saying the same thing twice. And there is no form in this kingdom on which a man may take the same words to three men and call the result a count. If there were three of me.*** **He has not counted anything into iron since the fortieth day of this month, seventy-three days.**

**HIS BEST ACT IN TWENTY DAYS, and he refuses to have it: he did not take either leaf off either board, and he did not go into the upper room, and he did not take the store list, and he did not ask what the thing is for.** Four refusals, all wanted, all on the page, all with a reason. **And the fourth one cost him: on Day 103 he struck out the only one of five lines that anybody in that room would have wanted, and the clause that stopped him is a clause he asked a clerk to write because he was right, and on Day 106 he wrote down that the clause will do a fifth job inside a month and it will not be his.**

**HIS WORST HOUR, and it is a real one and it is not in the book as an achievement: on Day 106 he asked a man for a true sentence, on purpose, knowing what he was doing, and he got one, and it was true, and the asking was the whole of the damage** — because the day a man comes to a rail and asks for a sentence, that sentence has a man in it, and the man is him, and the man with the column will come along later. **He wrote it down in four inches. He did not thank anybody. He wrote down that the two halves of this volume do not meet and that he is not going to be the man who says the second one is the answer, because he already said once this month that a thing he liked was the answer and was wrong about it in a loft with nine tons of grain on the floor.**

**POWER: none, and no stage, and no sense, and no reaching.** He attempts no perception in any of the ten chapters and is not described as reaching for one. He does not bind anything, does not learn a second perception, does not identify a copied answer, and **does not learn where a sound went and is not described as reaching for it.** The nine words on the flyleaf at the back of the day-book, Day 62, are a comparison and not a power and are not written as one. **No bell he has ever made may be unbarred, and the only power change this volume permits happens in the climax, at a cost, and it is not stronger.**

**HIS THIRD QUESTION, and the answer in the shape it now has: *a format cannot be refused, and now it has a rail in a public room attached to it.*** A court publishes, an office files, the difference is four inches long, there is no box anywhere in this kingdom that says who may read a thing, and **the absence of that box is the whole of the coercion and it is in a form.** A hundred and six days of learning to ask a person and being refused, and at the end of it a city of about nine hundred doors and **every one of them is a man.**

## Tamsin Rook

**Partly deaf for the rest of her life, permanently. She can see a bell move and feel a bell in a sill, a floor and a wall, and cannot carry a note. She is public in Kells by her own act and by nobody else's. NO AGE IS ON THE PAGE AND NONE MAY BE SUPPLIED.** She is not rescued, nobody holds anything over her, no chapter has her discovered she has been exposed, **and NOBODY ASKS HER FOR A REASON AT ANY HOUR IN CHAPTERS 111 TO 120** — including a woman at a pitch who offers her to a stranger in front of nine people, and the man she is travelling with, who has not asked her about her ear in twenty days and has written down once that he is not going to write it down again. **Her hearing is the subject of one sentence in ten chapters and she is talking about something else in it.**

**SHE DID NOT RING IT IN THIS BATCH.** She rang it once, in Chapter 0106, in a market of about nine hundred people, on her own account, and it is not for him and it did not help him and nobody asked her why. **It is still in her bag. She may ring it at most once more in the whole volume, on her own terms, and never for him. Chapter 0116 is the chapter in which she came closest to needing it: she writes that she has not rung it since the ninety-second, that nobody asked her why and nobody is going to, and that if anybody asks her in this city she will say there was a man with a box, and that is the true reason, and it is four inches long and it took her four seconds to get.**

**THE LEASH WAS TESTED IN THE OPEN FOR THE FIRST TIME IN THIS VOLUME, AND SHE REFUSED, AND THE REFUSAL IS ON THE PAGE WITH A REASON, AND HE DID NOT ASK AGAIN.** On Day 102, in a market of about nine hundred, in front of about nine people, a woman at a pitch of needles and thread said *you could ask her, she does that* — and he said **no**, out loud, in front of all nine, and gave the reason in about nine seconds: **he has no standing and she has nothing to stand on, and he would be asking her to spend the only thing she has in a room where he would be the only person who heard it, and he has not got a right to do that and he has not got a reason either, and he is not going to make one up at ten feet.** And she said her reason, once, out loud, to nine people and not to him: **because she said it in front of nine people, and a question said in front of nine people is a thing she will be asked again in four counties inside a year by somebody who heard it offered and not answered, and she has had one of those already, and she priced it in a smith's shop on the fifty-first and it took eleven years of work.** **It was not a ninth use and the count stays at EIGHT. A question offered in a market and refused in a market is not a question that was asked.**

**HER FINDING, in her own hand, in two halves, and she did not improve either.** *A box is cheaper than a witness and a witness can refuse* — a form with a box for the person who repeated a thing, about four hundred people a year, none of them wicked. And then, in Chapter 0116, the harder half: **a court will write a true thing down and keep it and it moves nothing. A thing said twice at ten feet in the open moves about four in ten and dies with the people who heard it. She had been doing the expensive one and calling it the strong one, and the difference between them is not the truth, it is who reads it afterwards, and she had had that the wrong way round for eleven years and a man at a pitch corrected it in nine seconds and he did not know he had.** And the part under both: **everything she has ever done needed her in it — her voice, her sentence, her hands, her slate, and a person to hear it, and she chose which person — and there has never been one occasion in eleven years on which a thing she had said came back to her as a thing somebody else had said. Until Day 102, when two people she had never spoken to each put a true sentence about their own business into a document in their own names, in open court, on their own days, and she was nine feet off and was not in the room for either of them.** Her finding about that: **the road has found a place after all and it is a queue, and a queue is not a place a person stands in, it is a place two strangers can walk into at the same time, and neither of them has to be asked, and the only thing they need is to have heard their own sentence come back at them once, in their own words, in the open, in front of nine people who were not the man who said it to them.** **There are two. There are two, and she has their names, and she is not going to write their names in her book and did not say them out loud at any hour.** **It is not a system and it is not a network and it is not going to be a thing she hands to anybody.**

**ON DAY 106 SHE SAID ONE THING TO HIM AND IT WAS HIS OWN SENTENCE.** Four inches, at ten feet, in the ordinary voice, and he did not improve it. *You have got a barrow and a bell and no keeper and no second person, and you have known that since you were nine feet off a door, and you have spent a hundred and six days finding out that the reason you cannot do it is arithmetic and not the Office.* And then: **Then you are going to have to be three men, and there is one of you, and I am not going to be the second, and if anybody in this city asks me which one I am I am going to tell them nothing at all, and you knew every word of that before you asked, because you did not ask.** And he said he did not ask, and she said **you did not ask, and you are not going to.**

**HER FOUR COLUMNS STAND AND NOTHING IS MERGED. The fourth has two lines in it and she has not improved either and is not going to.** The price side of the slate she carries is on the other face and it is the other way up. **She has written down that the third time she writes the non-use in her book she will be counting, and a woman who counts is a woman who is waiting to be owed, and so she has stopped writing it down, and he has stopped writing it down after once.** **Nobody is asking her for a place in anything. Her answer in a clerk's hall in Miremarket is untouched, and no chapter of this batch has anybody learn that it is there, and nobody may take it out of it, rescue her from it, hold it over her, ask her for a reason about it, or make her a spectacle.**

## The new people of Volume 03 Batch 0002 — all on the page with NO age

**A MAN'S YEARS IN A TRADE ARE A DURATION AND ARE NOT AN AGE. Every figure in this section that is a number of years is years at a job, and none of it may be converted into an age or restated as one.**

- **Nevin Holt**, a grain wharf on the flat, **thirty-one years on it**, who let a man with a barrow move nine tons for nothing on two separate mornings and did not thank him and did not remember him and told him so. **Told him about his own four bells on the tenth trip, unasked: the granary gable one is not his and on a shut day it is iron on a frame; the gantry has a rope and a boy and the boy was not there; the net-loft one is why a stranger has a place to sleep and is struck by whoever is on the loft and had not been since the rope was new. And there are about nine he can see and he has counted them about nine times in nine years and has never written it down, and if asked how many bells this city has he would say he does not know, and he would not be lying.** He is the source of **a bell is a man standing next to it**, and of the volume's cheapest line about the Office: **four buildings, about nine hundred rooms, a yard, and a bell over a gate that goes at the sixth hour, and the men go home at the sixth hour, and everything in it is duller than you think it is.** He is right and he is not a door and he is not going to be thanked.
- **Osmund Tarrant**, behind the counter of a store on the top terrace, **nine years** in that room. **Competent, wants nothing, and is not the villain of this volume and did not become one.** Told a stranger the whole of what a store is in about nine seconds without being asked, and then told him that the column headed what the thing is for has been empty every day he has been in the room, was empty before him and is not going to be filled in after him, **and that he was told when he came into the room that the column was for a reason and nobody has told him what the reason is in nine years, and he will not write down a word in a book he keeps for a district that he cannot answer for when somebody stands in front of him and asks.** **Offered him a printed sheet for nothing and watched him refuse it in four seconds without a word of expression; said he was the third person in nine years to refuse one and one of the other two was right. Refused to open the shelf, correctly, and gave the reason, and said the clerk four flights below would not walk a stranger up and he is right and I do not know his name and would not use it if I did. And said the sentence he has gone over in his own head about once a month for nine years: a man is not allowed to be angry at a list of wires, because there is nobody in it, and so the list is always open and the wall is always shut, and both of those are correct, and I have never once been able to make them both correct at the same time.**
- **Coll Salkeld**, a clerk of the river courts, **six years** in that hall, a pen behind his ear. **Gave a stranger the whole of a court in four seconds — a court is not an office, an office files, a court publishes, and the reason a court publishes is that a court is a room people are sent to and a room people are sent to has to be a room they can be seen in** — and the three requirements for swearing, and the absence of a reason-box, unasked. And on Day 104, walking, in about nine seconds, the whole of what a hearing is; **and then would not say one word more about who was in the room or what was said in it, and said so in the right order: I am not being careful, I would have told a man in a market in a fortnight, a man who says a true thing in a recorded room and then says it to a stranger has said it twice, and the second one is the one that is used.** He is right, he is not a door, he is not sorry, and he will say *I am not going to say* once if he is asked and will not say it again either.
- **Ormond Bly**, nine years keeping a yard of packing cases at the foot of the top stair, with a gate and a bell on it about the size of a soup plate that he strikes himself. **Has been in the upper room twice, once nine years back as a witness, for about an hour and a half, and was asked nothing either time, and has wanted to know what is up those stairs for about a year and has never asked.** Told a stranger, unasked, that he had watched him standing at a door at the second hour and that a woman four hundred yards off had watched it too. **Gave him a true sentence on Day 106 when asked, and then said in four seconds why it was worth nothing: the day a man comes to this rail and asks me for a sentence, that sentence has a man in it, and I am not going to be the first entry in a column.** He is right and he is not a door.
- **Marek Douse**, **six years** at the rail on the top terrace, a book of his own with a page for every day of the six, **no office, paid by the quarter, and not doing it because he was told to.** **On Day 106 the district had sent no sheet, there was nothing on the plate, about forty people who came for the reading stood about a minute and went in again, and he struck the bell four times anyway** — and said it out loud in the open in a yard with about two hundred people in it: **six years on that frame and four strokes and it has meant one thing since the frame was new, there is a sheet on the board, and I do not know whether I am to strike it today, and nobody has told me not to, and there is nobody above me to ask and if there were I should not know what to ask him.** He said it because a man with a barrow was standing in the middle of the yard and had not asked him a single question. **He is right and he is not a door and he is not going to be thanked and he does not know any of this has been written down.**
- **Piet Sarn**, **eleven years** sweeping that yard, with a broom, and never once in the upper room and never wanting to. **Said, to the boards and not to a stranger, that he knows nine hundred rooms by the light going out of one window at about the sixth hour and that that is all he knows, and that he has never said it out loud and has never had the occasion and has just had the occasion.** **He asked for it to go in a place where somebody had written it down and was given a book in a coat and not a copy, and said he could do what he liked with it and it was not about him, and swept on.** No age, no office, no interest, no debt.
- **Ord Marren**, nine rows in a market of about nine hundred on the middle terrace, **nine years**, a book of his own and no office, **and the rent put on those nine rows is the rent put on the wholesale stalls at the end of the row, which he has said out loud at his pitch perhaps a hundred times in nine years and which not one person in this city has ever said back to him, including his landlord, who does not need to.** He put a true sentence about it in a document in his own name, in his own hand, in open court, on Day 102, and had been named on a public board four days earlier and told a stranger what it costs to be named **about somebody else**, in the ordinary voice, in a market, without pointing. **He is right, he is not a door, he does not know a woman said his own sentence back to him, and he is not going to be told.**
- **Wil Brand**, a yard near the water, **eleven years** of a licence, the licence says what a yard may not do, and his yard has been doing the thing the licence says every day for eleven years, **and it is not a trick, it is his whole trade, and he has never once been able to say so in a room with a clerk in it because the moment he says it in a room with a clerk in it the eleven years is a conversation instead of a trade.** He put a true sentence about it in a document in his own name, in open court, on Day 102, on his own day, unasked. **He is not a hero and he is owed nothing and he does not know she said it.**
- **A carter with three horses and a boy on the second cart**, who said **no, not for nothing** to a stranger on Day 97 and was right then and was right again on Day 106 when the same stranger asked him to carry a thing up four flights. **He did not ask what the thing was, and said that not asking was the correct arrangement.** His reason is the volume's own rule in a workingman's mouth: **if I put a thing down in a yard that I was not told to put down and there is no form on which anybody wrote down that I was told, then I have made an entry in somebody's day that only I know about, and the only way that ends is somebody stands in front of me with a reason and asks me about it, and I have three horses and a boy on the second cart and I have not got a son to put in that gap.** Not named, not thanked, not asked anything.
- **A woman at a pitch of needles and thread in a market of about nine hundred, with a voice that carried, who offered a stranger his companion's skill in front of about nine people and was refused by him in front of all nine.** Not named, not thanked, not asked anything, and **she is not a villain and the offer was the ordinary kindness of a market woman and the refusal was clumsy and he is not going to improve either.**
- **A woman who does the writing in a market of about nine hundred**, unchanged from Batch 0001, and a woman who keeps a Crown board, unchanged. Both unthanked, unasked, and neither named.

## Relationship and civic movement at the end of the batch

- **Tomas and Tamsin:** a leash at **eight uses, held**, with a ninth offered in a market in front of nine people and refused in front of the same nine, **with a reason on the page, and he has not asked again.** She did not ring her bell and nobody asked her why. **He put no question on the slate on Day 106 and wrote it down once and then wrote that he is not going to write it down again, because she said in her own book that the third time she writes it she will be counting, and a woman who counts is a woman waiting to be owed.** She said his own sentence back to him, four inches, at ten feet, and told him he is going to have to be three men, **and that she is not going to be the second.** **Nobody in a city of nine thousand people has asked her for anything in twenty days. No binding bell, no confession scene, no rescue, and nothing said in this batch that either of them would call that.** Neither of them says what the road is for this time.
- **Tomas and his sister:** a leaf he did not take, a building he did not enter, a store list he read and left, and **one line on Day 106 in which the only new fact is that he is not going to write to her, because she did not ask him.** He is not a man who can summon her and he has stopped trying to be one.
- **Tomas and the Quiet Office:** it named him, held a hearing he did not attend, and wrote four hundred words about the shape of it. **Its bells are for paper, its coercion is a schedule, and a schedule does not have a face. It can make an answer for a person in about four seconds out of a column. It cannot stop three people answering for themselves. Neither of those is a victory and he is not going to say the second one is the answer.**
- **Tomas and the people who are right:** fourteen of them now, in five batches and four counties and a city, **none of whom is a door, none of whom wanted anything, and one of whom cost himself about four days and a hundred yards of corridor on a public wall and will never be told it was worth it, on Tomas's account.** **And three of them said one true thing in a yard in front of a stranger who had a barrow, and he is not going to be the man who says it was him.**
- **The volume's best civic act, again, is something he cannot attend:** two strangers in a city of about nine thousand put a true sentence about their own business into a document in their own names, unprompted, on their own days, and neither knows a woman with a slate said it back to them once, four inches, at ten feet, and neither is thanked.
- **The volume's worst civic act is his:** he asked a man for a true sentence on purpose, and got one, and the asking was the whole of the damage, and he wrote it down in four inches.
