## Tomas Vale

**A man of thirty-two on a road down off the water on Day 86, in the rain, with a barrow. He came down the water on the thirty-second, arrived in Miremarket on the evening of the fifty-seventh, and leaves on the eighty-sixth: twenty-nine days.**

**What he has, and it is the whole of what six weeks in this county produced, and he wrote it out on the last page in his own hand:** a list of about nine names that cannot be certified and cannot be unrolled; four columns and a slate with the price side the other way up; a witness in a corridor who was not thanked; a fragment of nine lines out of a book of numbers; a custody receipt in five clauses, all five standing; a frame in a cradle with four bolts made fast and no clapper; five receipts; a bag with a bell in it; and a woman beside him. **He has no place and has stopped looking for one and did not decide to stop and did not notice.**

**What he lost and did not get back:** the room over a draper's shop, the bench, five sheets, and the carbon that was the only copy of its form outside the building that holds the original. The use for being right. The belief that carrying a room was a right. The last person in four counties he could be angry at.

**What he gained, none of it a stage and none of it a power and none of it a sense:** that a careful life is a way of writing things down and a document is what a person is read by; **that a claim in metal is a thing a man cannot edit and he has had one and does not know where it went**; that a hall is not a person and never was; that a chore is a thing a man does for himself and a firm is not a person; that a copy of a Crown sheet is Crown property from the moment it is made; that a certificate is a certificate of metal and a note; that a district number is a filing reference and not a man; and that the first of the two questions he came with is answered and the second is not.

**THE RINGING, once, on Day 77, at the top of the hour, in a market of about nine hundred, at a block on the cobbles by the cross, in a town that is not his county. Four sentences, and then about four minutes of silence that he did not improve.** The claim is an apology: what he did, what he could not do, and the names of the people who did the rest, and the known facts, the unknown facts and his own fault, and it tells nobody what to decide. **And the bell did not do what a bell does: the claim went to the end of the market street and stopped, the way a voice stops, and he does not know whether that is the metal, or the claim, or the crowd, and he did not find out and is not going to, and the volume did not let him find it out as a consolation.** He stood by the block for about half an hour afterwards and asked nobody what they had heard.

**He named a clerk in that square on purpose and it cost that clerk about four days of his working life, and he is not going to be told it was worth it, and nobody is going to come and tell him, and if one does he will write down that he said it and the hour.**

**He asked a man with no standing not to write down a thing a man with a family said in a room, and wrote it down anyway, for the third time in ten days, and knows exactly what he is doing, and nobody has apologised and nobody has forgiven anybody.**

**POWER: none, and no stage, and no sense, and no reaching.** He attempts no perception in any of the thirty chapters of this volume and is not described as reaching for one. He does not bind anything, does not learn a second perception, does not identify a copied answer, and **does not learn where a sound went, and is not described as reaching for it, and says in his own hand on Day 77 and again on Day 86 that he never has and does not now.** The nine words on the flyleaf are a comparison and not a power and are not written as one in any chapter of this volume. **No bell he has ever made may be unbarred; his own plain carrier is in a sealed yard in Kells and he did not go near it and says he is not going to.** **He is a man with a bar on his own bell, a receipt with a fifth clause, and a road.**

## Tamsin Rook

**Partly deaf for the rest of her life, permanently. She can see a bell move and feel a bell in a sill, a floor and a wall, and cannot carry a note. She is public in Kells by her own act. She has no age on the page and none may be supplied. She is not rescued, nobody holds anything over her, no chapter has her discovered she has been exposed, and NOBODY ASKS HER FOR A REASON AT ANY HOUR IN ANY CHAPTER OF THIS VOLUME, INCLUDING A MARKET KEEPER, A MERCHANT, A WHEELWRIGHT, A SADDLER, TWO DYERS, AN OFFICER, A CLERK AND A MASTER OF THE MEASURE. Her hearing is discussed in two sentences across the fifty chapters and in both of them she is talking about something else. She rang nothing in Volume 02. Her bell is in her bag in her own country and is hers.**

**Her finding, and it is the finding of the volume's last half, in her own hand, and she did not improve it:** ***the road has finally found something, and what it found is a queue, and a queue is not a place and cannot be looked up, and I have spent eleven years learning to go to places.*** And the figure she will not improve: **a bell in a market takes about nine days to change a town; she has got one and it is seven; and she is not going to be the woman who improves a figure she has one of.**

**What she did in this batch, all on her own account and with nobody asking her:** said four claims back to the people who made them, in their own words, at ten feet, in front of about nine other people, **and the fourth was to a merchant who is owed about a hundred and fifty pounds by four families in a town of two thousand, which is the first time in eleven years she has done it to somebody who is owed money** — and she named the price herself, and it is that four households will hear that a firm cannot show its own answering and will stop answering their own lines, **and she said it anyway and is not going to be thanked for it.** Stood in a market of about nine hundred and did not ask her three questions of a town. Asked one question of one person and got a straight answer in about nine seconds and it went through her like a hand in a bell. **Came to a market with a slate and did not write one word on it, both times, and gave him the slate back blank, and would not explain it.** Stood at the bottom of an outside stair while two people did a thing and did not offer to come up. Stood about nine feet from a door in a lane and did not knock. And in Chapter 100, from ten feet off his left shoulder, in the rain, without stopping, **she said the one sentence that closes the volume, and it is a question, and he answered it in four words and did not improve it in the morning.**

**Her answer is in a clerk's hall in Miremarket, in an auditor's own book, with her name at the head of it and the hour against it, and it has been there since about the fifty-eighth, and he has known since Chapter 96 and has deliberately not asked for it, on the ground that she did not ask him. It is hers. It cannot be taken out of that building by anybody including her. Nobody rescued her, nobody held it over her, nobody asked her for a reason, and nobody made her a spectacle.**

**The leash: eight uses, five worked and three that cost the man who asked, and nobody has been refused a second time.** The eighth is Chapter 97, Day 83, at the bottom of a stair: he asked her to be in a room over a saddler's shop with about eleven men in it, **and she refused in one word and gave the first reason in eleven years that she has ever given for wanting a thing — a room of eleven men who work with their hands is the only kind of room she has never been in, she has wanted to be in one since she was a child, and wanting a thing is not a reason to go and get it.** He did not ask again. **Two non-uses in this batch, Chapter 93 and Chapter 96, and neither is a use of any number.**

**Her four columns stand and nothing is merged and the fourth has one line in it and she has not improved it and is not going to. She has given away two slates in a month and has not asked for either back. The price side of the slate she carries is the other way up and has been since the sixty-first.**

## The new people of Batch 0005 — all without an age, on the page

- **Cress Naylor**, an officer of the district's removals, about eleven years in the district, in a room over a chandler's shop four doors down from a draper's in a town of two thousand people, **and Tomas Vale had been in that town twenty-one days and had not known.** A wife, a boy, and a house on a street in this town. **He cannot produce a document and cannot say what a document is, and a hall is not a person, and that is why a hall cannot be asked anything and only a person in a room can.** He closed a line on the ninth hour of an evening in the winter, correctly, having been told at the door that the man had gone a month before, and did not ask a further question, and is right, and is not sorry, and is not brave, and is not a door, and is not converted. **He said his own part in a disappearance out loud, in a room, in his own name, and asked a stranger not to write it down, and will not say it a second time because if he says it twice then the first time was a performance and it was not one.** The person who went is not named, was not found, and is not going to be looked for.
- **Curtis Ilsley**, an officer of this county, with a form and a satchel and a printed slip and a number, whose work is papers. **He did not ask for the Crown object in the yard and stood four feet off it and asked for the half he did not want to be entered to be entered, and said he would be asked in about four years whether this town was clean and that the half he did not want to is the half that will not be believed.** He took five sheets at about the sixth hour of the eighty-first and receipted them one at a time and told Tomas Vale, unasked, that after the fourth hour there is one copy of that form in the world. He does not know Pell Rill and has his name on a form.
- **Will Preedy**, of a timber yard at the west end of Miremarket, in that yard since he was a boy, who said not one word in a counting house on Day 79 and wrote a new line in his own name and put his own hand against it and did not explain it and was not asked. **A chore for himself. Not a speech. Persuaded by nobody**, and Tomas Vale watched it and could not have stopped any part of it and did not try.
- **Ruth Dall**, one of the six households with a hand in a dead man's yard-book, paid about nine pounds out of a leather bag at about the seventh hour of Day 79, who said *two years* in a counting house and asked for it not to be read out. **She has waited two years for about nine pounds and there is no more of it and there was never going to be any more of it. No age is on the page for her and none may be.**
- **A dyer** who owns the house with two shut rooms above a vat room, who has a key to his own rooms and about four years of a room he could not let and about nine pounds a quarter out of it, **and who said so before anybody asked him, and who went up his own stairs on his own account and took nine lines out of a book that is not his and said he had no right before he was asked, and who would not be thanked twice because he has made his peace with the thirty-six pounds and does not want it unsettled.**
- **The market keeper** of Miremarket, a man with a book of his own and no office and about nine rows, thirty years in that square, who put a barrow's handles round rather than throw a stranger out, who told Tomas Vale to his face what naming a clerk costs the clerk, and who charged fourpence for a yard and told him not to thank him, and who on the last morning put two loaves and a piece of cheese on a barrow and said the price out loud **on purpose** so that nobody would be able to say the stranger had not paid for it.
- **The eleven men in a room over a saddler's shop**, a wheelwright who answered a wrong question in about nine seconds and called it the first sensible thing anybody had said there in four years, a miller, a saddler on the licensed side, and a master of the Measure.

## The people carried forward, and what this volume did to them

Right.
- **Ivo Trant** — **said the fifth clause of a receipt out loud in a street at about the sixth hour of Day 81, from memory, in about nine seconds, because it was the answer to a question that had been asked in a room, and said that it had taken a room off a man and that neither of them had to do anything, and said that the same clause was the reason five sheets went out of that room and that neither of them knew it and that he has been in that building twenty-two years and has never once thought about who copies what.** Correct. - **Bryn Pethick** — **gave the same true sentence a fifth time, in his own hand, with the hour, on the hall's own paper, and Tomas Vale would not take it and gave three reasons, the last of which is a clerk's whole trade: two versions of the same position in the same hand can differ, and the man who carried it was right about a copy of a sheet. ## Relationship and civic movement at the end of the volume

- **Tomas and Tamsin travel on Mercy Slee's slate, as far as he goes, and the road is a road down off the water and not a route with a destination.** Eight uses of the leash, three of them costing him. **They work apart and neither improves the other's half. She wrote a document nobody asked for and kept it. He asked a man for a question and got a page. She has the hand on the line and the practice of saying a claim back; he has a form and a receipt and a witness who told him not to be praised.** The last page of the volume is her shoulder read from behind him and a question asked by the person with the right to ask it, and his answer in four words, and he does not improve it in the morning. **No binding bell, no confession scene, no rescue, and nothing said in this volume that either of them would call that.**
- **Tomas and the six people in this town who are all right and none of whom is a door: Trant, Pethick, Nabb, Vowles, Naylor and Ilsley — all correct, none convertible, and two of them said a true thing out loud in a room, one of them was named in a market square, and one of them read a clause of Tomas's own receipt in a street because it was the answer to a question.** He is not angry at any of them and has not been able to be angry at anything in this town since about the sixty-fifth, and he wrote that down on the seventy-ninth and stopped looking.
- **Tomas and a household at the end of the east lane:** it asked him for the whole of it, in its own words, in a room, and it is the only thing in the volume he could not take with him, and it is in a kitchen in a drawer and it is not going to be asked about.
- **Tomas and the Bellwrights' Measure:** **he is on the wrong side of a correct argument about the bar and he cannot argue the other side without being a man who has no licence to argue either, and the split is not a defeat and nobody in the room is a villain, and the certificate he carried for twenty-one days is a certificate of metal and a note and it is still a real sheet in a master's hand with the hour on it and no copy.**
- **Tomas and the ward:** Kells is behind him and nobody in it is on the road. **The Crown notice about an originating workshop is still nailed to the ward board and has not been taken down.**
