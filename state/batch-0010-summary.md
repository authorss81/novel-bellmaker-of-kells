## The batch in one paragraph

**A bell is rung in a market in a town of about two thousand people and it does not do what a bell does.** A man with a barrow who has no standing to speak for any household in any ward writes five sentences on a sill and strikes out the one that matters, because a household of five refused him twice and because a clause of a receipt he asked a clerk to add to in another county is the reason. **He says four of them out loud in about nine hundred people, and the fourth names a clerk who was right, and the naming costs that clerk about four days of his working life, and a man with a book of his own says so at a trough and it is true. A local official who has never been asked what a document is says his own part in a disappearance out loud in a room, asks a stranger not to write it down, and the stranger writes it down. An inheritance is paid because a man who has been in a yard since he was a boy puts his own hand on his own line for himself and not for the firm, and the firm is answered and the firm is finished. A woman with a slate says four claims back to the people who made them, and the fourth is to a merchant who is owed a hundred and fifty pounds, and a queue in a market learns to do it and does not know it is doing anything. A room, a bench and five sheets are taken in about three hours by a man with a form, and there is no room in the form, and the reason there is no room in the form is that same clause. Nine lines of a book of numbers are taken out of two shut rooms by a dyer who owns the house and has no right, and a woman with about nine hundred names in a book writes the hour down in her own hand and keeps the sheet. A room over a saddler's shop in a river market town finds that the Bellwrights' Measure is two trades inside one word and that a master of the Measure said both halves of the split in one afternoon and did not see it. And at the end of it a man walks out of a town of about two thousand people down a road in the rain with a barrow he cannot pay with, and writes down the two questions he came down the water with, answers one of them, carries the other, and a woman a step off his left shoulder asks him the third one and he answers it in four words.**

**Nobody in this batch is wicked. That is not a softening; it is the engine, it held for Batch 0003 and Batch 0004, and it holds here without a single exception, including the officer who confiscates and including the officer who says the thing into the record and including a market keeper who charges fourpence a day for a yard and tells you not to thank him for it.**

## The required power change, and it is the only one

**One, and it is not a stage. Tomas Vale is at the first edge of Stage 1 (Near Toll) at the end of this batch exactly as at the end of Volume 01.**

**He rings a bell he did not make, once, in a public place outside his own county, whose entire claim is his own fault.** Chapter 91, the seventy-seventh, at the top of the hour, a market of about nine hundred, a block on the cobbles by the cross, about four minutes of silence afterwards which he does not improve. **The claim is in the metal and not in the ringer's mouth: a man ringing a bell he did not make says whatever the bell's claim is and cannot add to it, and what it is is an apology — what he did, what he could not do, and the names of the people who did the rest — and it names the known facts, the unknown facts and his own fault, and it tells nobody what to decide.**

**And the bell did not do what a bell does.** The claim went to the end of the market street and stopped there, the way a voice stops, at the place where the crowd stops. **He does not know whether that is the metal, or the claim, or the crowd, and he did not find out, and he is not going to find out in this county, and the batch does not let him find it out as a consolation.** He stood by the block for about half an hour afterwards and did not ask one person what they had heard, and the reason is on the page: a man who asks nine hundred people what they heard is a man who has begun a list, and he has a slate with nine names on it and he is not starting another one.

**He is not told where it went. He does not learn. He says in his own hand on the seventy-seventh and again on the eighty-sixth that he has never once in his life learned where a warning lands, and that he is not going to be told it as a favour.** He said four sentences in a market in Volume 01 and was never told where they went and he is not told where these went either.

**Nothing else was rung in this batch, and nothing was rung in Miremarket before it.** The Crown carrier is not rung in any chapter because it cannot be and the receipt forbids anybody to furnish a clapper, and it is the second clause of that receipt — *the frame stays in its reinforced padded cradle for the carriage* — that is the reason he cannot pay for anything. **Tamsin Rook's bell is not rung in any chapter from 51 to 100.** No bell he has ever made may be unbarred and his own plain carrier is in a sealed yard in Kells and stays there, and Chapter 100 says so in his own hand. **The nine words on the flyleaf from Day 62 are a comparison and not a power and are not repeated as one in any chapter of this batch.**
