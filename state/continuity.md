**This file is the binding canon. It is not a record of how the batches went** — the per-batch narratives are in `state/batch-000N-summary.md` and the batch-to-summary mapping is in `state/volume-index.md`. Everything below is a rule, a figure, a boundary or a clock that a later writer must obey. **Where two batches stated the same fact, the most complete statement is kept and the earlier one is gone; where a fact was corrected, the correction is recorded here as the fact.**

**THE MANNER OF COMPACTION, recorded because this project has been bitten by it: the previous version of this file was 259 KB and had grown by appending a full addendum per batch, each restating the last. A writer could not load it, and a writer that cannot load its own state drops required payoffs without noticing. This file is now the canon only. ## 1. Setting and series canon

- The setting is **Mereward**, a low-fantasy river country with nine historic bell wards. Kells is a highland market town where the Kells Water meets the old western road. **Sennet is the Crown capital, a river city of about nine thousand people built on three terraces above the Sennet River — the flat, with the wharves and the granaries; the middle, with the market, the river courts and the public counter at the foot of the top; and the top, with the Quiet Office, the Crown archives, and a bell about as big as a cartwheel on a frame over a gate. The sealed First Answer chamber is in the top terrace and is not opened, described, or approached in any chapter from 101 to 120.**
- The old **Hearthline** is a federated network built for local answers, physical redundancy, and withdrawal. The Crown's **Quiet Office** later uses Crown clappers to repeat one answer as if many people had freely consented. **Those institutional mechanisms remain unrevealed.**
- Bell resonance carries one bounded claim that a living person has accepted responsibility for making. **It is not mind reading, objective proof, prophecy, or compulsion.** A future warning is possible because someone accepts the duty to warn before the danger arrives.
- **Tomas Vale** is a skilled ordinary bellmaker. He began at Stage 0 (Hand) and is at the first edge of Stage 1 after one private binding in Chapter 7. **He gained no power in Chapters 11–110.** His external want is to protect Kells, save the Vale Bellhouse, and find his missing mother. His internal need is to share responsibility and stop confusing usefulness with authority.
- **Tamsin Rook** is the only slow-burn romantic partner. **Mara Vale** is Tomas's estranged sister and a separate family relationship. Oren Pike, Sabin Fell, and Nell Fenn provide ongoing consent, grief, craft, and consequence pressure.
- The final antagonist is Chancellor **Seris Dain**, and the final resolution breaks the central clapper, frees living anchors including Lira, and creates a federation in which rulers cannot compel every answer. **No ending material has been introduced early and no new final enemy may be introduced without flagging it.**
- **The first Hearthline bell's origin and the fate of the displaced Hushline settlements remain intentionally open and are not a new threat.**

## 2. The two house rules

1. **No weekday and no month is named in any chapter.** Days are numbered: *the seventy-seventh*, *the eighty-sixth*, *this morning*. A recurring day is **a market day, a round, a bell day, a three-day market, or by its number.** The four surviving month names are in §3.
2. **The hour numerals run 1 to 12 twice a day, and no half-day may be attached to an hour that a chapter does not attach one to.** If the prose says *the fourth hour*, then no state file, summary or day-book may say *the fourth hour of the afternoon*. **Write the half-day in the prose whenever you want one.** **There is exactly one clock and it is §5. There is no second copy of it in any summary file, because two copies of a clock in this project have already drifted apart.**

**And the rule underneath both: a fix is not a fix because a summary says so.** Every number, date, clock time, custody state, physical condition, age and name in a state file must be traceable to a specific chapter and line. **If a chapter does not give an age, a count or an hour, the state file records that it is not on the page.** **Run the sweep before you write and after, on the prose and on the state layer, and when you update the state layer search your own new text for the words you removed from the prose** — that is the direction the error has now gone more than ten times.

**Sweep every file you write for stray shell text before you save it.** A previous repair pass wrote the literal strings `EOF` and `head -5 state/open-threads.md` into the end of a state file, and a repair pass that writes shell text into a state file is the single most damaging thing this project can do.

## 6. The craft and civic law, consolidated

**Deduplicated from ten batch addenda. Every line is a rule the prose has established. None of them is a stage and none of them is a power.**

### 6a. What a bell is

- The fixed stripped Crown survey carrier is a waist-high mounted physical object whose missing clapper does not make it self-directing or prophetic; a connected physical route and a narrow bypass drive its sound. **A Crown survey carrier's ordinary survey clapper is distinct from the later political Crown clapper.** The former was removed; **no copied-answer mechanism has been exposed.**
- **A bound bell requires physically identifiable witness matter, a living keeper's specific accepted duty, and an informed answer.** A private handbell may have one answer; a local public signal normally needs three independent answers. **A certification is a witness and a witness is a person, and a room of people is not a person** — the district may certify within any one column and may not certify the three together, and **an empty column can be counted two ways and both counts are true and neither is a sentence, and a sentence is the one thing a Crown office has never had to check.**
- **A future warning can be valid because a keeper accepts the duty to warn before the danger.** The bell carries accepted responsibility, not objective truth, and it cannot compel a listener's decision.
- **The Bellhouse route is physically connected to route L-4 through old copper service cuts, splices, and bypasses. A route can repeat a claim, and repetition does not prove free consent.** A route not connected: the Bellwrights' cut to the market lane was cut by the Crown's own renewal trench on Day 15.
- **The market court's call bell is an UNBOUND iron handbell** — a rope, a striker on a thong, no wire beneath it, no groove in its crown, no wax. It was never bound, so it carries ordinary sound only; on Day 8 its job was to **count** the answers while the words and the record did the claiming, and its keeper was the court usher. **The bell counts, the people claim, and this must not be quietly reversed by later binding it.**
- **A bell's reach thins out instead of stopping, so a courier cannot find the edge of her own rule from inside the reach, and the bell cannot be trusted to carry even a keeper's boundary.** This is why Tamsin Rook reduces her offer rather than trying to measure her way out.
- **A bell's failure modes are ordinary and are recorded as ordinary faults: too bright** (it goes further than its maker wants and cannot be reduced, because a man who tunes a bell to stop at a distance is tuning it to a lie about how far it goes) **and repeat** (a bell within about eight feet of hard stone returns its own note a fraction later, and a thing that comes out twice is a claim and a copy of a claim, and a copy is how a sentence stops belonging to the person who said it).
- **A claim lives in four pieces of paper, not in the metal.** Tomas withdraws a claim by taking down the ward board's copy and the keeper's oilskin board line and burning both, because **a paper you keep is a paper somebody finds in nine years.** One bell may carry one claim at a time; **a bell that has been answered once and then carried twice is not a bell.**
- **A plain carrier is one with nothing in it** — no wax, no dressed groove, only the maker's mark and the date. **The work is not in the metal, it is in the four parts of the frame, and the three answers are the hard part, not the object.** **Witness matter that no living person could locate in ten years is a relic and not a witness.**
- **A carrier may not add anything, and the way to do that is to send the difficulty out deliberately.** The fourth rule Sabin Fell has never given anybody in this county is **take the person most likely to refuse, and ask them first.** The first answer is a claim; every answer after the first is a claim about the first; **and the order of the asking is itself a record.** **A bellmaker's own flaw against this rule is wanting an answer.**
- **A keeper's count can fall below three and the signal is still rung, and the fall is entered as what it is. A withdrawal is recorded as a withdrawal and never as a silence.**
- **A warning may name its own bar, its own keeper, its own fault and its own unknown, and must not name a decision.** The Day 28 warning names the known facts, the unknown facts, the keeper and the count of one, the duty, the maker's own name and the two craft faults, and **contains no instruction to any listener.** The district's finding of the same morning reads it back as evidence that the ward's bells are unaccounted for, and Tomas certifies it in a full court.
- **A claim in metal is a thing a man cannot edit.** It cannot be added to, cannot be taken back, cannot have an hour put on it, and cannot be shown to anybody on a page. **It is the one instrument he has that is not a document, and nobody in any building can ask to see it.** A man ringing a bell he did not make says whatever the bell's claim is and cannot add to it.
- **A bell cannot stop a fire, and a bellmaker who thinks it can has stopped being a maker.**
- **A carrier's book and a Crown roll are different objects and carry different things.** A carrier's book says the day, the thing, from where, to where, how many miles, and the carrier's name. **A carrier may not write three things: the owner, a debt, a price.** That is the only rule on that road that keeps a person out of a book. **Carriers carry parcels; people are not parcels and always were not.**
- **The Bellwrights' Measure's road map carries a dated, keyed mark for every ward that has ever taken its answers back since before the Crown laid a mile of copper; fifty-three are marked. This count is unrelated to the forty-one households on the L-4 answer schedule and the two numbers must never be conflated.** A map shows where a line runs and cannot show whose hand altered it.
- **The Measure is two trades inside one word and always has been, and they cannot both be the rule, and there is no room in this kingdom where both can be said.** A licensed control says a bell that cannot be traced is a danger to the public. Independent craft says a bell that can be proved is a danger to the man who proved it. **A master of the Measure said both, in one room, on one afternoon, and did not see them, and the man who learned them off him did not see them either.**
- **Fourth clause of craft law, canon: a bell made in a place under a declaration, out of a stock that cannot be traced, is a bell that can be used and cannot be traced, and that is a hazard to the public and not only to the trade.** The Measure's bar is on the bell and not on the maker, and **the Measure's bar binds Kells's county and not the next.**
- **A certificate is a certificate of metal and a note and nothing else, and it is for the day somebody asks where a bell came from, and it is worth that and not one halfpenny more.** A certificate is a promise to a stranger, and the stranger has to be alive and in the same county, and the only thing that lasts about a certificate is a person who saw it be made.
- **A Crown instrument can be lawfully granted out of a sealed workshop for one named hour, and the grant is a leash as well as an instrument.** Oren's own entered reason is the law of the chapter: **a watch which tells the Crown it has no instrument is handing the Crown a reason to invent one.**
- **A watch captain can comply and refuse at once, and the refusal narrows him.** It is a protection and it is a leash: the watch is now a record and not a route, and every woman in a doorway has been told in advance that the watch will come and write the hour and the place and will not stand there.

#### The law Volume 03 Batch 0001 added, and none of it is a stage and none of it is a power

- **There is a third kind of bell and this kingdom has no name for it on a form.** It is not a keeper's bell, because a keeper's bell has a living keeper's hand on it, and it is not a plain bell, because a plain bell is a thing somebody decides to hit. **It is a bell on a post with a bracket and a socket and a wire in the ground, and it is rung by somebody else's stroke about eleven miles off, and the person who decides when it rings is not the person who reads the sheet.** It is a description and not a coined word, and **no chapter may name it with a new noun.**
- **A Crown bell on a post is a notice.** The sheet under it is not a copy of the sound; the sound is the thing and the sheet is what the sound was for. **The sound arrives before the man with the book, and a man with a book cannot get to about two hundred doors in a day, and a bell costs nothing to send, and that is the whole of the coercion and it is arithmetic. THE FIRST SHEET HE READS UNDER A BELL IS DATED FOUR DAYS FORWARD OF THE RINGING, and a man who has kept the same board for nine years and has a book of every notice he has read out has never once written down how far ahead the bell goes, and there is no column for it and he has wanted to rule one about nine times in nine years and has not, because a column a man rules for himself is a thing he has to be right about.**

- **There are two kinds of paper.** *The record of what you said*, which is what four clerks in four counties and an auditor in a room over a haberdasher's shop have said, and which is true, **and the thing that says it for you** — a fitting, an instrument, or a box with a handle, issued out of a store against a district instruction with a number, **and when it is fitted the answer is made for you, in your own voice, and it is not a lie.** The second kind is not kept at the public counter, is not the clerk's business, and **he has never had one in his hand and has never been asked about them four times in nine years.** **THE BELL HAS NOW TAKEN A SECOND ANSWER, IN CHAPTER 0109, IN A CLERK'S MOUTH, AND IT IS THE ANSWER TO HOW AN OFFICE GETS INTO A BELL IN PART. IT IS NOT THE MECHANISM AND IT MAY NOT BE WRITTEN AS THE MECHANISM.**

- **There is no box on the form for a question.** There is a box for a person, a box for a district, a box for a thing, and a box for a reason, **and the box for a reason has to be filled in by a person with a standing in a district, and he has not got one, and the clerk has not got one either.**
- **A form is a thing you fill in with a reason, and a reason is the hardest thing in this kingdom to get anybody to put their name to.** A clerk at that counter, after about nine years, in his own mouth, on the record: **about four thousand people a year bring him a paper, and not one has ever asked him for the second kind, and they are all frightened of it, and being frightened of it is the correct thing to be, and nobody has ever said so out loud and that is why nobody has ever been able to write a form about it.**
- **A sheet in a column and a person in a house are two different things — and so are a bell on a roll and a household on a roll, and the second pair is the one this volume is about.**
- **A bell that is not sounding and is not broken is waiting.** A bell on a corner of a city of about nine thousand that has not been struck is a bell that will be struck at a particular hour by a particular man for a particular reason, and there are about nine hundred of them and about nine hundred written-down reasons. **About two hundred people in one yard came out of doors at the hour and let it ring four times and made no noise.** A man in a town of two thousand walks into a market. ### 6b. What a record is

- **A mark in a response column is not an answer and can still be used as one.** The officers read the office's own marks off the response column and treat them as the household's answer, beginning at line 22, the Fenn household, read as *six entered, two accompanying*. **The Crown's own notes about people who were not in the house were read by the Crown as the answer.**
- **The *Witnessed by* column is not cleared between rounds, so a line can hold the name of a person who is dead.** The Crown puts that line in front of a door, calls it a witnessed answer, enters the household's objection in a margin in a clerk's own name and initialled, and proceeds, **because an objection does not suspend the renewal.** **A form exhibited entire, a published procedure and a clerk with a conscience together make a round that is conducted properly and that is not the same thing as a round that is lawful.**
- **A procedure can make an office legible and cannot make an office stop.** A form can be answered with a form: a supplementary return carrying the objection, the name of a person present at the answering, **the reason field the summary form drops, and nothing on it to say which hand wrote the reason.** And a door-to-door slate asking whether a household intends to attend in person produces the same list the ward's procedure produces, **and cheaper, and by being kind.**
- **A household answers once, in one hand, and the office carries the hand forward indefinitely, and carrying it forward is lawful.** The district takes the return in, stamps it, **sends it back to the house to keep**; the next year's form arrives with the last certified answer and a line reading **confirmed by the household**. **Nobody falsifies anything and the household is answered for anyway.**
- **A form that has no field for a person is a form that has made a decision about that person without a person in the room.** Marta Vell's water bell is entered on a Fourmile schedule **with the keeper's field empty**, because the district's form has four fields and none of them fits a water bell. **A village can be entered in a schedule it never answered to.**
- **A trade already keeps, for its own reasons and in its own hand, the three things an office needs: who lives where, who buys, and who signed.** The Crown's roll is built out of that paper and the office is not stealing anything. **A column and a stamp only need somebody to be competent.**
- **A hand can be read across two kinds of paper.** The reading that finds it is wear-and-technology, not deduction, and the description that closes it is six words long — *forward slope, hesitating descender, same pause before g* — and **hesitating descender is a trade point and not a flourish, because a clerk's hand is never the same twice and a signer's hand is the same every time.** **A bill and a Crown form are not the same kind of document and no law says a man may not be the hand on both.**
- **A Crown office's own document is the only thing that can protect a child from it.** A Crown officer would not contradict his own district in a lane. **No chapter attributes a save to a bell, and the chapter says so in print.**
- **A person is the only thing in this kingdom that can be asked a question and can refuse it, and a hall is not a person and cannot be asked anything, and that is not caution and not a design.** It is why a form has four fields and none of them is the words.
- **The method, in Tamsin Rook's words, and it is the volume's working instrument.** Do not ask an office *who* is on a list; **a name is a thing you can be refused. Ask what the document is** — *is the thing you keep a record of what I said, or is it a record of what somebody else said about what I said* — **and then make the clerk put the answer in a document with his own name at the bottom of it. A thing a clerk holds is not a fact about anything; a clerk who has written down what it is has said a thing, and a thing somebody has said can be looked at by the next man and by the person you hand it to.**
- **A question asked at a door is in the asker's mouth for about nine seconds and the only trace of it in this kingdom is a hand that went down afterwards, and a hand is an answer and has never been anything else.** And there is no form in any district that has ever carried the words themselves.
- **A shut line is a man who has gone and answered, and so he is not lost: he is answered.** A line is not a document. A line is a place in a book where a thing is entered, and a removals officer enters about nine hundred a year and is not permitted to ask why anybody has gone and there is no box for it.
- **A chore is a thing a man does for himself, in his own yard, with his own hands, on his own day, and a firm is not a person and cannot do one, and a household is five people and can.** A house may hold money for a dead man only if the house can show its own answering, and you must be able to show a hand of your own before you may be entered in a book as handling a hand of somebody else's. **That rule is about four hundred years old or nobody knows how old it is, and it turns entirely on this and on nothing else.**
- **A hand against a line is the only record in this kingdom that a person stood in front of somebody and said something.** A hand is a record and a memory is a story, and a merchant will not pay stories, and **that is why a man dead four months has not been robbed, and it is not a comfort and it was not invented for a bag of money in a room.**
- **A claim said twice is the only thing in this kingdom that has ever moved a line, and it is about four inches long**, and it is a person saying another person's own words back to her at ten feet in the ordinary voice in front of about nine other people. **It is free when the person owes nothing and it is a document about four households when the person is owed a hundred and fifty pounds in a town of two thousand.**
- **A bell in a market takes about nine days to change a town, and the first example of it took seven, and the rule is written down with the seven beside it.** And what it changes is a queue, and a queue is not a place and cannot be looked up.
- **A document nobody has asked for is a thing that has not happened.** And a finding withdrawn in one county cannot be asked for in another, because it is on a shelf two days' road away and there is a form and the form is correct.
- **A town has decided a chore, which is the smallest thing a town can decide, and it is not going to be enough, and four women in this county have said so out loud and none of them has been thanked.**
- **A copy of a Crown sheet, made in a room, is that office's from the moment it is made, and holding it is not a thing any person may do, and there is no argument in it.** And the form that takes it has no room in it, **because the holder has no standing to hold a claim, and that is the first time in six weeks a paper has been on his side and it is on his side because it is empty.**
- **A district number is a filing reference and not a man.** And a reference going somewhere is not a person, and that is a smaller thing than anybody has been carrying.
- **A hole in a map is not a line, and a line is the thing this volume knows how to ask about.** The removals are in the same places in every copy, which is a fact about a hand and not about a fire, and means the removal was done to the plate and not to the copies.
- **A list of people who have spoken in public is a list of citizens, and the Crown will rename a document rather than lose it.** The district **kept the list and amended the heading in its own margin**, and **the word *standing* now means, in this ward's records, that a person stood in a lane and was heard.**
- **A clerk's list fills in anyway.** A clerk came down a road with a list and a copyist, read names at four doors, asked nobody anything, and the list filled in anyway; he came back nine days later with the same list and one door said the people had moved and he said thank you and the copyist struck it out and wrote something.
- **A district buys road sheets by the sheet, on a standing order in the spring, on a number that has not changed in six years and has not gone up with the price.**
- **The Crown's instrument is procedural three times over** — a form field headed *cause stated by*, a ground correction answered with a schedule and not a survey with a Crown deputy who signs his own name against his own works order, and an amendment in a margin. **Nobody answered a Crown document with a document and won.**
- **A clause written to stop an office taking a thing can be used by a person with a pen and an hour, and it will work on the man who asked for the clause.** The receipt's third clause is a hook and not a door. **The fifth clause is what makes a request checkable, because it is the reason a requester must be told, out loud, in front of other people, what he may not do with the object** — and the only way anybody has ever found to check a request is to make the requester say out loud what he wants to do, while he is saying it, and write it down while he says it.
