# Continuity State

## Bootstrap canon

- The setting is **Mereward**, a low-fantasy river country with nine historic bell wards. Kells is Tomas Vale's home; Sennet is the Crown capital and the sealed First Answer chamber.
- The old **Hearthline** is a federated network built for local answers, physical redundancy, and withdrawal. The Crown's **Quiet Office** later uses **Crown clappers** to repeat one answer as if many people had freely consented.
- Bell resonance carries one bounded claim that a living person has accepted responsibility for making. It is not mind reading, objective proof, prophecy, or compulsion. A future warning is possible because someone accepts the duty to warn before the danger arrives.
- A warning can include a primary claim from the person responsible for a plan and a separate keeper's accepted duty to expose or carry it. The opening bell joins Dain's planned operation with Mara's accepted duty to warn; it is not a hidden prophet's voice.
- Tomas is a male protagonist, age 32, a skilled ordinary bellmaker at Stage 0 (Hand). His external want is to protect Kells, save the Vale Bellhouse, and find his missing mother. His internal need is to share responsibility and stop confusing usefulness with authority.
- Tomas's central flaw is carrying every burden and decision alone. His fear is that releasing control will get someone killed. His final change is to relinquish sole network authority and accept a plural, imperfect Hearthline.
- **Tamsin Rook** is the only slow-burn romantic partner. **Mara Vale** is Tomas's estranged sister and a separate family relationship. Oren Pike, Sabin Fell, and Nell Fenn provide ongoing consent, grief, craft, and consequence pressure.
- The final antagonist is Chancellor **Seris Dain**, who uses the First Answer to make one accepted response govern all wards. The final resolution breaks the central clapper, frees living anchors including Lira, and creates a federation in which warnings can travel but rulers cannot compel every answer.
- No chapter prose exists yet. The Volume 01 and first-batch files are planning artifacts only.

## Hard continuity constraints

- Bells never read minds, compel obedience, guarantee safety, or solve problems by themselves.
- Tomas is competent from the beginning but cannot safely identify a Crown clapper, a criminal, or a hidden intention on first contact.
- Every major power gain includes a persistent sensory, relational, civic, or material cost.
- Stage 1 (Near Toll) begins only after Tomas's first deliberate binding. It permits only the narrow ability to hear whether a nearby answer is strained. Batch 0001 makes him pay both a failed small casting and a damaged tuning tool, plus one episode of ringing ears; permanent or lasting memory loss is reserved for later Stage 2 practice.
- The fixed stripped Crown survey carrier, Tamsin's stolen portable courier handbell, and Tomas's newly cast trial handbell are three separate physical objects. Their locations, ownership, wear, and custody must not be conflated. The fixed carrier is sealed in place in Chapter 8, moved to watch custody in Chapter 10, and must be explicitly recovered or released from custody before Tomas leaves Kells; no substitute stripped bell may appear.
- A valid binding names physical witness matter, a living keeper's specific duty, and an informed answer. A private handbell can use one answer; a local public signal normally requires three independent answers. One private answer must never be represented as Kells-wide consent.
- Batch 0001 may establish a pre-filed dawn inspection, a physical route explanation, and one private Stage 1 warning. It must not yet identify the copied-answer source, the Silent Muster's full name-transfer mechanism, the archive fire, the Bellhouse closure, or Tomas's departure.
- Dain is captured alive and faces ordinary law; no new final enemy is permitted.
- The central mystery is answered: the opening warning is a real planned crime carried through accepted responsibility and counterfeit consent, not prophecy. The first bell's exact maker and the complete fate of the Hushline settlements remain intentionally open.
