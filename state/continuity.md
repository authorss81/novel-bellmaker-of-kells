**This file is the binding canon. It is not a record of how the batches went** — the per-batch narratives are in `state/batch-000N-summary.md` and the batch-to-summary mapping is in `state/volume-index.md`. Everything below is a rule, a figure, a boundary or a clock that a later writer must obey. **Where two batches stated the same fact, the most complete statement is kept and the earlier one is gone; where a fact was corrected, the correction is recorded here as the fact.**

**THE MANNER OF COMPACTION, recorded because this project has been bitten by it: the previous version of this file was 259 KB and had grown by appending a full addendum per batch, each restating the last. A writer could not load it, and a writer that cannot load its own state drops required payoffs without noticing. This file is now the canon only. ## 1. Setting and series canon

- The setting is **Mereward**, a low-fantasy river country with nine historic bell wards. Kells is a highland market town where the Kells Water meets the old western road. **Sennet is the Crown capital, a river city of about nine thousand people built on three terraces above the Sennet River — the flat, with the wharves and the granaries; the middle, with the market, the river courts and the public counter at the foot of the top; and the top, with the Quiet Office, the Crown archives, and a bell about as big as a cartwheel on a frame over a gate. The sealed First Answer chamber is in the top terrace and is not opened, described, or approached in any chapter from 101 to 130.**
- The old **Hearthline** is a federated network built for local answers, physical redundancy, and withdrawal. The Crown's **Quiet Office** later uses Crown clappers to repeat one answer as if many people had freely consented. **Those institutional mechanisms remain unrevealed.**
- Bell resonance carries one bounded claim that a living person has accepted responsibility for making. **It is not mind reading, objective proof, prophecy, or compulsion.** A future warning is possible because someone accepts the duty to warn before the danger arrives.
- **Tomas Vale** is a skilled ordinary bellmaker. He began at Stage 0 (Hand) and is at the first edge of Stage 1 after one private binding in Chapter 7. **He gained no power in Chapters 11–110.** His external want is to protect Kells, save the Vale Bellhouse, and find his missing mother. His internal need is to share responsibility and stop confusing usefulness with authority.
- **Tamsin Rook** is the only slow-burn romantic partner. **Mara Vale** is Tomas's estranged sister and a separate family relationship. Oren Pike, Sabin Fell, and Nell Fenn provide ongoing consent, grief, craft, and consequence pressure.
- The final antagonist is Chancellor **Seris Dain**, and the final resolution breaks the central clapper, frees living anchors including Lira, and creates a federation in which rulers cannot compel every answer. **No ending material has been introduced early and no new final enemy may be introduced without flagging it.**
- **The first Hearthline bell's origin and the fate of the displaced Hushline settlements remain intentionally open and are not a new threat.**

## 2. The two house rules

1. **No weekday and no month is named in any chapter.** Days are numbered: *the seventy-seventh*, *the eighty-sixth*, *this morning*. A recurring day is **a market day, a round, a bell day, a three-day market, or by its number.** The four surviving month names are in §3.
2. **The hour numerals run 1 to 12 twice a day, and no half-day may be attached to an hour that a chapter does not attach one to.** If the prose says *the fourth hour*, then no state file, summary or day-book may say *the fourth hour of the afternoon*. **Write the half-day in the prose whenever you want one.** **There is exactly one clock and it is §5. There is no second copy of it in any summary file, because two copies of a clock in this project have already drifted apart.**

**And the rule underneath both: a fix is not a fix because a summary says so.** Every number, date, clock time, custody state, physical condition, age and name in a state file must be traceable to a specific chapter and line. **If a chapter does not give an age, a count or an hour, the state file records that it is not on the page.** **Run the sweep before you write and after, on the prose and on the state layer, and when you update the state layer search your own new text for the words you removed from the prose** — that is the direction the error has now gone more than ten times.

**Sweep every file you write for stray shell text before you save it.** A previous repair pass wrote the literal strings `EOF` and `head -5 state/open-threads.md` into the end of a state file, and a repair pass that writes shell text into a state file is the single most damaging thing this project can do.

## 6. The craft and civic law, consolidated

**Deduplicated from ten batch addenda. Every line is a rule the prose has established. None of them is a stage and none of them is a power.**

### 6a. What a bell is

- The fixed stripped Crown survey carrier is a waist-high mounted physical object whose missing clapper does not make it self-directing or prophetic; a connected physical route and a narrow bypass drive its sound. **A Crown survey carrier's ordinary survey clapper is distinct from the later political Crown clapper.** The former was removed; **no copied-answer mechanism has been exposed.**
- **A bound bell requires physically identifiable witness matter, a living keeper's specific accepted duty, and an informed answer.** A private handbell may have one answer; a local public signal normally needs three independent answers. **A certification is a witness and a witness is a person, and a room of people is not a person** — the district may certify within any one column and may not certify the three together, and **an empty column can be counted two ways and both counts are true and neither is a sentence, and a sentence is the one thing a Crown office has never had to check.**
- **A future warning can be valid because a keeper accepts the duty to warn before the danger.** The bell carries accepted responsibility, not objective truth, and it cannot compel a listener's decision.
- **The Bellhouse route is physically connected to route L-4 through old copper service cuts, splices, and bypasses. A route can repeat a claim, and repetition does not prove free consent.** A route not connected: the Bellwrights' cut to the market lane was cut by the Crown's own renewal trench on Day 15.
- **The market court's call bell is an UNBOUND iron handbell** — a rope, a striker on a thong, no wire beneath it, no groove in its crown, no wax. It was never bound, so it carries ordinary sound only; on Day 8 its job was to **count** the answers while the words and the record did the claiming, and its keeper was the court usher. **The bell counts, the people claim, and this must not be quietly reversed by later binding it.**
- **A bell's reach thins out instead of stopping, so a courier cannot find the edge of her own rule from inside the reach, and the bell cannot be trusted to carry even a keeper's boundary.** This is why Tamsin Rook reduces her offer rather than trying to measure her way out.
- **A bell's failure modes are ordinary and are recorded as ordinary faults: too bright** (it goes further than its maker wants and cannot be reduced, because a man who tunes a bell to stop at a distance is tuning it to a lie about how far it goes) **and repeat** (a bell within about eight feet of hard stone returns its own note a fraction later, and a thing that comes out twice is a claim and a copy of a claim, and a copy is how a sentence stops belonging to the person who said it).
- **A claim lives in four pieces of paper, not in the metal.** Tomas withdraws a claim by taking down the ward board's copy and the keeper's oilskin board line and burning both, because **a paper you keep is a paper somebody finds in nine years.** One bell may carry one claim at a time; **a bell that has been answered once and then carried twice is not a bell.**
- **A plain carrier is one with nothing in it** — no wax, no dressed groove, only the maker's mark and the date. **The work is not in the metal, it is in the four parts of the frame, and the three answers are the hard part, not the object.** **Witness matter that no living person could locate in ten years is a relic and not a witness.**
- **A carrier may not add anything, and the way to do that is to send the difficulty out deliberately.** The fourth rule Sabin Fell has never given anybody in this county is **take the person most likely to refuse, and ask them first.** The first answer is a claim; every answer after the first is a claim about the first; **and the order of the asking is itself a record.** **A bellmaker's own flaw against this rule is wanting an answer.**
- **A keeper's count can fall below three and the signal is still rung, and the fall is entered as what it is. A withdrawal is recorded as a withdrawal and never as a silence.**
- **A warning may name its own bar, its own keeper, its own fault and its own unknown, and must not name a decision.** The Day 28 warning names the known facts, the unknown facts, the keeper and the count of one, the duty, the maker's own name and the two craft faults, and **contains no instruction to any listener.** The district's finding of the same morning reads it back as evidence that the ward's bells are unaccounted for, and Tomas certifies it in a full court.
- **A claim in metal is a thing a man cannot edit.** It cannot be added to, cannot be taken back, cannot have an hour put on it, and cannot be shown to anybody on a page. **It is the one instrument he has that is not a document, and nobody in any building can ask to see it.** A man ringing a bell he did not make says whatever the bell's claim is and cannot add to it.
- **A bell cannot stop a fire, and a bellmaker who thinks it can has stopped being a maker.**
- **A carrier's book and a Crown roll are different objects and carry different things.** A carrier's book says the day, the thing, from where, to where, how many miles, and the carrier's name. **A carrier may not write three things: the owner, a debt, a price.** That is the only rule on that road that keeps a person out of a book. **Carriers carry parcels; people are not parcels and always were not.**
- **The Bellwrights' Measure's road map carries a dated, keyed mark for every ward that has ever taken its answers back since before the Crown laid a mile of copper; fifty-three are marked. This count is unrelated to the forty-one households on the L-4 answer schedule and the two numbers must never be conflated.** A map shows where a line runs and cannot show whose hand altered it.
- **The Measure is two trades inside one word and always has been, and they cannot both be the rule, and there is no room in this kingdom where both can be said.** A licensed control says a bell that cannot be traced is a danger to the public. Independent craft says a bell that can be proved is a danger to the man who proved it. **A master of the Measure said both, in one room, on one afternoon, and did not see them, and the man who learned them off him did not see them either.**
- **Fourth clause of craft law, canon: a bell made in a place under a declaration, out of a stock that cannot be traced, is a bell that can be used and cannot be traced, and that is a hazard to the public and not only to the trade.** The Measure's bar is on the bell and not on the maker, and **the Measure's bar binds Kells's county and not the next.**
- **A certificate is a certificate of metal and a note and nothing else, and it is for the day somebody asks where a bell came from, and it is worth that and not one halfpenny more.** A certificate is a promise to a stranger, and the stranger has to be alive and in the same county, and the only thing that lasts about a certificate is a person who saw it be made.
- **A Crown instrument can be lawfully granted out of a sealed workshop for one named hour, and the grant is a leash as well as an instrument.** Oren's own entered reason is the law of the chapter: **a watch which tells the Crown it has no instrument is handing the Crown a reason to invent one.**
- **A watch captain can comply and refuse at once, and the refusal narrows him.** It is a protection and it is a leash: the watch is now a record and not a route, and every woman in a doorway has been told in advance that the watch will come and write the hour and the place and will not stand there.
