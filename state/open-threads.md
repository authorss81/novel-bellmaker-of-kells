A roll does not need anybody's answer; a duty is a mouth; a mouth is for a claim; and the claim is made in a queue, in about nine minutes, in the ordinary voice, by about eleven thousand people a year who are not clerks and are not agents of anything.*
2. **Where are names sent when they are registered? — NOT ANSWERED, and this batch did not approach it, and the nearest thing it produced is not a name.** **On Day 107 a storekeeper said it in one sentence and put his pen in a drawer: a number is a place a thing is filed at. 3. **Which office is it. — ANSWERED IN THE ONLY WAY IT CAN BE, AND IN BATCH 0003 THE ANSWER STOPPED BEING A NAME AND BECAME A SET OF TABLES.** A name at last: **the Quiet Office**, and the name is the name of about nine hundred rooms and one format. **What the format now is, as of Day 116: a plate in a printer's room two streets off cutting about nine hundred forms a year; a four-inch box at the end of every one of them, full on nine hundred and empty on one; a rule that a sworn answer is a thing about a person and must be sent round; a book in a store that records what went out and when and is open while the wall behind the man is shut; and a court list of about nine hundred matters a year in the same five columns with the same empty fifth.** *A name is a thing you can be refused. ANSWERED, and answering it has closed nothing.**
