# Open Threads

These are active promises **after Volume 01, Batch 0005**. Volume 01 ends at Chapter 50. They are not resolved merely because a clue has appeared.

## The two questions Tomas carries out of Kells

**Stated on the page in Chapter 50, in his own hand, in his day-book, and not answered:** *Who taught a Crown office to turn one clerk's answer into a whole settlement's consent — and where are the names being sent when they are registered?* Underneath them, in the same hand and on the same page, the three documents that generated them: the **forty-second row under the last rule** of the L-4 schedule (first field **L-4**, persons field empty, response column **OFF. 411**, dated **nine days before the carrier rang**); the service ticket's second line, ***answered in office, L-4 ward roll, per local practice***; and **Ansel Stroud's unentered admission of the ninth day**, that he has been merging divided answers into single ones for eleven years and that a ward which cannot make up its mind is read as a ward that will not answer. **How a Crown office gets into a bell is unanswered and the volume ends on it.**

## Series mystery threads — all still open

- **How does a Crown office get into a bell?** The volume's open question. Chapter 50's custody review comes as close to it as the volume allows and stops: the technique is a **riffle file** and a chisel and a Crown pattern, and it names no person.
- **Who installed the first Crown clapper, and why did the Hearthline accept it?** Not named anywhere in Volume 01. The Crown clapper is not named in prose.
- **The Silent Muster's name-transfer mechanism.** The operation is never named to anyone in Kells; the warrant that arrests Mrs Wilde on Day 28 carries a *Witnessed by* column and not the word.
- **What did Lira Vale know when she became a living anchor, and what parts of her choice were coerced?** Untouched in Volume 01.
- **Where are the names physically sent when they are registered?** The second half of the volume's closing question. **Fifteen lines on the L-4 schedule** — nine *not returned*, six *accompanying* — are the live end of it, and a district in Fourmile has promised to answer them.
- **The exact origin of the first Hearthline bell.** Intentionally incomplete, and not a new threat.
- **How many Hushline settlements were erased, displaced, or absorbed into the Crown's route system?** Untouched.
- **The Hush of Kells is fixed at Reckoning 619**, twelve years before the present, and the bench-book for that year was destroyed in daylight in the Bellhouse yard on the twenty-eighth. What record, if any, survives of who ordered the final severing is now a question with almost no local record behind it. Oren's Day 11 statement about the gate book is unchanged and **is a record about a record, not about the night.**

## Civic threads Tomas leaves behind in Kells

- **Fifteen unanswered lines.** On the second of the month the district renewed office in Fourmile promised in writing to answer, in an officer's own name, any question a person puts about a line on the L-4 schedule, and regretted having described a schedule as non-response. **No household on any of the fifteen lines has yet come to the ward board to ask.** That is the live instrument and it belongs to the households, not to Tomas.
- **Mrs Wilde's column.** House 108 still carries **Ellen Wilde's** name in the *Witnessed by* column, in a dead woman's own hand, given two autumns ago, and the column is still not cleared between rounds. On the twenty-eighth a **Crown surveyor** countersigned the household's objection, which is written in the margin of the line, and signed nothing near the answer, so the surveyor's name is on the line and not in the column. **Mrs Wilde has asked in a lane what she is supposed to do with that, and about nine people tried to answer her, and nobody could.**
- **The Crown notice that the ward's practice originated in the Vale Bellhouse and that the commission will consider the standing of an originating workshop is still nailed to the ward board** and the shop it names is sealed and its records are destroyed. **Nobody has taken that notice down.**
- **The bearing.** A line of iron under the Bellhouse yard was opened in the open on the twenty-seventh, found to turn under the front of the shop and go out under the wall facing the north road, and a bearing was taken and read out in a lane and is in a Crown file in Fourmile. **Nobody in Kells said what it means.** Oren did not write it. Jerm entered that he did not write it and why.
- **Six people were arrested on the twenty-eighth, all six off the district's amended list of the seventeenth, and all six were released without charge.** The list and the amendment stand, and the Crown's renamed heading — *persons of this ward having public standing in matters of signalling, with dates* — is what made the arrests legible, and Oren Pike is the man who supplied it.
- **Six persons whose names were on that list were not arrested**, and nothing in this volume says why they were not.
- **The district copyist** who read three things into a market court in the rain and put a bearing in a finding and said *I have been a copyist for nineteen years and I have never in my life put a bearing in a finding* is unnamed in every book, and is the ordinary face of a document nobody has a name for.
- **Foreman Brann's gang's book** now says that the ground part of the Day 8 plan was not drawn by the road office and came up with the works order from Fourmile on a folded third sheet in a hand that is not a road hand, with *from the district* at the foot in a different ink. **The entries for the nineteenth are water-damaged and illegible and remain so.**

## People and things whose state is not on the page in this volume

- **The trial handbell.** Crown property. Produced unwrapped in a market court on the twenty-eighth, identified, never rung, rewrapped, and **not returned to the Bellhouse at any point in this volume. Chapter 49 does not state where it is after the twenty-eighth.** Its custody receipt carries Tomas's margin correction and a copyist's initial that is not agreement, and both are in his coat.
- **The market court call bell.** **Not mentioned in a single chapter from 41 to 50.** Its state is unchanged from Chapter 40 and nothing in this volume may be reported about it. The same is true of the wheelwright's counter bell.
- **The woman on the tailboard.** A woman of about forty on no warrant, entered in the watch gate book at entry fifteen on Day 10. **Still unidentified in any chapter in the volume.**
- **Ansel Stroud.** Said one true thing in a public room on the eighteenth and refused to write it down, and on the twenty-first said in the open that he had been asked by his office what he said and had answered truthfully. **He does not appear in any chapter from 41 to 50.**
- **Surveyor roles stay distinct.** Pever is present and accounted for. The missing docket surveyor is still missing and is named only as **Iven Sore** on paper. **Rhea Collis** is named, present, has certified two rounds and neither set of answers, countersigned one objection and one refusal, recalled herself on the 28th, and **withdrew a district finding on the 33rd**; she is not the missing bellwright and is not going to be made into the missing anything. Rudd, Teale, Cope and the copyist are not surveyors.
- **Iven Sore's withdrawn service entry is genuine-or-borrowed and remains so.** He repeated his Chapter 14 position word for word on the twenty-sixth and **still has not said he did not sign it.** The one thing he can support, put at a gate, is a question and not an answer: *did the Crown withdraw a commission the Measure granted, or did it withdraw a name?*
- **Sabin Fell has still never been told about the *Witnessed by* column** and has still not named what he carried past the gate in the Hush.
- **J. Vale and J. V.** The lock-register sign-out and the maintenance tag still echo. No name or suspect is attached in prose.
- **Mara Vale** does not appear in any chapter from 41 to 50.

## Relationship and character threads

- **Tomas and Tamsin travel together on a slate neither of them wrote first**, written by Mercy Slee, which places her route under mutual supervision, keeps her four terms intact, and puts a leash on both of them. **She refused his written request of the twenty-fourth, with no reason, and told him he had not pressed her once.** She said out loud in a lane that a warning does not require her to obey him. **Her hearing loss is permanent, it is public by her own act, nobody holds it over her, and nobody in this volume asks her for a reason.**
- **Tomas has lost the right to use a bell in this county and the right to speak for a ward, and both losses are on paper**: a professional bar on the plain carrier from the Bellwrights' Measure, and the fifth clause of a custody receipt that says the holder is not the keeper, has no claim, cannot certify, and may not speak for any household in Kells or in any ward.
- **Oren Pike** signed the last official act of eleven years in the chair on a receipt, and put the watch's mark under a district wax on a door he had no power to seal, and refused a district's question twice in public and paid for both refusals in people.
- **Nell Fenn spent the thing she carried for nineteen days** and cannot account for the spending of it, and the district's answer to it is the most careful piece of writing that office produced this month and the only one that frightened anybody.
- **Absalom Teage's son was not taken, and Absalom Teage was arrested three hours later**, and there are two Crown documents about that household that cannot both be true in one field, and the ward has spent two days failing to make them into one sentence.
- **Wenna Cray withdrew a duty in writing and was arrested the same afternoon**, and the record of her arrest names the withdrawal as part of the ground.
- **Sabin Fell is angry with Tomas and is entitled to be**, and the sentence he gave him — *I was in a hurry, and being in a hurry felt like duty, and it was not duty* — is the diagnosis of the protagonist's whole flaw and is on the page in another man's mouth.

## Chapters 51–60 pressure (Volume 02, Batch 0001)

- **Answer nothing by a bell.** The volume opens with a suspect traveling with a bell in a cradle he cannot ring, a district office with a real address, a slate with a leash on it, and a route that ends at a low drain gate.
- **Keep the five named bell objects and the archive distinct in their Chapter 50 states.** The plain carrier is barred and in a sealed yard in Kells. The trial handbell is Crown property and not in the Bellhouse. The market court call bell and the wheelwright's counter bell are untouched and were not mentioned in the closing batch.
- **OFF. 411 is a mark and not a man.** Do not name the Crown clapper, the higher official, or the person in that office. Do not answer how an office gets into a bell.
- **The fifteen lines and Mrs Wilde's column are the ward's, not Tomas's.** He may carry them as knowledge and must not answer them.
- **Tamsin's deafness is permanent and she is not to be rescued out of it.** She keeps her four terms, her bell, and her authority.
- **The withdrawn service entry bearing Iven Sore's name is still genuine-or-borrowed** and Sore has still not denied it.
