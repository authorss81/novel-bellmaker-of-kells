## The two questions Tomas still carries

Stated in his own hand in his day-book at the end of Volume 01 and **unchanged and unanswered**. He wrote in **Chapter 58, on the forty-second, at the Fourmile office**, that he has learned a great deal about **a column and a stamp** and **nothing at all about how an office gets into a bell**, and the day-book page where he says so is the batch's worst afternoon and his best. **The Day 40 sentence is a different sentence in a different chapter: Chapter 56, where he wrote that he came four days looking for a man in a room with a plan in it and found a column, a stamp and a habit. The two are not to be merged again.**

*Who taught a Crown office to turn one clerk's answer into a whole settlement's consent — and where are the names being sent when they are registered.*

**What the second half now has on the page, and what it still does not have:** the district has confirmed that a name is registered in a place, that the place has a form, that the form has four columns and every column is a thing a person did, and that the thing the office calls a roll is **not** one of the things its own forms recognise. **Nobody has said where a name goes.** District Officer Marten Cope put the question of transmission to his district in writing twice in eleven years, recorded both letters in his own file, had no answer to either, and has stopped the practice of leaving the requester's name out of the refusals book for a reason he cannot account for. **That is as far as it goes and it is a real advance and it answers nothing.**

## OFF. 411

**OFF. 411 is a mark on four papers in four hands and a district renewal desk, not a man, and it is not mentioned in any chapter from 51 to 60.** No person is named in it and none may be. The only person in this batch who has ever had to think about an office mark is **District Officer Marten Cope**, and what he says is that an office mark is a reference to a filing place and not a man, and that he can name his own district's desks and cannot say what a mark in a district his office does not work in refers to, **and will not guess at it in writing, because a guess that is written down stops being a guess in about a year.** **This is a refusal to name a person, not a naming of one, and no later batch may turn it into one.**

## Threads this batch opened, and what they are

- **The household of six, and five different dates in one hand.** Six certified returns in one drawer, one name, one hand, five dates, and the brother-in-law has not been in the house for six years. **Tomas records that he does not know how the dates got there and does not guess.** This is the batch's one unexplained physical fact and it is a live thread. It is the first thing on the page that a clerk's record cannot account for, and Batch 0002 may or may not account for it; if it is not accounted for, it must not be explained away.
- **The hand.** Forward slope, hesitating descender, same pause before g, and the downstroke of the d and of the g stopping and starting in the same place. It is on the current year's form at the household of six, on a smith's bill for about nine and six, and on four returns in one file in a building in Fourmile. **It is not a forgery, not a conspiracy and not a mechanism. It is a man called Hesk Rann who fills in forms for people who cannot write, has been doing it for about nine years, does not charge, and has never once been asked.** He is not owed anything by law and is not owed anything by the batch, and he is a person, not a hand.
- **The clerk with a list and a copyist.** On the page twice, about four years apart, told by two people who have never heard of each other and did not join them up. He read names at four doors, asked nobody anything, and the list filled in anyway. **Nobody has worked out whether it was the same man.**
- **The district's sheet of four wards and four streets**, on the back of a Crown cart four years ago, copied into a smith's book and taken back. **A list of streets is a list of houses and it came into a shop lawfully and the shop did not know.**
- **The fifth panel of the road sheet**, gone from every copy in the same rectangle in the middle of a run. It is the panel that says where an answer goes. It is not an edition and not a fire. **Ruben Sallow says it is not printed, which is not true, and he has a new plate and has shown a Crown man the plate once.**
- **The refusals book at Fourmile**, about two hundred and forty entries in eleven years, in which any person may now ask to see and copy. **The refusal of the fourth question is the first entry anybody has ever asked for, and the district's finding of the twenty-eighth — withdrawn on the thirty-third by a surveyor — is still worth nothing to anybody, because nobody has asked for it.**
- **Marta Vell's week.** She wrote a page in her own hand on the thirty-fifth, with the hour, and did not let him read it, and keeps it, and has asked for a week before she does anything with what she was told. **The first name written on that page is not his, and she will not give it for nine days.** Nine days from the thirty-fifth is the forty-fourth, and Chapter 60 is the forty-fourth, and **no chapter in this batch collects it.** It is owed — **and because the batch has moved on, the term has lapsed uncollected, so any collection downstream is past the day she named. It is his lateness and not her breach: she set the day and he did not come to it, and she is not obliged to hand over the name because a man arrived late, and the whole of what she is owed is that he says so before she has to.**
- **Mara's leaf.** A loose leaf folded in four between two pages of Amos Trewin's book, found in the spring-to-last-year gap, with two lines copied off a Crown form, five words beneath them — *I have not taken this*, which is five words and not four — and a copying-wax mark about the size of a thumbnail. **It is not confirmed. Tomas cannot tell whether it is his sister's hand or a copyist's, he put it back where it was, he wrote nothing else, and he told nobody, including Tamsin Rook.** It must not confirm itself in any later batch without the volume earning it.
- **The Answer Rolls.** Named **once**, in passing, unexplained, on the last page of Chapter 60, in the mouth of a ferry-man, with a phrase before it: *there are rolls at Miremarket that answer for people who never went.* He was gone up the path before either of them could ask a second question. **They are not reached, not found and not explained, and no character in this batch knows they exist.**
