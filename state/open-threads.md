# Open Threads

These are active promises after Volume 01, Batch 0004. They are not resolved merely because a clue has appeared.

## Immediate Volume 01 threads

- **The question about the ward's children — now the Crown's to ask and the ward's to answer.** On Day 21 the commission nailed a printed notice saying the district will consider *the renewal of the ward's own line, of the possession of the ground in which it is laid, and of the persons under seventeen in the Low Ward.* At the last hour Serjeant Teale read a fourth paragraph: the district **will accept any answer** to whether the ward intends to remove the persons under seventeen from the ward before the next renewal and to whom, **will certify it**, and **a certified answer will be a return.** **Nobody in the ward has answered.** Rudd has already shown his hand by offering Surveyor Collis's certification to any household on the route, and Mrs Corris's line — *nine of our doors, out of forty-one* — is the sentence the ward will have to beat. **Nell Fenn still has not got the two people to stand beside her**, and she has told Mercy Slee she is not going to say it alone. **This is the volume's central remaining civic question and it must not be resolved by a bell, a bellmaker, or a child being saved.**
- **The Day 28 renewal.** Posted again on Day 21, with the form to be exhibited entire, and with the notice that **the practice of the ward board and the market court in the matter of renewal returns is a matter for the district and no longer for the local court.** The Crown took one child on Day 21 with the form exhibited and a surveyor present. The question the Day 28 batch must answer is not *can the ward stop it* — it cannot — but *what a divided town does when it has run out of procedure.*
- **The Day 24 register return.** Oren Pike's seven-day bell register — hours, places, no names — goes to the district at the close of the seventh day. It carries **four entries for the market lane on Day 19** and *none* in the name column four times, and a Crown man in Fourmile is going to want a name for four strikes in a market lane. **Perry, a nineteen-year-old Crown road hand, has already said aloud that she does not know and that she is the best witness the Crown has**, which is the most dangerous sentence anybody in Kells has said this month. The Crown also intends to require keeper names for seven days from the twenty-first, and Oren has refused names once and will have to refuse again.
- **Tomas's four days of silence** end on the **Day 24**. He gave no explanation of the market lane after the fifth hour of the morning of Day 20. When he does explain, **the ward already has a version with the Crown's own phrase in it that he never said** — *at first light*, put into a gate on the ferry road by Ivo Prentice at half past the eighth hour on Day 19 and repeated four more times that night, and still being repeated.
- **Ivo Prentice's gate.** He is the Crown's petitioner and the ward's repeater, and he has not been asked anything. He petitioned with nine adults in four households to be put on a Crown line, and he has spent two nights telling neighbours what a bell said with a Crown phrase inside it. **The two are going to meet and neither of them knows it.**
- **The Bellwrights' Measure's road map.** **Gone.** Fifty-three dated and keyed withdrawals in a district file with a Crown receipt, read by an officer who asked the year of the first and was told dates, and who has said he must file it as a schedule of non-response. **Sabin Fell was not asked and did not know until the Day 16.** Sabin's own craft years and the fifty-three withdrawals remain two different numbers and must never be merged.
- **The *Witnessed by* column — now public, and the Crown has answered it.** Published as a question on Day 18 and read aloud by Mercy Slee; confirmed on Day 20 by Stroud, in public, as **printed** on the full return, empty on every Kells return, filled in a household's hand. The Crown answered with a **supplementary return** carrying the objection, the witness name and the dropped reason field — the trap — and with **Rudd's offer of a surveyor**. The column's first fill in eleven years was **Mrs Corris's own name on Day 20**, and the column is **never cleared between rounds**, which is how a dead woman's name came to be a warrant on Day 21. **This thread is now a live civic instrument and the next writer must not let it become a conspiracy.**
- **The nine *not returned* lines and the six *accompanying* lines.** **Still unspent. Still not public in any room in any chapter.** Nell Fenn made the count on Day 12 and has now told a second person — Mercy Slee, in a kitchen on Day 18 — that she is holding it and is not spending it today. **It is the strongest unspent evidence in the town and it becomes public only when somebody decides to spend it.** Do not confuse it with the children question, which is a separate silence and which the Crown has now asked out loud in its own hand.
- **The Fenn household fragment.** Six persons on the Crown's line against three with a fourth cut away, in Nell's own hand, witnessed by Oren as copyist. **Still unused.** She will not use it because using it puts Jory and Edan in a public argument, and nothing in Chapters 31–40 has reversed that decision, and nothing should.
- **The sixty-five.** **Two hundred and six** persons on the Crown's figure against **one hundred and forty and one** on the market court roll, entered in the day-book as *whose office is counting.* **The forty-one and the fifty-three remain unrelated records in two offices and must never be merged or remarked on as matching.**
- **The office mark OFF. 411.** A mark on four papers in four hands. Oren calls it **a district renewal desk, not a man.** **No character in Chapters 31–40 has named a person in that office and none may.** How an office gets into a bell is still unanswered, and Chapter 25's answer and Chapter 27's doorway remark both stand unrepeated.
- **The second line under the Vale Bellhouse yard.** Two flat iron bars a foot apart, wrapped in tarred rope and pitch, running out under the yard wall toward the millrace; a damp strip the length of the shop. **Known to three people: Tomas, Oren, Jerm.** Entered in the watch gate book in three sentences. Not the Bellwrights' cut, not in the father's bench-book, on no Crown sheet the watch holds. **The word *line* was not to be said in a lane that week and has not been.** A Crown office that learns of it will send an order with a spade in it, and the answer to *who put it there* in this town is one word long.
- **The Crown's ground plan.** Foreman Brann has said the first mark **came off a plan sent up with the works order on Day 8**, and that he will find out who drew the ground part of it and write it in the gang's book. **He has not reported that he has found it out.** It is the same habit as the Hush rope cut and the same question: a document that answers for a night.
- **The buried line and the ward's drain.** The Crown's line is laid across the ward's own drain and **bridged at four points**; the Crown's sheet shows the drain in a different ink, dashed, as *existing culvert*; the Crown line is being renewed and backfilled over a filled service cut in the lane the children walk. Deputy Crake signed Tomas's ground page in his own name against his own works order, and said that if the drain is what Tomas says it is, a rain puts a foot of water in that lane and Renn's gate will not come. **Hollis Renn's gate has not come up since Day 7 and the water that came on Day 19 was a day early and a cause Tomas had left out on purpose.**
- **The line under the Bellwrights' Measure's fifty-three** and the district file. **The Iven Sore name** is still genuine-or-borrowed and **he has not appeared in Chapters 31–40 and has still not said he did not sign the withdrawn service entry.** Sabin's conditional challenge remains live and undischarged on that point.
- **The woman on the tailboard.** A woman of about forty on no warrant, entered in the watch gate book at entry fifteen on Day 10. **Still unidentified in any chapter.** She should be answered late, not soon.
- **House 133.** The household that gave no answer and no name on Day 10 and whose thirteen-year-old was taken. **Nobody went back for eleven days.** On Day 20 Mrs Corris walked into a full market court and put her own name in the Crown's *Witnessed by* column, and the Crown took no child from her house. That is the first thing anybody has done for that door since the tenth and it is not forgiveness and it is not an answer.
- **House 108, the Wildes.** A household that had never been marked, with a dead woman's name in its column and a girl of ten taken on a witnessed return at the fifth hour and thirty minutes on Day 21. **Mrs Wilde has been trying for eight months in writing to get a name off a Crown page and has been told to wait.** She is the sharpest ordinary grievance in the ward and she has never been to the board.
- **J. Vale and J. V.** The lock-register sign-out and the maintenance tag still echo one another. **No name or suspect is attached to "J. V." in prose and none has been attached in any batch.**
- **Surveyor roles stay distinct.** **Pever** is present and accounted for. **The missing docket surveyor** is still missing and is named only as Iven Sore on paper. **Rhea Collis** is named, present, has certified two rounds as rounds and neither set of answers, has put four statements and five refusals on the record, has stated in the open that she is **not** the person on the withdrawn entry, and has now put a sentence into her own Day 21 return unasked about what a form is for. **The Crown officers, Rudd, Teale, the unnamed district officers and clerks, and the filing clerks are not surveyors.**
- **The two households on the ferry road who went to Fourmile for a Crown line** and **the Crown's cart still across the low-turn culvert**, a fortnight and more old, which on Day 19 turned out to be the actual cause of the water that came up the ward's drain, and which Prentice volunteered to a Crown officer as an argument against the ward.
- **Gib Hask, Day 15 onward.** Gib Hask's six men, a crowbar, a spade and a coil of rope were at the low end of the turn for the whole of Day 15, and were not there on Day 16, and **no chapter in this batch has him put the bar in the ground.** His grievance is real and permanent and the Crown line is now permanently bridged over a drain that has no consent behind it. **He must not be written as a villain to be defeated and nobody may talk him out of a bar.**

## The outstanding carrier comparison

Oren arranged in Chapter 3 to compare Tamsin's courier handbell against the fixed Crown survey carrier. **It has still never been made, in any batch.** The carrier sits in the watch store and the joint receipt on the Crown file expired at the fourth hour of Day 10 without a custody review, and **Chapters 31–40 did not stage it.** If a later batch stages it, it is a new custody event and it needs its own receipt, its own witnesses and its own reason.

- **How does a Crown office get into a bell?** Still the open question. Chapter 25 raised it and deliberately did not answer it; Chapter 27's doorway remark is still unrecorded on purpose.
- What did Lira Vale know when she became a living anchor, and what parts of her choice were coerced?
- Where are the names transferred by the Crown's **name levy** physically sent?

## Relationship and character threads

- **Tomas asked the ward for a piece of iron before the act and was still wrong.** He also carried a bell into a market lane and rang it four times and asked nobody, and a girl of nineteen has a broken wrist because he wrote to her and asked her to stand where his words reached. He stopped it himself, recorded it in figures, named the two people who refused him, told the keepers before the ward, and is silent for four days. **The next test is not whether he apologises. It is whether he says the thing out loud and lets the ward have it.**
- **He put Tamsin Rook at the bottom of his list of four for two days and then moved her to the top and told her so in writing before he asked her anything.** She refused at once, in writing, and gave a reason her own terms do not require. He asked **before** the act. That is the growth, and it is on the page, and **she has not been thanked and has not asked to be.**
- **Tamsin has rung nothing in this batch, is owed nothing, and is not to be rescued.** She has stated four reasons in public for not ringing, the fourth of which is the one she has told only him. She keeps her route, her four terms and her bell.
- **Oren has narrowed his own authority twice** — once on the keeper name and once on the ward board's practice — and has traded the loyalty of his two best men for a gate with eyes in it. He is not Tomas's protector. **He is now a man whose district has his name, whose list of eleven names is in a file, and whose watch may be required to supply keeper names for seven days from the twenty-first.**
- **Sabin gave the rule he has never given anybody and refused protection, and found out that his life's work is in a district file with a schedule-of-non-response reading on it.** He has still not named what he carried past the gate in the Hush, and **he has never been told about the *Witnessed by* column.**
- **Nell has published a practice and lost the ability to keep one piece of her household's grief out of public argument, and has been told to her face that she made the decision happen and did not notice.** Her alignment is unspent. Her two people are unasked. She is sixteen and has now been named in a Crown register.
- **Halden has been right twice, out loud, and has paid for it in standing.** He is a man who signs his own name against his own works order and who says *I would rather have nine houses and a clean return* on a morning when he had thirty-seven houses and a child.
- **Stroud is the mechanism's ordinary face and has now said one true thing in a public room that he refused to write down, and has been asked by his office what he said.** Rudd is a man who makes his objection true and then offers a surveyor anyway, and the offer is the thing the ward has to beat.
- **The market court usher has now refused a fourth thing about her bell**, in open court, and the Crown's conduct of the round and Rudd's offer have both left it alone. Her distinction between a court hearing a matter and a court handing a thing to a man with a case is the ward's most useful sentence and it is still on the record.

## Chapters 41–50 pressure

- **Answer the children question out loud, in public, in a divided column, and let the answer be a choice nobody chose.** Not a bell. Not a bellmaker. Not Tomas.
- **The day after a round, in a town that has three hundred and forty people who came out in the dark and one child in a wagon.** The Day 19 residue of people who will move for anything is still there, and so is a Crown office that has learned to ask at doors.
- **Tomas must keep the route fragment, the answer columns, the copy of the schedule, the custody receipt he corrected, and a bell that is the wrong one.** He may not gain a power. He may not un-ring the nineteenth.
- **Keep the four named objects plus the plain carrier distinct.** The plain carrier is a legally plain handbell with a bar of the ward's drain in it and it must not be quietly upgraded into an instrument.
- **Do not burn the archive, close the workshop, execute a broad arrest sweep, or let Tomas leave Kells until the batch's own climax, and the arrival is the climax.**
- **The line under the yard, the ground plan that was drawn on Day 8, and the crowd at the market lane on the night of the nineteenth are three unanswered questions that all come from paper, and none of them is the mechanism.**

## Ending constraints

- Tomas refuses sole First Keeper authority and breaks the Crown clapper.
- The final Hearthline is federated, physically redundant, publicly maintained, and able to withdraw.
- Tamsin keeps a partial hearing loss; Tomas permanently loses direct magical route-hearing and the magical clarity of Lira's voice.
- Dain is captured alive and judged under ordinary law.
- The final world is freer, slower, and more expensive to maintain, not perfectly safe or harmonious.
- **The Vale Bellhouse is sealed and Tomas leaves with the same fixed stripped Crown survey carrier, in its mounting frame, in a reinforced padded cradle, with a route fragment and a question about who designed the copied-answer mechanism. Nothing in Chapters 31–40 has touched that arrangement.**
