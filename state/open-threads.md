# Open Threads

These are active promises after Volume 01, Batch 0003. They are not resolved merely because a clue has appeared.

## Immediate Volume 01 threads

- **The Day 21 revised round.** The Crown has revised the L-4 round and posted it for **the twenty-first day**, with the form to be **exhibited in full** and **any person admitted as a witness**. This is now the nearest hard civic deadline in the volume and the obvious place for the Crown to try again, this time with the form visible and a witness column that households have been told to look at.
- **Day 15, the low lane.** A Crown road gang is marking out with a line and a peg for a renewal of the buried line tomorrow, in the lane where the copper runs and where the children walk to the landing. **Gib Hask has six men, a bar and a spade**, has asked the market court in open session to say to his face whether it objects, and nobody has. Oren has no lawful power to stop him and has said so in the book. **Neither party intends what will happen, and both intend to be there.**
- **The eleventh hour of Day 15.** Oren must produce the **Bellwrights' Measure's road map** — fifty-three recorded ward withdrawals — to the district in the L-4 matter, and has entered that he does so under compulsion and not as a witness to its contents. A Crown seal on that map in a district file is a permanent fact about the craft, and Sabin has not been asked how he feels about it.
- **The *Witnessed by* column.** Established by Clerk Stroud on Day 12 and known to **Nell Fenn, Mercy Slee, Stroud and the reader**. The Crown form behind the schedule has a column for the person who was in the room when the answer was given; it is empty on all four hundred and six Kells returns he has filed in eleven years; it takes a name in a household's own hand; and a summary form drops the reason an answer was entered in office. **Nell burned her slate in the washhouse fire on the same afternoon.** Making this public is a decision with a cost, and it is the strongest unspent piece of evidence in the town.
- **The Fenn household fragment.** The only surviving paper that contradicts one specific line of the schedule — six persons on the Crown's line against three with a fourth cut away on the ward's paper. It is a copy, in Nell's own hand and spelling, witnessed by Oren as copyist. **She will not use it, because using it means putting Jory and Edan in a public argument.** That is a decision and not a failure, and the next writer must not let it be quietly reversed by a convenient event.
- **The sixty-five.** The Crown's own figure is **two hundred and six persons** entered as answering on route L-4. The market court ward roll for the same round is **one hundred and forty-one persons**, across **thirty-eight** households against the Crown's **forty-one**. The difference of sixty-five persons is entered in the day-book as a question Tomas cannot answer: *whose office is counting.* **The forty-one and the fifty-three remain unrelated records in two offices and must never be merged or remarked on as matching.**
- **The office mark OFF. 411.** Nameable as a mark on four papers in four hands, and Oren calls it **a district renewal desk, not a man**. **No character has named a person in that office and none may yet.** Chapter 25 deliberately left the mechanism — how an office gets into a bell — unanswered, and Collis's doorway remark in Chapter 27 is unrecorded on purpose and Tomas did not write it down.
- **The *not returned* and *accompanying* marks.** Nine lines and six lines, and every household the Crown took a child from is on one of those fifteen. The Crown's own notes about people who were not in the house were read by the Crown as the answer. **This alignment is not public in the ward.** It is Nell Fenn's own count, made on a slate from the ward board and from knocking in Chapter 26, and it has not been said in a public room in any chapter since. It is the thing a revised round could repeat more efficiently, and it becomes public only when somebody decides to spend it. **Do not confuse it with the other thing Nell withholds in public:** on Day 14 she declines to raise the question of the ward's children until she has asked two people to stand beside her, and that is a separate silence about a separate fact.
- **The woman on the tailboard.** A woman of about forty who was on no warrant, entered in the watch gate book at entry fifteen: *A woman rode on the tailboard and was not on the warrant.* Nobody has identified her. She is the most efficient unexplained thing in the volume and should be answered late, not soon.
- **Line 34, house 133.** A household that gave no answer and no name, whose line read *two returned, one accompanying*, and whose thirteen-year-old was taken. Nobody asked them anything. They are still there and nobody has gone back.
- **The Crown's four notices.** Three nailed on the evening of **Day 13** — the revised round for Day 21 with the form exhibited and any person admitted as a witness; the market court call bell and every bell joined to a line to be exhibited and described at the next sitting; and the declaration that the ward-board practice **originated in the Vale Bellhouse**, whose standing the commission will consider. The third is an attack on Tomas's trade, not on the town, and it has not yet had a consequence. The fourth was nailed on the **morning of Day 14**: a shorter notice convening the market court at the tenth hour to take the bell matter and requesting the court to produce the bell. The difference between a notice and a summons is the whole of the escalation and nobody had to explain it.
- **The Crown clerk who merged the divided answers.** Ansel Stroud, who admitted on the night of Day 9 that he has merged divided answers for eleven years because a ward that cannot make up its mind is read as a ward that will not answer; who sat behind a public Crown counter and wrote two sentences in a household's margin; and who filed the ward's three positions as a single response and asked to read it both ways. He is frightened, competent, useful, and **the mechanism's ordinary face**. He is also the man who will be asked who let him do it.
- **The market court call bell.** On Day 14 the court **produced its keeper and not its bell.** The usher stood up, which was not a thing she did, stated the three facts of the bell in open court, refused to have it described, exhibited and carried by an office that has not put a hand on it, and the clerk entered that the court had surrendered nothing. Crake said the commission had noted the distinction and would take it up with the district. The bell is still in the loft and still unbound and the distinction between a court hearing a matter and a court handing a thing to a man with a case is now the ward's most useful sentence.
- **The two households on the ferry road who went to Fourmile to ask the Crown for a bell.** They want a Crown line because only a Crown order moves the cart at the culvert, and the works order is a fortnight old. They are the corruption the volume is about, arriving on foot, for a good reason.
- **The cart at the low-turn culvert.** A Crown road gang's cart across a drain under a Crown works order, with Bevin Sarr's eleven witnesses on the record. The one thing in this volume that a Crown line would immediately fix, and therefore the most likely thing to be traded for.
- **Iven Sore's conditional challenge.** Live and only partly discharged. Sabin Fell has said on the page that Sore may discharge it, and that he will want a room, a witness and a day, and that Tomas should give it to him without asking why. **The genuine-or-borrowed question about the withdrawn service entry is still open and Iven Sore has still not said he did not sign it.**
- **J. Vale and J. V.** The lock-register sign-out and the maintenance tag still echo one another. **No name or suspect is attached to "J. V." in prose and none has been attached in any batch.**
- **Surveyor roles stay distinct.** **Pever** is present and accounted for. The **missing docket surveyor** is still missing and is named only as Iven Sore on paper. **Rhea Collis** is named, present, has countersigned one line of the removal record, has put four statements and five refusals on the record, and has stated in the open that she is **not** the person on the withdrawn entry and will not be made into him. The Crown officers, the black-case man, Officer Rudd, the case-man and the filing clerks are **not surveyors**.

- **What nobody in the ward has said out loud.** On Day 14 Nell Fenn told Tomas that the ward has spent eight days arguing about a column and **has not once said the word *children* out loud in a room where a Crown man was writing**, and that this is not a question that passed but a question four hundred and eleven people have agreed to be quiet about. Her slate carries **thirty-one households with somebody under seventeen in them**, of which **three lost a child on Day 10**. She has said she will ask **two people** whether they will stand beside her before she says it, and she has told Tomas in advance that he will not be told who, because if he is told first he will turn up. **He has been told to be told nothing, and he has been told that in advance.**

- **The Bellwrights' Measure's road map.** The district has ordered the watch to produce the fifty-three recorded withdrawals by the eleventh hour of **Day 15**. Oren will produce it under compulsion and has entered that he is not a witness to its contents. **Sabin Fell has said his piece in the yard, asked Tomas to write it down in his own words, and asked not to be in the room when Tomas decides what to do with it.** A Crown seal on that map in a district file is a permanent fact about the craft and every ward on it was a refusal, not an agreement.

## The outstanding carrier comparison

Oren arranged in Chapter 3 to compare Tamsin's courier handbell against the fixed Crown survey carrier. **It has still never been made, in any batch, because the carrier sits in the watch store and the joint receipt on the Crown file expired at the fourth hour of Day 10 without a custody review.** The Day 6 yard scene was a supervised reading of the wear only. The Day 13 walk was a reach measurement, not a comparison. **If a later batch stages it, it is a new custody event and it needs its own receipt, its own witnesses, and its own reason.**

## Relationship and character threads

- **Tomas wrote Nell Fenn's name into a Crown document without asking her.** He told her himself the same afternoon, she made him state the cost in the order *three children, then my name*, and she said he has never once asked her for anything that was not already written. **He has not yet asked her for anything at all.** The next test is whether he asks before the act rather than after it.
- **Tamsin has told Tomas he has no standing to know** in advance whether she will answer a request or refuse one, and he has written it down. She has lost the ability to carry, the towpath income, and most of the usefulness of the instrument she is most expert in. She must not be rescued out of it, and she must not become the reason a route is trusted.
- **Oren has conceded a lawful seizure, entered his own conduct, denied his own gate book's authority in his own hand, and refused a promotion out of the ward.** Crake has told him the district office will hear about him by the end of the week. He judges Tomas on evidence and is not Tomas's protector, and he has now been made to choose between a lawful order and a lane, in public, with a signature on it.
- **Sabin has warned Tomas that the trade will call the taking a judgment on the name within a fortnight** and that he will not protect him from it. He still has not named what he carried past the gate in the Hush.
- **Nell keeps a record in her own hand and is now holding evidence she cannot safely use.** She must not be turned into an argument Tomas can win.
- **The commission has escalated and Halden has not become a villain.** Three notices went onto the ward board at eight o'clock on the evening of Day 13 and a fourth, shorter summons was posted before the eighth hour on Day 14, and Halden's name is on none of them; he is on the page in Chapters 21, 22, 24, 25, 27 and 30, for filing Collis's sentences with his report, for reading his own answer out in open court, and for one sentence dropped into a gap in the proceedings on Day 14. He wants a record that says the ward was warned. He may escalate further and must stay legible. **Do not attach a duration to the escalations; the chapters give posting times, not spans.**
- **Ansel Stroud is a person with a family and a file and a conscience and no standing**, and the volume is better for him than without him.

## Chapters 31–40 pressure

- Carry the Day 15 lane, the Day 21 round, and the question about the ward's children that **nobody managed to say in a public room on Day 14** and that Tomas entered in the day-book as having passed.
- Build a warning from **a plain carrier and a divided town**. Tomas has no signal apparatus: the trial handbell is in Crown custody, the working bells are in Crown custody, the millrace bell is under a no-ringing order, and the only bell he can sound is a customer's counter bell that carries no claim.
- Let the **three questions** work and also cost something, and let the *Witnessed by* column either stay private or be published at a price.
- Tamsin's handbell route is about to be damaged in a way that is **not her fault and not anybody's fault**.
- Do not burn the archive, close the workshop, execute a broad arrest sweep, or let Tomas leave Kells. Those are Chapters 41–50.

## Ending constraints

- Tomas refuses sole First Keeper authority and breaks the Crown clapper.
- The final Hearthline is federated, physically redundant, publicly maintained, and able to withdraw.
- Tamsin keeps a partial hearing loss; Tomas permanently loses direct magical route-hearing and the magical clarity of Lira's voice.
- Dain is captured alive and judged under ordinary law.
- The final world is freer, slower, and more expensive to maintain, not perfectly safe or harmonious.
