## The three questions, and the state of each

1. **How does a Crown office turn one clerk's answer into a whole settlement's consent? — ANSWERED on Day 86 and untouched in this batch.** *It does not need to. A roll does not need anybody's answer; a duty is a mouth; a mouth is for a claim; and the claim is made in a queue, in about nine minutes, in the ordinary voice, by about eleven thousand people a year who are not clerks and are not agents of anything.*
2. **Where are names sent when they are registered? — NOT ANSWERED, and nearer, and it is nearer by a different amount than it was.** **On Day 90 a man with about nine years at a board in a town of about eleven hundred people said, in a square, unasked, in about nine seconds: *a name does not go anywhere, a name stops. There is about eleven thousand of them a year and there is a man and a barrow and about nine of these sheets, and I should think a very great many of them do not go past Ashmoor at all, and that is not a conspiracy and it is not a wall, it is a queue with nobody at the front of it who wants anything.*** **He said *I think*, twice, and asked for the word to be the word, and he does not know what is past Ashmoor and has never known and refused to guess. Tomas asked him nothing else and has said in his own hand that he is not going to go back and ask him anything else this year.** **OPEN.**
3. **Which office is it. — ANSWERED IN THE ONLY WAY IT CAN BE, ON DAY 96, AND THE ANSWER IS WORSE.** A name at last: **the Quiet Office.** And the name is the name of **about nine hundred rooms and one format**, and the question is not about a room, and nobody can tell him because nobody can. **A format does not have a door, cannot be refused, cannot be asked a question, cannot be argued with in a market, and has no face. A name is a thing you can be refused and a format is not a name. He has spent ninety-six days learning to ask a person and be refused and he has got to the end of the road and there is nothing at the end of it to ask. Nobody in this city is wicked. About four thousand people a year take a paper to a counter and are frightened of the second kind, and being frightened of it is the correct thing to be, and nobody has ever said so out loud and that is why nobody has ever been able to write a form about it.** ANSWERED, and answering it has closed nothing.

## The new question, and it is on a board in a capital

**A paper or instrument of which I am the keeper, of a kind used to repeat an answer, being of the kinds entered in the store list of the district.** That is the notice on the top terrace in Sennet and it is the whole of the volume's forward pull.

**WHERE A CROWN CLAPPER COMES FROM IS PARTLY ANSWERED AND IS NOT ANSWERED.** Provenance is answered: **a store in every district, a room, a counter, a book, a man, a shelf, an instruction, a number.** Named by a smith at an arch in about four seconds on Day 89 and by a clerk at a counter in about nine seconds on Day 95, and by two men who were not the same man twice. **Nobody has seen one. Nobody will say what it does. Tomas refused a theory four seconds before it was offered and has written down that he refused it and did not want it. The store list is open at a counter and the counter takes papers and not people and he has a form he cannot fill in.** **THE OFFICE, THE OFFICIALS ABOVE A DISTRICT, AND ANY PERSON IN OFF. 411 ARE ALL STILL UNNAMED, AND OFF. 411 IS NOT ON A PAGE IN ANY CHAPTER FROM 51 TO 110.**

**WHY THE OLD NETWORK ACCEPTS ONE IS NOT APPROACHED AND IS THE OPENING PRESSURE OF VOLUME 04.** It is seeded and not stated: a route that answers is a route that works, and a keeper who withdraws is a keeper whose district goes quiet. **No chapter from 101 to 110 says any of that, and no later batch may have a character say it as a conclusion before this volume has earned it.**
