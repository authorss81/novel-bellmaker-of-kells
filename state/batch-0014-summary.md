## What the batch was for, and what it actually is

**The Office copies him, somebody is arrested on his account, a sister chooses once, and three people begin to negotiate.** Tomas Vale swore four true sentences about himself in open court on Day 115 for fourpence. A fair copy went out of a room with a lamp in it on a cart with about nine other sheets at about the sixth hour of Day 116. A woman with a tray of pins and a hammer put two nails in it about four inches apart on a public board in a market, and about two hundred people read it in about nine minutes. A district is required to send an answer round. **Nobody in this batch is wicked, and that is the engine and not a softening, and it held for the officer who made the arrest, for the person arrested, for the woman with the pins, and for the man in the market who said a true thing and was right.**

## The ten chapters, in order, and what each one paid

1. **0131 — The Name Of A Thing On A Board Is Not A Person (Day 117).** A market, a board, a woman with a tray of pins, a woman selling needles, and a man who says yes in a market to a stranger. **The reading is the cheap part: about two hundred people read his four sentences on the first evening in about nine minutes, and the only person in the world who can take one of them off a board is paid the same rate for a Crown sheet as for a handbill about a horse, and does not read either, and there is nobody above her and nobody below her, and if she took one down there would be nobody in the world who ever knew.** And the reason there is no form anywhere in this kingdom for asking a district to un-say a thing: **a district has never once had to, because every one of the about eleven thousand lines a year is a thing somebody wanted to be true.** He stands in a market for about two hours and says nothing to anybody, which is what a man does in a market of about nine hundred people, and it is also what about nine people an hour do at a rail in a court hall four streets off, and about four hundred people a year go into that hall and have a true sentence of their own written on the wall for fourpence, and none of the three figures is about him.
2. **0132 — A Person Who Is Right, On His Account (Day 118).** **The batch's arrest, and the reason the batch exists.** A Crown market slip with six fields, four of them fillable by anybody standing there and one of them the name of the person who repeated a thing; about four hundred people a year put a name in it and none of them are wicked. **Hob Kersey** keeps a pitch and a hand-cart in the middle terrace market, has been named on a public board himself four days earlier, and on the hundred and sixteenth used the sheet on the wall as a precedent and said his own three true things about his own pitch in front of the market keeper with about nine people hearing. What moved him was four inches at ten feet from somebody he cannot identify and never will. **A district's paper is made with four boxes and one of them a day and the fourth saying *the district's business* in three words, and the district has given him a capacity — a claimant; a claimant can be ordered to produce and a witness cannot — and he is on a bench in a room off a lane with a form in it.** **Nyle Prout** is the district round and is right and refuses correctly in about four seconds: if Tomas says who he is then a district has a matter with his name at the head of it, a sworn answer goes round, there is no form by which a man who has been named can be unnamed, and the man on the bench would still be on the bench. **Tomas does not say.** The price is nine days, and the reason a man loses nine days over a thing he did in the open is the same reason a wharfman lost nine days in Fourmile — nobody will own a thing they cannot account for — and the objects are not the same objects and both are written out so that nobody adds them.
3. **0133 — She Chooses Once (Day 119).** **On the next board along on the top terrace there is a district's paper about a person in a market, up two days, with a woman's name at the head of it that is not his, and he reads it and cannot tell whether it is hers and refuses to decide** — two things he has seen himself inside one day in one city is a man arranging a thing, and a name at the head of a sheet is not a person. He does not take it off the board; that is the fourth time and the same decision. Then he goes down to the river courts and **does not read the new top row of a public sworn board and does not say why in that room**: a woman swore a true thing about herself, in her own name, for fourpence, at a counter, on the hundred and eighteenth, and a clerk tells him the count without being asked, and nobody asked her why because there is no box on that form for a reason. **If he reads it then she has answered, and she published an instruction saying she will not answer.** And the thing he cannot get round: **neither Kersey nor he can say what put the man on the bench, because two true things make a story and a story is not a form and a story dies with the only two men who can tell it.** He does not come back up the four flights. Third time, third and worst of the three reasons from the ninety-first.
4. **0134 — Three Sets Of Terms And No Reunion (Day 120).** **A room over a shop on the middle terrace that he pays for by the hour, because a man who is given a room is a man who owes.** Three sets of terms, said out loud, in the ordinary voice, in the open, and no paper. His four clauses, of which the hard one is his own: **no paper, he does not keep the terms and cannot produce them in four years, he will not take the same words to two people and come back with two the same, and he will not ask either of them for anything and will not say thank you.** Hers: **she is not going to be the second of anything, nor the first, nor a count, nor a room that three people meet in — said on the hundred and sixth, written down, underlined, unchanged in fourteen days — and she will hear a thing once and will not hear it twice.** The third set is Mara Vale's published standing instruction, read twice and not a third time, **and she does not know the arrangement exists, so it is made without her and it will hold whether she ever reads it or not, and if she comes in a year she is the fourth person to a thing already standing.** He says plainly that he wanted to be the clerk of it and is not going to be. The slate is not used and the non-use is not written down.
5. **0135 — The Rule Is Older Than The Room (Day 121).** **He sits down.** Three hours on a bench in a public room, and the reason is four seconds long: a bench is where a man waits to be served and he is not here to be served, **and there is no box in this kingdom on which a person has to say why they are on a bench.** The cheapest question again, in four seconds, and the new finding it returns: **the fourth box is the ground and is full on about nine hundred matters a year and is what a district's business knows, and the fifth column on the board is what the thing is for and is empty on about nine hundred a year and is a question nobody in this kingdom can answer — two columns ruled the same way four inches apart, one full and one empty, and neither proves one word about the third.** He reads the row of the man on the bench in about nine minutes and has everything there is to have about that morning and can do one single thing with it: **a complete account of a thing and the ability to do anything about the thing are two different objects.** And the last finding: the strongest fact he has and the second strongest are both facts a man in a room can see by reading, and neither can be carried, and the difference between carrying and reading is four inches long and is the same four inches as a court that publishes and an office that files, **and it is not about a building.**
6. **0136 — What A Man Does With A Proof He May Not File (Day 122).** Four instruments in a street with nothing in his hands, three refused, and the objection to the fourth is not about him: **his mother's name would be in it.** Then **half an hour in a gateway reading the crown of a bell he did not make** — a caster's mark and a day, the plainest instrument in this kingdom, saying the maker's name and the day and not one word about anything, not sounding and not broken and waiting — **and nobody stops him, and that is the finding: the arrangement that has let him into every room in this city for twenty-seven days is the same arrangement that lets him stand at a gate for half an hour.** The shape of a warning and the arithmetic against it: **one keeper, one carrier, one answer, all of them him, and a bell one man put up and one man struck is a man talking, and a man talking is the one thing a warning cannot be.** The chapter's finding is the trade and not a feeling: **the only thing nobody can take off him is the only thing he cannot use, and that is not a punishment, and a maker's whole life is work that cannot be edited and he has finally made one.** He puts his hand on the crown of the plain carrier and rolls it up and makes it fast — the second time in sixteen days, and he writes down that it is the same act and a different reason.
7. **0137 — The Market That Is On The Ground And The Room That Is Not (Day 123).** **Tamsin Rook's POV, and the chapter is the market's own fact: from the top of a market on the ground you cannot see the man at the bottom of it, and that is the only reason anybody has ever been able to say a true thing out loud in a market without an office hearing about it in nine minutes.** She has a true thing and there is nobody to say it to: **a man is on a bench over a thing somebody said to him in the open for nothing, and he cannot say what put him there and the man who could say half of it will not say it, and between the two of them there is a true fact that cannot be entered and cannot be produced, because two true things make a story and a story is not a form.** She says **four inches of his own words back to him** at ten feet in the order he said them and does not explain and is not thanked. She says the price of the fourth instrument out loud, about a head and not about him. **She does not take her bell out of the bag and writes one word about that to nobody.** Her finding: **two people in this kingdom hold something that cannot be produced, and it is not a partnership and it is not a friendship and she is not going to put a name on it, because a name is a thing that can be asked about.** Her four columns stand, the fourth has two lines, the price side is the other way up, and the count is eight and it is eight.
8. **0138 — The Clerk Who Wrote Two Things Down (Day 124).** **His four sentences are on a public wall above two rows belonging to two men he has been told about once each, four inches, at ten feet, and neither of them knows he is in this city** — Ord Marren, and a man with a barrow yard and eleven years of a licence who wrote eleven years on the back of his own hand in chalk. The new facts about a public board: **about nine rows fit, the bottom is rubbed out when there is no room, and the record of every row is in a book in that room which does not leave the building** — so the only place his sentences will be in five years is the district's copy on a cart, and he put it there on purpose. **The same three words — *the district's business* — are in the fourth box of a district's paper about a man who kept a gate in a yard on the hundred and third**, and a box that wants a reason gets three words nine hundred times a year, and the three words are not a lie. The clerk's three answers unasked: a chore is a thing a person does for himself and a firm is not a person; a sworn thing without an hour is a story; **a man who has been entered in a column once does not get entered twice for nothing.** Tomas does not thank him and **does not tell him that a file is made of every row and that his has been in four towns**, because the day a man comes to a rail and asks for a sentence that sentence has a man in it, and he would be putting a man in a column to make himself feel better about a thing he is not allowed to read. **The finding: a name at the head of a thing is above every name under it, and I cannot un-name a man and I cannot un-name myself, and those are the same sentence.**
9. **0139 — What The Bell Would Say, And Why He Does Not Say It (Day 125).** **A road walked end to end and nine bells counted**, which is the batch's one sustained physical action over a whole district. Three questions at each — do you strike it, who does, what is it for — an answer at four and nothing at five, **and the answer at all nine is the hour.** About nine hundred bells in this city and not one a keeper's bell and not one a plain bell somebody decided to hit, and a bell they have heard every day of their lives is not a signal, it is the hour. The shape of a warning, and the thing he cannot unhear: **on the hundred and sixth a bell went over a gate in a yard with nothing on the plate, and the yard let it ring and went in again, and the man who struck it said out loud that he does not know whether he is to and that there is nobody above him to ask — and every one of the nine hundred in this city is that yard, so a tenth is not a signal, it is one more hour, and a true thing in that position is a false one.** And the last finding: **a bell is not a document and a document is not a bell; the one he can do nothing with is his and the one anybody can use is the only sentence he has ever managed to get about himself, and the instrument that would carry a true thing out of him in one evening is the wrong shape for it.** Third time on the crown of the plain carrier.
10. **0140 — The Three Of Us, Written Down By Nobody (Day 126).** **The chapter's action is that he says nothing to anybody in three places**, and it is the direct answer to Batch 0002's structural debt. An hour in a market at a board where his own sheet has been up since the hundred and fifteenth, and a market has been held every day since and nobody has counted and nobody is going to, and **a district's paper lying loose at the right-hand end with the nails in the board and the sheet not on them, and the woman with the pins two boards along, and he reads the name from nine feet and does not stand there until she comes.** An hour at a rail with his four sentences third row down and two rows above his unread on purpose. Half an hour at a counter with no queue, and **Perrin Lowe: there is no form for a man who wants nothing and there is not going to be one.** The finding: **he arranged none of it, and the arranging is the whole of the damage, written in the same words as the asking on the hundred and sixteenth — the instant a man with no standing arranges a thing, the thing stops being about anything except him.** And the arithmetic: not a network, not scalable, not a warning, nobody can be entered in a column because of it, the other two have both said no to being anything, **and he has got one of the three answers and not two and not three, and the one is his own, and a no said in advance is not an answer to a claim because there was no claim, so there are three noes and not one agreement.** The closing sentence, which he is not going to improve: **a man who has proved an edit with a form and published a true sentence about himself on a public wall and lost the only sentence anybody had ever said about him is not going to be the man who says any of that was a victory.**

The month sweep returns nothing else. NO WEEKDAY AND NO MONTH WAS DRAFTED INTO THIS BATCH AND REMOVED, AND THAT ZERO IS RECORDED AS A MEASURED ZERO RATHER THAN AS A CLEAN SWEEP, because a state file that reports a clean sweep it did not have is the failure this project produces most often.**
2. **Every daytime hour in Chapters 131 to 140 is bare, and the ninth hour of the evening is the only half-day carried, and it is carried in all ten chapters.** **There is not one half-day on a live daytime hour in this batch and not one retrospective half-day either.** The clock is `state/continuity.md` §5a and there is no second copy of it in any summary file.

The in-world uses are a man's own day-book and are allowed.
- **THE RESERVED WORD WAS DRAFTED INTO FIVE PLACES AND ALL FIVE WERE REMOVED BEFORE THE FILES WERE SAVED**, at 0136 (three) and 0139 (two). The chapters now say **nothing in it that can strike** and **the receipt forbids any person from putting anything into it that could**, which is the same object described without the word, and the object description is unchanged.
- **The duplicate-line sweep returns ONE repeated line across Chapters 130 to 140 and it is deliberate:** *Whenever a building shows me a way of reading a person's own sworn word, I think about hers*, quoted in Chapter 0131 and written in the day-book of Chapter 0130, because it is a man's own habit written out on the ninety-eighth and quoted again on the hundred and seventeenth. **THE CLAIM THAT THE LONGEST VERBATIM RUN INSIDE A CHAPTER IS FORTY-ONE WORDS, IN CHAPTER 0140, WAS A HALF-TRUTH AND IS CORRECTED BY THE POST-BATCH REVIEW PASS: the run was real but it was a day-book re-transcribing three narrator paragraphs word for word, which is the defect §10c ruled on, and it has been re-derived. ## The live state at the end of the batch, in one paragraph

Tomas Vale is on a step at the foot of the top stair of a city of about nine thousand people at about the ninth hour of the evening on Day 126, **thirty-one days into the city**, with a barrow, a frame in a cradle four feet off him with four bolts made fast and nothing in it that can strike, a plain carrier rolled and against the wheel unstruck and forty-nine days untouched, **thirteen documents none of which is about him**, a public record of his own hearing that says the person named produced nothing, **a copy of his four sentences on a board in a market and about two hundred people read them in about nine minutes on the first evening and no later figure because nobody counts**, a market slip with his name in the fourth box in a book, **a district's paper with four boxes in a room off a lane with a name in the first of them that is not his and a man on a bench in it**, a store line in his head and not on a page, one empty fourth box out of about nine hundred forms and nobody looking at it, eleven lines nobody may copy, **a sister who has made a choice he did not read and will not find out about**, a market and a price list and a woman with a bag of pins who is paid the same for a Crown sheet as for a handbill about a horse, a clerk at a desk who has never once looked at the same row twice, **a bench in a public room that nobody will ask a man about, and nine bells in a mile of river road that are all the hour, and a district's paper on a public wall on the top terrace with a woman's name at the head of it that he cannot tell him from a hundred others and has refused to decide**, a notice lying loose on a board because the woman with the pins had not got to it, and a man with the only proof in the world and no instrument for it, and one of the three answers and not two, and the sentence *if there were three of me*, **carried forward and not resolved.**

## What Batch 0005 has to carry, and it is six things

1. **The arrest of Chapter 0132 stands and is not overturned.** Hob Kersey is on a bench, or has been, in a room with a form in it, held on a capacity the office assigned him, and nothing has been charged with him because there is nothing to charge him with, and nobody in this kingdom can say what put him there. **And a district's paper about a person in a market went onto a public board on Day 126 with a name at the head of it.**
2. **The sworn sentence and the copy of it.** Four sentences, about ninety words, in his own name, on a public wall, going round in a cart; about two hundred read it on the first evening; about nine of them will carry a name into four towns inside a year; and a district's paper about the row carries **three words in its fourth box** that are not a reason.
3. **Eleven lines in his head and nowhere else.** They cannot be got off him, cannot be given to anybody, cannot be made into metal, and there is no plate in a printer's room two streets off that cuts one.
4. **The three-person arrangement**, begun as a negotiation in a room he paid for, with three sets of terms and no paper and no plate and no clerk, in which he is not the clerk and is not the one owed, and the other two have both said no to being anything, and one of them has published an instruction to that effect and means it. **He has got one of the three answers and not two.**
5. **The plain carrier**, unstruck at forty-nine days, **and a route map he has not got yet.** Neither may travel before the climax, and Chapter 0150 is the climax.
6. **The sentence *if there were three of me*,** earned again in Chapter 0140 and **not resolved, and it is the sentence Volume 04 opens on.**
