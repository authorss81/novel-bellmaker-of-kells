## The batch in one paragraph

For twenty-nine days a name on a roll had been a mark in a column. **In this batch a name read on a roll begins, in about four years, to be read as a duty — by the household first, before any office — and a duty is a mouth, and a mouth is for a claim. The list turns out to be made in about nine minutes a year in about nine thousand market queues by people who are not clerks. A household that has answered twice believes it has agreed, and honours its debt, and puts a child on a cart. Tamsin Rook's reason for being on the road turns out to be a name in a town four counties away that has not been answered this year or the year before, and a name is not a place. The truth costs somebody who is not Tomas and is not refunded. The batch ends with a merchant's house on a roll, about fifty-one pounds that cannot be claimed, and a list of nine names on a slate that cannot be certified at all.**

**Nobody in this batch is wicked. That is not a softening; it is the engine, it held for Batch 0003, and it holds here without a single exception, including in a counting house and including the woman who put a child on a cart.**

## The two things this batch had to do that Batch 0003 did not

1. **The list begins to be a list of duties and the first person it costs is not Tomas.** A name is read aloud in a market queue by a neighbour who is not an agent of anything and is owed nothing. **Four years later the household has settled among itself, out loud, in a kitchen, which of the five of them it is going to be, and it settles it correctly. Honouring it puts a child on a cart. The child is not punished, is not an offender, is on nothing, is not named, has no age on the page, and is not found, not placed, not rescued and not brought back.** The whole price of a thing that took four years to become a duty was **one sentence from a woman at a rope stall, in about twenty-five words, to a woman in a queue, in the ordinary voice, with no document in it and no document in it now.**
2. **The truth costs somebody other than Tomas, and the somebody is not a villain.** **Paid in Chapter 83 and it is not undone at the end of Chapter 90 and it is not undone at the end of the volume.** One true sentence, out loud, in a room, in his own name, about a household he has no standing in. It went through a room and into a street, and a clerk — who is right, and who is not sorry, and who did not ask to be there — did the only thing there was to do with it, in the correct form, out of the correct column, **because a line in dispute cannot be shut on paper and a cart is the only instrument this kingdom has for a line that cannot be shut on paper.** Nobody apologises and nobody is forgiven. **And in Chapter 89 a second true sentence is said out loud in a merchant's own name about his own trade, and it is the best sentence in the batch, and it costs six households fifty-one pounds, and he refuses to bend it on purpose and asks for it to be written down that he refused.**

## The batch's civic law, and it is now binding

- A roll is a book of answers and **there is no book of names.** The names have to be somewhere, because a number has to mean something to somebody; they go **up**; and **there is no form in this kingdom that says what a district does with a number between the first of a round and the last of it**, and the person who would know is a clerk of eleven years who has never been upstairs and is not going to be.
- **A name on a roll becomes a duty in about four years, and the household reads it that way first, before any office, and it is read out of a queue by a neighbour, and the neighbour is usually right about who they are.**
- **A duty is a mouth. A mouth is for a claim. And the claim a household makes on its own name is not a claim against the office — it is the claim that the household is the one being asked.**
- **Being on a list that reads as a duty is not the same as being on a list that reads as a debt, and by the time it is a debt it cannot be got back by asking.**
- **A household that has been sent to twice reads a third visit as a debt coming due, and answers, because answering is what you do with a debt.** A mark in a column reads to a household as an answer, because a hand against a line is a hand against a question, and the only thing a hand against a question can be is the answer to it. **Nobody has ever told that household what the question was.**
- **Honouring a debt is the only thing anybody has ever taught the word *duty* to do.**
- **The list is made in queues, about nine minutes a year, about eleven thousand a year, by people who are not clerks, and it cannot be stopped and there is nothing to correct, because there is nothing there.** Two women said each other's names in a market.
- **A household can act on a practice and cannot act on a person. A refusal in a kitchen is not a record and goes nowhere. The only thing a household can put into a building is an answer, and an answer closes a line, and a closed line is the one thing in this kingdom that stops a cart. So the only power a household has is the power to answer.**
- **An office is for the district's answer. Four words, from a competent woman on a landing who gave him a paper and was poorer for the asking.**
- **A house may handle a dead man's money only if the house can show its own answering. You must be able to show a hand of your own before you may be entered in a book as handling a hand of somebody else's.**
- **A roll line in a trade's name carries every person who has ever been in the trade. A line is answered by one hand, and the hand belongs to whoever is at the counter.**
- **A mark in a column is not what ruins anybody. A line being open is.** And four men in a yard can read an open line as a debt the *firm* owes for thirty-one years, and every one of them will do it for himself in about nine seconds, and none of them will do it for the firm, **because the firm is not them and never has been.**
- **A word costs nothing in a building, and that is the only defence anybody in this kingdom has found against a line.**
- **An answer is a person and may be withdrawn. A duty is a mouth and cannot. Neither the roll nor the list needs anybody's answer, and the list was never in a hall.**
