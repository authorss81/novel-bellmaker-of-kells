# Batch 0007 Summary — Volume 02, Chapters 61–70 (Days 45–55)

Ten chapters, `chapters/volume-02/chapter-0061.md` … `chapter-0070.md`, written against `workspace/volume-02/batch-0002/PROMPT.md` and `outline/volume-02.md`. Chapter 60 is Day 44 and says the boat leaves on the tide on the forty-fifth at the top of the light; **this batch runs Day 45 to Day 55, one chapter per day, and no day is renumbered and no two chapters share a day.** Days 46 and 47 are the river and are inside Chapters 61 and 62.

## The shape of the batch

**Days 45–47, the river (Ch 61–62).** Ch 61 is Tomas on the water with the frame in its cradle. He works out wrongly, in front of Abel Rundle, what *the carriage* is under the receipt's second clause, and is corrected by the man he paid, and the correction goes in the record. He does not tell the ferry-man what is in the frame and does not lie about it: he says it is Crown property under a receipt, that the third clause says a request may be made by any person and not only an officer, and **he would rather Rundle asked now.** Rundle does not ask. He books the frame's place anyway and says one thing about the receipt, which Tomas writes down. Ch 62 is Tamsin on the same water: she wants to know whether a river road has a map and whether its fifth panel is gone, finds that the cut is in the plate, reads a stranger's road sheet off the far side of a table without asking to see it, and **gives the slate away to a person on the boat and does not ask for it back.**

**Days 48–50, Helling and Fourmile (Ch 63–65).** Ch 63 is a second district office, the same four questions, a form with no box for a reason, **the same hand on a return in a second district's file, and the name Pell Rill on an auditor's return quoted approvingly** — the first time that name is on any page in the novel. He is refused a copy and given the return itself to read where it sits. Ch 64 is Tamsin at a landlord's, not at a door: the same three questions she asked at nine doors, put to the man who is owed four months' rent by people who are not the ones who are leaving, and she is refused, and the refusal is reasonable. Ch 65 is Fourmile used properly: a demand refused in eleven seconds, Form nine of the second series, the book of refusals read aloud for two hours, the one hundred and ninetieth entry in a clerk's hand dated a year back from the twenty-sixth, the two hundred and seventeenth entry (the water bell) dated, the file of four returns and four districts with no shared bellwright, market day, parish or office, and the fifth question — *what is the book of refusals* — answered in writing in his own name. **Cope's finding: a shelf is a thing you can be shown; a door is a thing you walk through; Tomas has been asking for doors his whole life.**

**Days 51–55, past the county stone, at Amos Trewin's and in Bramden (Ch 66–70).** A smithy and a market town, a three-day market running the fifty-first to the fifty-third, a river post that goes down on the second and comes up on the fourth, and a bell bought, certified, rung once, and priced.

## Object custody and condition after Chapter 70

- **The fixed stripped Crown survey carrier** — with them, in its mounting frame, frame in its reinforced padded cradle, **the retaining bolts made fast at every place the frame has been set down**, no clapper, and the receipt forbids any person from furnishing one. **It is not rung in any chapter of this batch and it is not present in Chapter 68's market square.** Set down and bolted: the yard of a smith's shop at the four miles past the stone (the fifty-first and fifty-second), the yard behind a draper's shop in Bramden from the fifty-second to the fifty-fifth, **the yard behind the district office at Helling on the forty-eighth, which the chapter stages when he reads the deputy's sheet on the steps with the frame in the cradle behind him in the yard**, and a room in a town four days back (Day 50, Fourmile). **The receipt is not voided, broken, renegotiated or extended. All five clauses stand, including on the morning it is inconvenient** — and the second of them, *the frame stays in its reinforced padded cradle for the carriage*, is the reason he cannot pay for a bell with his barrow, because the cradle is bolted to the barrow's bed.
- **The plain carrier (Tomas's own bell)** — **IN THE SEALED YARD AT KELLS. It is not on the road and it is in no chapter of this batch.** Barred on the twenty-sixth from any public place in this county; origin not certifiable; no certificate can issue; the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and the licence is not stripped. He has not stood inside that yard since the twenty-eighth.
- **The trial handbell** — Crown property, never returned, **not mentioned in any chapter from 51 to 70.** No custody event is staged for it in this batch.
- **Tamsin's stolen courier handbell** — hers, in her bag. **Not rung in any chapter of this batch.** Her four terms of the thirteenth day stand, unvaried. Nobody asks her for a reason at any hour. Her deafness is permanent; she can feel a bell in a sill and in a floor and in a wall and cannot hear a note, and she says so, and is not rescued, and is not asked for a reason.
- **The millrace bridge bell** — in the sealed yard of the Vale Bellhouse. Not on the road, not mentioned.
- **The market court call bell and the wheelwright's counter bell** — not mentioned in any chapter from 41 to 70.
- **The Vale Bellhouse archive** — taken and destroyed; the yard sealed; nothing recoverable. The Crown notice about an originating workshop is still nailed to the ward board in Kells and is not on this road.
- **THE NEW BELL — the only bell in this volume that he owns and is allowed to be proud of, and it is a bell with nothing in it.** A **plain carrier**, **fourteen pounds two ounces**, bought on the fifty-second at the smithy four miles past the county stone for **one pound fourteen shillings**, of which **the metal is three shillings and fourpence at Trewin's own price out**. Cast by **Roald Byre of Ardwick** at about the eleventh mile in the spring. **No wax in the crown, no dressed groove, only the caster's mark and a day.** The making is complete: **mould and pattern in Byre's book against the day struck in the crown, mark and day in the metal, the hand being the punch**, and the five are joined at the mark. The metal is traced **back through two shops to a Crown store with a Crown document and a day on it**, and the store keeps no further record, and **that crease is permanent and no more of it exists.** It is **not cast by Tomas and he cannot cast it.**
  - **Certified** on the fifty-second at about the third hour of the afternoon in the room over the saddler's shop, by **Rowan Ketch, master, for this company**, in his own hand, with the hour, on one sheet, **with no second copy and none left behind**, and **witnessed in the room by six persons of the town whose names are on the circuit sheet beneath, one of whom wrote his own name himself.** It is **the first certificate any object in this volume has had that is not a Crown receipt and not a bar.**
  - **Rung three times in its life.** Once by Ketch, **struck twice on a leather, an inch apart, with a wooden shaft, to sound the note before signing** — which is one occasion and two blows. Once by Tomas, **on the fifty-second, at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, on a sill in a room over a draper's shop, on a leather, with the door shut and one person in the room** — and half the sound went out of a window and could not be got back. And once, **in public, in Bramden market square at the top of the hour on the fifty-third, by Tomas, in front of about nine hundred people, and that is the only public ringing of it in the batch.** **Three occasions, and any later writer who says "twice" is counting occasions and not blows.**
  - **He has not rung it since the fifty-third.** In Bramden it stands on a sill in a crate with the sacking off, and the frame stands in the yard behind with the bolts made fast, and the batch closes with it in a crate.
  - **No claim of any kind is in it.** That is the volume's argument, not a disappointment, and it is stated by Ketch, by Tomas, and by a woman with a barrow in a market square.

## Non-bell documents now on him, all traceable

3. **NEW — a sheet in a deputy's own hand at Helling, Day 48, given to him at the counter and read twice.** Asked of **Ilma Carrow, Deputy District Officer** (entered in the record as **I. C.**, no age): *what is the schedule for this district — is it a record of what the households of this district said, or is it a record of what somebody in this building said about what the households of this district said.* She **looked at him for a moment, went and got a sheet, and wrote for about eleven minutes**, and gave it to him, and it is signed **— I. C., Deputy District Officer, this office, this day, about the sixth hour.** What it says: **it is a record of what this office holds to be the answer of a household, and it is not a record of what any household said, because no household in this district has ever said anything to this office in a room with a person in it; it is made out of returns received at that counter, entered by the hand of whoever brought them; where a household has sent a return it is a record of what the household sent and it is a good record; where a household has not sent a return and the year closes, the line is carried.** And then, in the same sheet, in the same hand, unprompted: **she does not know what it is carried from, she has asked twice and been told it is carried from the standing entry, she has never found a standing entry, she has been four years in this post and has never asked the district.** **It is the only one of his sheets not written by a man who was being careful.** It is a fourth-district instance of the finding that recurs all batch — *a record of what the office holds to be the answer of a household, and not a record of what any household said* — and **no state file may report it as a different question from the one Pell Rill was asked on the fifty-fifth.** **The chapter's own numeral for it is "the fifth sheet he has" (`ch63:146`); that is the chapter's figure and it is left exactly as written, and a later writer who needs to count sheets must not silently restate either figure.**
4. **A carbon of Form nine of the second series**, entered at Fourmile on the fiftieth at about the ninth hour, given to him without being asked, **the only copy outside that building.** On its back is his own reading-out of **the one hundred and ninetieth entry** of the book of refusals.

## What the batch pays, and what it does not

- **THE NINE DAYS AT OCKLEBRIDGE ARE PAID, AND THE ANSWER IS NO.** Tomas wrote the letter himself at about the fifth hour of the morning of the fifty-second, before he could talk himself out of it, and tore the leaf out of the day-book. **The whole of what he owes her is said in it: he did not come on the forty-fourth, the term was hers, a term that runs out has run out, and the reason he did not write on the forty-fifth is that on the forty-fifth he found out he was going to want something, and a man who wants something should not be the one to reopen it.** He asks **one** question and it is not the name: *does the nine days still stand.* The letter went down on the fifty-second at about the tenth hour and cost eleven pence and is dated the fifty-second; the post is at Ocklebridge on the fifty-fourth. **Marta Vell refused, in four lines and a paragraph, in her own hand, with the hour, and it is the first refusal of a term she set.** She gives two things: *you did not write on the forty-fifth and I heard that afterwards from a carter and I would have written on the forty-fifth*; and **a fact — the day they entered her bell is the day it goes in the book, and it is not the day anything happened at her door, and there was no man at her door on any day at all, and it is earlier, and she has not been able to find out by how much.** **She gives no name and says she is not going to be asked for it a second time.** Tomas records that the fact is **not one of the five dates** — it is a date against a line about a bell in a book with a number on the spine, not a date against a certified return in a drawer in a house of six — **and that holding a sixth date of the same kind did not make him feel cleverer, and that he is still not going to guess how the five dates got there, and the thread stays open.**
- **The leash is used a fifth time and worked a fifth time.** Counting Mercy Slee's writing of the slate on the thirty-first: the flat fork on the thirty-fourth (she refused him), the shop on the thirty-eighth (she asked him), the ninth door on the forty-third (she refused him once), and **on the fifty-third at about the eighth hour of the morning, when he asks her to stand where a market of nine hundred can see her next to the bell, and she says no, once, in one word, and gives him a place instead of a want, and he does not ask again that day.** It cost them both: he rang with nobody he trusts beside him and the square filled the gap in nine ways, and about eleven people in that square said out loud that they had seen the woman write, and she was ten feet off. **She is the only person who has ever rung at all in this volume and she has not rung.**
- **The five dates are not accounted for** and are not explained away, and are not a forgery and not a conspiracy. Nobody in this batch is wicked.

## The document that has to be read wrong on purpose

**The approving return of a practice.** It recommends that where a household is returned as unable to make up its mind, the office record **one hand** against the line and treat the line as answered, and it calls that **a settlement of the difficulty**, and **the difficulty is a household**, and it gives a reason that is true: a household is not a person, a schedule does not hold open, a second round is a cart and a man and a day, and a man will not come twice to one door. It says the household is informed and may come and dispute the line at any hour the office is open. **Every word of it is correct. It is the best piece of paper in the book and Tomas spends four days failing to find the false sentence in it, and the two things not said in it are that a household is informed *by some means at some point*, and that one hand does not know what the other four hands wanted.** He does not denounce it. He gives it to one person and asks her to decide and then does not visit her again.

**Pell Rill's number**, given in a room, in a room of one, and **never in any document:** about eleven thousand held lines a district year at about nine minutes of a clerk's day; the office loses **about four hours a day**; two hundred working days, **eight hundred hours**; a clerk at **eleven pence an hour** to the district; **about forty pounds a year**; about two per cent of the district's answer; and about three hundred and sixty pounds a year across nine districts. **And the half that is his and not the district's: the truth buys a household four hours of argument a day that it did not ask for, and the argument is a cart and two men in a kitchen and either they agree — in which case the line was never *unable to make up its mind*, they had not been asked — or they do not, and the line is still open, and there is no third.** He also said that he has recommended one practice that was **not** sound, in a year in another district, that it stood four years, and that it was found by accident by a person who had no reason to be looking and had not been paid to be. **He is not converted at the end of the chapter. Nothing in the chapter explains a copied-answer mechanism, names the Crown clapper, identifies a higher official, names a person in OFF. 411, or touches how an office gets into a bell.**

## The line the batch ends on

Tamsin Rook's answer to *what is the document* is **in an auditor's own hand in an auditor's own book, in a town two days down the water, with her name at the head of it and the hour against it**, and she gave it knowingly, on purpose, and priced it three days earlier, and she is not going to be sick about it. **Rill sent for her himself, behind Tomas's back, told her precisely what it would cost, told her she might say no, and did not ask a second question — and that is the first time in nine years an answer has been given to him by somebody he did not meet, and it is why he is the most dangerous thing in the book so far, because he is the first person who is not hiding from them.** He offered, twice, to be asked anything. He told Tomas in advance that Tomas has no standing in a clerk's hall. **He did not name the town and Tomas does not have it.**

**A record has to be kept somewhere. It is a shelf and a man and about eleven thousand lines, and it is two days down the water, and there is a town with a clerk's hall in it, and the two words are still not on anybody's page in this batch and are not spoken by anybody in it.**
