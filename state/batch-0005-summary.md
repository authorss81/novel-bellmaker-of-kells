## Batch result

The Crown arrived on the twenty-eighth with a form, a list and two lines, and the ward answered it out loud in four columns and one bell went over a market town before the wagon did. The archive burned in the yard at the third hour in front of a hundred and forty people, the workshop was sealed at the fifth, and Tomas Vale was taken out of his own lane at the sixth hour of the evening. On the thirty-third a district clerk in a real town withdrew a finding, a watch captain released a bell in a cradle that cannot be rung, and a bellmaker left Kells before dawn with a question he cannot answer.

**Day 22 — a procedure that worked and one child, on the same sheet.** A printed extract of Surveyor Collis's return came up from Fourmile on the morning post and was read at the ward board: thirty-seven doors, the form exhibited entire, **twenty-two objections in margins in a clerk's own name and initialled, of which nine record the answer *I am not permitted to say*, one person removed at the fifth hour and thirty minutes, and the *Witnessed by* column filled in two lines and not cleared between rounds.** It carried Collis's four sentences about a form and a round that is conducted properly and is not lawful. Tomas Vale handed his own fifty-one-line page to **Mercy Slee** and kept nothing, and was asked about the market lane four times and refused four times, and at the fifth ask, by **Absalom Teage** in a lane with about sixty people, gave the same reason he gave on the twentieth and would not improve it. And **Absalom Teage then began asking one question at each door — *what did you do on the twenty-first, and who saw you do it* — and got to nineteen doors by the second hour of the afternoon without being told anything by anybody.** The Crown's answer that day was not a notice; it was a **second-series supplementary return** to every household, with the reason field the ward had demanded, a witness field, no signature required, and a deadline at the close of the twenty-sixth.

**Day 23 — the column is a practice, not a story.** Nell Fenn asked **Mrs Wilde of 108** to come to the board and was refused for about nine seconds and then got the hardest bargain of the month: she will read the question at the **side** of the clerk's table with her back to the lane, and she names her own price in advance, and the price is that she reads her own question. Nell published a second question in a household's own words — *is there a name in the Witnessed by column on my line; is that person living; whose hand is it in* — and knocked it at eleven doors. **The never-cleared column was found in other houses, including a house the Crown has never marked and never visited, which carries a dead woman's name in a witness column and no child on any line.** A Crown officer came to the ward board to ask whether a list of households intending to remove a child exists, and Nell had the word **none** entered in three hands.

**Day 24 — the district names a man, and Tomas asks in writing.** A district form at the second hour required keeper **names for seven days commencing with the twenty-first, a day already elapsed**. Captain Pike refused the second time in a long entry of eight paragraphs, the second of which is his own arithmetic: that he knows who sounded the four bells in the market lane on the nineteenth, has not said it in any book, and is entering that fact so that he will not hold a private file. **He names nobody.** The bell register went to Fourmile in a leather wallet and a district rider read it aloud on the gate step in a lane in front of nine people: **twelve bells, twelve times *none*.** The district's return named, **before the register had arrived, the officer who would read it — District Officer Marten Cope, of the district renewal office at Fourmile** — and Oren read the name out and entered it. In the evening, at the low drain gate, Tomas said the four days' worth of things out loud once, including that the market lane was his fault and that **she could not tell whether he rang it once or four times** because she cannot hear a note and never will. He wrote his request — a paper, on foot, at doors of her own choosing, her own first term, with a finding on the other side of the fold — gave it to Jerm as witness, and did not press. She took it and the chapter does not say what she did with it.

**Day 25 — the answer is a decision about who answers.** The ward's position was stated in a market court with about two hundred and thirty people in it, in three columns, and the third column was empty. The turn is that **a certification is a witness and a witness is a person, and the district can certify anything inside a column and cannot certify the three together** — and an empty column can be counted two ways and both counts are true, where a merged answer is a sentence, and a sentence is the one thing a Crown office has never had to check. **Oren Pike certified the room and refused to certify the contents, the first time in eleven years he has done that.** Between about thirty and forty households then came and said one of three things themselves. **Mrs Corris of 133 put her hand up in a full court and took the Crown surveyor's certification, and said out loud why, and the ward did not treat it as betrayal and she said so herself.** The district's reply was an instruction to **Crown Officer Ambrose Rudd** to attend at the ninth hour on the twenty-eighth with four things, the third of which is the authority to open the ground of the Vale Bellhouse yard, the second of which is the two series of supplementary returns with the house numbers of those who returned them, and the fourth of which is the full return entire, plus a fourth line under the fold requiring the watch to say whether any bell in the ward is bound to a claim and by whom, and not accepting *none* as an answer or a refusal.

**Day 26 — the Measure is right, and about the right thing.** **Iven Sore** read four clauses of craft law into a room and entered a **professional bar on the plain carrier**: it was cast in a yard under a Crown declaration, out of a hearth stock that cannot be traced, so its origin cannot be certified, and an uncertifiable bell can be used and cannot be traced, and that is a hazard to the public and not only to the trade. The bar is on the **bell, not on Tomas's licence**; the Measure was asked by four houses to extend it to the licence and refused, and Sore said he was one of the four and is still right and is right about a different thing. Tomas did not defend himself and said out loud, in the room, **that he does not know what a bell is for.** And in that room, which Sabin Fell had asked not to be in, Tomas decided to put the Measure's road map on the record in writing — the district filed it as a schedule of non-response and the map's own key distinguishes four reasons — and Sabin found out within three hours and came up the lane at dusk and **was angry, and was entitled to be, and said: *I was in a hurry, and being in a hurry felt like duty, and it was not duty, it was the other thing.*** At the door, alone, Sore put the one question he can support about the withdrawn service entry, which is **whether the Crown withdrew a commission the Measure granted, or withdrew a name** — and the genuine-or-borrowed question is exactly where Chapter 14 left it, because Sore repeats his own incomplete answer and still does not say he did not sign it.

**Day 27 — the spade comes, and it is Tomas's own record that sent for it.** The authority reached the ward because **Oren enclosed a fair copy of the watch gate book's entries for the fifteenth with his return of the seventeenth**, and his own three sentences about a damp line under a bellmaker's floor are why a district sent a schedule. **Surveyor Rhea Collis came into the ward on the twenty-seventh with a spade of her own and made two objections on the page: the authority, and a signal sounded from a gate bracket with no duty, no limit and no place where it stops.** The three decided separately and the yard was opened: the Crown wax was broken **at the Crown's own request**, the floor came up at the fourth hour, the bench-books came out into daylight in front of forty people, and at about the eighth hour of the evening the line was lying open in a trench and **it did not go toward the millrace; it turned and went out under the front of the shop toward the north road.** A bearing was taken and read out in a lane. **Nobody said it**, Oren did not write it, and Jerm entered that he did not write it and why. **Deputy Reeve Crake applied in writing for the millrace bridge bell** — a properly made paper, with a keeper, a limit and a return date — and **Oren granted it in the lane before about thirty people, naming Jerm, and entered his own reason: that a watch which tells the Crown it has no instrument is handing the Crown a reason to invent one.** Collis, who had come back for the ground, wanted the register and said why — that a certified record of unaccounted bells is a finding of civic disorder on a two-line form, and she would rather write *not established* — and did not get it.

**Day 28 — the arrival.** Before light, **Gib Hask's six men took ninety feet of Crown backfill out of the low-turn culvert with a bar**, which is illegal, which nobody talked him out of, and which brought **Renn's gate up for the first time since the seventh day** and put sixty people at a flood gate where a Crown form with two lines on it was waiting to be filled in. Wenna Cray **withdrew from the bell in writing, in her own words, before it was sounded and without knowing whether it would be**, and the withdrawal is recorded as a withdrawal and not a silence, and Tomas rang with **a count of one**. The warning was rung once at about twenty minutes to the sixth hour, out of the wind, in words that name the known facts, the unknown, his own duty, his own bar, his own keeper and his own fault, and that **tell nobody what to decide**, and it is on the page in the copyist's fair hand. **Jerm sounded the millrace bridge bell twice at the north gate at the sixth hour under a lawful grant, and the two sounds were ninety seconds apart.** The Crown came up the west road and the half-closure was a bar, a chain and a man with a lamp, and a wagon **went over the bank at the mill narrows and took the top four of Alis Marrow's mother's steps into the water.** The ward did four things in four columns and nobody merged them: nine households moved, twenty-one stayed, eleven children in eight places, four doors refused. **Tamsin Rook carried her stolen handbell the length of her route and rang it once at a door, because Foreman Crane asked her to at a quarter past five and named what he wanted repeated, which was *come to this door*.** Nell got thirty-one children off the landing in forty minutes. **Rudd produced the four things at the ninth hour and found that the third had been discharged yesterday by a Crown surveyor who was not asked to**, and read out a district finding that the ward's bells are unaccounted for, **and Tomas Vale stood up in a full market court and certified his own words as the Crown's evidence against himself, and had the district's sentence read out three times by a copyist with a cough.** **One child of nine was not taken, at house 114, because a carter had filled in a Crown's own box in his own hand on the twenty-second day, the Crown's list and the Crown's district file disagreed, the cart from Fourmile did not come on the morning of the twenty-sixth, and a Crown officer of twenty-six would not be the man who contradicted his own district in a lane. Nobody in Kells may say the bell did that.**

**Day 28, later — what burns.** Six persons were arrested, each on a named ground, and **Oren Pike wrote the record of each arrest in his own hand and gave each person a copy**, which is the only paper any of them will have. The six are **Gib Hask, Mrs Wilde of 108, Wenna Cray, Ivo Prentice, Louisa Tace of 44, and Absalom Teage** — the last of them the man whose own return saved his son. **House 108 was marked again and no child was taken and Collis countersigned the objection and not the answer**, which put a surveyor's name in a *Witnessed by* column beside a dead woman's for the first time. The six names were read out in the afternoon and **all six came off the district's amended return of the seventeenth, in Oren's own hand, and Oren wrote the paragraph of his life he has not taken out.** At the tenth hour Rudd produced a **fifth schedule**, which says the workshop's records are Crown material, are to be taken and copied, and that **the originals are to be destroyed in the yard in daylight in the presence of the ward with the hour entered by a copyist.** Tomas gave up the L-4 papers himself, off the shelf, naming each one aloud. Then the copyist came to the bench-books, and one of them is for **Reckoning 619**, and it is the only record in the world of what a bellwright's shop wrote in the year of the Hush, and it was burned in the ash-pit in three goes at the third hour in front of about a hundred and forty people. **Tomas did not ring anything and did not ask, and the reason is the sentence of the volume: a bell cannot stop a fire, and a bellmaker who thinks it can has stopped being a maker.** He tore one leaf out of the running bench-book and gave it to **Bevin Sarr**, who has refused three times in this month to be a witness and is on no list. The yard was sealed at the fifth hour with a district wax and the watch mark under it. Tomas was taken at the sixth hour and asked for one thing, which was to see his custody receipt, and read the margin aloud in a lane, and the copyist's initial is still not agreement. The last thing out of the yard was a page.

**Day 33 — the custody review.** Days 29 to 32 are compressed at the top of the chapter: taken down the north road to a district building at Fourmile on the night of the twenty-eighth, **Mrs Wilde released on the thirtieth** because the district could not hold a household of record on a witness column without a witness and Collis refused to certify her line again, **the remaining five released without charge together on the thirty-first** on Crake's own single sheet, **Crake relieved of the Kells commission for recording disorder and co-operation on one paper**, the Crown cart off the low-turn culvert on the thirty-first, the backfill put back on the **thirty-second**, uncompacted and unchecked, **Foreman Brann's entry on the thirty-second in the gang's book that the ground part of the Day 8 plan was not drawn by the road office and came up with the works order from Fourmile on a folded third sheet**, and **Nell Fenn spending the nine *not returned* lines and the six *accompanying* lines** as a question the district has answered correctly and carefully and has also written down, in a district file, that a girl of sixteen of line twenty-two asked about her own line and that the office does not propose to ask why. At the third hour of the morning of the thirty-third, at the watch house, a **custody review** is held, and **the comparison Oren Pike arranged in Chapter 3 is made at last**, as a new custody event with its own receipt, its own witnesses and its own earned reason: a district officer who cannot answer *whether any bell in this ward is joined to any line, wire or ground* has written and needs the only instrument in the ward whose manner of working is recorded. **Tamsin Rook unwrapped her own bell and set it on the cloth.** The reading shows a **technique and not a person**: both bells were altered at two removes from a Crown pattern, one made to carry further and one made to carry nothing, with the same cut of **riffle file** on the courier bell's re-cut crown and on the survey carrier's crown, and **the mechanic — how a Crown office gets into a bell — is not answered and is not approached.** Collis's return **withdraws** the district's finding that the ward's bells are unaccounted for and makes no finding against the watch or any person, and it cost the Crown nothing. **Oren releases the same fixed stripped survey carrier to Tomas Vale in its mounting frame, the retaining bolts released only for transport and made fast again at every place the frame is set down, the frame in its reinforced padded cradle, with four clauses and a fifth clause Tomas asked for and Ferrow wrote.** Tamsin gives back the Day 24 request with one line on the outside of the fold: **no, and no reason, and you did not press me once** — and travels with him on a slate written by **Mercy Slee**, which neither of them wrote first, which puts a leash on both of them, and which she read out to each of them separately. She says out loud in a lane that **a warning does not require her to obey him.** The Vale Bellhouse sign hangs crooked over a locked door. On the river road at about twenty past seven she rings the handbell once, waits, and gets an answer from **Ocklebridge, a village of about two hundred that has not yet heard the Crown's order**, and she does not repeat it for anybody and nobody asks her for a reason, and Tomas Vale is not the man who sent it.
