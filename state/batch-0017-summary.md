## What the batch was for, and what it actually is

**A man who has proved a road can be walked now has to get somebody to walk it, and the only two people who said yes to a day are a carter with a barrow and a girl on a wall, and a road is nine miles long, and the honest figure on the page is four days and four miles, which is a mile a day with a barrow, and that is the rate for a full walking day and not the rate for the whole road, which Chapter 0160 gives as nine miles in ten days.** This batch took those four sentences out into the country they were taken from, past the yard of the coach inn, up about seven miles of road nobody has drawn for him, and into a street of about forty houses with a fire going in it.

It paid eight things and refused to inflate any of them. **No road is found, restored, registered or explained. The rectangle is not explained and is not joined to anything. The panel for the district above the inn is not restored and its name does not go in the book. Nobody is entered anywhere as a household. Nothing is rung, nothing is bound, no stage comes, no sense comes, and nobody is thanked in ten days.**

**Nobody in this batch is wicked and that is the engine, not a softening.** It held for Pell Okell, who said yes before the sentence was finished and then asked for it to be written down that he had thought about it; for a girl who will not let a man with a barrow near her basket and who tells him she would rather have had a different reason for a true thing; for a man with a dog and a gun who said no before Tomas opened his mouth and who has been waiting five days to find out whether Tomas kept a list of the nine noes, and had not, and is not going to; for a woman at a door under a bank who said nothing should be blamed and filled in her own gate herself; for a district's road surveyor who has two true numbers about one length and will not enter either of them; for a man at a fire who took a name out of a district's mouth because it is a word a cart said; for a man with a shovel who told him a price and then told him he was not being thanked for it; and for five people in a street of forty houses, one of whom told him to go up his lane.

## The ten chapters, in order, and what each one paid

1. **0161 — Two Barrows On The Same Nine Miles And A Figure That Is Not A Rate (Day 147).** A flat at the top of a lane, a bend, the yard of the inn he walks past and does not go into, two barrows, a man with a load of nothing. **Pell Okell says he noticed Tomas did not ask him twice and wants that in whatever he is writing.** A fifth name that is not a name: the nine miles between two ends has never been called anything, and what he calls it is a word two men use because they both use it, and the word is the only currency he has. **The finding: a barrow is one reason for a man to stop and I have been writing that down as a road for ten days; one barrow gave about one question a day and two gave six people, and about four in ten of what any of them says is right.** And the arithmetic, corrected on the page: **four days and four miles is a mile a day with a barrow, and nine miles in ten days is a different figure, and there is no figure for the days that were not whole, and a road does not have a rate.** And the not-asking at twenty days, four inches long and not one word longer.
2. **0162 — A Number She Said In A Market And A Number She Said On A Wall (Day 148).** A low wall, a dew-dried seat, a basket, two barrows, a man with a dog. **She is counting the fences above the inn and has got to two hundred and six and told him before he asked, and she told him the thing he had not thought of: the two numbers are about two different pieces of ground, and if he writes them in the same hand one under the other he will have made a thing that is not there.** A gate where a fence was, put up well, and a different man who wanted a gate and has never spoken to her and did not pay her, and she says out loud that Tomas is not the reason. **She will not let him touch the basket and she gives the reason before he can get near it, and he does not cross it, and the chapter says that is a day and not a discovery.** And **Rodge Ollis says no before he opens his mouth, says he has been waiting five days to find out whether Tomas kept a list of the nine, and Tomas did not and is not going to**, and a bridge at the fourth mile that is boarded, which is on two lines of its own and joined to nothing.
3. **0163 — A Bypass Is A Road And Not A Claim And The Households Above The Cut Are The Ones That Lose A Voice (Day 149).** A bank, half a mile of new road on top of it, a ditch, stone stacked at the side, a stake with one word nailed to it and a line under it in a smaller hand. **A carter says what a bypass is for in one sentence: so a loaded cart can get through. And nothing is shut.** Then a woman comes out of a door under the bank and says the thing, to the carter and not to Tomas: **a man used to fill the soft places and came past about twice a year and she used to put her hand on his barrow and point, and he is not gone, he is where he has always been, he is paid for the road with a name on it, and a man does not mend a road he cannot write down.** She has a board over a soft place nine feet across and has moved her own gate. **The finding: a bypass takes the traffic and it takes the man who comes past twice a year, and that is the whole of what a road takes off a place.** And the third written refusal to join the rectangle to anything, and a stake's small print not asked about for the second time and refused for the second time.
4. **0164 — A District's Man Who Is Right About The Procedure And Cannot Be Reached About The Standing (Day 150).** A tripod, a chain on a reel, a kerbstone, a gate lane, a step. **Sim Searle, a district's road surveyor, has two true numbers about the length of the bypass — six hundred and forty on a sheet four days off that neither of them has, and six hundred and fifty-one by a chain that sags in grass — and puts nothing in, because the sheet's number was there before he came and a man four days off will choose the one that was there first.** A column a man rules for himself is a thing he has to be right about, and a notice-reader in a market town has wanted to rule one for nine years and has not, and that is twice in ten days. **And the sheet comes out of the coat in the open air for about nine seconds and goes back in, and he asks nothing and Tomas asks nothing, and the three things he gets are: the two blacks are one ink wet and dried, which makes a hundred and forty-nine days of finding smaller and not larger; the rules stopping short, he cannot account for; and he cannot read a run either, and there is no form on which anybody is taught.** And the standing, which is the whole of it: **a ward, a day, a number, a ground, and a reason box that wants a standing he has and Tomas has not, and if he filled it in the district would take the observation and make it the district's, and the whole of what has gone wrong in a hundred and fifty days is a thing taken off one person and put in a column by somebody who was being kind.** He is right. He is not a door. Nobody thanked him.
5. **0165 — The One Thing Four People Agree About Is A Bridge, And It Is The Only Thing They Can Check Themselves (Day 151).** **THE ONLY CHAPTER OF THE BATCH WITH NO SEVENTH HOUR**, and the day-book is the shortest he has written in four days. Two stringers and a deck: the bypass has a return in it because a new road is a thing a district can point a line at, a bridge is a repair, and a repair has no new line, and the timber is a man's who cut it about nine years ago and forgot about it. **Then Isa Trevail arrives with a barrow of lime and says out loud that she is walking round because there is a mile of road and not because anybody asked.** And three people, unasked, in about four minutes, in three different vocabularies, say the bridge should be open, and he does not ask a fourth and does not improve one and does not put the three in the same book as each other, and says so out loud once, and none of the three agrees with him about that. **A bridge is the only thing on this stretch a man can check himself, and that is the whole reason it is the only thing they agree about, and it is also the reason it is the least useful thing the four of them could have agreed about.**
6. **0166 — A Man Who Has Mended His Own Work Once And Will Not Do It Again, And Nobody Is Going To Thank Him For It (Day 152).** **The batch's second promise.** A flat, a heap of stone, a shovel, a hod, a soft place in an old road, a foot of stone proud of the ground. **Ned Adley, who is paid for the district's new road once a year by somebody he has met twice and never in this county, and who does everything else because it is soft.** He has done his own mend once, in the wall of his own yard, in the street he lives in, with stone he bought himself, in about a day and a half, and he does not think of it as the best work he has ever done, and the reason was not pride and he said so first: **a mend nobody can see is a mend somebody turns up one morning with a paper in their hand, and there is nowhere in this kingdom to stand at a counter and say *I have mended my own and here it is*, and a man who says it has put himself in a column, and a thing in a column is not a thing anybody owns after that.** So he does it twice, once properly and once with a lip you could catch a heel on. **He says not one word about a service cut or a bypass and Tomas puts none into him, and there is no figure of nine days, a lighter or a cart anywhere in his mouth, and those three belong to a different man at a wharf in a different county four days of this road behind him.** And the finding: **a claim in metal cannot be edited and cannot be shown to anybody on a page, and a mend cannot be edited and cannot be put on a page at all, and they are one instrument pointed at two trades.** And then he asks for the truth to be kept out of a book, and Tomas keeps it in, and says on the page that there is no form in this kingdom on which a man with a barrow may be asked to leave a thing out, and that the absence is the whole of the trade he used to be in. Nobody thanked him.
7. **0167 — Four People Asked The Same Question In The Same Words And Four Answers, And The One Man Who Would Carry A Sentence Is The One Man He May Not Put In A Column (Day 153).** A flat above a hedge, and Tomas goes down off it, because a man who asks questions from above a hedge is a man arranging a thing. **The same question four times, out of earshot, in the same words in the same order: what is on this road between the inn and the ford. Carts. Fences. A bridge and eleven trips round it. And one word, *soft*, and then three places, and then *I have been on that road on my own account longer than the heap of stone has been there, and I am not paid for this stretch and I did not come here for you.*** Then the question he came for, asked the same way, and four answers: **no, because a barrow of lime is one thing and a sentence is heavier; me, and I would want paying, and not for the walking, I would want somebody to have written down what I said so that if I say it different in a different place one of us can be found; no, because I have said four true things in three days and two of them are not about this road, and a sentence that grows every day is a man who has been talking; and yes, before the question was finished, unpaid, unasked, wanting a form, and knowing he is right to want it and that he is not going to get it.** And nine seconds of silence, and the chapter says it is the first silence in a hundred and fifty-three days that had nothing in it at all. And the three instruments, carried forward and not improved.
8. **0168 — Forty Houses, A Well And A Market On The Ground, And Nobody Here Is Hiding From Anything (Day 154).** A flat at the top of a lane, about forty houses, a well, a lean-to with a fire, about sixty people standing about on the ground, an egg board, a key on a string, a bucket. **The same question four times: is there a road that comes to this place. There is a lane, and a lane is not a road, and there has never been a road. There is a heading with a number on it in a district's book and I have never in my life seen a road that goes where it says. The inn draws a cart four days a week and the cart turns at the ash. And a fifth person who would not answer the question and answered a better one: it is not that nobody knows we are here, everybody knows, there is just nothing to put down.** One man tells him to go away. **And the third refusal to join the rectangle to anything, written down for the last time in this county, on the rule that two empties in one day in one building may be joined and three empties in three counties may not, and the chapter says that a man who writes a rule a fourth time has started to want it to be a pattern.** And the finding, which is small and negative: a road is a fact about the people who walk it, a street of forty houses is a fact about the people who live in it, a drawing is a fact about neither. And a question he had in his mouth and did not put there, and which one it was.
9. **0169 — A Place Can Be Absent From A Schedule And Present Under A Different Name, And He Cannot Prove Which Of Two Words Is The One On The Paper (Day 155).** **The batch's fourth promise and its best day.** **Kilnhead, at a door, from a kiln that is a heap of bricks about a hundred yards up the lane. Wetherin, in a district's mouth, from a wood four miles over that has no house in it. Wetherin Dyke, for the lane, from the man at the gate, who has never seen two of them on the same thing and would not know which was proper if he were asked.** He lays the schedule against the wall of the lean-to and looks at it and takes it out and puts it back and takes it out and does nothing with it, and that is the whole of what happens, and it takes about nine minutes. **And the finding: a place can be absent from a schedule and be standing here with a fire going in it, and a place can be on a schedule under a name nobody at a door uses, and both of those are true at once of the same forty houses, and the two of them have nothing to do with one another except that he is holding both.** And **five things he cannot do, written down because he wanted very badly to be able to do one of them: he cannot prove this street is the rectangle, he cannot prove it is not the rectangle, he cannot prove it is on a sheet at all, he cannot tell you whether a heading says Kilnhead or Wetherin or something he has not heard from a cart, and he cannot tell you which of two names is proper.** None of the five is a discovery and none of them is a step. And a man at a fire who says *it is a fire, and a name, and the name is mine and I have never had to defend it, and if I ever have to I will say it in one word and I will not say the other one at all, and I would like you to go up your lane now.* And her own entry, in her own hand, at the ninth hour of the evening.
10. **0170 — Four People, One Bridge, Twenty Days Of Not Asking, And A Road That Goes On Whether He Can Read It Or Not (Day 156).** **THE BATCH CLOSE.** A flat at a gate lane, a road past a turning, four people, a shovel. He says the thing out loud in the ordinary voice — I am going up this road, the barrow is not money, the paper is not authority, I have no standing in this district or the last three, I am not going to ask any of you for anything, if you walk it that is yours and I will not say thank you, if you go home that is yours and I will not ask you why — **and nobody makes a sentence of it, and the fourth says nothing at all and picks the shovel up and comes.** Then the only question anybody has asked him in ten days that he could answer inside a minute: **what happens if the timber is never bought. It stands. And if nobody puts a return against it. It stands, and it will be three boards in another nine years, and a hand's width of daylight, and about nine people a year will be right about it and about nine people a year will go round it, and neither of those two nines will ever be written down next to each other, and nobody will be able to say afterwards whether the bridge was shut or abandoned, because those are the same word for a bridge.** And a girl with a basket says the true thing about it: **the bridge is the only thing on this road you cannot get into a column, and that is why it is the only thing we all said the same thing about.** And the ten days' list, the three ways of moving a thing, and **a true sentence is the smallest thing anybody in this kingdom can carry and he has four people and not one sentence, and the man who would have carried one wanted a form, and he cannot give him a form and is not able, and those are two ends of one fact.** And the not-asking at twenty days, and **the road goes on whether I can read the drawing of it or not, and it always has, and I have a hundred and fifty-six days of that and I am not going to spend the next ten pretending to have found something.**
