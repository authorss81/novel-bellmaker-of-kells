## What the batch was for, and what it actually is

**A man in a city of about nine thousand bells tries to put a true sentence into a bell, and the Office copies the signal and turns it into a warrant, and the warrant is a copy of his own bell, and it is not a lie. A woman standing in a sill in a road takes the worst of what comes through and loses more hearing and nobody asks her for a reason. A sister inside the building chooses a record over a man, gives him nothing at all, and does not meet him — and the means he walks out with arrive from the officer in the coat, unasked, because there is no form that lets a district keep a man.**

It did that by refusing every door that was open. **Nobody in this batch is wicked and that is the engine, not a softening, and it held for the man in the coat who served the warrant, for the clerk four floors up who put one line into one column in about four seconds, for the man at the rail who does not know whether he is to strike a bell, for the clerk who came down four flights and struck four times on somebody else's paper, for the district round, and for the man the warrant is about.** Tomas Vale was arrested lawfully, was not rescued, was not let go by anybody who came to help him, and walked out of a city of about nine thousand people on foot because there is no form in this kingdom that lets a district keep a man.

**The Crown is not defeated. A notice came off a board on Day 135 because it was done and nobody came down four flights to take it off. A store issued a fitting on Day 128 against somebody else's instruction and wrote it in a book with a number, a district and a day. The man who served the warrant is not relieved of anything for serving it properly. The warrant stands. An archive four streets and four flights up is intact because the person who could have given it away did not. No bell he has ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom on the last night of the volume.**

## The ten chapters, in order, and what each one paid

1. **0141 — A Bell In A City Where Every Bell Is The Hour (Day 127).** A post and a bracket and six hours, and no bell to go in it. He walks both terraces counting what bells are fixed to and the answer is four words: **a gate, a board, a post, a yard, and all four have an owner with a document.** The batch's finding is the batch's engine: **he has been refusing the instrument for ten weeks and every reason he wrote down in three counties and a city is true, and none of them is the reason, and the reason is that in this city a sound is the hour, and he had been angry at a yard for twenty-one days without knowing he was angry at his own reason.** He makes the claim in four sentences in about nine minutes in his own hand, does not write it out, does not say it to anybody. **And at about the eighth hour he puts his hand flat on the crown of the plain carrier for the fourth time in a hundred and twenty-seven days and it is the last one and he knows it while he is doing it.**
2. **0142 — The Place A Bell Is Not (Day 128).** A rope hung back where it hangs; a mile of Wharf Road walked looking for a piece of iron that belongs to nobody; **a fitting issued out of a store against an instruction and written in a book in front of him, and Tarrant who has told him he would like him to leave;** and the cheapest question in this kingdom asked for the fifth time, *is this the same form*, answered in about four seconds: **no, it is a return about a thing in a ward, and its fourth box is not the ground, it is the keeper, and the keeper's box is filled on about nine hundred a year and empty on one.** He could have filled it in. He did not, because a name in that box is a keeper accepting a duty for a household that has not been asked, **and that is the fifth clause's seventh job and it is the reason there is no household in this city he can put a bell in front of.** The chapter's finding: **the thing that has protected him for a hundred and twenty-eight days is the thing that stops him, and a man who cannot be found cannot hang a bell where a street can be reached by it.**
3. **0143 — What Three Are, And Who Said So First (Day 129).** An hour of a room paid for in cash, a claim read out once in the same words in the same order to one person. **She gives an answer that is not a yes and is not a no: she can do the part of it that names what it does not know and tells nobody what to decide, and she cannot do the part that wants a person in a road, and she says she is not going to pretend, and she says do not ask her again, and he writes it down that he will not.** He does not ask the other one and he does not go up four flights and he does not write the reason down. **He has it in his mouth in the market for about four minutes and does not say it, and she is four feet off and does not stop him.** The finding: **the three he needs are not a quantity of people, they are a quantity of freedom, and he cannot manufacture a third anybody and would not want to, and if there were three of me it would still be him three times.** The slate is not used and the non-use is not written down.
4. **0144 — A Warrant Is What An Office Does With A Real Signal (Day 130).** Six boxes on a slip on a table in an empty room and **not one of them is for a sound.** Then the top terrace, a yard, about two hundred people at the sixth hour, a bell about as big as a cartwheel, a bracket, a socket, a wire going down the inside of a post, and a man with a book of his own and no office who strikes it four times whether or not there is anything on the plate. **At about the seventh hour, with about forty people left in the yard, he puts his hand on the bell and strikes it once, and nobody stops him and nobody asked him, and about nine minutes later a form in a room four floors up has a line in it with a day on it, a number at the top of it, and a fourth box that says a person made a signal.** **He wanted to put the four sentences in it and could not: a man ringing a bell he did not make says whatever the bell's claim is, and the claim in that bell was the hour, and the only instrument that cannot be edited is the one he used and the edit was not his.** The chapter's finding: **the cheapest act and the most expensive one are the same act, and he cannot tell anybody he did it, because a bell does not carry words and does not carry who struck it.**
5. **0145 — Nine Miles Of Copper And A Woman On A Sill (Day 131).** An open district's trench, a notice that says *wires* and not *wires belonging to anybody*, a clean cut from above with a burr underneath, nine miles of ground and eleven miles of run, a gate with a bell on a frame that went while he was standing at it. **A man stacking sacks in a yard is asked one question and talks about his father and about a day a line stopped answering, and it is the volume's one permitted seed about why the old network accepts a route that answers, and the chapter says on the page that it is a man talking about his father and not a finding.** He works out where she is standing and stands at a corner for about four minutes and does not go to the road. 6. **0146 — He Is Given The Choice And Is Not The Person Who Gets To Make It (Day 132).** A form on a table in an empty room with a fourth box four words long and true on it, read standing up, and a string he ties back the way he found it when he notices it had not been tied. **Then a trench being worked at about the fifth hour, a bar, a lamp, a man at the top of a wall with a plate in his hand, and at about the sixth hour nine seconds through a ground and a wall and a sill and him, and he took it because there was nobody else standing in it, and he perceived nothing and learned nothing and is not going to be told.** She takes the worst of it, on a sill and a floor and a wall, and cannot tell one thing from another, and nobody in that street asks her for a reason. **She says one sentence: you take it or I take it, and I have already decided, and I am telling you after. 7. **0147 — A Room With A Form In It, And The Form Is A Copy (Day 133).** Arrest on the middle terrace, lawfully, on a sheet with his name at the top of it. **The warrant is a copy of his bell — not of the words, because there were no words, but of a sound, and a thing which comes out twice is a claim and a copy of a claim, and a copy is how a sentence stops belonging to the person who said it, which he had worked out about a woman eleven days ago.** Not a forgery; a copy. This is the short form, and it exists so that a later writer does not read the paragraphs above and take them as current.**

**Seven things in the paragraphs above were wrong when they were written, or were missed when the paragraphs were written, and are now corrected in this file, in the chapters and in the state layer. None of them was a plot change.**

1. **The hearing crowd was the wrong number, in seven places across four chapters.** It was *about nine hundred people*, which is the volume's motif figure for bells, rooms, entries and matters and is also the market on the middle terrace. The yard holds **about two hundred at the sixth hour and about forty at the seventh**, he strikes the bell at the seventh, and the hearing crowd is **about forty**. Corrected at 0144 (three places), 0146 (three places — a seventh-hour yard of two hundred, which the review did not raise), 0147 (two places) and 0150 (four places). **The market of about nine hundred on the middle terrace is a long-established figure and was not touched.**
2. **Four interval figures were wrong.** *The evening of the day before yesterday* for a bell rung on the hundred and thirtieth, in 0147 and three times in 0150, is now *the evening of the hundred and thirtieth*. *Forty-six days* for a reason written on Day 91 and read on Day 134 is now *forty-three*. *Sixty days* for an interval beginning on Day 116 and read on Day 136 is now *twenty days*.
3. **The days-in-Sennet counter had stopped moving.** Day 136 less Day 95 is **forty-one days**. 0149 said thirty-five on Day 135 and is now forty; 0150 said thirty-five and is now forty-one; the two state files said thirty and now say forty-one. **The hearing was thirty days behind him in the state files and is thirty-three — it is Day 103 at the second hour and nothing may move it.**
4. **Chapter 0150 put the wrong bell in the wrong place.** The gate bell he struck on the top terrace is **not one of the nine bells in a mile of river road, is not a tenth of anything, and does not join that count.** The count of nine in a mile is two, as 0150 says, and is correct.
5. **Chapter 0146 had a staging gap and a sentence that contradicted its own title.** He now goes in off the road and puts his hands on the frame of the opening, and says in his own hand that he has not worked out why. **The title is canon and is unchanged**; the day-book sentence now says what the scene can support, which is that she handed him the carrying and not the deciding, and that what was not on offer was whether it runs, how far, and what it is for.
6. **The state layer asserted the opposite of the batch in two adjacent paragraphs of `state/character-state.md`**, and one of its lines was a week out of date: *no letter from Kells is permitted in this batch and none came* was false, and Oren Pike's reading of a charge **is** this batch's work, in Chapter 0149, and `outline/volume-03.md` requires it there.

**Three things outside the batch were also corrected, and they are corrected in the file that was wrong and not in the manuscript.** `NOVEL_SPEC.md` said *one hundred and forty chapters* and *Chapters 11–140* in two clauses and said Mara *gave him the means to go*; it now says one hundred and fifty and Chapters 11–150, and it says she gives him nothing. `NOVEL_SPEC.md` and `state/current.md` both called `outline/ending.md` the statement of what Volume 04 opens on; **that file has no Volume 04 section**, and both now point at `outline/volume-03.md` and say what `ending.md` actually is. And `outline/volume-03.md` said in two places that the three-person arrangement is made **in writing** and named **Nell Fenn** as a volume-level relationship beat she does not have; **the outline is corrected and the prose was not touched**, on the Volume 01 audit precedent.

7. **Six narrator meta-leaks, the class this project has now found six times.** The house rule at `state/continuity.md` §10b allows a man's own day-book and a store's ledger to say *in this book* and does **not** allow the narrator to. **Six instances sat in the narrator's bolded prose and not in a day-book**: *in this book* at 0141, 0145 and 0148, now *in my own book*; *in this chapter* at 0145, now *in the hundred and thirty-first*; and *three volumes* at 0146 and 0150, now *in four counties and a city*, which is the exact replacement §10b used for the identical phrase in Batch 0002. **The whole of Chapters 101 to 150 is swept and clean of the disallowed forms.** Note the direction: every other error of this project travels from the prose outward into the state layer, and this one travels **inward**, from an allowed in-world use in a day-book into the narrator's mouth two chapters later.

**What was re-measured after the pass and came back clean:** no `##` heading in any of the ten chapters; every daytime hour bare and the ninth hour of the evening the only half-day in all ten; no weekday and no month named; the carrier's interval fifty on Day 127 through fifty-nine on Day 136, one per chapter; the register at thirteen in every chapter that states it; all five clauses standing and the frame out of every building since the thirty-third; no age on the page for anybody including the six new people of this batch; nothing rung by anybody, his own bell barred in Kells, and Tamsin's bell in her bag and nearest her hand in 0143 and still in the bag in the chapter she loses hearing in; no narrator meta-leak anywhere in Chapters 101 to 150; and no stray shell text.
