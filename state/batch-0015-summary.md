## What the batch was for, and what it actually is

**A man in a city of about nine thousand bells tries to put a true sentence into a bell, and the Office copies the signal and turns it into a warrant, and the warrant is a copy of his own bell, and it is not a lie. A woman standing in a sill in a road takes the worst of what comes through and loses more hearing and nobody asks her for a reason. A sister inside the building chooses a record over a man, gives him the means to go, and does not meet him.**

It did that by refusing every door that was open. **Nobody in this batch is wicked and that is the engine, not a softening, and it held for the man in the coat who served the warrant, for the clerk four floors up who put one line into one column in about four seconds, for the man at the rail who does not know whether he is to strike a bell, for the clerk who came down four flights and struck four times on somebody else's paper, for the district round, and for the man the warrant is about.** Tomas Vale was arrested lawfully, was not rescued, was not let go by anybody who came to help him, and walked out of a city of about nine thousand people on foot because there is no form in this kingdom that lets a district keep a man.

**The Crown is not defeated. A notice came off a board on Day 135 because it was done and nobody came down four flights to take it off. A store issued a fitting on Day 128 against somebody else's instruction and wrote it in a book with a number, a district and a day. The man who served the warrant is not relieved of anything for serving it properly. The warrant stands. An archive four streets and four flights up is intact because the person who could have given it away did not. No bell he has ever made is unbarred anywhere in this kingdom on the last night of the volume.**

## The ten chapters, in order, and what each one paid

1. **0141 — A Bell In A City Where Every Bell Is The Hour (Day 127).** A post and a bracket and six hours, and no bell to go in it. He walks both terraces counting what bells are fixed to and the answer is four words: **a gate, a board, a post, a yard, and all four have an owner with a document.** The batch's finding is the batch's engine: **he has been refusing the instrument for ten weeks and every reason he wrote down in three counties and a city is true, and none of them is the reason, and the reason is that in this city a sound is the hour, and he had been angry at a yard for twenty-one days without knowing he was angry at his own reason.** He makes the claim in four sentences in about nine minutes in his own hand, does not write it out, does not say it to anybody. **And at about the eighth hour he puts his hand flat on the crown of the plain carrier for the fourth time in a hundred and twenty-seven days and it is the last one and he knows it while he is doing it.**
2. **0142 — The Place A Bell Is Not (Day 128).** A rope hung back where it hangs; a mile of Wharf Road walked looking for a piece of iron that belongs to nobody; **a fitting issued out of a store against an instruction and written in a book in front of him, and Tarrant who has told him he would like him to leave;** and the cheapest question in this kingdom asked for the fifth time, *is this the same form*, answered in about four seconds: **no, it is a return about a thing in a ward, and its fourth box is not the ground, it is the keeper, and the keeper's box is filled on about nine hundred a year and empty on one.** He could have filled it in. He did not, because a name in that box is a keeper accepting a duty for a household that has not been asked, **and that is the fifth clause's seventh job and it is the reason there is no household in this city he can put a bell in front of.** The chapter's finding: **the thing that has protected him for a hundred and twenty-eight days is the thing that stops him, and a man who cannot be found cannot hang a bell where a street can be reached by it.**
3. **0143 — What Three Are, And Who Said So First (Day 129).** An hour of a room paid for in cash, a claim read out once in the same words in the same order to one person. **She gives an answer that is not a yes and is not a no: she can do the part of it that names what it does not know and tells nobody what to decide, and she cannot do the part that wants a person in a road, and she says she is not going to pretend, and she says do not ask her again, and he writes it down that he will not.** He does not ask the other one and he does not go up four flights and he does not write the reason down. **He has it in his mouth in the market for about four minutes and does not say it, and she is four feet off and does not stop him.** The finding: **the three he needs are not a quantity of people, they are a quantity of freedom, and he cannot manufacture a third anybody and would not want to, and if there were three of me it would still be him three times.** The slate is not used and the non-use is not written down.
4. **0144 — A Warrant Is What An Office Does With A Real Signal (Day 130).** Six boxes on a slip on a table in an empty room and **not one of them is for a sound.** Then the top terrace, a yard, about two hundred people at the sixth hour, a bell about as big as a cartwheel, a bracket, a socket, a wire going down the inside of a post, and a man with a book of his own and no office who strikes it four times whether or not there is anything on the plate. **At about the seventh hour, with about forty people left in the yard, he puts his hand on the bell and strikes it once, and nobody stops him and nobody asked him, and about nine minutes later a form in a room four floors up has a line in it with a day on it, a number at the top of it, and a fourth box that says a person made a signal.** **He wanted to put the four sentences in it and could not: a man ringing a bell he did not make says whatever the bell's claim is, and the claim in that bell was the hour, and the only instrument that cannot be edited is the one he used and the edit was not his.** The chapter's finding: **the cheapest act and the most expensive one are the same act, and he cannot tell anybody he did it, because a bell does not carry words and does not carry who struck it.**
5. **0145 — Nine Miles Of Copper And A Woman On A Sill (Day 131).** An open district's trench, a notice that says *wires* and not *wires belonging to anybody*, a clean cut from above with a burr underneath, nine miles of ground and eleven miles of run, a gate with a bell on a frame that went while he was standing at it. **A man stacking sacks in a yard is asked one question and talks about his father and about a day a line stopped answering, and it is the volume's one permitted seed about why the old network accepts a route that answers, and the chapter says on the page that it is a man talking about his father and not a finding.** He works out where she is standing and stands at a corner for about four minutes and does not go to the road. The finding: **the instrument that would carry a true sentence out of him in one evening is also the only one that runs through a sill she is holding, and he has been refusing it for a hundred and thirty-one days and has never once asked who stands in it.**
6. **0146 — He Is Given The Choice And Is Not The Person Who Gets To Make It (Day 132).** A form on a table in an empty room with a fourth box four words long and true on it, read standing up, and a string he ties back the way he found it when he notices it had not been tied. **Then a trench being worked at about the fifth hour, a bar, a lamp, a man at the top of a wall with a plate in his hand, and at about the sixth hour nine seconds through a ground and a wall and a sill and him, and he took it because there was nobody else standing in it, and he perceived nothing and learned nothing and is not going to be told.** She takes the worst of it, on a sill and a floor and a wall, and cannot tell one thing from another, and nobody in that street asks her for a reason. **She says one sentence: you take it or I take it, and I have already decided, and I am telling you after. He did not decide anything and he did not cut anything. He could not keep his feet for about four minutes.** And at about the seventh hour **a clerk comes down four flights with a printed sheet and a number at the top of it and strikes a bell about as big as a cartwheel four times and does not read the sheet**, and about two hundred people come out and make no noise and go in again.
7. **0147 — A Room With A Form In It, And The Form Is A Copy (Day 133).** Arrest on the middle terrace, lawfully, on a sheet with his name at the top of it. **The warrant is a copy of his bell — not of the words, because there were no words, but of a sound, and a thing which comes out twice is a claim and a copy of a claim, and a copy is how a sentence stops belonging to the person who said it, which he had worked out about a woman eleven days ago.** Not a forgery; a copy. The fourth box is four words and a place and it is true. **He says nothing all day. He does not claim to be anything, does not say a name, does not ask the question he wants to ask about the man on the other bench, and does not put a question on the slate. He is not sorry for the bell.** Mara is inside the building and does not come, a door at the end of a corridor opens and closes, and he does not read the plate and does not ask — and he writes that one down, because it is not a non-use of the slate and the difference is four inches long.
8. **0148 — The Record, The Map, And Eleven Lines In His Own Hand (Day 134).** **There is no form in this kingdom that lets a district keep a man and none that lets a court keep a man, and a matter goes where a matter goes and the person named in it goes with it, and a schedule is part of a paper.** A bundle is undone on a table in a corridor and a schedule folded in three is put into his hands by a man who was not asked to do it, and there is no box on any paper in this kingdom that wants a man to give one back. **The map he cannot read, with about a third of the way along a run a rectangle about two hands together with nothing in it, not torn and not cut, the paper smooth and uncreased, the rules stopping short, the ink a slightly different black, and the numbers either side going on without a break — and he does not find out in a corridor, does not write it down, and does not join it to anything in three counties.** Then, on a stair landing, **he writes the eleven lines out in his own hand for the third time and keeps it, and the reason is about him and not about her.** And in a lane, at about thirty feet, **four lines go into the apron of the woman who lets a room by the hour, and he does not go over, does not ask, and does not thank her.** And on the top terrace, a board with four drawing-pins in it and the nails in the board and the sheet not on them, and he does not take it down. That is the fifth time and the reason is four inches long and has not improved in forty-six days.
9. **0149 — A Man Reads A Charge And Will Not Be Told What It Means By The Man It Is About (Day 135).** A charge on a rail in a public room, four paragraphs, and a man who cannot read it and asks in the ordinary voice what the thing is. **He knows the answer, it is eleven words, and he does not say them**, and the chapter says the reason: if he tells a stranger in a room, that man will say a name four times inside a year in four towns, every one of them right the first time and more wrong after that. **A charge is a sheet; a hearing is a room; a sheet can be produced by a stranger and a room cannot be produced by anybody, because a room has to be there on the day.** At the arch, Perrin Lowe with the shutter half down: **the notice that called him to a hearing is off the board because it was done, and nobody came down four flights to take it off, and the man who is holding the warrant is not going to be relieved of anything for doing his job.** And at about the seventh hour, **the one letter permitted from Kells in this batch, from Oren Pike: he has read the first charge against the Office's warrant in his own hand, has written down that he read it on his own account, has not answered the paragraph Tomas sent, and has said so, so that Tomas cannot afterwards claim he was told what it meant by the man it is about.** He reads it four times and does not write back. **It is a letter and not a fourteenth document and he has not counted it.**
10. **0150 — A Road Out Of Sennet On Foot, And Not A Victory (Day 136).** A step at the foot of the top stair, four minutes, and a barrow. A matter going up the river on a district's cart in a day or two, and a person named in a paper goes with the paper, and he is not going to be put on a cart. **A cart is the district's and a barrow is his.** The middle terrace, a woman selling needles, nine bells in a mile that he does not stop at. Then the whole of the hundred and thirty-six days in order: **he arranged none of it, and the arranging is the same sentence as the asking with one letter changed.** And the cost, and the arithmetic, and the last image. **The register is thirteen; the two things in the coat that are about him are a leaf in his own book with eleven lines on it and a copy of four sentences of his in a district's keeping going round on a cart; a copy of eleven lines out of a head is not a copy of a court record and is not a fourteenth.** And the last line: *One of the three answers. Not two. Not three. If there were three of me.* — carried forward, not resolved.
