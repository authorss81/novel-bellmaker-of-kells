# Batch 0003 Summary — Chapters 21–30

**Volume:** 01 — *The Bell Before the Crime*
**Batch:** 0003
**Status:** Complete
**Day span:** Day 10 to Day 14 (five days). The seven-day stay ends at the dawn of Day 10.

## Batch result

The dawn came, and it was lawful, and nobody stopped it.

Chapters 21–23 all sit inside Day 10 and run in sequence: the custody hearing in the first hour of light, the inspection from four o'clock into the morning, and then the paperwork and the cost. **Oren Pike** began the day by entering two objections in open court — that the market court's call bell is unbound iron on a rope with no line, and that the custody order's *thereafter as the commission shall direct* reaches the millrace bridge bell — and then conceded the Crown's claim to the trial handbell and the eighteen remaining shelf bells anyway, on the record, because he did not have the votes and because he would not put two of his men in a lane in the dark. He pulled the seizure forward into the court at twenty past one, in front of about a hundred and forty people, and entered that **he chose the hour**, which is the difference between a captain and a man who lets a thing happen to somebody else. Tomas did not contest the seizure of his own bell and did not accept the Crown's merged single-response page to buy it back. The bell was listed by condition, wrapped in oiled linen with its ash frame, and carried down the lane; the L-4 posting sliver was lifted out with two fingers and packed separately so that anybody could read it.

**The Day 10 inspection is the batch's real event, and it is a civic catastrophe rather than a fight.** The Crown produced **no per-household authority** and proceeded on the response schedule alone. Surveyor **Rhea Collis** said so at the gate, in the open, before the round opened, and worked off the schedule on the record rather than refuse and let a worse man certify it. So the round was a renewal visit conducted against the ward's own recorded answers, and **a mark in a response column became the Crown's warrant**. Three children were taken. At 114, Absalom Teage, a carter, produced the slate his household held from the posting day, and his wife said four words at the door: *He is not on your paper.* At 121, Sena Dunn asked *which child* four times. At 133 a household gave no answer and no name. Twenty-two doors were marked. Oren recorded each removal separately, by household, with the hour and the household's own answer, countersigned that one line by Collis, and then wrote the hardest sentence of his life: he had two men, a warrant and two hundred people behind him, and he did not put them between children and a lawful order, because a captain who does that ends the ward's only protection. Gib Hask's argument — that the Crown's buried line was laid under the ward without consent — was made in the street and answered in the street by a twenty-six-year-old officer, which made it public and useless, and Oren chose not to write that down.

Chapter 23 is the chapter where Tomas changes something by writing. The custody receipt described the object as *signal apparatus, workshop-bound, capable of public sounding*. He cannot change the custody and stops trying, and changes the **description** instead, in the margin, in front of a Crown copyist: one claim, one keeper's duty of his own, one informed answer given privately and never publicly, not a response of the ward, court, watch or Crown, the article bound and unspent. And then he adds the one piece of information the Crown did not have — that the answer was given by **Nell Fenn** — without asking her. He goes down the hill and tells her, and she tells him he put it in writing before he asked, makes him state the cost in the order *three children, then my name*, and says he has never once asked her for anything that was not already written. That is the volume's lesson arriving as an invoice rather than as a speech. **Sabin Fell** gives him the other half: within a fortnight the word will not be *contested*, it will be *Vale*, and four houses in the Measure will say a man who loses a bell and loses a child in a morning should not hold a licence.

**The midpoint reversal lands in Chapter 25 and it is documentary.** Tamsin, who cannot follow a conversation in a crowded room, reads paper. Ferrow had stopped his reading of the schedule at the ruled line below the forty-one households, as readers do, and had looked under the ruling twice without touching it. She matched the office mark **OFF. 411** across four papers in four hands — the response schedule, the culvert works order dated before the warning, the maintenance ticket's *answered in office* line, and the inspection order itself — and then read under the ruling. There is a forty-second row. The first field says **L-4** where every other line says a house number. The field where all forty-one hold a count of persons is **empty**. The response column holds the office mark. The date is **nine days before the carrier rang**, and the order it answers is dated three days before the carrier. Six days. A bell carries a claim that a living person has taken responsibility for; it does not need to see anything. Somebody in that office accepted a duty about an inspection six days before the inspection was written, entered it in an ordinary form in the ordinary week, and the bell carried it. **It was not prophecy. It was filing.** Tamsin refuses to name a person, refuses to let Tomas invent one, and hands him only the arithmetic. Her professional horror is specific and is the best thing in the batch: the wax laid over the older groove in the crown of her own handbell, which she has called an accident for eight months, is a *technique*, and the same technique lives in a district office in ink on a form with a column and a return cycle. She has been carrying a Crown practice round her neck and calling it a thief.

**The rest of the batch is the town learning to use that without a bell.** Chapter 26 gives the batch its second reversal, from Nell's side: nine lines on the schedule read *not returned* and six read *accompanying*, and **every household the Crown took a child from is on one of those fifteen lines**, while every other marked door is on a plain line. The Crown's own notes about people who were not in the house were read by the Crown as the answer. Nell gets one precise question into a filing room and one answer out of it — the Crown form has a column headed **Witnessed by**, empty on every Kells return, which takes the name of a person who was in the room when the answer was given, and a summary form drops the reason an answer was entered in office — and then **burns the slate in the washhouse fire**, because the one document that would have been evidence is about her own brother and father, and she will not put them in an argument. Chapter 27 pays for restraint: Collis gives three true sentences and five refusals, and will not be made into the missing bellwright, and says in a doorway what Tomas will not write down. Chapter 28 publishes **a checkable procedure instead of a conclusion** — three ordinary questions any household can put to a Crown clerk about its own line, plus a standing invitation to correct the record in public — and Tomas declines to speak first at his own publication, because a claim voiced by its maker is the thing he has spent the volume learning not to do. Oren compels a public Crown counter under the arcade with one sentence, and **Absalom Teage** uses the three questions successfully: the line is read, the day is given, the hand is refused, and a Crown clerk writes two sentences in a margin in his own name and initials them. Oren's memorandum of the eleventh day takes the Crown's citation of his own gate book as evidence of the ward's consent and denies it in his own hand, in open court; and on the **noon hour of Day 11 he reads his own written refusal of Crake's district appointment in open court, with Crake reading his own answer back in full.**

**Chapters 29 and 30 pay the personal prices.** Tamsin walks her own boundary with Jerm and a slate and rings once at seven places and finds that the reach does not stop, it thins out — it answers forty yards above the stage, at the low drain gate itself through four feet of stone, and a mile out at the first cottage past the toll shed where the same woman has twice come down to ask who was calling the market. **She cannot find where her rule stops being true, so the bell cannot carry even a keeper's boundary**, and she reduces what she offers to paper on foot, a bell rung only at a door that asks, a bell that is nothing more than a *call to come to a door*, and no repeating for anybody. The note is gone out of her hearing and she can no longer carry at all. The towpath's nine coppers a month is gone. She tells Tomas she will answer a request and refuse a request and that he will not be able to tell in advance, and he writes that he has **no standing to know**. On Day 14 the ward states **three positions in three columns and merges none of them** — keep the record and take no route, ask the Crown for a line because only a Crown order moves a cart, or take the copper out of the ground — and a Crown filing clerk asks to read the whole thing both ways so that the room can hear the single sentence the ward's three positions become inside one response column, and says the weapon lasts about a day. Tomas refuses four times to tell the room what to do, takes back the wheelwright's counter bell he could not dress on Day 3 because a bell with a live claim in it stood four feet away, and dresses it in an hour and ten minutes. The batch ends with a Crown road gang marking out in the low lane for a renewal tomorrow, in the lane where the copper runs and the children walk to the landing, and Gib Hask's two men at the low end with a bar and a spade.

## Object custody and condition after Chapter 30

- **Fixed stripped Crown survey carrier:** waist-high, mounted, no survey clapper, child-sized wax handprint, route damage. **Still in the Kells watch store** in its mounting frame and reinforced padded cradle, retaining bolts still released from the Chapter 10 transfer, red watch seal on the lower brace. No chapter in this batch moved it. **The joint receipt on the L-4 file expired at the fourth hour of Day 10** (Collis's line, Chapter 22) and no custody review is on the page; the carrier is not in dispute in any chapter of this batch. Crown ownership still unresolved. **The comparison Oren arranged in Chapter 3 — the courier handbell against the fixed carrier — has still never been made and is not made in this batch.**
- **Tomas's trial handbell:** **in Crown custody since about twenty-five to four on Day 10, an hour before the round opened.** Listed by condition in open court, wrapped in oiled linen with its ash frame and carried down the Bellhouse lane by Watchmen Ardle and Cask. **Never rung in this batch and never rung at all.** The L-4 posting sliver was removed from the frame with two fingers and packed separately in the packet. Its custody receipt carries Tomas's margin correction and the Crown copyist's initials. It is **bound and unspent** and cannot carry a second claim until the duty is answered or the binding is deliberately unmade. It is not in the Bellhouse, and Tomas cannot ring it on any matter.
- **The eighteen remaining Bellhouse shelf bells:** taken in a handcart under a rope at the same hour on Day 10, into Crown custody. The **millrace bridge bell remains on the north wall of the yard** and is the **only bell in the Bellhouse that Tomas may ring**, under Oren's no-ringing order **re-made by Oren's own hand on Day 10 afternoon**, which continues until either the Crown lifts it in writing or the watch lifts it. Halden Crake has said the word *bridge* four times in one sentence and has not yet applied for it.
- **Tamsin's stolen courier handbell:** in her possession throughout. Not rung for any public purpose in this batch. Her own Day 9 rule stands. On Day 13 she established that **the note has gone out of her hearing** and that she can no longer carry; and that the bell's reach does not stop at any edge she can find. **The one ring she made outside her route was declared out loud, by her, against her own rule, and entered in the watch book as an exception and not as a precedent.** She has reduced her offer to four terms and retains full authority.
- **Market court call bell:** still the **unbound iron handbell kept by the court usher**, rope, striker on a thong, no wire and no crown groove, and Jerm's **Day 8** inspection record testifies that it has no line. It **rings nothing in this batch**. On Day 13 the commission posted a notice about it and on the morning of Day 14 a second, shorter notice **convening the market court and requesting the court to produce the bell**. The court **produced its keeper and not its bell**: the usher stood up and stated the three facts of the bell in open court, refused to have it described, exhibited and carried by an office that has not put a hand on it, and the clerk entered that the court had surrendered nothing. Crake said the commission had noted the distinction and would take it up with the district.
- **The wheelwright's counter bell:** returned to the Bellhouse bench on Day 14, eleven days late, and dressed — hung a quarter inch longer than it was — over an hour and ten minutes. The only bell Tomas can sound in this volume, and it is a customer's, for a cart, and carries no claim.

## Power state after Chapter 30

Tomas is **still at the first edge of Stage 1 (Near Toll) and gained nothing in this batch.** His one perception — whether a nearby answer is strained — gave him nothing in Chapter 25, because the claim is not near him; it is in a filing office in the district town. He identifies no source, no emotion, no copied answer, no official, and no future event. The reversal in Chapter 25 is a **documentary and civic discovery made from pages already in play**, and he cannot hear or feel it. No deep read, no forced-echo detection, no archive impression, no permanent memory loss. The one moment of his craft in the batch is not supernatural at all: he observes that a Crown summary page is written with a fine pen because a summary is made for somebody who was not in the room.

**Tamsin** has no resonance and no supernatural perception. **Sabin Fell** has not named what he carried past the gate in the Hush. **Collis** is an ordinary accredited Crown surveyor with a seal, an ordinary professional limit, and an ordinary professional price to pay for using it.

## The reserved reversals, and what they did not resolve

**The midpoint reversal is earned and is on the page:** the warning was not a prophecy. A Crown office registered a future civic order as an accepted public duty, dated six days before the order was written, and the bell carried a claim with a living keeper's acceptance behind it.

**Still unresolved and deliberately so:** the copied-answer mechanism and how a Crown office gets into a bell at all; the political function of the Crown clapper; the Silent Muster's name-transfer mechanism; the identity of the higher official; who entered line 34 and who put a woman on a tailboard; what a surveyor's office does with a form that has a *Witnessed by* column; whether OFF. 411 is a person or a habit; the name **Iven Sore** and whether the withdrawn service entry is genuine or borrowed; and **Lira's fate**.

**Not done, by design:** the Vale Bellhouse archive does not burn, the workshop is not closed, no broad arrest sweep, and Tomas does not leave Kells. The carrier is still in the watch store. Those are Chapters 41–50.

## Review repair pass (batch-0003)

This batch was reviewed once after it was written and repaired in place. Plot, day map, character beats, the reversal, and every object custody state are unchanged; the pass fixed internal arithmetic, a mis-stated clock, a retcon of an earlier chapter, and three facts that the state files had stated more confidently than the prose.

- **The Day 10 clock did not work.** The custody hearing had drifted into a small-hours slot that contradicted the posted notice and the round's own four o'clock. It now convenes at half past two, the seizure is taken at ten past three, and the figures are read aloud at twenty-five to four, an hour before the round opens. The Crown's phrase *the first hour of light* is left on the notice and is not on the page as an arithmetic claim. The state files had recorded the trial handbell as taken at 4:40, which is the hour the 114 child was taken; they now say about twenty-five to four.
- **Gate-book entries fourteen to nineteen** now carry the three removals at the hours the removal record gives them, name the woman on the tailboard at entry fifteen, and drop the rain line. **Watchman Cask has no clock on that gate and says so**, which is why the book has times he wrote down afterwards.
- **Chapter 25's date arithmetic was impossible** as first written — a twenty-second day before and a twenty-fifth day before, which is three. It now reads nine days before the carrier against an order dated three days before, said aloud twice, with the subtraction done as words by Jerm so that a witness holds the arithmetic and not Tamsin.
- **Chapter 25 no longer retcons Chapter 20.** Ferrow is not accused of a reading that is not in the chapter. The finding is now a new discovery under a ruling at the foot of the page, and the reason nobody found it is that nobody reads under a ruling, which Ferrow and Tamsin each say out loud.
- **Chapter 30 no longer predates its own scene.** The three-answer test was six days before Day 14, not eleven; the merged single-response page was four days old, not eleven; and the Crown's answer came overnight, not in eleven hours. The three Crown notices of Day 13 are no longer re-nailed verbatim on Day 14 — the Day 14 notice is a new, shorter summons against the market court's bell, so the escalation reads as a step and not as a repeat.
- **The market court usher now speaks**, which she had not done since Chapter 18 and which the batch's second-newest live threat required. The court produces its keeper and not its bell, and the distinction is on the record. **Sabin Fell appears a second time**, on the evening of Day 14, about the Measure's road map going into a district file.
- **Counts that were wrong are now right**: the shelf held eighteen bells, not eight; three households lost a child on Day 10, not eleven households; the two removals at 5:10 and 5:50 are two children in two entries; Collis was conducted to nine of the twenty-two doors and did not see the other thirteen marked, which is now stated in Chapter 22 and entered by her as the first of her four statements.
- **Tamsin's Day 13 walk is now on the page as a breach.** She says out loud that she is about to ring a mile outside her own route with nobody having asked her, which her written Day 9 rule forbids, and Jerm enters it in the watch book as an exception and not as a precedent at her insistence. Without that, the chapter that spends ten chapters defending her rule broke it silently.
- **Nell keeps no second slate.** The slate she burns is the only one; the one she carries home is a fresh sixpence slate bought that morning with nothing on it. Her fragment copy is dated and located correctly: made on the ninth evening at the watch house table, in her own hand, with the clerk pushing the pen across to her.
- **Tomas's craft beat was moved into the prose.** The state had claimed a fine-pen observation about a summary page that was in no chapter; it is now in Chapter 25, in his own hand in the day-book, where it belongs and where it is a maker's observation about paper rather than a power.
- **Prose.** The narrating stamp *the correct X* was cut from ten places and kept where a character is being corrected rather than the reader. The repeated *X, which was Y* construction was broken in the worst twenty instances. One stray non-English token in Chapter 28 and one garbled line on the Crown custody form in Chapter 23 are gone. No weekday is named anywhere in the batch except the chandler's forward-looking "from Monday", and no month is named at all; Chapter 15's June is the only month on the page in the novel.
- **A claim the state was making and the prose was not.** The state said Tomas's perception "returned nothing" in Chapter 25. There is no perception in Chapter 25 at all. The state now says so, because a state file that implies a perception invites the next batch to write one.
