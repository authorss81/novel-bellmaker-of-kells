## Where he is, and what he has, and what he has lost

**Tomas Vale is in Sennet on Day 116, on a step at the foot of the top stair of a city of about nine thousand people, at about the ninth hour of the evening, with a barrow.** He came up the road from Miremarket on the eighty-seventh and arrived on the ninety-fifth, and it is **twenty-one days**. He has a bar on his own bell, a receipt in five clauses, **a hearing three days behind him that he could not answer and did not attend**, a printed slip and an unfilled form, a store four streets and four flights up he has been into three times, **a sworn sentence of his own on a public wall in a market**, and a route map he has not got yet.

**HE HAS, and it is the same list he wrote out flat under a hedge in the rain on Day 86, and it has not grown by one document in thirty days:** a list of about nine names that cannot be certified at all; four columns and a slate whose price side is the other way up; a fragment of nine lines, one hand, a column of district numbers, a column of days, and a third column empty from end to end; a custody receipt in four clauses and a fifth, **all five standing**; a frame in a reinforced padded cradle on the barrow with four retaining bolts made fast and no clapper in it, and he has never once been inside it; five receipts from an officer of a county he has left; a bag with a bell in it that nobody can certify; and a woman ten feet off his left shoulder.

**AND HE HAS, NEW IN THIRTY DAYS, NONE OF IT A DOCUMENT AND NONE OF IT A STAGE:**

- **A city of about nine thousand bells that is about the hour and not about the bells.** A man in a town of two thousand walks into a market; **a man in a city of nine thousand comes out into his own door at the hour, and every bell in the place is one of the hour's appointments.**
- **A berth on a net-loft on the flat and a man's name.** **Nevin Holt**, a grain wharf, thirty-one years on the flat: **a bell is a man standing next to it — the man and the hour and the reason; take the man away and it is iron.** And the reason it is not the whole of it: **a cut wire does not stop a bell, it stops a wire, and the bell on the other end of it does not know and goes on being a bell.**
- **The wire, and the reason it matters: a bell you can blame and a wire you cannot.** A wharfman will lose nine days, a lighter and a cart on the road rather than own a mend, **because a mend that is in nobody's book is a fault nobody can see, and a fault nobody can see is a fault he is asked about some morning by somebody with a reason.** The same reasoning, in a carter's mouth, refuses to carry an unaccountable thing up four flights.
- **A counter that takes papers and not people, and a court that publishes and an office that files, and the difference is four inches long.** **The river courts on the middle terrace keep a sworn board on a public wall that anybody may read, and its form has three boxes and no box for a reason, because a person swearing to a thing about himself does not owe anybody a why. - **A store, and a man behind the counter, and a number.** **Osmund Tarrant**, nine years: a room, a counter, a book, a man, a shelf, an instruction, and **a column headed what the thing is for, empty from end to end, and it is empty because a man will not write down a word he cannot read back, and he is right. - **A number that goes somewhere.** **4, 1 8 2 — the district of Netherby — the eleventh — coupling.** A repair to a fixed stripped survey carrier, entered in a store's book on the top terrace, against an instruction, in a district he has never stood in. **And a number is a place a thing is filed at, and a reference going somewhere is not a person, and that is a smaller thing than anybody has been carrying.**
