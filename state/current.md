## Where he is, and what he has, and what he has lost

**Tomas Vale is in Sennet on Day 106, on a step at the foot of a stair on the top terrace of a city of about nine thousand people, at about the ninth hour of the evening, with a barrow.** He came up the road from Miremarket on the eighty-seventh and arrived on the ninety-fifth, and it is **eleven days**. He has a bar on his own bell, a receipt in five clauses, a **hearing three days behind him that he could not answer and did not attend**, a printed slip and an unfilled form, a store four streets and four flights up he has been into twice, and a route map he has not got yet.

**HE HAS, and it is the same list he wrote out flat under a hedge in the rain on Day 86, and it has not grown by one document in twenty days:** a list of about nine names that cannot be certified at all; four columns and a slate whose price side is the other way up; a fragment of nine lines, one hand, a column of district numbers, a column of days, and a third column empty from end to end; a custody receipt in four clauses and a fifth, **all five standing**; a frame in a reinforced padded cradle on the barrow with four retaining bolts made fast and no clapper in it, and he has never once been inside it; five receipts from an officer of a county he has left; a bag with a bell in it that nobody can certify; and a woman ten feet off his left shoulder.

**AND HE HAS, NEW IN TWENTY DAYS, NONE OF IT A DOCUMENT AND NONE OF IT A STAGE:**

- **A city of about nine thousand bells that is about the hour and not about the bells.** A man in a town of two thousand walks into a market; **a man in a city of nine thousand comes out into his own door at the hour, and every bell in the place is one of the hour's appointments, and about nine hundred of them are waiting and the count of them is a thing nobody keeps.**
