His number — about eleven thousand held lines, four hours a day, eight hundred hours, eleven pence an hour, about forty pounds a year, about three hundred and sixty pounds across nine districts — **is corrected on the page in Chapter 79 by a district officer who is right, and the correction does not make it a solution: three hundred and sixty pounds is the nine districts that adopted the practice, there are nineteen under the county, the other ten spend it and spend more, and if all nineteen stopped it the figure would be about seven hundred and sixty pounds a year, which is about the price of a small bridge and is not money anybody finds in a place anybody looks.**
