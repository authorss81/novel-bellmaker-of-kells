## The shape of the batch

**Days 56–57, the river (Ch 71).** A carrier's boat and not the post, because the post goes on the second and comes up on the fourth and the fifty-second was the second. A boatman asks what is in the crate and Tomas gives him the answer nobody can hear and does not lie: **he bought a bell to say something with and he does not know what**, and a man with a wood-covered book writes *a bell* and says it is the shortest honest entry he has made in nine years. **A clerk's hall is not a district office and a river knows the difference**, and a barrow does not go into a town that has one, because a carrier's book says what a thing is and a clerk's hall has a man in it who writes down what a thing is, and two books about one barrow is a thing a carrier does not want. **Miremarket** comes off the wood cover of a man's book in a column headed for the place a load is going, and it is the first name for a place Tomas has had out of a document in a month. He tells Tamsin Rook in advance that he is going to pay a man two shillings and sixpence to carry a barrow he could carry and a shilling for a yard and for not asking, out of five shillings, and she does not stop him and tells him the reason he is doing it, and he is right about the reason. The frame is bolted at every place it is set down; nothing is rung; no clause is broken; the fifth clause is used twice and both times as a fact about himself.

**Day 58, the town and the two words (Ch 72).** He pays the two men, the frame goes into a yard behind a draper's and is bolted, the crate goes to a smith's yard as a smith's consignment, and he has told her that too. **Ivo Trant**, man in charge of the shelves, no age, twenty-two years in the building, is pleasant and correct: nothing in this kingdom by which a man who is not a district officer may ask a clerk's hall for anything; here is a form; there is no box on it for a reason. The town is not hostile and does not think it is anything: a carter says the round comes twice, the money comes with it, and that is the reason nobody here is unhappy. Tomas asks his question nine times in a market town and gets nine true answers and can use none of them, **which is the first time in five weeks the talking has got him nothing.** At about the sixth hour a clerk in the street says the two words: **the answer rolls.** A clerk's word for a set of shelves, standing for the rolls where the answers go, nothing grander, and a book of about eleven thousand a year, and **you can certify anything inside a column and you cannot certify the column**, because a column is not a household and a household is not a person and a person is the only thing in this kingdom that can be asked a question and can refuse it. He gets a name at the end of a lane from a woman who will not give him a name and does not know whether the woman has a husband.

**Day 59, the lane (Ch 73, Tamsin).** She goes for a page and gets a room. **Bess Marrow**, no age, household of five, one of them a mother who cannot write, nine columns in one hand for about nine years. She reads the book in the room with the woman looking at it, does not take it and does not copy it, and finds a fact about a date: **in eleven places the book and the roll do not agree, and in nine of the eleven the day the hall holds is a day the round was not in that street.** She does not ask whether the boy is on a roll, and has not asked, and will not, and gives the reason in her own hand. Her bell is not rung. Nobody asks her for a reason.

**Day 60, the round and the room behind the hall (Ch 74).** The round is in the town and about a hundred and forty people put their own hands into a book at a table in a yard, and there is not one person in that yard who has been swallowed. He asks the front door a question about the work and finds in nine seconds a door that is not a door, and Ivo Trant tells him the hole has been there for twenty-two years and has never been thought about. He then stands in the street for five hours and says in advance that he will ask one clerk one question and not ask a clerk anything in that town again for a week. **Elias Nabb**, no age, four years in the answering column and eleven years in the hall, one child and a house, answers *what is the document* in his own name, on that office's own paper, with the hour, and adds two sentences nobody asked him for: **the practice is a settlement of a difficulty and the difficulty is a household, and I did not write that, and I think it is right**; and **I have never been asked what the mark means, in four years, by anybody, and I am not sure, and I am not going to ask, and if I am wrong then four years of my hand is wrong.** He cannot be made to say what a column ought to be, and he says so in advance so that nobody wastes a quarter of an hour. And then: **he is the only person in that building who has ever been asked a clerk a question**, and the clerk, standing up with his coat on, says that either that is the one thing the trade has or it is a man talking to himself, and that there is no difference between the two and neither of them will know for about four years.

**Nobody is a villain in that room. Nobody is lying. Nobody is tired. **And Tomas Vale stood against a wall for two hours and ten minutes and did not say one word**, and the reason is in the day-book: *on the sixty-fourth I was asked what I wanted and I said I did not know; on the sixty-second I found out that the whole of my argument is an argument about consent and the roll is not a document about consent; **and I asked them to be on it. **And the finding the batch ends on, and it is the thing Batch 0004 is made of:**

> ***A refusal is a record. A record is a sentence that other people may read.*** Tamsin Rook's whole practice for eleven years and nine doors and eight refusals written as eight refusals and not as eight silences is to keep a refusal from being read as nothing. **In this town a refusal is read as a household that has not answered. A household that has not answered is a line open. A line open is a second round. A second round is a cart and a man and a day.** The two practices are not opposites; they are the same practice in two places, one of them protecting a refusal and the other not, and neither of them knows the other one exists. **He has spent six weeks believing the ward was in the right place and the office was in the wrong place, and he has never once walked eleven yards in the same lane as a woman with a slate, and that is not a discovery about this town. It is a discovery about a street.**
