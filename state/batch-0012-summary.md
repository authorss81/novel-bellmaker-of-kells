## What the batch was for, and what it actually is

**Three terraces, made walkable.** Tomas Vale arrived in Sennet on Day 95 with a barrow, thirteen documents none of which is about him, a hearing in seven days he could not answer, a printed slip and an unfilled form, a store four streets and four flights up he had not been to, and a third question that had been answered and closed nothing. This batch's job was to make a reader able to walk into a capital: the flat and the wharves, the middle terrace and its market and its river courts, the top terrace and its yards — and under every roof the same finding in a different costume. **The Office's ordinary bells are for paper. The coercion is a schedule. A schedule does not have a face.**

It did that, and it did it by refusing to spend anything. **No Crown clapper is seen or named. No fitting is described. No mechanism is opened. No official above a district is named. OFF. 411 does not appear on a page. No name is transferred and no child is found by a document. The First Answer chamber is not opened, described or approached. Nobody in this batch is wicked, including the storekeeper, including the court clerk, including the man who is the reason the hearing is going to go badly.** Tomas gained no stage, no sense, and no perception, attempted none, and is not described as reaching for one.

## The ten chapters, in order, and what each one paid

1. **0111 — The Flat, And Nine Hundred Bells Waiting (Day 97).** The flat, the wharves, a net-loft. Nine bells in a mile, none sounding, none broken. **A city of about nine thousand bells is about the hour, not about the bells** — a man in a town of two thousand walks into a market, a man in a city of nine thousand comes out into his own door. Holt's sentence, **a bell is a man standing next to it**, which Tomas writes down, disbelieves, and is wrong to disbelieve two days later. Two hours on a grain wharf for nothing, nine trips and then eleven, because **a man with a barrow cannot pay for anything, and the second clause of his own receipt is why.**
2. **0112 — A Counter That Takes Papers And Not People (Day 98).** The arch, the repainted notice, Perrin Lowe correct and unapologetic. **There is no box on the form for a question, and the box for a reason wants a person with a standing in a district.** And then the batch's first structural finding: **a court publishes and an office files, and the difference is four inches long.** The river courts' sworn board — nine rows going back years, no disgrace on any of it, **no reason-box, because a person swearing to a thing about themselves does not owe anybody a why.** Tomas finds the only door in this kingdom with no reason-box on it and does not go through it, **because he has nothing true and his own to swear.**
3. **0113 — What A Wharf Knows About A Wire (Day 99).** The craft chapter and the batch's turn. **A cut wire does not stop a bell; it stops a wire, and the bell on the other end of it does not know and goes on being a bell.** Holt's refusal to mend his own line in his own loft — **a mend that is in nobody's book is a fault nobody can see, and a fault nobody can see is a fault he is asked about some morning by somebody with a reason** — is the seed of Volume 04's central civic fact, and it arrives as a grievance about nine days' delay and a cart on a road. **A bell you can blame. A wire you cannot.** Everything he has been afraid of in a hundred and seven days is **a wire and a column and a habit, and none of those three is a person**, and that is the relief and it is not a comfort. **He corrects Day 97 in his own hand with nobody asking him to.**
4. **0114 — The List, And The Man Behind The Counter (Day 100, at the third hour).** The store, four flights up, nobody stops him on any of them. **Osmund Tarrant**, nine years, competent, wants nothing, is not the villain of this volume and does not become one. **A column headed what the thing is for, empty from end to end, and the reason it is empty is the best reason anybody gives in four counties: a man will not write down a word he cannot read back.** Tomas is **offered a printed sheet and refuses it in four seconds** and gives the Miremarket reason out loud. He asks for the shelf and is refused correctly. **A list that is always open is not a thing a district can put a date against, and a form has four boxes and one of them is a day.**
5. **0115 — The List Is Open At A Counter (Day 101, at the third hour).** Nine sheets, about nine hundred rows, about ninety words in the fourth column, **and the fifth column empty, and so nobody in this kingdom knows what any of the ninety is for, and that is not a conspiracy and not a secret.** **Nobody asks him who he is, and that is the first time in a hundred and one days.** He asks one question and it is not the question he came up four flights with. **He does not ask what the thing is for, and writes down that he did not, and the reason is that a man asked that has three answers and all three cost him something.** The three nines written out in a row: rooms, bells, entries, **three different nines and a number that can only be told apart by which sheet it is on is a filing reference.** He leaves with nothing.
6. **0116 — Two People Who Will Write A Thing Down (Day 102, Tamsin Rook's POV).** The batch's cost and its best civic act. The leash is **offered in the open in a market in front of about nine people and refused in the open in front of the same nine**, and the reason she gives is the first in eleven years she has given for a **method** rather than a want. **Not a ninth use. The count stays at eight.** Then the instrument, used on other people: **Ord Marren** and **Wil Brand**, two strangers, each put a true sentence about their own business into a document in their own names, in open court, unprompted, for fourpence, and it moves nothing. All she did was say their own sentences back to them, four inches, at ten feet, and then stand nine feet further off. **She was not in the room for either of them.**
7. **0117 — The Day Before A Room (Day 103, the hearing itself NOT in the chapter).** Five lines written on a step at the first hour; **four minutes outside a door at the second hour, and nothing whatever happened.** **A claimant is summoned to produce and a witness is not, and the difference is not a difference of truth.** And the page's best legal finding: **his own notice is a wall and a door in one sheet — it means nobody can ask him where it is, and it means nobody can ask him for it.** Then, at about the seventh hour, **he strikes the fifth line out with a pencil in one stroke**, and the line he strikes is the only one anybody in that room would have wanted, and the clause that stops him is a clause he asked a clerk to write because he was right. **Preparing a hearing is preparing a bell, and a bell cannot be edited after it has been rung, and he has never in his life handed a thing to another maker to finish.**
8. **0118 — The Man Who Was In The Room Does The Only Thing There Is (Day 104).** **A hearing is a room where one person's answer, once given, comes out of a column as the answer of the whole matter. Four seconds, correct form, correct column, a man doing his job, and the same hand and the same column as a town of two thousand.** And then the chapter: **a clerk of the upper room went into the river court hall at about the second hour with a key, because the room shuts at that hour, and put his own hand against a line on the sworn board, in his own name, in the open, in front of about eleven people.** About four days of his working life, a hundred yards of corridor, and a district's paper whose fourth box says *the district's business*. **It is a chore. A chore is a thing a person does for himself, in his own yard, on his own day, and a district is not a person. It is not scalable, it is not a network, it is not the thing he has been looking for, and it is the only thing that has ever moved a line.** Tomas is the reason and does not ask and is not going to be told it was worth it.
9. **0119 — The Thing That Was For A Clerk And Not For The Man (Day 105).** **A thing that happened in a room in a capital does not travel to a market in a capital** — eight of nine asked say *what hearing*. A market keeper tells a stranger what it costs to be named **about somebody else**, and Tomas stands four feet off him and says nothing, and will be told later that a man in a market told him. Then: **an office files. A court publishes. The difference between those two words is four inches long and it is the only thing anybody in this kingdom has got wrong in their own favour, and they have got it wrong on purpose, and I do not think anybody in the Quiet Office has ever been told it is a choice.** He reads the record of his own hearing standing up in about eleven minutes. **Four hundred words. The last entry in the office's own hand is nine words long: *The person named produced nothing.*** Four protections printed on a nail in a market two days' road back, and not one of them is in the file. **The only document about Tomas Vale in the world is an inventory of his own hands, written by somebody who has never seen him.** A letter from Kells arrives, eleven days late, and is not a charge.
10. **0120 — If There Were Three Of Me (Day 106).** Three terraces, one day, and nothing rung. The three-answer rule stated whole and **not used since the fortieth day of this month, seventy-three days.** A carter with three horses is asked to carry a thing up four flights and says **no, not for nothing**, and does not ask what the thing is, and is right, and the reason is a man who mends his own wire. **Ormond Bly is asked for a true sentence on purpose and gives one, and says why it is worth nothing: the day a man comes to a rail and asks me for a sentence, that sentence has a man in it.** At the sixth hour, in the Office's yard, the district has sent no sheet and **the man with the book strikes the bell four times anyway** — **Marek Douse**, six years, book, no office, **I do not know whether I am to strike it today, nobody has told me not to.** At the seventh hour **Piet Sarn**, eleven years sweeping that yard, **knows nine hundred rooms by the light going out of one window.** Three people, three true things, three different matters. **If they had been about the same thing, in three places, on the same day, that would have been a warning. A man can be one of the three answers and cannot be two of them. If there were three of me.** He unrolls the plain carrier on the step, puts his hand on the crown, and puts it back. Tamsin says his own sentence back to him four inches at ten feet. And the two halves do not meet, and neither is a victory. Ends with a hearing behind him, a plain bell he has not rung, and a route map he has not got yet.
