# Chapter Summaries

## Chapter 0001 — The Bell Without a Clapper

Tomas receives a waist-high fixed Crown survey carrier at the Vale Bellhouse before first light and discovers that its survey clapper is missing. The carrier rings without being struck; Tomas measures the warp twice and distrusts the agreement because his hand was steady twelve years ago while something that should have been loud went quiet. He clears his own flood bell out of the casting room to make space, soaks a paid job to keep the carrier documented, and sends the customer a delay notice. He documents a child-sized wax handprint, a near-cut coupling with a thin jumper, and a note attributed to Mara saying not to call it an answer. Surveyor Pever certifies the damage, says the order came out of the survey store rather than from him, and leaves. Tomas locks the carrier in the casting room, preserves the marks, and leaves with a route fragment and a warning that outlasted anything the cut coupling could account for.

## Chapter 0002 — The West Road Warning

Tomas follows the old route to the Low Ward and asks witnesses separately what they heard, recording a low sound, a pulse under the stones, and a child's fragment about children and dawn. Halden orders him to keep the carrier away from the public and shows him the L-4 notice, giving the route number and the dawn but refusing the filing date, so Tomas leaves the landing office without knowing whether the order predates the bell. Tomas gives an ordinary spoken warning, names what he knows and does not know, and receives a courier note about Tamsin Rook's missing handbell.

## Chapter 0003 — The Stolen Handbell

From her own point of view, Tamsin investigates the theft of her palm-sized courier handbell from the north-gate lockbox. She traces a Crown cord and green wool, refuses a house-by-house search, learns that her hearing was damaged by forced resonance, and follows a square-key clue to the cooper's loft. She recovers the altered handbell and keeps it. Oren arranges a watch-supervised comparison that **has not happened yet**; what she does allow on the page is unwrapping the bell once on the top step of the Bellhouse stair, in full view of the lane, so Tomas can lean over it from the doorway and read the widened wear without touching it — a private, unwatched exchange. The record contains a J. Vale key sign-out that may implicate Tomas or be a convenient lie.

## Chapter 0004 — What a Bell Can Carry

Tomas asks Sabin Fell to explain the old craft rules instead of pretending the warning is self-explanatory, and costs Sabin a customer: a woman with a fish-scale clapper walks out over Tomas taking the bench, and warns she will send the miller elsewhere. Sabin defines witness matter, a keeper's specific duty, and an informed answer, while withholding Hearthline history and naming no route failure beyond relay distortion. He then demonstrates the refusal test on himself, refuses to carry the warning to the wool market, and makes Tomas write the refusal down verbatim instead of improving it. He alludes to having once carried something past the gate that reached one house and not the next, then declines to say what it was, so his Hush guilt is hinted but still unnamed. He refuses to bind the carrier or place his name under Tomas's claim. Nell Fenn arrives with a traced child handprint and asks for a bell to bring her missing father home, saying her father went to answer a Crown summons.

## Chapter 0005 — The Child Who Was Not Named

Tomas visits Nell's Low Ward room and matches the traced handprint to her eight-year-old brother Jory, not her father Edan. Jory and Edan answered the previous Low Ward renewal round, six and five days before Nell speaks, not the L-4 posting; the board posts each round a week ahead, and a lodger notes the round is not special to their street. The landlady reports a woman in the room above who went looking for her son and never returned, and Tomas records the address but not the name, since nobody with a right to it had asked. The household fragment is incomplete, its date beneath the seal reads three days before the carrier rang, and the record treats Nell as present while marking Jory as not returned. Tomas refuses to promise a bell can find her father, tells Nell he will ask her mother before questioning her, and earns her qualified trust by asking what she wants to know first.

## Chapter 0006 — The Order Before the Bell

At market court, Tomas makes the clerk write the L-4 notice's filing date, route, and seal on a public board, accepting that his investigation will be visible. The order was filed three days before the carrier rang and permits custody of unregistered minors, but Tomas cannot yet identify who signed, copied, or accepted it. He traces a physical splice near the court, then learns from Oren that the inspection came through a watch route and that the local surveyor's service entry has disappeared.

## Chapter 0007 — A Warning Without an Order

Under a no-public-ringing order nailed to the Bellhouse door by Oren himself, Tomas casts a new trial handbell from ordinary stock. A first blank cracks and is remelted, and a correction splits his old tuning file. Using a sliver of the market-court L-4 posting as witness matter, wedged into the bell's frame so anyone can remove and read it, Tomas accepts only a narrow duty to warn about the possible inspection, and Nell gives one informed answer limited to carrying the words to one neighboring household. After two private notes, Tomas senses only that Nell's answer is strained and suffers ringing ears; the north-gate bell repeats the two-note pattern that evening.

## Chapter 0008 — The Watchman’s Account

Oren records Tomas's account of the Hush rope cut, including the fact that the cut saved one child but ended the warning before it reached Ewan. He seals the fixed carrier in its existing frame without moving it, includes the route fragment in the receipt, and orders a no-public-ringing condition for the trial bell. Oren's grief remains active, but he treats Tomas as a person who must publish evidence and accept custody rather than as an innocent or a permanent enemy.

## Chapter 0009 — The Missing Surveyor

Tomas finds the survey store partly cleared, follows the physical L-4 line, and discovers a service ticket saying the carrier's clapper was removed and the carrier disconnected after a warning was filed; no keeper answer is recorded. He feels the two-note initiating vibration travel through a narrow bypass before the north-gate bell repeats it, links a J. V. tag to the earlier J. Vale register entry without naming a suspect or choosing a conclusion, and records the route. Tamsin retains her recovered handbell, carries the route number as a record rather than a signal, and Halden sets a midnight deadline and says the commission's paper is coming.

## Chapter 0010 — The Answer Is Not Consent

Halden's temporary writ, delivered inside the deadline he set at the gate, names the fixed carrier, Tomas's trial handbell, and the loose relay bells. Tomas refuses to ring the private bell as a town command or order families to move; Nell, her mother, Tamsin, and Oren each state only what they personally choose to do. Oren invokes the writ's narrow pending-hearing clause, and Halden signs a seven-day joint receipt placing only the fixed carrier in neutral watch custody while it remains in its mounting frame. The trial and working bells stay at the Bellhouse under a no-public-ringing order; Tomas loses paid work, stands in the empty dust-outline where the frame stood, and accepts a three-answer public test before the rescheduled dawn.

## Chapter 0011 — The First Refusal

Day 3, the first full day of the stay, Tomas finds he has three blank witness slips and no lawful bell. He rules out the bound trial handbell, the loose relay bells, and the north-gate watch bell, and leaves a fourth candidate he does not want to write: the market court's unbound iron call bell, which the court clerk will not lend for words nobody in the room has accepted. Sabin teaches the rule that governs the test — write the claim once, read the same words to each person, write what they actually said, never treat two matching answers as a stronger one than a third that disagrees. Oren sets out the four-part frame: physical witness, keeper's duty, three independent answers, and a carrier that may not add anything. Tamsin warns that a public tone is larger than the claim under it and points out that a version of Tomas's warning ending in *before dawn* is already circulating at the landing. Five witnesses come to the market court. **Bevin Sarr refuses**, because a slip with his name on it would be filed in the same office as the ward list that holds his unpaid entry; Oren records the refusal in his own words, unsigned at his request, and Tomas does not call it sabotage. The witnesses who remain insist the test must ask a real question about the water, not only about the Crown order, and the clerk will answer for the bell only when a keeper of standing accepts a duty for it.

## Chapter 0012 — A Promise Before the Rain

Day 4, morning, in rising rain, Tamsin meets **Hollis Renn** at his low flood gate under his written duty, which he reads off an oilskin board: the keeper is the man who has read the mark with his own hand in the hour before, the words said at the gate are the words the keeper himself can support, and nothing is added in the ward or above the landing. She rings her handbell once at the gate with a boy listening on the landing stage and a crown of wax laid over an older groove, and the note carries past its authorized boundary. She pays for the ring in ringing ears and a nosebleed, and sets the rule that a duty governs words while metal governs distance. Adults, including Foreman Crane, want the bell used to close the low lane; she refuses and offers to knock door to door instead. Tomas arrives with a rod and no finished claim. **Wyman Dace**, the bridge keeper, accepts a promise written on slate — to walk the west road and report before evening what he finds, and never to say the bridge is safe — because a man who says a bridge is safe is asking the river to prove something it has not agreed to. Renn accepts only his own duty, refuses to carry a road claim, and will not ring until the gate has been walked and the windlass has turned twice under load. A boy reports that the low lane is under and a Crown road gang's cart is across the culvert with nobody coming.

## Chapter 0013 — The Low Ward Does Not Answer

Day 4, midday into afternoon, after Tamsin's morning, Nell asks her mother first and is refused permission to answer for the household, then writes her own question, pins it to the ward board, and reads the identical words to three adults. **Foreman Crane** accepts a duty to carry the words to the landing and stop there. **Wenna Cray** asks for time until she can find the round paper for the woman who went looking for her sister. **Garn Fisk** refuses the route and refuses to be named. A Crown officer asks who called the meeting; Jerm records the answers in the watch book instead. Nell keeps the household fragment, decides to have it copied in daylight at a board rather than hidden, and publishes only that three different choices were made. Tomas wants to write the refusal as fear; Nell refuses, and he accepts her record. He tells her he will not hand her a question to ask. The Bellwrights' Measure's notice arrives: the private binding and route work are held to be a hazard to public order, and the trial handbell must be submitted for guild inspection or the workshop's licence is challenged.

## Chapter 0014 — The Bellwright's Condition

Day 5, late afternoon, Tomas goes to Sabin, then to the Measure's temporary room above the fish market, where **Iven Sore** — the living master, and the name on the withdrawn docket — inspects the trial handbell without sounding it, because the no-ringing order is in force, and files that limitation himself. He lifts the L-4 posting sliver out of the bell's frame with two fingers and calls the wedging correct, because a paper any person can remove can be removed by anyone. He unrolls the Measure's road map and shows a dated, keyed mark for each of the forty-one wards that have taken their answers back since before the Crown laid a mile of copper, then says plainly that a map shows where a line runs and a hand does the altering. Asked whether he withdrew the L-4 service entry, he answers that he can support never holding a Crown commission, cannot support the rest, and will not say he did not sign it, because a denial made in a recorded room is a sentence that can be used in either direction. He offers a certified record, a copy of the map, and a seat in the room where a route is built, in exchange for a master key and a licence making the Bellhouse answerable for every route answer; Tomas refuses both. The Measure issues a **conditional challenge** — the material inspection plus a published withdrawal procedure lodged at the market court — and not a ban. Sabin refuses to give the guild an opinion, which is what keeps his name off their record. Oren's Crown officer blocks the bell's trip at the court arch; Jerm reads the order and lets it pass, and the officer logs a complaint.

## Chapter 0015 — The Sealed Workshop

Day 6, morning, Halden brings an inventory and sealing order. Oren separates four object classes aloud in the yard, seals only the eight connected working bells, puts his own watch mark on the same wax so it takes two hands to remove, records the rest, and enters that nothing in the inventory moves ownership or makes a private answer into a public one. Tomas sets the bound trial handbell out in public himself and shows that the posting sliver lifts out with two fingers. The **watch-supervised comparison Oren arranged in Chapter 3 is performed here**: Tamsin unwraps her handbell on the cloth, Tomas reads the wax over the older groove from a distance without touching it, and the record states that the widening is a fact about metal and not evidence the ward agreed to anything, with no custody changed and the route neither disclosed nor required. The miller takes his daughter's feast bell to a Miremarket maker; the chandler's work goes when Nell declines. Tomas nearly hides the trial bell in the casting-room cellar stair and does not, because Tamsin tells him a manufactured route cannot be repaired by anybody including its maker. Oren requires a public account of the Hush rope cut before the town will answer him.

## Chapter 0016 — The Rope in the Market Court

Day 6, late day, in the rain, Oren holds a public account rather than a trial and says so. Halden objects in the record. Tomas says he cut the frame's lifting line to reach a child trapped under the counterweight, that the line was already carrying a warning, and that the cut is the reason the warning ended before it reached the ward. He states that he cannot prove what the tone would have done had the line held. **Wen Anner**, the child he saved, refuses to be used as absolution or as condemnation and has it entered that he is angry at both. A Low Ward witness enters that a thin sound followed the frame's fall and that people moved because of what they saw, which corrects Oren's private belief without clearing Tomas. Oren enters three conditions on any public signal — name known, unknown and next keeper inside the words; any household or keeper may withdraw with no reason, fee or consequence, recorded as a withdrawal and never as silence; and such a signal is not a municipal act. The crowd is not satisfied and the record survives. **Gib Hask** demands the Crown's buried line be pulled out of the ward, and Oren records both positions as unreconcilable. A message from the low gate says the west road has gone soft and the sill is climbing.

## Chapter 0017 — What the Water Owes

Day 7, all day, the water crests. Tomas measures an undercut at the north end of the west road's low section, finds the Crown road gang's cart across the culvert with the foreman's signature on it, and issues one bounded claim naming the second mark, the cart, the undercut and every sentence about what he cannot tell anybody. **Wyman Dace** accepts and reports with two additions of his own; **Wenna Cray** accepts only with the Crown's cart stated in her sentence, only as far as the ward board, and with the cost to herself named in advance; **Hollis Renn** refuses to carry anything of anybody's. Renn finds the gate's pawl slipping with gravel packed behind the sill, so the ring comes late and four houses at the foot of the ward take water to table height; the chandler loses his flour. Tomas's cost is a loss of balance in the crowded lane and renewed ringing ears. Bevin Sarr makes a Crown officer say in front of eleven witnesses that a works order held the cart. Four households moved goods, two waited and lost a day, six stalls lost the market's trade, and a household's pig drowned. The chapter ends with three answers that agree with nothing and the question of how a bell carries three positions without merging them.

## Chapter 0018 — Three Answers, One Danger

Day 8, the water falling, Tomas lodges the withdrawal procedure at the market court, satisfying the Measure's second condition. The market court usher brings the unbound iron call bell and states she is its keeper and will count every answer including the ones she dislikes. Halden objects in the record and then gives his best argument: he is not there to stop the assembly, he is there because if a child drowns the Crown will send a clerk to ask whether the ward was warned, and he wants that answer on paper. **Sena Dunn withdraws in open court** because two Crown officers are present and her name is already on two rolls; Tomas begins to build her leaving into an answer he could use and Nell stops him — *she is not a silence you get to fill*. Before the court sits Tomas puts the four agreements on the page, one name per sheet, and watches three of them change: **Wenna Cray withdraws to the ward board**, **Hollis Renn sends four words — *I keep a gate, not a court* — and withdraws**, and **Foreman Crane substitutes**, using Tomas's own published procedure to withdraw from two doors on his row with no reason given. The claim is read twice in identical words, and three answers are given: Crane accepts with that boundary, Dace asks for time until he has walked the road again, and Sarr refuses the route and agrees to the refusal being read aloud unsigned. Dace returns before the court closes and his answer is entered as an **amendment in his own column**, not a fourth answer. The usher counts one, two, three on the iron, including the refusal, and refuses to count the amendment again. When a man shouts that the Crown is calling the ward, the usher refuses Tomas a fourth stroke and Oren answers in words. **Surveyor Rhea Collis** arrives off the north road, states she is not the officer who certified the carrier and never held the L-4 entry, and her line on the roll bears no withdrawal. By evening the ward's version has collapsed to two answers both agreeing, and a Crown clerk arrives with an order treating the three-answer record as a **single local response** on L-4 and demanding the slips.

## Chapter 0019 — The Cost of a Public Tone

Day 9, morning, Tamsin is worn down by the counted strokes and cannot follow speech in a crowded room. A woman tells her a bell said everybody must leave; the version did the damage, and Tamsin is more useful in thirty seconds of truth than in a whole afternoon of assembly. At the watch house, the Crown's filing clerk wants the three answers as one packet and Jerm refuses. Tamsin logs the handbell's real reach with Jerm — seven of eleven between the north gate and the landing heard it, three of five on the ferry road beyond it heard it, and the second cottage past the toll shed heard nothing at all — and writes a rule: the handbell is a repeater, not an author; it repeats only; it rings only with a named person beside it who can say what it means; it rings for the whole of a record or none of it; and it is never rung to fill a withdrawn place or to carry only the convenient parts. Tomas arrives with a plan and an instruction. She stops him, makes him ask, and says yes on her own terms: on foot, once at the gate, no Crown wages, the route ending where the route ends unless a person asks, and a village that will hear it late and a house that will not hear it at all. Tomas admits he has been handing her work and calling it a favour, and writes the admission into the record. At noon Halden posts an **emergency custody hearing for the first hour of light on Day 10**, before the rescheduled dawn, over the whole of the workshop's signal apparatus, on the ground that a workshop which can sound an answer without the ward's agreement is more dangerous than before.

## Chapter 0020 — The Hearing Before the Hearing

Day 9, evening, at the watch house, Oren lays the whole documentary record out in the order it was made and says he did it so that nobody can object to an order that flatters Tomas. The order predates the sound, the line was cut and bypassed afterwards, the service ticket names nobody, and the market court bell is iron on a rope with no line, so the Crown's claim that it is a connected route is a claim rather than a finding. The Crown's filing man asks for a single packet, is refused, and privately admits to Oren that he has merged divided answers into single ones for eleven years of filing because a ward that cannot make up its mind is read as a ward that will not answer. Tomas's beautiful, impossible promise — that Kells will be warned before dawn and no child will be taken by surprise — is read by Nell and Tamsin, torn up, and replaced with the duller sentence Tomas had abandoned on Day 8. Nell dictates a copy of the household fragment herself and signs for it. Oren opens the L-4 file in front of a Crown copyist. Its service ticket now reads *answered in office, L-4 ward roll, per local practice*. Its sixth sheet is a schedule headed *ROUTE L-4 — RESPONSES ENTERED BEFORE SERVICE*: forty-one households, one mark each, an office number and no signature, dated the day each round was **posted**. Line twenty-two is the **Fenn household, six persons**, with two marked *accompanying* and no names, where the ward fragment says three with a fourth line cut away. Tomas tries to name the two accompanying and the clerk asks which two; he answers that the page does not say and that supplying them would hand the Crown two people to go and find, and the clerk enters *two persons marked accompanying, unidentified on the page*. The clerk enters the count, the office number and the date and refuses the conclusion; Tomas enters the same and refuses to draw the line for the town. Oren tells Tomas he will not stop the seizure of the trial bell. The batch ends with the dawn still ahead, the custody notice on the board, and the Vale Bellhouse open under three stacked marks on its door.
