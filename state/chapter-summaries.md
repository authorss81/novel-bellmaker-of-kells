# Chapter Summaries

## Chapter 0001 — The Bell Without a Clapper

Tomas receives a waist-high fixed Crown survey carrier at the Vale Bellhouse before first light and discovers that its survey clapper is missing. The carrier rings without being struck; Tomas documents its warped lip, a child-sized wax handprint, a near-cut coupling with a thin jumper, and a note attributed to Mara saying not to call it an answer. He locks the carrier in the casting room, preserves the marks, and leaves with a route fragment and a warning that remains unexplained.

## Chapter 0002 — The West Road Warning

Tomas follows the old route to the Low Ward and asks witnesses separately what they heard, recording a low sound, a pulse under the stones, and a child's fragment about children and dawn. Halden orders him to keep the carrier away from the public and shows him a dated L-4 inspection notice without allowing him to claim its full meaning. Tomas gives a plain spoken warning, names what he knows and does not know, and receives a courier note about Tamsin Rook's missing handbell.

## Chapter 0003 — The Stolen Handbell

From her own point of view, Tamsin investigates the theft of her portable courier handbell from the north-gate lockbox. She traces green wool and Crown cord, refuses a house-by-house search, learns that her hearing was damaged by forced resonance, and follows a square-key clue to the cooper's loft. She recovers the altered handbell, keeps it, and agrees only to a supervised comparison; the record contains a J. Vale key sign-out that may implicate Tomas or be a convenient lie.

## Chapter 0004 — What a Bell Can Carry

Tomas asks Sabin Fell to explain the old craft rules instead of pretending the warning is self-explanatory. Sabin defines witness matter, a keeper's specific duty, and an informed answer, while withholding Hearthline history and insisting on a refusal test. He refuses to bind the carrier or place his name under Tomas's claim. Nell Fenn arrives with a traced child handprint and asks for a bell to bring her missing father home.

## Chapter 0005 — The Child Who Was Not Named

Tomas visits Nell's Low Ward room and matches the traced handprint to her eight-year-old brother Jory, not her father Edan. The household fragment is incomplete, Jory and Edan were taken before the posted L-4 notice, and the record treats Nell as present while omitting Jory. Tomas refuses to promise a bell can find her father, offers to investigate the record, and earns Nell's qualified trust by asking what she wants to know first.

## Chapter 0006 — The Order Before the Bell

At market court, Tomas makes the clerk write the L-4 notice's filing date, route, and seal on a public board, accepting that his investigation will be visible. The order was filed three days before the carrier rang and permits custody of unregistered minors, but Tomas cannot yet identify who signed, copied, or accepted it. He traces a physical splice near the court, then learns from Oren that the inspection came through a watch route and that the local surveyor's service entry has disappeared.

## Chapter 0007 — A Warning Without an Order

Under a no-public-ringing notice, Tomas casts a new trial handbell from ordinary stock. A first blank cracks and is remelted, and a correction splits his old tuning file. Using the posted-notice sliver as witness matter, Tomas accepts only a narrow duty to warn about the possible inspection, and Nell gives one informed answer limited to carrying the words to one neighboring household. After two private notes, Tomas senses only that Nell's answer is strained and suffers ringing ears; the north-gate bell repeats the two-note pattern that evening.

## Chapter 0008 — The Watchman's Account

Oren records Tomas's account of the Hush rope cut, including the fact that the cut saved one child but ended the warning before it reached Ewan. He seals the fixed carrier in its existing frame without moving it, includes the route fragment in the receipt, and orders a no-public-ringing condition for the trial bell. Oren's grief remains active, but he treats Tomas as a person who must publish evidence and accept custody rather than as an innocent or a permanent enemy.

## Chapter 0009 — The Missing Surveyor

Tomas finds the survey store partly cleared, follows the physical L-4 line, and discovers a service ticket saying the carrier's clapper was removed and the carrier disconnected after a warning was filed; no keeper answer is recorded. He feels the two-note initiating vibration travel through a narrow bypass before the north-gate bell repeats it, links a J. V. tag to the earlier J. Vale register entry without choosing a conclusion, and records the route. Tamsin retains her recovered handbell, carries the route number as a record rather than a signal, and a Crown holding writ arrives.

## Chapter 0010 — The Answer Is Not Consent

Halden's temporary writ names the fixed carrier, Tomas's trial handbell, and the loose relay bells. Tomas refuses to ring the private bell as a town command or order families to move; Nell, her mother, Tamsin, and Oren each state only what they personally choose to do. Oren invokes the writ's narrow pending-hearing clause, and Halden signs a seven-day joint receipt placing only the fixed carrier in neutral watch custody while it remains in its mounting frame. The trial and working bells stay at the Bellhouse under a no-public-ringing order; Tomas loses paid work and accepts a three-answer public test before the rescheduled dawn.
