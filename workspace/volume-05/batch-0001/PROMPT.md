## The form of the work, and the measurements this project actually uses

**A chapter is a complete scene with movement and a changed situation. One chapter, one day, and the events in a chapter belong to the day the chapter names.** Open a chapter on a person who is not Tomas, inside that person's own work, and let Tomas come into it — **Volume 04's closing batch did this in eight of its ten and that is the number to beat, not to copy.** Three or four of your ten should run most of their length on **one sustained exchange between two people with the work visible and the theme nowhere stated aloud.** Day-books are a man's own account of a day and **not a transcript of it, and a speech belongs to the mouth that said it**: a day-book re-derives, it does not re-print.

**Four figures are a shape and five are a template, and a chapter with one line taken out of a frame that is otherwise identical is a chapter with a line missing, not a variation.** The hours must differ in shape between your ten chapters and not by subtraction from one skeleton. Day-time hours will be 1 to 12 and bare; the ninth hour of the evening is the only half-day and opens every day-book.

**The four measurements this project makes, and the method for each, because a figure without a method is not a measurement.**

1. **Word counts per chapter and for the batch, whitespace, whole file.** Ordinary chapters run about 2,200 to 3,200 words. **Do not pad and do not split a complete scene to hit a number.** Every chapter must be above 1,800 words and inside the 2,000 to 3,000 band the last two batches held, and the day-book must be under thirty-five per cent of its chapter.
2. **Duplicate prose, three ways, and the third one is the one that matters.** (a) Every **distinct** paragraph pair inside a file, longest exact shared run in words — and **never a paragraph compared with itself**, which is a defect the Volume 04 audit's own first instrument had and which produced a false batch maximum of eighty. (b) Every line of twenty-five words or more against the chapter's own prose. (c) All forty-five file pairs. **And run a fourth test the exact one cannot see: a near-duplicate, where a passage is reprinted with its connectives altered.** Chapter 0197 of Volume 04 carried a day-book column printed twice that **all three** of that batch's exact tests passed, because the two copies differed by a handful of words and the longest exact run was twenty-three against a threshold of twenty. **A re-derived day-book is exactly where this happens, because the temptation is to reach for the sentence already written.** For the near test: align two units word by word, merge aligned blocks across gaps of eight words or fewer, and flag any region of twenty words or more where at least seventy per cent of the words align and the longest **exact** run inside it is under twenty.
3. **The *about* family**, counted as a family and not as *about nine* alone, and per file, and the per-file figures must **add up to the total.** A sweep that removes the measure is the failure this project has committed most often: a regex that deleted *about* also deleted the quantity in **164 places** in one batch and five of those sentences had to be rebuilt by hand.
4. **The day-book, the custody paragraph, the register paragraph and the *which office is it* paragraph, named and measured per file.** Three blocks may be carried word for word and are exempt: **the custody formula, the receipt's fifth clause wherever it is named, and the closing *which office is it* paragraph** — because re-deriving a receipt's own wording in a man's own book on ten different days would make it a different receipt ten times. **An exemption without a measurement is a hidden repetition, so give the per-file size of every exempt block and the volume-wide total with the method named.**

**Standing wording you may carry on purpose, and must name if you carry it:** the custody formula; the fifth clause; the *which office is it* opener with the paragraph after it re-derived every day; the day-book opening line; and any motif sentence you invent — **and if you invent one, it will be measured against you in the next pass, so name it the day you make it.**

**Sweeps to run on the prose before you write the state layer and again after, and to run on the state layer after you write it: no weekday, no month, no age construction, no `##` heading, no `hour of the (morning|afternoon)`, no `EOF` / `head -` / `tail -` / stray fence in any file you save, per-line bold-marker parity, the boundary names, and the word *clapper*.**

**An age is a number about a person. A man's years in a trade are a duration and are not an age, and a duration may not exceed the only age on the page. A new person enters with no age, and a further recorded exception is a canon change no batch may make.** Volume 04's closing batch gave a new boy the age thirteen **eight times** in one chapter while its own sweep reported that no age was given for anybody, and **the repair pass had to find all thirteen by reading the file.** A duration that looks like an age is a duration: *nine years of a mark, nine years of a post, four years of a rate, about eleven years in a room, four hundred yards, a hundred and forty feet, about eleven years old* is about a thing and not about a person. **Old** and **an old man** and **an old woman** are descriptions and are permitted and may not be converted into a number by a later writer.
