One chapter, one day, and the events in a chapter belong to the day the chapter names.** Open a chapter on a person who is not Tomas, inside that person's own work, and let Tomas come into it. **Batch 0002 and Batch 0003 both did it in ten of ten, and Batch 0003 beat Batch 0002's ten openers, and the ten of Batch 0003 were: a man at the bottom of a grave, a man at a trough with nine staves standing in it, a woman on her own step with nets hung behind her, a man at an oven drawing a batch, a woman walking a crooked line of stones in a ford, a man with a pointer going round a schoolroom, a man on an upturned pail splicing reed, a man with a flat book at a milestone, a man cutting the foot of a peat bank, and a man hanging a field-gate hinge on a post.** **DO NOT OPEN TWO CHAPTERS ON A MAN WITH A BESOM, A BROOM, A BOOK OR A CHAIN, BECAUSE THOSE FOUR ARE USED, AND DO NOT OPEN TWO OF YOURS ON A SPADE, A BENCH, AN ANVIL, A STEP OR A BOOK. TEN KINDS, ONE TO A CHAPTER, IN CHAPTER ORDER.**

**Three or four of your ten should run most of their length on one sustained exchange between two people with the work visible and the theme nowhere stated aloud.** Batch 0003 did it in four — the gravedigger and the hole, the ford-keeper and the stones, the schoolmaster and the chair, the nailer and the hinge — and **the schoolmaster's stoppage was plain and not a refusal, and that is the class you want to beat. A sustained exchange in your batch must not be a refusal and must not be an agreement, and it must not be a fourth grammar for the same sentence.**

**Day-books are a man's own account of a day and not a transcript of it, and a speech belongs to the mouth that said it: a day-book re-derives, it does not re-print.** **BATCH 0003'S OWN RECORD IS THAT ITS DAY-BOOK SHARES RAN 30.24 TO 34.90 AND THAT IT TOOK PROSE ADDITIONS IN EIGHT CHAPTERS AND FIFTEEN DAY-BOOK RE-DERIVATIONS TO GET THEM UNDER THIRTY-FIVE, AND THAT THE HIGHEST IT EVER REACHED ON THE WAY WAS 41.34 IN CHAPTER 0230, AND THAT NINE OF ITS TEN CHAPTERS LANDED ABOVE THE THIRTY IT WAS AIMING AT. WRITE TO A TARGET OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE PER CENT, EXPECT TO TIGHTEN, AND RE-DERIVE YOUR OWN REPAIRS BACK DOWN RATHER THAN CUTTING THE BATCH'S PROSE TO HIT THE NUMBER.**

**The hour lists must differ in shape between your ten chapters and not by subtraction from one skeleton.** Day-time hours will be 1 to 12 and bare; the ninth hour of the evening is the only half-day and it opens every day-book and appears exactly twice in every chapter. **A retrospective bare hour of another day is permitted and a retrospective half-day is not, and there is no eighth hour anywhere in Chapters 161 to 230 and none may be drafted into yours.** **THE THIRTY SETS ALREADY IN USE ARE NOT YOURS AND NONE OF YOUR TEN MAY EQUAL ANY OF THEM. Batch 0002's ten, in order: first/second/fourth/sixth/seventh; second/third/fourth/fifth/seventh; second/fourth/fifth/sixth/tenth; first/second/third/fourth/sixth; second/fourth/fifth/sixth/seventh/eleventh; first/third/fourth/fifth/seventh; second/third/sixth/seventh/ninth/twelfth; fourth/fifth/sixth/seventh; first/second/fourth/fifth/sixth/seventh/tenth; second/third/fourth/sixth/seventh. **Standing wording you may carry on purpose, and must name if you carry it:** the custody formula — *My own bell is in a sealed yard in Kells and the bar is on the bell and not on the licence, and it does not travel* — **twenty-seven words**; *The five clauses stand and I am not going through them*, **eleven words**; *The register is thirteen*, **five words**; the *which office is it* opener, **four** words, once in each chapter with the paragraph after it re-derived every time and that paragraph **not** exempt; the day-book opening line, re-derived in shape in all ten and no two of them the same sentence; and any motif sentence you invent. **If you invent one it will be measured against you in the next pass, so name it the day you make it.** **Give the per-file size of every exempt block and the volume-wide total with the method named, because an exemption without a measurement is a hidden repetition.** **METHOD: exact string match of the block against the file with all runs of whitespace collapsed to one space; one occurrence counted per match; a WORD is an `[A-Za-z'’-]+` token of the matched string.** Batch 0003 carried the four blocks above, 46 words a file and 460 words across its ten files out of 35,085, and the unique standing wording is 46 words carried ten times. **IT ALSO CARRIED THE EIGHTY-EIGHT-WORD STANDING BLOCK OF THE STRIKE COUNTER, THE CUSTODY FORMULA, THE HANDBELL CLAUSE AND THE NOTHING-STRUCK CLAUSE, WHICH IS ITS CROSS-FILE MAXIMUM AND IS NAMED RATHER THAN EXCUSED — AND BATCH 0003 BROKE IT BY VARYING THE CONNECTIVE TISSUE AROUND THE THREE EXEMPT BLOCKS IN SEVEN OF ITS TEN CHAPTERS, WHICH IS THE MOVE AND THE ONLY MOVE, BECAUSE THE THREE EXEMPT BLOCKS THEMSELVES MAY NOT VARY. **Duplicate prose, four instruments, and they are run because a batch that certifies its own cleanliness without running them has failed:** (a) every DISTINCT paragraph pair inside a file, longest exact shared run in words, a paragraph never compared with itself; (b) every line of twenty-five words or more against the REST of that chapter's own prose, the line excluded; (c) all forty-five file pairs; (d) a near-duplicate — align two units word by word, merge aligned blocks across gaps of eight words or fewer, and flag any region of twenty words or more where at least seventy per cent of the words align and the longest EXACT run inside it is under twenty, because that is a reprint with its connectives altered and all three exact tests pass it. **Run (d) pairwise, each distinct prose unit against each distinct day-book unit.** Batch 0003's figures on its finished files: intra-file maximum **fifteen** words, per file 7 / 10 / 14 / 10 / 9 / 11 / 10 / 11 / 8 / 15, and the fifteen is a stage sentence and a speech sharing an opening in Chapter 0230; line-against-the-rest **fifteen**; cross-file **eighty-nine** day-book against day-book and **twenty-eight** prose against prose; and the near test flags **twenty-eight regions, of which TWO are a day-book recording a person's spoken words, TWENTY-SIX are the day-book re-deriving the day's own stage, and NONE is the class that matters, which is the day-book re-printing the narrator's analytic prose. **The *about* family, counted as a family and not as *about nine* alone, and per file, and the per-file figures must add up to the total.** **METHOD, NAMED BECAUSE BATCH 0002 PUBLISHED IT WITHOUT NAMING IT: a CASE-INSENSITIVE match on the substring *about*, which catches a sentence-INITIAL *About*.
