## What this batch is, in one paragraph

**The Unpaid Names are a list.** For twenty-nine days a name on a roll has been a mark in a column. In this batch a name read on a roll begins, in about four years, to be read as a **duty** — by the household first, before any office, because a household that has been sent to twice does not want to be sent to a third time — and a duty is a mouth, and a mouth is for a claim. **A child is moved. Tamsin Rook's reason for being on the road turns out to be a name on a roll in a town four counties away, and it refuses to answer. The truth begins to cost somebody other than Tomas. And the batch ends with a merchant's house on a roll and an inheritance that cannot be claimed while the roll stands.**

**Nobody in this batch is wicked. That is not a softening; it is the engine, and it held for the whole of Batch 0003 and it holds here.** The enemy is a column, a stamp and a habit.

## The two things this batch must do that Batch 0003 deliberately did not

**1. The list begins to be a list of duties, and the first person it costs is not Tomas.** In Miremarket the roll is a bookkeeping practice defended by competent tired people and nothing has happened to anybody about four pounds a quarter. **In this batch a name is read aloud as something a household owes, and a household acts on it, and the acting is what costs.** A child is moved. The cost must be concrete, specific, borne by named people with no ages, and **it must be a cost of obedience and not of malice** — a father who loves his son sends him, or a mother who is owed something, or a household that believes it has been asked and has not been told no.

**2. The truth costs somebody other than Tomas, and the somebody is a person who is not a villain.** This is the batch's hard requirement and it is the one the previous four batches have avoided. It may not be a wound, it may not be a death, it may not be a rescue, and it may not be undone at the end of Chapter 90. **Somebody who is right has said something true, out loud, in a room, in their own name, and it cannot be unsaid, and the person it was said to does not get it back, and nobody in this batch apologises for it and nobody is forgiven.**

## The two house rules, repeated because every batch has broken one of them

1. **No weekday and no month is named in any chapter.** Days are numbered: *the sixty-seventh*, *the seventy-seventh*, *this morning*. A recurring day is **a market day, a round, a bell day, a three-day market, or by its number**. **Volume 01 ended clean after fourteen lines carrying weekday names were removed across nine chapters, the previous batch drafted ten more into Chapters 66–70 and all ten were removed before those chapters were saved, and this batch's predecessor drafted seven more into Chapters 71–80 — five before the first save and two during the review repair, and the seventh was a weekday reintroduced by a repair pass, which is the direction this error has now gone nine times. All seven were removed. This is the failure class. Run the search, before and after, on the prose and on the state layer.**
2. **The hour numerals run 1 to 12 twice a day, and no half-day may be attached to an hour that a chapter does not attach one to.** If the prose says *the fourth hour*, then no state file, summary or day-book may say *the fourth hour of the afternoon*. **Write the half-day in the prose whenever you want one.** **Volume 01 found four such invented half-days in the state layer, Batch 0002 found two in the prose, and Batch 0003 found and fixed seven weekday names in the prose, recorded every bare hour as bare, and — on the second review of it — was found to have left a second, drifted copy of the clock in `state/batch-0008-summary.md` with three half-days in it that no chapter has, and that copy is now deleted. Both directions happen, and the state layer is the more reliable-looking of the two. The chapters are the record, and there is exactly one clock for any batch, in `state/continuity.md`.**

And the rule underneath both: **a fix is not a fix because a summary says so.** Every number, date, clock time, custody state, physical condition, age and name in a state file must be traceable to a specific chapter and line. **If a chapter does not give an age, a count or an hour, the state file records that it is not on the page.**

**The sweep must be run before you write and after, on the state layer as well as the prose**, and **when you update the state layer at the end of the batch, search your own new text for the words you removed from the prose** — that is the direction the error has gone eight times now.

**Sweep every file you write for stray shell text before you save it.** A previous repair pass wrote the literal strings `EOF` and `head -5 state/open-threads.md` into the end of a state file, and a repair pass that writes shell text into a state file is the single most damaging thing this project can do.

He reads it back to the woman it belongs to and she changes one word in it, and he makes the change, and it goes in the third column and not the fourth, because it is not a thing nobody could answer.**
- **Emotional change:** **He understands at last that the thing he has been building for six weeks is not a way of protecting anybody. It is a way of making certain that there is a document, and a document is what a person is read by, and he has been careful all his life and careful has turned out to be a way of writing things down.** And what he has left is a list that cannot be unrolled, four columns, a slate, a witness, **and a woman beside him who has said nine words on a landing and will not be thanked for them.**
- **Power or resource change:** No power, no stage, no sense, and he does not identify a copied answer and does not learn where a sound went. He gains **nothing he can act on, a list of about nine names, a column that a clerk can certify and a list that cannot be certified at all, and the knowledge that a name on a roll begins in about four years to be a duty.**
