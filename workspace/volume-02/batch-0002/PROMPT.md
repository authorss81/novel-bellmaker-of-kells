### Chapter 0061 — The Boat

- **POV:** Tomas Vale.
- **Location/time:** The river, Day 45 into Day 46, morning into the second day.
- **Immediate goal:** Get a locked frame in a cradle from a river landing to somewhere he can ask a question, without breaking a receipt he wrote himself.
- **Resistance:** A barrow is a thing you own and a boat is a thing you are given a place in, and he has not been a passenger since he was nineteen years old and does not know how to be one. **And the object is in the cradle with the bolts made fast, and the cradle is the thing the receipt says may not leave the carriage, and a boat's deck is not a carriage and he has to work out, in front of a ferry-man, what the carriage is.** He works it out wrongly the first time and is corrected by a man he paid, and the correction is in the record.
- **Information revealed:** **A receipt is written to be read by strangers, and the moment it is on a river it will be read by a stranger, and the first thing a stranger will want to know is what it is.** And: the object has now travelled, and **the district has withdrawn a finding and nobody has asked for the withdrawal, and a boat is a place where a man who is not going to be asked anything says what he is carrying.**
- **Action or decision:** He does not tell the ferry-man what is in the frame, and he does not lie about it either; he says *it is Crown property and I am carrying it under a receipt and the receipt says who may ask for it and you are a person and not only an officer, and you may ask for it now or in four days and I will produce it either way, and I would rather you asked now.* **The ferry-man does not ask now.** He books the frame's place anyway and says one thing about the receipt that Tomas writes down.
- **Emotional change:** He is a man who has spent a month insisting that other people handle the paper, and he is now the paper. **He learns what his own fifth clause feels like from the outside** and writes it down and does not improve it.
- **Power or resource change:** No power, no bell. He gains **a passage and a debt to a man who did not ask.**
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** **The frame never leaves the cradle and the bolts are made fast at every place it is set down, and nothing is rung.** The receipt is not broken and the fifth clause is not invoked as a threat.
- **Ending type:** Discovery.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The water does the only thing water does, and it does it in front of a man who has been in a lane in the rain in a different month — **a boat puts a barrow, a frame and a man in the same line of sight for four days, in public, on the water, and there is no lane to go round.**

### Chapter 0062 — The Fifth Panel On Water

- **POV:** Tamsin Rook.
- **Location/time:** The river and a landing, Day 46 into Day 47, morning into the evening.
- **Immediate goal:** Find out whether a river road has a map, and if it has one, whether its fifth panel is gone.
- **Resistance:** A river does not print maps, and every map she can buy is printed inland, and the ones inland have a fifth panel cut out of all of them, and the panel she wants is the one that names an office. **And the resistance is a person:** the only person aboard who has bought a road sheet in eleven years is a man who bought his in a town two hundred miles up the river and has carried it as a decoration, and he will not sell it and will not be asked to, and she has to find a second copy, and a second copy costs a day's walk or a shilling she does not have.
- **Information revealed:** **The cut is in the plate, so it is on every sheet printed from that plate in every year since, and a thing removed from a plate does not have a date; a thing removed from a copy has a date and this has no date, which is worse.** And: on water, the routes are not roads, they are **tides and landings and stopping places**, and a river's return does not go to an office by road at all.
- **Action or decision:** She does not steal the sheet and does not ask twice. She **writes down what she can see of it from the other side of a table without asking to see it**, and she puts the description on the slate — which she fished out of a sluice two days ago and dried — and **she gives the slate away to a person on the boat, and does not ask for it back, and says why.**
- **Emotional change:** She finds that the thing she is good at is being careful with paper in public, and that the cost of being careful is that nobody credits her, and **she decides in this chapter to be credited or not to be good, and gets it wrong, and the chapter says so.**
- **Power or resource change:** No power. She gains a description in her own hand, a copy of a sheet from a different year, and **a person on that boat who has now been told what she is reading and has not been asked to help.**
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** **This is not the Bellwrights' Measure's road map**, which is in a district file in Fourmile read as a schedule of non-response, and whose **fifty-three** withdrawals are not on any map and must never be conflated with a count on one. **Her bell is not rung and nobody asks her for a reason.**
- **Ending type:** Discovery.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The landing puts them in a town with a district office in it, and a district office in a town is a building with an address, and **a building with an address has papers in it, and papers have names on them.**

### Chapter 0064 — The House They Left

- **POV:** Tamsin Rook.
- **Location/time:** The town and a lane off it, Day 49, from the fourth hour.

### Chapter 0065 — The Office Used Properly

### Chapter 0066 — Asking For A Bell In The Open

- **POV:** Tamsin Rook.
- **Location/time:** Past the county line, a smithy and a room over a shop, Day 51, morning into the afternoon.
- **Immediate goal:** Get a bell bought, in the open, in front of people, with a record behind it — and she is not the buyer.

- **Information revealed:** **A master of the Measure may certify a bell he did not make, out of a stock with a record, on a making that is complete — mould, pattern, mark, date and hand — and that is the whole of the craft law of Chapter 46 applied the other way round.** And: **the bar on Tomas's plain carrier is on the bell and not on the licence, so the making of a traceable bell is available to a man whose last bell is barred, and a room of tradesmen can see that in about nine seconds and does.**
- **Action or decision:** She gets a room to say the thing out loud in, and the smith, and the master, and **she reads Tomas's own receipt aloud, all five clauses, to about eleven people, and then reads the finding of the Measure aloud, and then asks the master the one question that is hers to ask: may a man buy a bell he has not made, out of metal somebody else sold with a record, and be told the whole of what he is buying before he pays?** And the answer is yes, in the open, in front of everybody, and the room hears it.
- **Emotional change:** She does the thing Tomas has been doing all volume — she puts a question in a room and gets an answer in her own name — **and she discovers that it is easier when the question is about paper and much harder when it is about metal, and that the difference is that a man can be ruined by a bell and cannot be ruined by a form.**

- **Ending type:** Decision.
- **Next-chapter pull:** A bell that may be bought and must be paid for, and a man with no money worth the name, and a woman with a bag and no bell in it that she will sell.

### Chapter 0068 — Whose Mouth
