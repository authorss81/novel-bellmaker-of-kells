## The written arrangement, and the relationship rule

**Neither Tomas nor Tamsin wrote it. Mercy Slee of 119 wrote it on a slate on the thirty-first evening and gave a copy to each of them separately, in different rooms, and neither saw the other read it.** It is four lines and it is on the page in Chapter 50. In summary: she travels with him on her own account as far as he goes and may stop at any hour without giving a reason; **her route and her bell remain hers and the four terms of the thirteenth day stand and are not varied by him, and no bell of his carries her and no bell of hers carries his;** neither of them uses the other's road without asking first and an answer may be no and the person who asked may not ask again that day — **which is a leash on both of them and Mercy Slee wrote the word *leash* on it herself**; and nothing in it is a promise and nothing in it can be enforced, and it is written down because a thing that is only in two people's mouths is a rumour.

**The Day 24 request is refused.** He asked, in writing, on the evening of the twenty-fourth, with Jerm as witness, whether she would carry a finding on foot to doors of her own choosing on the twenty-eighth. **She said no, gave no reason, and told him on the towpath on the morning of the thirty-third that he had not pressed her once.** He must not re-open it, and he must not treat the refusal as a wound.

**She has said out loud, in a lane, that a warning does not require her to obey him.** That is the standing arrangement between them now, and the road is where it is tested.

**No binding bell. No confession scene that resolves the relationship. No line in which she stops being the person who decides.** She may be the person who stops the cart, sets the pace, refuses the inn, reads the road, and says the thing that is wrong. He may not decide for her and he may not protect her from her own decisions.
