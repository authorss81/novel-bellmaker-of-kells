## The two house rules, repeated because they are the two this project breaks most

1. **No weekday and no month is named in any chapter.** Days are numbered: *the hundred and thirty-seventh*, *this morning*, *the day before yesterday*. A recurring day is **a market day, a round, a bell day, a three-day market, or by its number.** The four month names in five places are all in Volume 01 and no batch after Volume 01 has added one. **A weekday was drafted into Chapter 0150 in the last batch and removed before the file was saved, and it is recorded as a one and not as a clean sweep.**
2. **The hour numerals run 1 to 12 twice a day, and no half-day may be attached to an hour that a chapter does not attach one to.** If the prose says *the fourth hour*, then no state file, summary or day-book may say *the fourth hour of the afternoon*. **Write the half-day in the prose whenever you want one.** **Every day-time hour in Chapters 101 to 150 is bare; the ninth hour of the evening is the only half-day carried, it is carried in all fifty chapters, and it is the opening hour of every day-book.** Keep it that way. **The two exceptions in the whole volume are both closed retrospectives belonging to the days they name — the fifth hour of the morning of the thirty-third, and the second hour of the morning of Day 103 — and neither may become a live hour.**

**And the rule underneath both: a fix is not a fix because a summary says so.** Every number, date, clock time, custody state, physical condition, age and name in a state file must be traceable to a specific chapter and line. **If a chapter does not give an age, a count or an hour, the state file records that it is not on the page.** Run the sweep before you write and after, on the prose and on the state layer, and when you update the state layer search your own new text for the words you removed from the prose — that is the direction the error has now gone more than ten times. **Sweep every file you write for stray shell text before you save it.** A previous repair pass wrote the literal strings `EOF` and `head -5 state/open-threads.md` into the end of a state file, and a repair pass that writes shell text into a state file is the single most damaging thing this phase can do.

## The batch in one paragraph

**A man who cannot read a map walks nine miles of road with a barrow he cannot pay for and finds out that the map was never going to tell him where to walk.** Tomas Vale has spent a hundred and thirty-six days finding out that an institution is a format and not a villain, and he has walked out of a city of about nine thousand people with a warrant that stands behind him, eleven lines in his mother's handwriting in his coat, a physical route map with a rectangle about two hands together gone out of the middle of a run, and a woman beside him whose hearing is worse than it was ten days ago. **Nobody in this batch is wicked, and that is the engine and it has held for one hundred and fifty chapters, and it holds here — including the notice-reader who is right, including the smith who refuses the theory four seconds before Tomas can take one, including the woman with nine hundred names who will not say what her book is, and including the bench that will not carry a sentence he cannot certify.** **About four in five of the people he asks say no, in the ordinary voice, and he does not argue with one of them.** The batch ends with four sentences from four people who do not agree with each other, and a man at the end of nine miles who has never once been asked what it is called.

## The five things this batch must pay

1. **A road can be walked. That is the whole of the batch's promise and it is not to be inflated.** He walks nine miles and does not find the section that is missing; a second man does find it, because he was born at the end of it. **No road is found, restored, registered or explained. The rectangle is not explained. The panel above the coach inn is not restored. Nobody is entered anywhere as a household.**
2. **He stops treating the schedule as an instrument.** The mid-batch turn at Chapter 0155 is negative and it is the point: **a schedule is a drawing of a road and a road is a fact about the people who walk it, and a man who has been carrying a drawing and asking it for directions has been carrying a picture.** He can see wards and numbers and a scale. **He cannot see a run, he has never been able to, and there is no trade in it and this volume may not give him one.** The way forward is four other people's eyes, and that is the first time in three volumes another person has been the instrument.
3. **The travelling group is met, not recruited.** Eleven people asked, two yes, nine no. **A no is a no and he does not argue with one and he writes down the two yeses and none of the nine noes, because a man who keeps a list of refusals is a man waiting to be owed.** Both of the two must be able to walk away in a later chapter. The group has agreed about **one thing** and it is a bridge.
4. **A true sentence is the smallest thing anybody can carry and it is the only instrument he has.** A claim in metal cannot be edited and a sheet can be, and that difference is four inches long. **A thing said twice at ten feet moves about four in ten and dies with the people who heard it over about nine years; a thing written down can be produced against you by a stranger for forty years; a route is a person on foot and it costs a day.** Being believed by somebody in this kingdom is worth exactly nothing: there is no form on which belief is entered.
5. **Tamsin Rook is further reduced in hearing in this batch, and it is hers to price.** She says what it costs her, in her own words, on her own day. **Nobody asks her for a reason, nobody holds it over her, nobody treats it as a casualty of his decision, nobody rescues her out of it, and nobody treats her as a person who has been exposed.** She may ring her own stolen courier handbell **at most once in this whole volume, on her own terms, and never for him**, and nothing in the ten cards rings it.

## The form of the chapter, because this project has drifted and been pulled back

**No `##` section heading in any chapter. Not one. Volume 01 has none, Chapters 0101 to 0120 have none, Chapters 0131 to 0150 have none, and the twenty-nine that were in Chapters 0121 to 0130 were removed at the volume close as scaffolding, with no sentence of prose deleted with them.** A `---` rule between scenes is the house style and is welcome. A heading that states the finding, the price or the shape of the section it sits over is a beat sheet, and a chapter that reads as an outline with scenes attached to it is a defect.

**The day-book is a man's own book, in his own hand, and it must RE-DERIVE and not RE-PRINT.** §10c and §10g ruled on this twice and the closing batch carried it in nine chapters until the volume-close pass broke the runs: *the day-book was re-printing three narrator paragraphs word for word, which is a defect and not a virtue.* **A motif or a ritual sentence may be carried forward word for word — the *Which office is it* closing paragraph, the fifth clause's wording wherever the clause is named, the custody formula, the fourth clause of the three-person arrangement, the four sentences of a bell's claim, the arranging-is-the-damage sentence — and a repeat a later chapter must carry word for word stays.** Everything else in a day-book reaches the same finding by a different road.

**A man in a day-book counts things because he is a maker and cannot help it, and the round numbers are not one metronome for every man.** *About four seconds* belongs to the Office and to a plate. *About nine seconds* survives only as a man's own established tempo or as Tomas measuring himself. **Tamsin Rook's instrument is the only thing that still runs on a nine-second metronome, because it is hers and she built it that way, and that is characterisation and not a tic.**

**Nobody in this batch is wicked.** The notice-reader, the smith, the woman with the book and the bench are all right, and being right is not a door, and nobody is thanked.

**The prose is the thing.** Natural paragraphs, generally two to six sentences. A one-line paragraph only as a deliberate beat. Do not repeat *held*, *steady*, *plain*, *counted*, *breathed*, *wrote* and *nodded* as mechanical filler. Do not make every chapter the same shape: this batch is roughly two chapters of walking and trade, one of institutional pressure at a board, two of character and relationship work, one of a controlled experiment with the map, two of social pressure in a room, and two of cost.
