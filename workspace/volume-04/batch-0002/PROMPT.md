## The two house rules, repeated because they are the two this project breaks most

1. **No weekday and no month is named in any chapter.** Days are numbered: *the hundred and forty-seventh*, *this morning*, *the day before yesterday*. A recurring day is **a market day, a round, a bell day, a three-day market, or by its number.** The four month names in five places are all in Volume 01 and no batch after Volume 01 has added one. **Two weekdays were drafted and removed before the files were saved in the last two batches and are recorded in `state/batch-0016-summary.md` and `state/batch-0015-summary.md`.** Season words are permitted and are on the page: *the spring*, *a dry summer*, *in the winter*.
2. **The hour numerals run 1 to 12 twice a day, and no half-day may be attached to an hour that a chapter does not attach one to.** If the prose says *the fourth hour*, then no state file, summary or day-book may say *the fourth hour of the afternoon*. **Write the half-day in the prose whenever you want one.** **Every day-time hour in Chapters 101 to 160 is bare; the ninth hour of the evening is the only half-day carried, it is carried in all sixty chapters, and it is the opening hour of every day-book.** Keep it that way. **The two exceptions in the whole of Volume 03 are both closed retrospectives belonging to the days they name — the fifth hour of the morning of the thirty-third, and the second hour of the morning of Day 103 — and neither may become a live hour.** **Chapter 0156 carries one eighth hour and it is in a woman's own hand in her own book; that is the whole precedent and it is not a licence.**

## The batch in one paragraph

## The form of the chapter, because this project has drifted and been pulled back

**No `##` section heading in any chapter. Not one.** A heading that states the finding, the price or the shape of the section it sits over is a beat sheet, and a chapter that reads as an outline with scenes attached to it is a defect. A `---` rule between scenes is the house style and is welcome.

**The day-book is a man's own book, in his own hand, and it must RE-DERIVE and not RE-PRINT.** §10c and §10g ruled on this and the volume-close pass broke the runs. *A motif or a ritual sentence may be carried forward word for word — the *Which office is it* closing paragraph, the fifth clause's wording wherever the clause is named, the custody formula, the fourth clause of the three-person arrangement, the arranging-is-the-damage sentence, the four sentences of a bell's claim, and a true sentence said in a bench room and written in its own day-book because the rule it states IS the fourth clause and cannot be restated in other words without being a different rule. **Everything else in a day-book reaches the same finding by a different road.*** **The measured batch longest for Chapters 151 to 160 is thirty-nine words and that one is the standing fourth clause; nothing else in that batch is above twenty-four, and nine day-book re-derivations were re-written to get there. Measure yours.**

**A man in a day-book counts things because he is a maker and cannot help it, and the round numbers are not one metronome for every man.** *About four seconds* belongs to the Office and to a plate, and in the last batch to a smith refusing a theory, a hauler answering a question about a road, and a man born at the end of a road. *About nine seconds* survives only as a man's own established tempo or as Tomas measuring himself. **Tamsin Rook's instrument is the only thing that still runs on a nine-second metronome, because it is hers and she built it that way, and that is characterisation and not a tic.**

**Nobody in this batch is wicked. Being right is not a door, and nobody is thanked.**

**The prose is the thing.** Natural paragraphs, generally two to six sentences. A one-line paragraph only as a deliberate beat. Do not repeat *held*, *steady*, *plain*, *counted*, *breathed*, *wrote* and *nodded* as mechanical filler. Do not make every chapter the same shape.
