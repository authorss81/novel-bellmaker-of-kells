## THE SIX LESSONS OF §10r, AS REQUIRED METHOD AND NOT AS ENCOURAGEMENT

These are not observations. Each one is a defect that reached a finished file in this project, most of them more than once, and each is now something this phase must actively do.

1. **A sweep that returns zero tells you what it looked for and nothing else.** Volume 04 Batch 0005 published a prose-cleanliness claim that was false in five places, an orphan-mark sweep reported as returning zero that returned two, and an age claim that was false thirteen times — all three while every sweep in the same file reported clean. **Run the checks you did not think of, and read the finished prose by eye.** The cheapest audit instrument in this repository is a person reading a file, and it is the only one that has ever caught all four of Batch 0005's prose defects.
2. **Re-derive every published figure from the files. Do not carry a figure forward from a summary.** Batch 0005 published a per-file word list that did not sum to its own stated total, a day-book list that missed its stated total by 163 words, and an *about* family wrong in four of ten files — and then derived a rate from the wrong count and reported it as *the same rate as Batch 0004's* when it was not. **A total that lands on a previously published figure is not confirmation.** A total is confirmed by its per-file figures adding up, and that is the only test.
3. **A duplicate-prose test that counts only exact word runs is blind to a passage reprinted with its connectives altered.** Chapter 0197 carried a duplicated day-book column that **all three** of the batch's own duplicate tests passed, because the two copies differed by a handful of words and the longest exact shared run was twenty-three against a threshold of twenty. **A re-derived day-book is exactly where this happens, because the temptation is to reach for the sentence already written.** Run the near-match test as well as the exact one, over the day-book blocks specifically.
4. **The do-not-age list is a binding rule and a new person enters with no age.** Thirteen ages reached the page in Batch 0005 across two chapters — *a boy of thirteen* eight times — while the batch's own sweep reported that no age was given for anybody. **A man's years in a trade are a duration and are not an age.** Sweep every chapter for age constructions, not only the last ten.
5. **An hour list and a day-book count as a template, not as a shape.** §10o recorded five subtractions inside one skeleton and called that a template rather than five shapes. **Check that each chapter is one day**, and check that the events in a chapter belong to the day the chapter names.
6. **Never cite a figure to a page that does not carry it.** §10q found a figure attributed to a chapter's day-book that was this file's own subtraction and is on no page at all, and a figure of 231 that contradicted its own ten correct per-file counts. **If a number is a subtraction, say so and name the two chapters it came from. If it is on a page, cite the page.**
