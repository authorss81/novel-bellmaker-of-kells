## The form of the chapter, because this project has drifted and been pulled back

**No `##` section heading in any chapter. Not one.** A heading that states the finding, the price or the shape of the section it sits over is a beat sheet, and a chapter that reads as an outline with scenes attached to it is a defect. A `---` rule between scenes is the house style and is welcome.

**The day-book is a man's own book, in his own hand, and it must RE-DERIVE and not RE-PRINT.** §10c and §10g ruled on this and the volume-close pass broke the runs. *A motif or a ritual sentence may be carried forward word for word — the *Which office is it* closing paragraph, the fifth clause's wording wherever the clause is named, the custody formula, the fourth clause of the three-person arrangement, the arranging-is-the-damage sentence, the four sentences of a bell's claim, and a true sentence said in a bench room and written in its own day-book because the rule it states IS the fourth clause and cannot be restated in other words without being a different rule. **Everything else in a day-book reaches the same finding by a different road.** **The measured batch longest for Chapters 161 to 170 is twenty-six words, in Chapter 0168, and that one is the batch's own standing custody sentence carried word for word twice in the same file on purpose; nothing else in that batch is above twenty-four. Twenty-one re-derivations were made in that batch and the longest run found was seventy words, in Chapter 0170's day-book, which was a re-print of the three instruments. Measure yours.**

**A man in a day-book counts things because he is a maker and cannot help it, and the round numbers are not one metronome for every man.** *About four seconds* belongs to the Office and to a plate, and in the last two batches to a carter saying what a bypass is for, a surveyor looking at a sheet, a man saying yes before a question is finished, a smith refusing a theory, a hauler, and a man born at the end of a road — **and to nothing else.** *About nine seconds* survives only as a man's own established tempo or Tomas measuring himself. **Tamsin Rook's instrument is the only thing that still runs on a nine-second metronome, because it is hers and she built it that way, and that is characterisation and not a tic.**

**Nobody in this batch is wicked. Being right is not a door, and nobody is thanked. THE REVERSAL IS NOT A CONSPIRACY AND NOBODY IS A VILLAIN IN IT — the Crown's own reason is written down by a man who is right, and the reason is a form.**

**The prose is the thing.** Natural paragraphs, generally two to six sentences. A one-line paragraph only as a deliberate beat. Do not repeat *held*, *steady*, *plain*, *counted*, *breathed*, *wrote* and *nodded* as mechanical filler. Do not make every chapter the same shape. **The three volumes have been drifting toward one shape — a fourth hour, a fifth, a sixth, a seventh, and a day-book at the ninth hour of the evening — and Batch 0002 broke it once, in Chapter 0165, by having no seventh hour. Break it more than once here, and break it in the prose and not in the day-book.**
