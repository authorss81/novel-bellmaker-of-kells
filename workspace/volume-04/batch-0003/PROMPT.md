## The form of the chapter, because this project has drifted and been pulled back

**No `##` section heading in any chapter. Not one.** A heading that states the finding, the price or the shape of the section it sits over is a beat sheet, and a chapter that reads as an outline with scenes attached to it is a defect. A `---` rule between scenes is the house style and is welcome.

**The day-book is a man's own book, in his own hand, and it must RE-DERIVE and not RE-PRINT.** §10c and §10g ruled on this and the volume-close pass broke the runs. *A motif or a ritual sentence may be carried forward word for word — the *Which office is it* closing paragraph, the fifth clause's wording wherever the clause is named, the custody formula, the fourth clause of the three-person arrangement, the arranging-is-the-damage sentence, the four sentences of a bell's claim, and a true sentence said in a bench room and written in its own day-book because the rule it states IS the fourth clause and cannot be restated in other words without being a different rule. **THE NINETY-THREE-WORD CUSTODY CORE IS ONE OF THESE AND IT IS MEASURED AND NAMED: *All five clauses stand. …and the warrant stands.* is byte-identical in all ten chapters of Batch 0002, and the sentence-bounded block around it runs to one hundred and twenty-two words.** Everything else in a day-book reaches the same finding by a different road.*

**MEASURE YOURS ON THREE TESTS AND NOT ONE, because the single test this project ran for four volumes is structurally blind to two whole classes of defect and §10n finding 1 is the proof.** **Test one is the §10c test: the longest common word-run between any two paragraphs in the same file. Test two is the line-level near-duplicate scan: the longest common word-run between any two lines of the same file carrying twenty-five words or more, which is the only test that can see a blockquote entry that has been printed twice on adjacent lines inside itself — that is how Chapter 0168 stood at a hundred and ten duplicated words through a whole batch and through its own certified sweep, because a blockquote entry is one paragraph to test one and two lines to test two. Test three is the cross-file run over every pair of your ten chapters, which is the only test that can see a sentence the batch carries on purpose in all ten.** **The three figures for Batch 0002 are twenty-six words intra-file, twenty-six line-level, and one hundred and twenty-two cross-file, and the first two are its own standing sentences and the third is its own custody formula.** **THE RULE THE THRESHOLD GOT WRONG, AND IT IS THE RULE THAT BUILT THE REPAIR LIST: a run goes on your repair list if it is at or over the standing figure OR if it re-prints the beat the chapter is built on, AT ANY LENGTH. Chapter 0170's twenty-five-word re-print of the protagonist's one declaration to four people sat one word under the line and went unrepaired for that reason and not because it was good. A central beat is not measured by its length, and a file that reports one number has not reported the file** — a second run that ties a file's own maximum is on no list, and Chapter 0168 had two of them at twenty-six.

**THE THIRD COLUMN IS A RULING ON THE DAY AND NOT A PARAGRAPH OF THE PROSE AGAIN, AND THIS IS THE ONE THAT THE SENTENCE-LEVEL RULE COULD NOT CATCH.** Every chapter of Batch 0002 stated its finding three times: in the prose, again in the third column of its own day-book, and again in the closing paragraph of that same day-book. **The sentence-level re-derivation rule was satisfied — zero verbatim sentence re-prints across three hundred and twenty-one day-book sentences — and the repetition happened at the level of the chapter, which is the level the rule does not measure.** **State a thing the number of times the scene needs it and not once more.** And the size: **Batch 0002's day-book is twelve thousand five hundred and ninety-nine words of twenty-six thousand nine hundred and forty-eight, which is forty-seven per cent of the batch, and seventy-one per cent of the batch's words sit inside bold markers. Both figures are in the record at §10n finding 3. BRING BOTH DOWN AND STATE THE FIGURES YOU HIT IN YOUR OWN SUMMARY — this is not an instruction to write less, it is an instruction to notice what you are doing, because a day-book that has become the book rather than the man's account of the day is the failure this batch's predecessor walked into.**

**A man in a day-book counts things because he is a maker and cannot help it, and the round numbers are not one metronome for every man.** *About four seconds* belongs to the Office and to a plate, and in the last two batches to a carter saying what a bypass is for, a surveyor looking at a sheet, a man saying yes before a question is finished, a smith refusing a theory, a hauler, and a man born at the end of a road — **and to nothing else.** *About nine seconds* survives only as a man's own established tempo or Tomas measuring himself. **Tamsin Rook's instrument is the only thing that still runs on a nine-second metronome, because it is hers and she built it that way, and that is characterisation and not a tic.** **AND MEASURE THE FAMILY AND NOT ONE MEMBER OF IT, WHICH IS THE CORRECTION §10n FINDING 5 MAKES: *about nine* is the figure four volumes have been tracking and it is one member of a tic. Across Batch 0002 the family was *about the* 71, *about a* 52, *about nine* 40, *about forty* 18, *about four* 18, *about it* 18, *about two* 15, *about sixty* 5 — three hundred and six instances of the word before anything in the ten chapters. A fall in one member of a family is not a fall in the tic; it is a change of which member you happened to be counting, and *about forty* and *about two* are the same tic wearing different numbers and went unswept for four volumes. Report the family. A pass that quotes *about nine* on its own is quoting half a measurement.**

**EVERY CHARACTER SPEAKS IN HIS OWN TRADE'S GRAMMAR AND NOT IN THE NARRATOR'S, AND THAT IS BINDING ON ALL TEN CHAPTERS.** Batch 0002's defect, named at §10n finding 6, is that a carter, a girl counting fences, a woman with a shovel and a district's road surveyor all speak in the narrator's aphoristic sub-clause-heavy register, **and the effect is the one AGENTS.md's *show information through character action, conversation, work and consequences* exists to prevent: people explaining the theme to each other instead of acting.** So: **a carter counts loads and gives a distance; a woman with a shovel names a surface and a lip you could catch a heel on; a clerk names a form, a box and a procedure and nothing else; a man with a level names a number and says he cannot account for the other one; a girl with a number she has not finished says the number is not finished.** **A second mouth is not a way of stating the theme. If a character can be removed and the theme still lands, the character is a mouthpiece and is cut or given something of their own to want that is not the theme.** **Tomas is the only character permitted the long sub-clause, and even he earns it.**

**AND TAMSIN ROOK IS ON THE PAGE, IN HER OWN PRESENCE, AT LEAST ONCE IN THESE TEN CHAPTERS — which is not a change of her state and lifts nothing.** **She has no dialogue in Batch 0002's ten chapters and that is a consequence of a rule rather than a defect: the fourth clause of the arrangement bars him from asking her for a reason at any hour, including him, and that bar is not lifted here to give her a line.** **But a man and a woman standing nine feet apart for ten days is a scene, and the batch wrote her one entry in her own hand and no scene, and a chapter in which she is a clause inside a custody recital is not her being in the chapter.** **She may be on the page in her own presence — in the road, at the fire, at the wall, doing the thing she does with a wall — without being asked anything, without being reduced, without being rescued, without a price and without a reason, and the whole of the standing prohibition on her is untouched by this.** **Her own hand in her own book is the easiest form and it has precedent in Chapters 0156 and 0169. Do not make it a book entry ten times over.**

**Nobody in this batch is wicked. Being right is not a door, and nobody is thanked. THE REVERSAL IS NOT A CONSPIRACY AND NOBODY IS A VILLAIN IN IT — the Crown's own reason is written down by a man who is right, and the reason is a form.**

**The prose is the thing.** Natural paragraphs, generally two to six sentences. A one-line paragraph only as a deliberate beat. Do not repeat *held*, *steady*, *plain*, *counted*, *breathed*, *wrote* and *nodded* as mechanical filler. Do not make every chapter the same shape. **The three volumes have been drifting toward one shape — a fourth hour, a fifth, a sixth, a seventh, and a day-book at the ninth hour of the evening — and Batch 0002 broke it once, in Chapter 0165, by having no seventh hour. Break it more than once here, and break it in the prose and not in the day-book.**

**A TITLE IS A NAME AND NOT A BEAT SHEET, ABOUT TWENTY WORDS, AND IT MAY NOT STATE THE CHAPTER'S FINDING.** Batch 0002's ten titles ran from fifteen to thirty-three words and Chapter 0167's ended in a clause about what a man *may not put in a column*, which is the chapter's conclusion and not its name, **and ten titles that long read as labels somebody stuck on a scene and the reader can tell.** **A title names the thing, the place or the person. It does not tell the reader what the chapter concludes, and it does not contain the word *and* three times.** **The ten titles of Batch 0002 are not changed and are not to be changed: they are cited ten to twenty-eight times each across four state files and they are the record of that batch. This rule is forward and it starts with yours.**
