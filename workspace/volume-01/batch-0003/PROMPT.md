### Chapter 0021 — The First Hour of Light

- **POV/location/time:** Tomas; the market court, **Day 10**, the first hour of light, one hour before the rescheduled dawn.
- **Goal:** Keep the answer columns, the withdrawal column, the withdrawal procedure, and the rope account in the room, and stop the Crown from converting a divided warning into a single municipal answer before the town has seen the dawn.

- **Turn/reveal:** Oren concedes the Crown's claim to the **trial handbell and the remaining working bells** on the custody order, because he does not have the votes and will not put his own men in the street, but he refuses to allow the merged page to be read as the ward's answer, and he gets the columns read aloud one at a time over the merged page in open court.

### Chapter 0022 — What the Dawn Inspection Found

- **POV/location/time:** Oren; the north gate, the west road, and the Low Ward landing, **Day 10**, dawn into morning.
- **Goal:** Certify the inspection that was lawfully ordered, keep the watch from becoming an instrument of a record nobody consented to, and find out what the Crown intends to do when it arrives.

- **Turn/reveal:** The Crown produces **no signed per-household authority** and instead relies on the L-4 ward response schedule. The inspection therefore proceeds as a renewal visit against the ward's own recorded answers, which means the people who marked *not returned* are not counted as present and their lines are eligible for whatever the order permits. Some children are taken. Some households are marked. A removal proceeds against a family whose recorded answer says they agreed.
- **Decision/consequence:** Oren records each removal separately, by household, with the time and the household's own answer, so the record shows a removal made against a recorded consent — and he accepts that he could not stop it, because a man who puts his men between children and a lawful order is a man who ends the ward's only protection. - **Power/resource:** No new power. Oren trades a lawful order for a defensible record, and the town sees a real cost.
- **Ending pull:** Surveyor Collis asks, in front of witnesses, to see the answer schedule her inspection was conducted under, and Oren has to decide whether to hand her a Crown page that belongs to her own commission.

### Chapter 0025 — The Order Was Entered

- **POV/location/time:** Tamsin; the market court, the watch archive, and the survey store's emptied shelves, **Day 11**, midday into evening.
- **Goal:** Turn an office number into a thing a person can be shown, using the route she actually walks and the record she actually has.

### Chapter 0027 — The Surveyor Who Will Not Sign

- **Goal:** Get an accredited Crown official to state on the record what the schedule is, without asking her to break her commission or to lie.

### Chapter 0028 — Publishing a Practice

- **POV/location/time:** Tomas with Oren, Tamsin, Nell, and local witnesses; the market court and the ward board, **Day 13**, full day.
- **Goal:** Publish the finding as a practice that can be checked, without claiming who does it, without a bell, and without a crowd that decides what it means.
- **Resistance:** Publishing a discovery with no name attached is the easiest kind of writing to get wrong; a Crown officer objects on the grounds that a public accusation of falsified answers endangers the ward; the ward's copy of the schedule is unofficial; some families want the finding suppressed because it proves they were written down; the Unrung want the whole thing torn out of the ground.
- **Turn/reveal:** The town publishes a **checkable procedure** instead of a conclusion: three ordinary questions any household can put to a Crown clerk about its own line, plus the page's own facts, plus a standing invitation to correct the record in public. No bell is rung. Tamsin's reach problem is solved by paper and by people on foot, and one household uses the three questions successfully against a clerk, which proves the procedure works and does not prove the ward is safe.
- **Decision/consequence:** Tomas refuses to speak first at his own publication, because a claim voiced by its maker is the thing he has spent the volume learning not to do. Oren's watch and the ward board become co-holders of the page. The Crown's response is procedural and immediate: a notice of further questions on route L-4.
- **Power/resource:** No new power. The reversal is public and usable, the town has a practice, and Tomas has lost the authority to interpret it.
- **Ending pull:** The Crown's notice reopens the question of the **market court bell** and of any route Tomas might build, and Tamsin says the notice is not really about bells.

### Chapter 0029 — The Route She Will Not Give

- **POV/location/time:** Tamsin; the towpath, the north gate, and the road above the landing, **Day 13**, evening.
- **Goal:** Decide what she will and will not carry now that a Crown paper has been shown to do what her bell was altered to do.
- **Resistance:** Her hearing is worse; her bell's over-reach is now a known technique, and every village above the landing has heard a Low Ward signal it did not choose; the Crown has asked questions about the route; Tomas is tempted to treat her refusal as a problem to solve; her own rule says the route ends where the route ends unless a person asks.
- **Turn/reveal:** She walks her own boundary and counts it, and finds she cannot tell, from inside the reach, where her rule ends and the alteration begins — which means the bell cannot be trusted to carry even a keeper's boundary. She therefore reduces what she offers: paper on foot, a named keeper beside her, no repeat without a person asking, and the handbell used only as a signal to come to a door, never as the warning itself.
- **Decision/consequence:** She tells Tomas she will answer a request and refuse a request and that he will not be able to tell which in advance. Tomas accepts it and writes down that he has no standing to know. The cost is concrete: she loses the paid work that route used to bring, in a week when the workshop has no income.
- **Power/resource:** No resonance power. Tamsin keeps the handbell, sets a real boundary on it, and gains authority over her route that nobody, including Tomas, can override.
- **Ending pull:** A rider comes up the towpath saying two families from the low road have asked the Crown for a route of their own.
