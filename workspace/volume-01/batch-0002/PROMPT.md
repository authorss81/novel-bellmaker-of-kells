## Required power and consent boundaries

Tomas is at the first edge of **Stage 1 (Near Toll)**, not a higher stage. He can sense only whether a nearby answer is strained. He cannot identify a source, emotion, context, criminal, copied answer, or future event; he cannot read the Crown mechanism or predict a tone. His first binding already cost him a failed small casting remelted into the trial handbell, a split tuning file, and one episode of ringing ears. Do not give him permanent memory loss, deep contextual reading, effortless route control, or a new supernatural shortcut. Any additional experiment must show preparation, uncertainty, material or civic cost, and a limit.

A public local signal normally requires **three independent answers**, each from someone who understands the claim and can refuse or withdraw. A private handbell may have one answer. A disagreement among the three answers must remain visible; Tomas cannot select the answer he likes and call it consensus. A warning can name known facts, uncertainty, and the keeper's duty, but cannot tell residents what decision to make. Do not use a forced echo, copied answer, or Crown clapper as a diagnosis yet.

## Relationship and tone requirements

Keep Tomas competent in ordinary bellcraft and route inspection while showing his flaw: he wants to carry every decision so no one else can make the wrong one. His growth is measured by asking, recording refusal, and accepting that a useful warning can still damage relationships and work. Tamsin remains skeptical, injured, practical, and autonomous; their slow-burn relationship advances through work and disagreement, not instant attraction or a binding bell. Oren's grief over Ewan remains active, but he must judge Tomas through evidence rather than permanent revenge. Nell's choices must not be treated as parental permission. Sabin can teach rules but cannot protect Tomas from consequences. Halden is competent and frightened by civic disorder, not a cartoon villain; he may escalate without becoming the final enemy.

Use concrete sensory work and ordinary civic consequences. Do not explain the whole Hearthline history, the Quiet Office's final design, or the series mystery in lectures. Keep System-like presentation rare; no stat panels, game language, or modern technical terms. Avoid turning every chapter into a fight or a notification.

## Batch movement and chapter cards

### Chapter 0011 — The First Refusal

- **POV/location/time:** Tomas; Vale Bellhouse workroom and market court, **Day 3**, the first full day of the seven-day stay.
- **Goal:** Design a lawful public test of the warning without using the bound trial handbell as a town command.
- **Resistance:** The no-public-ringing order covers the trial and loose relay bells; Halden's officers monitor the workshop; Tomas wants a single decisive test; Tamsin warns that a public tone will be treated as broader than its chain.
- **Turn/reveal:** Oren and Tomas separate the test into physical witness, keeper duty, and three independent answer slips. A plain workbell or other lawfully available carrier is considered, but no existing named bell is quietly repurposed.
- **Decision/consequence:** Tomas drafts a public claim that names the posted inspection, the known uncertainty, and each household's freedom to decide. One invited witness refuses to participate before the test begins. Tomas records the refusal instead of treating it as sabotage.
- **Power/resource:** No new power. Tomas gains a public test frame and a documented refusal.
- **Ending pull:** The remaining witnesses insist that the test must include a real flood or bridge question, not only the Crown order.

### Chapter 0012 — A Promise Before the Rain

- **POV/location/time:** Tamsin; river landing, towpath, and a low flood gate, **Day 4**, morning, after the Day 3 test design.
- **Goal:** Test whether an ordinary promise can travel farther than its authorized route without acquiring commands that no one accepted.
- **Resistance:** Rain swells the Kells Water; the local flood gate has a history of false alarms; Tamsin's hearing makes repeated tones painful; adults want her to use the recovered handbell to settle the dispute.
- **Turn/reveal:** Tamsin and Tomas compare the handbell's altered wear with an ordinary flood keeper's written duty. A bridge keeper accepts a specific promise to inspect and report before evening, not a promise that the bridge is safe.
- **Decision/consequence:** The keeper answers only after residents are told what the claim can and cannot establish. The route carries a real warning and a refusal; Tamsin chooses to limit the handbell's reach and accepts that delay may cost trust.
- **Power/resource:** No resonance gain for Tomas. Tamsin retains her handbell and gains a documented route boundary; the flood gate remains a physical maintenance problem.
- **Ending pull:** Water rises faster than the ordinary keeper can inspect, forcing the town to decide whether an imperfect warning is still worth hearing.

### Chapter 0013 — The Low Ward Does Not Answer

- **POV/location/time:** Nell; Low Ward rooms, public board, and the edge of the west road, **Day 4**, midday into afternoon, in sequence after Tamsin's morning.
- **Goal:** Gather three informed answers from people who may have to bear the consequences of a warning without asking them to speak for one another.
- **Resistance:** Families disagree about Jory, Edan, the inspection, and whether any bell is safe; Nell's mother will not let her answer for the household; Halden's officers ask who authorized the meeting.
- **Turn/reveal:** One adult accepts a duty to carry the warning to the river landing, one asks for time to locate Jory's record, and one refuses the route entirely. The three responses are independent and cannot be merged into a single public answer.
- **Decision/consequence:** Nell keeps the household fragment with her and publishes only the fact that three different choices were made. Tomas does not call the refusal cowardice or ask Nell to change it.
- **Power/resource:** No new power. Nell gains a clearer understanding of witness and refusal; Tomas gains a practical model for disagreement.
- **Ending pull:** The Bellwrights' Measure sends notice that Tomas's private binding and route work violate craft safety unless he submits the trial handbell for guild inspection.

### Chapter 0014 — The Bellwright's Condition

- **POV/location/time:** Tomas; Sabin's shop and the Bellwrights' Measure's temporary inspection room, **Day 5**, late afternoon.
- **Goal:** Defend the trial handbell and the public test without surrendering the workshop's independent status.
- **Resistance:** Iven Sore, the living master of the Bellwrights' Measure, argues that unsafe resonance and a Crown route require one licensed master bell; the guild offers technical expertise only if Tomas accepts a central key; Sabin refuses to speak for him. Tomas also wants to put the docket directly to Sore: the withdrawn service ticket carries Sore's own name, and Tomas needs to know whether a clerk borrowed it or Sore stood on the west road himself. Sore must not give a straight answer that settles whether the name is genuine or borrowed.
- **Turn/reveal:** Iven distinguishes craft safety from consent and reveals that the Measure's old route maps preserve a record of local withdrawal, but the map cannot tell Tomas who altered L-4. Do not reveal the full Hearthline history or Iven's later political role.
- **Decision/consequence:** Tomas accepts a neutral guild inspection of the trial handbell's material and tool damage but refuses a master key or a license that would make the Bellhouse responsible for every route answer. The guild issues a conditional challenge rather than a ban.
- **Power/resource:** No resonance gain. Tomas gains a technical inspection record and a new institutional pressure; the trial handbell remains bound and at the Bellhouse.
- **Ending pull:** Halden uses the Measure's challenge to argue for immediate custody of all loose relay bells.

### Chapter 0015 — The Sealed Workshop

- **POV/location/time:** Oren; Vale Bellhouse yard and north-gate approach, **Day 6**, morning.
- **Goal:** Enforce the temporary holding order without allowing the Crown to turn a disputed warning into a blanket confiscation.
- **Resistance:** Halden orders inventory and seizure of loose relay bells; customers fear the Bellhouse; Tomas wants to hide the trial bell; Tamsin warns that secret removal will make the route look manufactured.
- **Turn/reveal:** Oren separates the named fixed carrier, the bound trial handbell, ordinary working bells, and Tamsin's recovered handbell. The Crown may inventory or seal some loose bells, but cannot treat the private answer as a town-wide permission.
- **Decision/consequence:** Oren records a partial inventory and keeps the workshop under a stricter no-ringing condition. Tomas loses access to paid repair stock and accepts the public humiliation of a seal on his own door; at least one customer leaves.
- **Power/resource:** No new power. The fixed carrier remains in watch custody; the trial handbell remains at the Bellhouse; other bells' legal status becomes more fragile.
- **Ending pull:** Oren orders Tomas to explain the Hush rope cut in the market court before the next public test.

### Chapter 0016 — The Rope in the Market Court

- **POV/location/time:** Oren; market court, Bellhouse yard, and the old north-gate route, **Day 6**, late the same day.
- **Goal:** Hear Tomas's account publicly and decide whether the warning can be tested without excusing the old failure.
- **Resistance:** The crowd wants a confession, a conviction, or a promise that no one will die again; Tomas wants to use the present warning as proof that he has learned; Oren refuses to let grief become either absolution or permanent punishment.
- **Turn/reveal:** Tomas states that he cut the rope to save a child and that the cut ended a warning before it reached Ewan and others. He cannot prove what the old tone would have done if carried farther. The public learns a fact, not a supernatural explanation.
- **Decision/consequence:** Oren records the account, the witnesses correct details, and Tomas accepts a condition: any public test must name his limits and allow a keeper or household to withdraw. The crowd remains unconvinced, but the evidence survives.
- **Power/resource:** No resonance gain. Oren supplies a public witness record and Tomas loses the fantasy that secrecy can protect the town.
- **Ending pull:** The rain rises over the low road, making the public refusal test urgent but not safe.

### Chapter 0017 — What the Water Owes

- **POV/location/time:** Tomas; west road, flood gate, and Low Ward landing, **Day 7**, the day the water crests.
- **Goal:** Issue one bounded warning about a real, visible danger while leaving the decision to each affected person.
- **Resistance:** The gate may be sound or trapped; a false alarm could close the market and starve families; a true alarm could cause people to cross an unsafe road; Tomas's ringing ears return when the route is crowded.
- **Turn/reveal:** A physical inspection confirms water is rising against a low section. Tomas's claim names the inspection, the visible water, and what he can and cannot promise. One local keeper accepts a duty to check the west road; another refuses to forward the signal until the gate is inspected.
- **Decision/consequence:** Tomas does not force the refusal. The plain warning reaches only the people who chose to receive it; some move goods, some wait, and some argue that the Crown caused the danger by blocking the road.
- **Power/resource:** Tomas uses ordinary diagnosis and a bounded warning without gaining a deeper read. The cost is a temporary loss of balance and renewed ringing ears.
- **Ending pull:** Three independent answers are finally available, but they do not agree; the town must decide whether a divided warning is still a warning.

### Chapter 0018 — Three Answers, One Danger

- **POV/location/time:** Tomas with Oren, Tamsin, Nell, and local witnesses; market court and a neutral public bell site, **Day 8**, with the water falling.
- **Goal:** Test a three-answer public signal that preserves the known danger and the disagreement about action.
- **Resistance:** Halden threatens to stop the ringing; one witness withdraws; Tomas wants to speak for the missing fourth voice; the crowd mistakes any repeated tone for a Crown command.
- **Turn/reveal:** Three independent people answer the same bounded claim: one accepts the duty to warn neighbors, one asks for time and a bridge check, one refuses the route but agrees to record the refusal. A plain, lawfully authorized bell carries the three responses without adding obedience.
- **Decision/consequence:** Tomas reads the answers as separate records, not a vote. The signal reaches the Low Ward unevenly, preserves the refusal, and prevents the town from treating silence as consent. Some people call the test useless because it does not produce one clear action.
- **Power/resource:** Tomas remains at the edge of Stage 1. The successful public test is a social and civic achievement, not a new supernatural power; his physical and relational costs continue.
- **Ending pull:** A Crown clerk arrives with an order that treats the three-answer record as a single local response and demands the Bellhouse surrender its test slips.

### Chapter 0019 — The Cost of a Public Tone

- **POV/location/time:** Tamsin; market lane, watch house, and the river route, **Day 9**, the morning after the Day 8 test.
- **Goal:** Preserve the three separate answers and decide how much further her recovered handbell may carry them.
- **Resistance:** The altered handbell repeats beyond its authorized route; Tamsin's hearing aches after the public tones; the watch wants the slips as one packet; Tomas wants her to keep the route moving.
- **Turn/reveal:** Tamsin establishes that the handbell can repeat the known signal only as far as its worn route permits. She refuses to let the bell add a fourth answer or turn a refusal into silence. The separated slips are copied into ordinary public records.
- **Decision/consequence:** Tamsin chooses a slower physical route for the next warning and accepts that a village may receive it late. Tomas must ask for her help rather than assign it; they establish a practical boundary, not a romantic vow.
- **Power/resource:** No new resonance power. Tamsin retains the handbell and gains authority over its route boundaries; Tomas gains a partner who can refuse him.
- **Ending pull:** Halden announces that the public test has made the Bellhouse more dangerous, not less, and schedules a broader custody hearing.

### Chapter 0020 — The Hearing Before the Hearing

- **POV/location/time:** Tomas; market court, Vale Bellhouse, and the north-gate watch store, **Day 9**, the evening before the Day 10 rescheduled dawn.
- **Goal:** Keep the three-answer record public and prevent the Crown from converting a divided warning into a single municipal answer before the formal hearing.
- **Resistance:** Halden invokes emergency custody, the Crown commission claims the test bell is a connected route, Oren's watch is divided, customers fear another seal, and Tomas is tempted to make a direct promise to save the town.
- **Turn/reveal:** The public record shows that the L-4 notice preceded the carrier, that the route was physically altered, and that the three answers were separate. It still does not identify who copied an answer or why the official system wants one response. Halden's broader seizure is not yet executed; the formal hearing remains the next legal pressure.
- **Decision/consequence:** Tomas refuses to issue a town-wide command, Oren records the three answers separately, Tamsin limits her handbell's route, and Nell keeps the household fragment. The Bellhouse remains open under temporary restrictions, but its ordinary work and paid contracts continue to suffer.
- **Power/resource:** No copied-answer reading, no deep resonance, no new stage. Tomas keeps the route fragment and a practical public record; the fixed carrier remains in neutral custody; the rescheduled dawn is not saved or canceled.
- **Ending pull:** Tomas and Tamsin find a survey-office docket showing that a clerk's response was entered for a whole Low Ward household, setting up Chapters 21–30's formal reversal without revealing the mechanism or higher official too soon.
