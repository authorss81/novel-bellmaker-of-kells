### Chapter 0031 — The Lane at the Bottom of the Ward

- **POV/location/time:** Tomas; the low lane below the Low Ward, the landing stage, and the washhouse, **Day 15**, morning.
- **Goal:** Keep a Crown road gang and a rope-maker with a bar from each other over a length of ground Tomas is the only person in town who understands, without becoming the man who decides what happens in the lane.
- **Resistance:** the Crown gang has a works order and a foreman who signed for the cart at the culvert; **Gib Hask has six men and a bar and has asked a court to tell him to his face and has not been answered**; the low lane is the road the ward's children walk to the landing; Oren has no power to stop either party and has said so in the book; two of the gang's men are Crown officers' sons and one of them is nineteen.
- **Turn/reveal:** Tomas can tell, from the ground, that the Crown gang is not renewing the line at the point they have marked out, and that the marked point is where the old service cut runs under the ward's own lane. **A Crown crew digging for a bell line is standing on the road the children use.** He says so out loud, in the lane, to the gang, to Hask and to whoever is standing there, and the gang's foreman hears it, and the foreman does not argue.

He gets the marked line moved two yards, in the open, with the foreman's own hand on the change, and he enters what he said and why. The Crown's foreman tells him the marked point came off a plan and he will find out who drew it. **Hask's men are still at the low end with the bar when the work finishes.**
- **Power/resource:** No new power. Tomas uses ordinary ground knowledge — line, peg, chalk, the depth at which service cuts run — and gains nothing supernatural and one uncomfortable piece of attention.
- **Ending pull:** a Crown clerk is on the lane within the hour asking who told the foreman to move the mark, and Tomas understands that being right in a lane is now a thing that gets written down.

### Chapter 0037 — A Plain Bell

- **POV/location/time:** Tomas with Sabin Fell; the Bellhouse yard, the Measure's temporary room, and the millrace, **Day 19**, morning into afternoon.
- **Goal:** Build one carrier that can carry a bounded claim across more than a room, from materials that are legally in his hands, and take the three answers that Oren's frame requires.
- **Resistance:** the casting room is empty of licensed stock; the millrace bell is under a no-ringing order he cannot lift himself; **the wheelwright's counter bell is a customer's bell and using it for a warning without asking is exactly the thing this volume has been about**; Sabin will teach rules and will not supply a design, and the Measure may treat anything cast under this licence as a hazard; the Crown's bell register means a new bell is a documented object; and **Tamsin will not carry a claim**, so the route question is not available to him.
- **Turn/reveal:** the answer is not a better bell. It is that a carrier **may not add anything**, and the most honest carrier Tomas owns is a bell that has no claim in it at all — so the work is not in the metal and is in the four parts of the frame, and the three answers are the hard part, not the object. Sabin gives him one rule he has never given him, and it is a rule about **choosing keepers who will refuse**, and choosing them costs Tomas something personal.
- **Decision/consequence:** Tomas casts or assembles a plain carrier of ordinary stock, records its condition in the ordinary way a maker records a condition, and goes to ask three people, one at a time, with the same words, and **two of the three refuse**, and the count is a count of two, and he does not go looking for a fourth to make it look like three. He records the refusal as a refusal.
- **Power/resource:** No new power, and a material cost. Any new experiment must show preparation, uncertainty, and a limit, and **the limit is that the carrier reaches a lane and not a ward.**
- **Ending pull:** the plain carrier works, on two keepers, across a distance nobody in this town expected, and someone on the far end of it heard it and did not know who had sent it.

### Chapter 0038 — Further Than the Rule

- **POV/location/time:** Tomas; the west road, the low ward, and the landing, **Day 19**, evening.
- **Goal:** Extend the bounded warning far enough to be worth having, and find out what that costs.
- **Resistance:** the carrier may not add anything and distance is a property of metal and route, not of consent; **two keepers are two answers and a public signal needs three**; a warning extended further is a warning heard by more people than agreed to hear it; the Crown's bell register is running and every hour of a sound is written down; and a woman on the ferry road whose box Tomas helped pack will ask him a question about a box.
- **Turn/reveal:** **the extension saves a few people and causes a panic and a costly false alarm, and all three happen in the same hour.** The claim Tomas wrote is true; the part of it that named a cause is wrong; the far end of the route hears a true sentence and fills in the rest themselves, and a market lane empties, and a cart is lost, and somebody is hurt who would not have been hurt. **He does not get to call it a false alarm, because the alarm was true and the panic was his fault.**
- **Decision/consequence:** Tomas stops the extension himself, in the open, without waiting for permission and without asking anybody to make the decision for him, and he records what the extension cost in the day-book in figures and in words, including the injury, and he does not offer an explanation for four days. ### Chapter 0039 — The Form, Exhibited

- **POV/location/time:** Nell with Stroud, Mercy Slee and Oren; the public Crown counter, the ward board, and the market court, **Day 20**, full day.
- **Goal:** Get the Crown to show the form in a public room and get a household to ask about the *Witnessed by* column in front of witnesses.

- **Turn/reveal:** the form is exhibited, and **the column is there, and it is printed, and it is headed exactly as Stroud said.** A household asks about it in public and gets an answer that is longer than a clerk's answer should be: the Crown does not deny the column, does not explain it, and **offers to fill it**, which is the answer nobody in the ward expected and which is worse than a refusal, because an office that offers to enter a witness is offering to choose the witness.
- **Decision/consequence:** the ward refuses the offer in public, in three columns, and the refusal is itself a public act with a date on it. **A Crown officer then offers to have a Crown surveyor certify a household's answer instead**, which would solve the ward's problem and hand the Crown the witnesses, and a household has to decide whether to take it.
- **Power/resource:** No new power. The ward gains the strongest published fact in the volume and pays for it in visibility, and one household is now being courted by the Crown for a reason it has not told its neighbours.
- **Ending pull:** the night before the round, two Crown officers are in the ward asking households, politely, whether they intend to attend in person — and every household that says yes is a household that has been written down.

### Chapter 0040 — The Twenty-First Day

- **POV/location/time:** Tomas, with the ward; the ward board, the low ward, and the market court, **Day 21**, morning into evening.

- **Turn/reveal:** the round happens, with the form exhibited, and **it goes badly for the Crown in the way a thing goes badly when a town has a procedure and a clerk has a conscience** — and it goes badly for the ward in the way a thing goes badly when the Crown can simply come back.
