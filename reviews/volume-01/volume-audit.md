# Volume 01 Audit — *The Bellmaker of Kells*, Chapters 1–50

**Phase:** volume close and audit. **Audited:** `outline/volume-01.md` against the five batch summaries, the six state files, `reviews/volume-01/batch-0001.md`, `batch-0004.md` and `batch-0005.md`, and **Chapters 41–50 read in full**. Chapters outside 41–50 were opened only where a finding pointed at a line, and every such opening is listed in the second table with its reason.

**Method.** The audit worked from the summaries and the state layer first and opened prose second, so that a claim in a state file was never treated as evidence about a chapter. Every number, date, clock time, custody state, physical condition, age and name below is cited to a chapter and a line. **Where a chapter is ambiguous, the chapter's version is recorded and the ambiguity is declared rather than resolved.**

**Scope discipline.** This phase did not rewrite the volume. Twenty-six lines of prose were changed in sixteen chapters, every one of them a short edit: fourteen lines carrying weekday names in nine chapters, eight ages the narrator had no business supplying, one leaked name, and three lines that gave the volume's climactic ringing an hour the rest of the chapter contradicts. **Where the volume and the outline disagree, the finding is here and the outline is corrected in `outline/volume-01.md`.** No chapter was restarted, renumbered or reordered, and no planned plot was changed.

**Result: the audit passes.** `outline/volume-02.md` was written after this file, as `PHASE_SYSTEM.md:179` requires.

**1. The weekdays, and why the earlier record was short by eight.** The state layer and the audit prompt both named four instances: Chapter 4, Chapter 23, Chapter 33 and Chapter 40. A sweep of all fifty chapters found **fourteen** lines carrying weekday names in **nine** chapters. The missing eight were Chapter 11 (*on Saturday*), Chapter 35 (*on Monday*), Chapter 36 (*on Saturday*), Chapter 37 (*on Friday*), and four in Chapter 38 (*on Saturday* twice, *the Saturday tomorrow*, *his market was on the Saturday*). This is the failure class the batch reviews have produced four times: a state file asserting a clean sweep that had not been run. The audit ran the sweep.

Ten of the fourteen were **derivable from the page** and were given the numbered day the chapters already fix: Chapter 35's trench was dug on **the fifteenth**, when the Crown gang pegged the low lane; Chapter 36 and Chapter 37 both point at **the twenty-first**, the round; Chapter 33's three lines become **the nineteenth**, which is what Marda Hodler's own arithmetic fixes — *it costs me four days to wait*, counting the four days of her own life on which she does not move, the sixteenth, seventeenth, eighteenth and nineteenth. Chapter 38's four lines become **the twentieth**, the market day that Chapter 38 itself puts at tomorrow, and the day-book's becomes *his market was the twentieth*.

Four were **not derivable**, and inventing a numbered day for them would have been the worse error, so the day-anchor was removed instead and the reason is on the page: Chapter 4's *It was about Tuesday* became **market day**, which is what Chapter 38 anchors and is a day that recurs rather than a day of the month; Chapter 11's *A boy said it on Saturday* became **at the landing**, because no chapter dates the boy; Chapter 23's chandler's *from Monday* became **from the start of next week**, which keeps it a forward reference and keeps the sentence cruel; Chapter 40's *I am not going to on Saturday either* became **at the next one either**, which is a refusal with no date in it, which is what Tamsin is actually saying. **The Chapter 23 record in `state/continuity.md` was extended, not dropped**, as required, and now carries all thirteen with the replacement used in each case.

**2. The Crown clapper.** This is the finding that mattered most, because it is the only one that would have told the next volume a thing the volume has not earned. Foreman Crane's refusal to answer a warning on Day 16 turns on what he believes the last warning was carried by, and he named the mechanism in his own mouth. Three state files then asserted the opposite. The repair keeps Crane's reasoning exactly and removes the name: *a clapper nobody in this ward put in, and I have still not worked out whose it was.* The fact is on the page from Chapter 1 — the carrier rings without being struck — and the *whose* is still open, which is the whole of what Volume 02 is allowed to inherit. `state/batch-0004-summary.md` had quoted the old sentence, and was corrected with the chapter.

**3 and 4. Ages, and the line the audit drew.** Eight ages were removed, all of them cases where a listed character had **one** age on the page and nothing to support it: Mrs Corris at `ch39:75`, `ch40:21`, `ch40:106` and `ch50:169`; Foreman Brann at `ch31:17` and `ch46:189`; Mrs Wilde at `ch45:155`; and the household of forty-four at `ch42:175` — eight lines in seven chapters. **`ch40:21` was missed on the first pass of this audit and was caught only when a second reader went looking for instances the first pass had not already fixed.** That is the failure this file exists to record, and it is why the sweep is repeated rather than trusted. The line the audit drew, and states plainly so that it is not a hidden judgement: **a single stray instance of an age for a listed character is a defect and is repaired; a recurring narrator's estimate that five batch reviews have already seen is a characterization and is recorded for the author.** Mercy Slee's *about forty* (finding 14) falls on the second side of that line, and the four month names (finding 13) are a rule-compliance question rather than a prose defect. Both are recorded with chapter and line. **No later writer may treat either as settled.**

**5 to 11. The clock, and what a state file is not allowed to do with an hour — including when the state file is right and the chapter is wrong.** The rule is the volume's own: the numerals run 1 to 12 twice a day, and **a state file must not attach a half-day to an hour that no chapter attaches one to.** Four Day 22–24 errors and two Day 28 hour-numbers were in the state layer, and one Day 28 sequence was in the wrong order — the trial handbell at the third hour of the afternoon had been placed *before* the arrests that end at the second. All six are fixed to the chapter and the whole batch clock has been rewritten as the page gives it. Two things were deliberately **not** tidied: Chapter 44's *fourth hour* and *eighth hour*, which carry no half-day, are now recorded as carrying none; and Chapter 48's two hours for Wenna Cray — *the fifth hour* on her own record and *about a quarter to six* in the lane — are both recorded and the chapter's ambiguity is declared. The state files' third figure, *about a quarter past five*, was on the page nowhere and is gone.

**10a. The hour of the volume's climactic ringing, and the near-miss this audit is most glad it caught.** Three state files said the warning was rung at *twenty minutes to five*. The first pass of this audit read two lines of Chapter 48, decided the state layer was wrong, and rewrote five files to *twenty minutes to the sixth hour*. **It was one pass too lucky.** Chapter 48 gives the hour three ways: Cray's own written record and the narration of the act both say the sixth hour; the district's finding, Tomas's written certification and the closing reflection all say the fifth. The state files had adopted the second figure because the second figure is on the page. Left alone, the audit would have handed Volume 02 a state layer that was certain and a chapter that was not. **The arbiter is Jerm's watch-book entry at `ch48:91`, stamped the fifth hour, which is consistent with 5.40 and not with 4.40**; the two statements of the act beat three restatements of it; and a Crown finding and a maker's certification are documents that have to agree with the record they are both read out of. The three lines were aligned to *twenty minutes to the sixth hour in the morning*. **The general rule this adds: a state file that agrees with part of a chapter is not thereby right, and neither is a chapter that disagrees with itself. Both have to be read whole before either is corrected.**

**12. Where the outline was wrong and the prose was right.** The brief for this phase is explicit that a volume is not rewritten to make an outline true, so all six disagreements are corrected in `outline/volume-01.md` and none in the chapters. The substantive ones are the two attributions. The outline says **Tomas saves one child**; Chapter 48 spends a hundred and fifty words refusing that sentence in print — *Nobody in Kells may say the bell did that* and *A bell cannot get a Crown officer to leave a door alone, and the reason this boy is in his kitchen is that a cart did not come up a hill* — and the outline said **Tomas saves the surviving register fragment**, when the fragment was taken off a bench by Bevin Sarr, who is on no list, and handed to Nell Fenn by a man who was told to move on and did not. **Both were done by other people and the volume says so.** The outline now says so too.

**Not changed, and deliberately.** No plot, no card, no day map, no character outcome, no ending, and no volume-01 direction. Tomas gains no stage and perceives nothing; Tamsin is not rescued and her deafness is permanent; the Crown's answer about the persons under seventeen is answered in print and the answer is a form; the planned ending of the series is untouched; **no new final enemy was introduced**; **Pell Rill remains a Volume 02 adversary to be earned and not summoned**; and **OFF. 411 is still a mark on four papers in four hands and a district renewal desk, not a man.**

**Boundaries this audit held to.** It did not answer how an office gets into a bell. It did not name the Crown clapper, the higher official, the Silent Muster's name-transfer mechanism, or any person in OFF. 411. It did not settle Iven Sore's service entry. It did not restore the Reckoning 619 bench-book or invent a surviving copy of it, and it added no fact about the night that a witness could not have — **the only local record of that year is gone, and Oren Pike's Day 11 statement about the gate book remains what it has always been, a record about a record.** It did not restore Tamsin's hearing, rescue her, treat her as exposed, or let anybody hold her deafness over her or ask her for a reason. It gave Tomas no new stage, no new sense, and no way of knowing where a warning landed. It brought no Master Key, charter, commission or Crown licence into existence. It edited no controller file, and `state/phase-ledger.json` is controller-owned, is stale by design, is not read by the runner, and is not described as authoritative in any file written here.
