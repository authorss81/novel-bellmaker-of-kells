## Repair notes

**1 is the finding that would have propagated a lie.** The prompt the Batch 0005 writer left behind was not a writing prompt at all; it was a volume's worth of invented structure with no volume outline behind it, sitting in the one unmarked `PROMPT.md` in the tree, which is exactly what `find workspace -name PROMPT.md | sort` dispatches next. It has been deleted. The replacement is `workspace/volume-01/audit/PROMPT.md`, and it does the work `PHASE_SYSTEM.md` reserves for this phase: audit `outline/volume-01.md` against the produced summaries, write `reviews/volume-01/volume-audit.md`, **and only then** create `outline/volume-02.md`, `outline/batches/volume-02-batch-0001.md` and exactly one Chapters 51–60 prompt. It carries forward every fact the deleted prompt had researched, so nothing is lost, and it states the rule the deleted prompt broke: **where the volume and the outline disagree, the fix is a finding in the audit file and a correction to the outline, not a rewrite of the prose.**

**2 and 3, the weekdays.** Chapter 48 is the one that mattered, because the whole scene is a Crown document and a cart, and the document had to say a day. The day is **the twenty-sixth**: it is the day the district file already printed ("delivered to Fourmile on the twenty-sixth"), the day Teage's brother-in-law was to bring the cart, and the day the cart did not come. The repair makes the scene *tighter* than it was, because the Crown's own stamp and the household's own hand now name the same day and disagree about what happened on it, which is the entire point of the chapter. Chapter 50's child is no longer leaving on a named weekday either; he is leaving because a cart did not come on the morning his father's form named, which is the sentence the volume has been building to.

**5, 6 and 12, the clock.** Chapter 49's Day 28 now runs: the fifth schedule at the tenth hour, the arrests from the tenth hour to the second of the afternoon, the trial handbell produced in the market court at about the **third**, the Crown's party up the Bellhouse lane at half past three, the burning at the **fourth**, and the sealing at the fifth. The three state files that had copied the internal error now carry the corrected hour, and the Crown's own inventory — a document, not a narrator — says the fourth hour, which is what makes the fix authoritative.

**7, the sound off the river.** The prose is kept; the hedge is not. *"He heard — or did not hear, and he could not have said afterwards which of the two it was"* is the only sentence in the batch that reads as a second sense, and it is the sentence that made three state files' flat claim untraceable. It now reads as what it almost certainly was: a man in a dark workshop hearing one sound off the water, unable to identify it, with nobody near enough to ask. The chapter's last line — *and nothing else* — lands harder for it. The state files no longer claim the batch contains no perception of any kind; they say precisely what is on the page and what is not read in it.

**13, two clerks.** This one had teeth. `open-threads.md` named **Ansel Stroud** as the man who admitted to merging divided answers for eleven years, while the same file said Stroud appears nowhere in Chapters 41–50 — and Chapter 50 says only *a filing clerk*. Stroud is the district clerk who took the column questions in Chapters 36 and 37. He is not that man. All four state files now keep them separate and forbid attaching a name to the filing clerk that no chapter has attached.

**17, Mrs Corris.** The rule is not "she has no age"; it is "the narrator does not supply one." She says *I am sixty-five* in her own hand on her own Crown return and *I am sixty-five* in her own mouth in a full market court, and a woman in a lane repeats it back — all of which is on the page and is canonical. What was removed is the narrator supplying it four more times as a fact about her. The remaining four instances are attributed, and `character-state.md` now records that the age rests on her own statement and nothing else.

**19, the supplementary returns.** Three returns on the board on the twenty-fifth is correct: 114 and 133 answered by the morning of the twenty-third, and 121 was written on the afternoon of the twenty-fourth, which Chapter 45 dates. Chapter 41 was the file that was wrong, and it now says two.

**24, Ocklebridge and Marta Vell.** The review's objection was not to the chapter. It was that a named place and a named person appear for the first time in the volume's last two pages and in **no state file at all** — so the next phase would have inherited a road with a village on it and no record of either. All three state files now carry them, with the age attributed to the only person who gives it and with the live thread stated plainly: **her water bell has been entered on a schedule in a building in Fourmile as a bell of this ward with no keeper against it, and nobody has told her, and Tomas did not.**

**26 and 27, the filler words, fixed in part and honestly.** `wrote` is not mechanical filler in this novel. It is a novel about day-books, custody receipts, ward books, gang's books, an arrest record and a *Witnessed by* column, and most of its instances name a real act of writing a real document. Mechanically stripping it to four a chapter would have cost the prose more than it gained. What was removed is the nine repetitions that were doing nothing: Chapter 47 from 24 to 20, Chapter 49 from 25 to 21, Chapter 50 from 29 to 24. `counted` in Chapter 45 went from six in twenty lines to two, and the surviving pair — the thesis at the top and the echo at the bottom — is the point of the passage. `steady`, `breathed` and `nodded` are at zero across the batch and `plain` at two.

**28, the Chapter 43 two-hander, kept.** It is forty lines and it does recap. It is also the only scene in the volume in which Oren Pike states in his own mouth that he built a town out of names without entering a ward, that he supplied all four documents himself, and that the remedy was to put his own arithmetic in the record where a district could use it against him. Tomas pushes back at line 121 and Oren changes his decision at line 123. A recap that ends in a decision is not a recap. It was assessed and left alone, and the review file says so rather than pretending the finding did not exist.

**Two defects the review did not catch, found and fixed here.** `ch43:95` had a doubled conjunction — *arrived in this ward in the same two weeks, **and And** I would have told you* — now corrected. And `state/open-threads.md` had been carrying a shell heredoc's leftovers, so the whole of `state/`, `bible/`, `outline/`, `reviews/` and `workspace/` was swept for that class of accident; the sweep is clean, and the audit prompt tells the next phase to run it again before it saves anything.
