## Found by this pass, not by the reviewer

**11. A DAY-BOOK BLOCK PRINTED TWICE IN CHAPTER 0168, 110 WORDS IDENTICAL, ON ADJACENT LINES INSIDE ONE ENTRY — the most serious defect in the batch, and no standing test could see it.** The third column appeared twice. The copies were not byte-identical: the second carried one extra clause about the bank at the seventh hour and used a different pronoun for the rule. **That is the only reason it survived, and the reason is structural** — a byte-identical duplicate is caught instantly by the per-line bold-marker parity test, because a duplicated bold span is an odd marker count, and a copy that differs by one clause is even on every line and passes everything. The paragraph-pair method is blind to it by construction, because a blockquote entry is one paragraph and both copies were inside it. **The redundant copy is deleted and the superset kept whole, so nothing is lost and the column sequence is intact.** This is the third duplicated-block defect in this project and the third no standing test could see (§10l finding 1 was 110 duplicated words in `state/current.md`). **The line-level near-duplicate scan is now a standing test on every chapter and every state file, at 25 words a line and a 20-word shared run.** A whole-file scan of all twenty Volume 04 chapters and every state file returns Chapter 0168 and nothing else in the prose; Chapter 0158's 39 words is the standing fourth clause already audited at §10h, §10i and §10l.
