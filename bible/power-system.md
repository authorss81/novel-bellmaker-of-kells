# Power System: Bell Resonance

## Core principle

The world's magic is **bell resonance**: a bell can carry a claim about the world when a living person has personally accepted responsibility for making that claim. The bell does not verify the claim. It verifies that a person was willing to stand behind it.

A promise, warning, or apology is therefore a form of sound with a social cost. A bell can say, “I know the west bridge is unsafe and I accept the duty to warn before anyone crosses.” It cannot say, “The west bridge is unsafe” as an oracle. The difference is the reason a false warning is possible and why the network can be abused.

The system is called **resonance**, not magic. A bell's tone, the metal's memory, and the physical route create the effect. There are no combat spells, invisible energy meters, or instant solutions to social problems.

## What a bound bell requires

A bell becomes a useful carrier when three conditions are present:

1. **A witness matter:** the maker tunes the bell around a real object, place, or act that can be physically pointed to. A bridge pin, market key, flood mark, or a strip of cloth from a public register is enough. The object does not possess a person or a soul.
2. **A keeper's acceptance:** one living person knowingly accepts a specific responsibility. “I will return with the missing tally,” “I will warn before the flood,” or “I acknowledge that I was wrong” can be carried. “I deserve to be loved” cannot.
3. **An answer:** a second person who understands the claim may answer, refuse, repair, or ask for help. A local public signal normally requires three independent answers. A private handbell may have only one.

The keeper must be free enough to withdraw. If a person is threatened, drugged, paid under threat, or told that refusal will destroy their family, the bell records a **forced echo**: the appearance of an answer without a freely accepted responsibility. Forced echoes can be detected, but they can still be amplified by a Crown clapper.

A bell has one primary claim, but a warning may have two people in its chain of responsibility. The person who knows or intends the underlying action can accept the claim; a different person can accept the duty to warn, refuse, or delay. The keeper does not have to believe every fact in the claim. The opening warning works because Dain accepts responsibility for the planned operation while Mara accepts responsibility for exposing it. Their roles meet in one bell without creating two competing commands.

## Three kinds of bell

### Promise bells

A promise bell carries an accepted commitment. It rings when the commitment is fulfilled, deliberately broken, or formally released by the people who made it. A promise is not a contract enforced by the metal. A person can be late, afraid, imprisoned, or unable to keep it; the bell records the broken state and the circumstances must be investigated.

A promise bell cannot promise that someone will love, obey, or never change. It can carry a promise to return, to tell the truth, to leave a bridge unbarred, or to publish a record.

### Warning bells

A warning bell carries a danger its keeper accepts responsibility for naming. It may ring before the danger occurs because the keeper has already accepted the duty to warn before it arrives. That makes a future warning possible without prophecy.

A warning must identify a real threat or a clearly bounded uncertainty. “The north road is unsafe” is too broad to be useful unless the keeper says what they know. “The north bridge has lost its west support; do not send the evening cart until I have checked” is a legitimate accepted warning.

A warning bell cannot tell a listener what decision to make. It can say what the keeper knows, what they are promising to do, and when their duty changes. A panic created by an unclear warning is a social failure, not proof that the bell lied.

### Apology bells

An apology bell carries an unfinished acknowledgment: “I was there,” “I did this,” or “I will answer for the part I chose.” It can be offered to a living person, a family, or a community. It does not grant forgiveness, restore a dead person, or prove that the apology is complete. The bell is useful because it makes a private acknowledgment difficult to conceal forever.

## What bells cannot do

Bells cannot:

- read thoughts, motives, memories, or hidden identities;
- reveal objective guilt or a complete account of a past event;
- predict a future that no living person has accepted responsibility for;
- compel a person to speak, move, forgive, obey, or remain alive;
- raise the dead or speak with a dead person's consent;
- create a promise that the maker did not witness being accepted;
- guarantee that a warning is accurate, useful, or obeyed;
- repair a broken road, cure a disease, or substitute for a trained physician;
- make a person safe merely because a powerful ruler owns the bell.

Tomas can inspect whether a claim was freely accepted, contradicted, or copied. He cannot inspect whether the claim is true in every factual detail.

## The four-step craft

1. **Cast:** melt and work the bellmetal around the chosen witness. Poor alloy, a cracked mould, or a hurried pour makes an unreliable carrier.
2. **Tune:** shave and shape the lip until the note can be heard without forcing. A bell that is too bright can overpower nearby answers; a dull bell may be safe but useless.
3. **Bind:** state the bounded claim aloud or in the ordinary ritual of the workshop, name the keeper, and let the maker step back. A bound bell cannot safely be made by a maker who secretly wants a different answer.
4. **Test and install:** test refusal before crisis. A bell that only works when everyone agrees is not a consent bell; it is a crowd instrument and will fail under pressure.

The maker may later mend, retune, or unbind a bell. A forced echo should be exposed and the answer withdrawn, not quietly replaced with a more flattering sound.

## The physical network

A bell is local. A signal can travel farther only through a physical route:

- **bellwire:** braided copper laid in shallow channels along roads and walls;
- **water channels:** fast-running, carefully tuned water can carry a short resonance between river towns;
- **stone anchors:** cut stones that preserve a note across a gap;
- **handbells:** portable carriers used by couriers;
- **relay bells:** a bell that repeats a received sound, adding its own small distortion.

A route has a direction and a cost. Broken wire, a missing keeper, a damp anchor, or a human refusal can interrupt it. A message can be repeated but not edited after the first toll. A relay may refuse to forward a message, and a network that ignores refusals becomes coercive.

A clapperless or stripped bell can still be driven by a connected route pulse or by another bell, but wind and ordinary vibration cannot ring it. This is how a disabled Crown carrier can sound inside a locked workshop without implying that the object is alive or self-directing.

## Living anchors

A living anchor is a person physically connected to a major route or the First Answer chamber. Their accepted memory of a route can stabilize a relay when no keeper is present, keeping a signal from dissolving into conflicting noise. The anchor does not become a mind-reading battery and does not have to know every message carried by the route. Their contribution is limited to the route they inhabit and can be overwhelmed if they are ill, asleep, or cut off from the anchor stone.

An anchor must accept the arrangement while able to withdraw. A coerced anchor is a forced echo with a body attached. The Crown's use of Lira Vale as an anchor is therefore an abuse even though she once made a real, limited choice to keep the damaged network alive. Removing an anchor can release stored memories and may break every route that depended on it; it is not a free rescue button.

## Holding rings and temporary wards

A **holding ring** is a small arrangement of bells, wire, and an anchor that holds a boundary steady for a limited time. It can keep a flood gate from slamming shut, hold a bridge section in place, or keep a forced answer from spreading through one room. It does not create a general magical barrier.

A holding ring lasts from a few dozen breaths to half a day, depending on the bell's strength and the number of keepers. It requires a living keeper and a physical anchor. If the keeper withdraws, the ring fails. The maker pays a sensory cost, and the people protected by the ring must still maintain the physical object.

## Costs and failure

Every useful resonance has a human or material price.

- **Casting cost:** heat, metal, labor, damaged tools, and risk of permanent hand or lung injury.
- **Listening cost:** sustained reading causes ringing ears, nosebleeds, vertigo, and a temporary inability to distinguish nearby bells.
- **Memory cost:** a deep read or a forced route can blur a personal memory. The cost is chosen by the maker, so a person can give up a skill memory, a face, a route, or a piece of their own history. No cost is refunded by later growth.
- **Relational cost:** a warning can be true and still damage a family, a trade route, or a town's trust. The craft cannot promise that the right choice feels good.
- **Civic cost:** every relay needs maintenance, witnesses, and a public process for refusal. The system is strongest where people maintain it together and weakest where one person claims to maintain it for everyone.

A bell fails in specific ways:

- **Unbound bell:** ordinary sound with no memory beyond its own ringing.
- **Echoed bell:** a sound repeats without a current keeper; listeners feel old emotion but receive no reliable claim.
- **Copied bell:** a Crown clapper repeats one answer as if many people had made it.
- **Conflicted bell:** two accepted claims compete, producing a wavering or contradictory tone. It must be separated, not forced into agreement.
- **Broken route:** a bell rings locally while the message dies halfway along the line.
- **Compromised bell:** the keeper has changed their mind but fears the cost of withdrawal. The system records the conflict, not a magical excuse.

There is no automatic reset and no hidden ability that repairs a failure without a person changing the network.

## Tomas's power stages

These are stages of practice and responsibility, not visible levels. Tomas may be competent at a stage and still be defeated by a person who knows a different rule.

### Stage 0 — Hand

Tomas can cast ordinary bells, read a material flaw, and keep a small workshop safe. He has no supernatural reading and can still be deceived by a well-made Crown bell. This is his starting competence, not a deficiency.

### Stage 1 — Near Toll

Tomas can bind one short, clearly bounded vow to one bell in a single location. He can hear whether a nearby answer is strained, but cannot identify the person behind it or the emotion causing the strain. Binding one claim makes the bell unavailable for another until the claim is answered or deliberately unmade. The generic Stage 1 cost is time, a risk of a failed small casting or damaged tool, and a single episode of ringing ears; a particularly demanding first binding may incur both material costs. Stage 1 does not permanently blur a personal memory; that cost belongs to the deeper reading introduced at Stage 2.

### Stage 2 — Listening Ear

By careful practice and failed readings, Tomas can hear the emotional and physical context of a recorded sound. He can distinguish a freely accepted answer from a forced echo after the fact, though a sincere lie still sounds sincere. Deep reading costs temporary deafness and may permanently blur one personal memory. He cannot use the ability to interrogate a mind.

### Stage 3 — Threadwright

Tomas can carry a signal through several linked bells and identify the first weak link. He can protect a small room with a holding ring. A route longer than his concentration becomes noisy, and a false answer at one relay contaminates the next. The cost is physical exhaustion, a lost route memory, and a new obligation to maintain the lines he opens.

### Stage 4 — Mender

Tomas can expose a forced echo and sever a single copied duty without destroying the underlying bell. He must identify the false transfer and take responsibility for the consequences of the break. The cost is legal and moral exposure: he can be sued, ostracized, or ordered to repair what he has unmade. This stage makes him a target of the Quiet Office.

### Stage 5 — Hearthmaker

Tomas can design a local route with at least three independent keepers, including a maker, a resident witness, and a public record keeper. The route can carry a warning or a request for help, but it cannot accept an answer on behalf of someone who has not answered. Tomas must share control and accept that a node can withdraw. He loses the right to keep the only master key.

### Stage 6 — Roadcaster

Tomas can send one unalterable warning across several wards using redundant routes and human relays. He cannot choose who hears it or force a response. Each journey takes a physical toll on the relay keepers, and a message can be delayed by a refusal. The power is less impressive than a command and more useful than a rumor.

### Stage 7 — Unbinder

Tomas can cut a Crown mandate at its point of transfer, provided he can name the original obligation and show where consent was manufactured. He cannot release a freely chosen vow, and he becomes liable for the immediate danger created by the cut. At this stage, his ability to carry a personal promise is permanently reduced; the network can call on him, but he cannot call on it as a private extension of himself.

### Stage 8 — Many-Handed Keeper

Tomas can coordinate a network of independent nodes and hear the condition of a whole route, not the private thoughts of its people. He is outvoted or ignored by three local answers if he tries to override them. The cost is constant contact with other people's needs and the loss of a private, authoritative relationship with sound. He becomes a facilitator rather than a ruler.

### Stage 9 — Open Answer

In the final volume, Tomas can make a bell that carries an accepted refusal and help separate the First Answer's central relay from the local network. “Unmade” means the bell has no permanent central keeper or fixed claim; it can receive one freely accepted refusal when Tomas chooses to use it, but it cannot be preloaded with a command. He cannot make a universal answer or compel the realm. Creating the Open Answer costs him the direct hearing that has defined his gift, including the magical clarity of his mother's voice. He can still work with metal and people, but the magical part of his ear must now be carried by others.

## System presentation

There is no game interface and no visible stat sheet. When Tomas receives a strong impression from a bell, the prose may contain one short in-world line, such as:

> The answer was copied.

Use this device sparingly. A System-like panel is allowed only when it expresses a concrete rule the protagonist must act on; it never lists statistics, skills, rewards, or probabilities. The world itself supplies the information through sound, work, witnesses, and consequences.
