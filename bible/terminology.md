# Terminology Bible

## Naming discipline

Use the terms below consistently in prose. Keep ordinary physical words ordinary: a bell is not a weapon by default, a clerk is not secretly a mage, and a network is not an abstraction until a person maintains a wire or turns a wheel. Avoid game language, numerical ranks, and vague terms such as “energy,” “mana,” “system,” or “chosen one.” If a term is not in this file, add it to the bible only after its rule and limits are clear.

## Core craft terms

### Bell

A cast metal instrument that produces a tone. Most bells mark ordinary time, work, weather, mourning, or public gathering. A bell becomes **bound** when it carries an accepted responsibility. An unbound bell still matters, but it carries no claim beyond the physical sound.

### Bellmetal

The copper-and-tin alloy used for most bells, with a small controlled amount of lead added by the maker. Bellmetal is a material, not a magical mineral. Its tone depends on casting, cooling, shape, and tuning.

### Bellhouse

A workshop and watch-room for bells. In Kells, the Vale Bellhouse is Tomas's home, workplace, and archive. A bellhouse may be a public building, a private shop, a monastery room, or a roadside shelter.

### Bellwire

Braided copper used to relay a tone between bells. It is buried in shallow stone channels or strung along a protected wall. A break in a route stops a signal; it does not erase the bell's memory.

### Bellframe

A wooden or metal frame that holds a bell, clapper, and tuning pins. A Crown clapper is a special fitting, not a normal part of a bell.

### Casting

The process of melting and pouring bellmetal. Casting can injure a maker and can produce a bell that is permanently out of tune.

### Tuning

The physical shaping and shaving of a bell's sound. Tuning is slow work, and a bad tune can be more dangerous than a quiet bell.

### Binding

The deliberate act of giving a bell one bounded accepted claim and a living keeper. Binding is not ownership. The keeper can answer, withdraw, or be released only through a recognized process.

### Mending

Repairing a damaged bell or route. Mending can preserve a claim, change its tone, or expose that its answer was forced.

### Unmaking

Carefully releasing a binding rather than destroying the bell. Unmaking is difficult and can leave a dangerous unresolved sound. The final **Unmade Bell** is cast without a permanent central keeper or fixed claim; it can carry one freely accepted refusal at the moment of use, but it is not a magical bomb.

### Clapper

The moving part inside a bell that strikes its inner wall. A normal clapper follows the bell's tuning. A Crown clapper repeats a registered answer through a route.

## Claim and consent terms

### Vow

A specific responsibility accepted by a living person. A vow is not a wish, a legal oath by default, or a feeling. “I will bring back the count by sunset” may be a vow. “I wish to be loved” is not.

### Keeper

The person who accepts and maintains a vow or a route. A keeper can be a citizen, a watchman, a maker, a farmer, or a child acting within a clearly limited duty. Ownership of the bell does not make someone its keeper.

### Witness

A person who understands enough of a claim to answer it, question it, or refuse it. Witnesses are active participants, not decorative listeners.

### Answer

A free response to a bell. An answer may be agreement, refusal, a request for time, a repair, an offer of help, or a statement that the message is unclear. “Yes” is only one possible answer. A valid answer requires an understanding of the claim and a meaningful ability to withdraw.

### Forced echo

A response that a Crown clapper or an institution repeats as though a whole community had freely accepted it. A forced echo can sound identical to a free answer, but it is brittle, repetitive, and difficult to sustain. Tomas can detect a forced echo; he cannot detect every lie inside a genuinely free answer.

### Plain bell

An unbound bell used for ordinary sound, work, or a local signal. Plain bells are the fallback when a network fails and the final form of public warning. They do not carry a magical claim.

### Plain voice

A message that states what is known, identifies who is responsible for the next action, and asks for an answer. It never tells a community what its answer must be.

### Warning

A claim that a named or bounded danger exists and a keeper accepts responsibility for naming it. A warning can concern a future event if the keeper has accepted the duty to warn before it arrives.

### Promise

A claim that a named person or group will do a specific thing. The bell can mark fulfillment, breach, or release, but it cannot compel performance.

### Apology

An accepted acknowledgment of a person's part in an act or failure. An apology bell records the acknowledgment; it cannot force forgiveness or erase consequences.

## Network and place terms

### Mereward

The river country where the story begins. It contains Kells, Sennet, the nine historic wards, and the roads between them.

### Kells

A western highland town and Tomas's home. The Kells Water, the north gate, the Low Ward, and the Vale Bellhouse anchor the opening volumes.

### Sennet

The capital and seat of the Crown. Its old First Answer chamber is the central relay used by the mandate network.

### Ward

A historic circuit of roads, bellhouses, and settlements. A ward is geographic and social, not a magical barrier. A local community may share a ward while refusing a common answer.

### Hearthline

The original federated network of the nine wards. It was designed for local answers, physical redundancy, and withdrawal. The Crown later converted parts of it into a centralized mandate system.

### First Answer

The oldest surviving relay chamber. Originally a place to compare local answers during emergencies, it is now sealed inside Sennet and used as the Crown's central authority. It cannot be described as a god, machine, or living monster; it is a network of people, metal routes, and stored claims.

### Hush

The network failure of Reckoning 509. “Hushed” means that bells and routes have stopped carrying reliable answers. It does not mean every bell is physically broken, and it does not mean the old history is fully known.

### Holding ring

A temporary physical arrangement of bells and an anchor that holds a boundary or keeps a forced response from spreading. It is not a general shield and cannot stop an army.

### Route

A physical connection for a signal. A route can be broken, redirected, delayed, or withdrawn. “Network” refers to the maintained collection of routes; it never implies that every bell is magically connected.

### Relay

A bell or handbell that receives and repeats a signal. A relay may distort the tone, add its local witness, or refuse to forward the message.

## Faction terms

### Crown of Sennet

The legal and military government. It maintains infrastructure and uses the bell network to coordinate public life. The Crown remains in the aftermath but loses the legal power to turn a bell response into a universal command.

### Quiet Office

The bureau that records people, property, public duties, and bell answers. It is the central institutional antagonist of the early volumes, not a single secret order with one mind.

### Bellwrights' Measure

The craft guild of bellmakers, tuners, and signal-ringers. It maintains craft standards and road maps. Its internal dispute over licensing and consent shapes the middle volumes.

### Hearthkeepers

Local people who maintain a route and collect independent answers. The title does not guarantee wisdom, consent, or moral goodness.

### The Unrung

A loose movement that removes coercive or unreliable bells. The Unrung include careful saboteurs and people who oppose all central infrastructure. Their arguments must be taken seriously even when their tactics are destructive.

### River Cantons

Trade towns along the Mere River. They want coordinated warnings and protection from the Crown without surrendering local control.

### Cloister of Orra

A mountain archive and repair house that preserves Hearthline inscriptions and flood records. Its records are valuable but incomplete and politically situated.

## Historical and plot terms

### Quiet Edict

The Reckoning 493 law that allowed Crown clerks to answer for households and settlements. It is the legal foundation for the forced-answer system.

### Renewal

The Crown campaign requiring households to renew a civic oath. Refusal removes legal standing and can lead to seizure of land, tools, or children.

### Silent Muster

The planned Kells operation that begins the novel. It would remove unregistered children from the Low Ward, use their names as relay anchors, and burn the bellhouse archive. The opening warning bell describes its intended effects before the operation occurs.

### Name levy

The Crown practice of treating a person's legal name as a transferable resource used to settle debts, duties, and network obligations. It is not magic by itself; it becomes magical when a registry transfers the name into a bell.

### Crown clapper

A bronze fitting designed to repeat one accepted answer as a civic mandate. It can make a local signal travel farther and can force a false consensus. Removing it does not automatically restore trust.

### First Watcher

The historical name for the keeper who guards a local Hearthline route. The title is mostly ceremonial by the present day; Tomas is not the First Watcher and does not inherit the role.

## Canon distinctions

- **A vow is not an oath.** A vow is the private act of accepting responsibility; an oath is a public or legal form that may or may not be free.
- **An answer is not obedience.** Refusal is an answer.
- **A bell is not a person.** It carries a claim and a route; it does not want, judge, or own the keeper.
- **Resonance is not mind reading.** It can carry accepted sound and emotional context, not a complete private mind.
- **A warning is not prophecy.** It can concern a future event because someone accepts the duty to warn before the event.
- **A mandate is not consent.** It is a forced echo amplified by a Crown route.
- **A plain bell is not weak.** It is the fallback that preserves ordinary sound when a magical route fails.
- **A maker is not the owner of a message.** The maker's responsibility is to make the claim accurate as far as the available evidence allows and to preserve the answer's limits.

## Usage rules in prose

- Capitalize proper nouns and formal offices only: Hearthline, Crown, Quiet Office, Bellwrights' Measure, First Answer. Lowercase generic terms such as warning bell, keeper, route, and forced echo.
- Prefer concrete verbs: ring, cast, file, cut, answer, withdraw, mend, route, copy, file, refuse.
- Use “heard” for a person and “carried” for a bell. A bell does not “know,” “want,” or “see.”
- Avoid repeatedly calling every sound an echo. Reserve “forced echo” for a specific political and magical failure.
- Do not use “level,” “skill tree,” “cooldown,” “status,” “system,” or numerical ranks. Describe the practice, the limit, and the cost.
- If a new term is introduced, it must have a plain-language meaning, a hard limit, and at least one example in the relevant bible or outline.
